HEAD AND NECK CANCER IN CHILDREN

not adult SCC: lymphoma, sarcoma, neural, thyroid · recognise the lump that does not behave, take the right tissue, keep the airway safe ✎

1What it is

  • Second commonest childhood death after trauma · ~12% of childhood cancers in H&N · SCC extremely rare

2The books differ

Difference = series and age range · in practice: lymphoma first · under 2: RMS and neuroblastoma

3Red flags

  • Node > about 2 cm, growing, or not shrinking after about 6 weeks
  • Supraclavicular at any size · hard, fixed, matted
  • Fever, sweats, weight loss, pallor, bruising, liver/spleen
  • Stridor, dysphagia, cranial nerve palsy, Horner, proptosis
  • One-sided nasal/nasopharyngeal mass · aural polyp + facial weakness
Examine ears, nose, mouth, whole neck, axillae, groins, abdomen, skin · eye: proptosis, Horner, heterochromia · re-examine once well

4Investigate

  • Ultrasound first: cystic vs solid, node architecture, guides biopsy
  • FBC + film, LDH, LFT · HVA/VMA if neuroblastoma · EBV if NPC
  • MRI/CT primary + neck · CT, bone scan, MIBG, marrow as oncology directs
Chest X-ray before any GA · anterior mediastinal mass → airway or cardiovascular collapse on induction · anaesthetist + oncologist · local anaesthetic or another site

5Taking tissue

  • Talk to oncologist + pathologist before
  • Lymphoma: excise a whole node, send fresh, not formalin (flow, cytogenetics, molecular)
  • US core if pathologist confident · FNA rarely enough for typing
  • Deep primary: endoscopic or under anaesthetic

6Lymphoma

  • Hodgkin: rubbery supraclavicular nodes · up to a third B symptoms or itch · Ann Arbor · chemo ± RT · early cured ~90%
  • NHL: 2–12, widespread, fast (Burkitt) · B vs T · combination chemo
  • One tonsil much bigger, fast, odd, nodes → tonsillectomy, sent fresh

7Rhabdomyosarcoma

  • Commonest childhood soft tissue sarcoma · ~43% under 5 · H&N commonest site
  • Embryonal ~60% (better, botryoid) · alveolar ~20% (worse)
  • Orbital: proptosis, best · parameningeal (nasopharynx, sinuses, middle ear, ITF): skull base spread, unfavourable · other sites
  • Biopsy + chemo + RT · surgery if no disabling loss · five-year ~90% resected → ~40% metastatic
Bleeding aural polyp + facial palsy in a young child = RMS until proved otherwise · send every polyp

8Neuroblastoma

  • Commonest under 1 · sympathetic chain · only ~5% in neck
  • Firm fixed mass + Horner + heterochromia · stridor, dysphagia, CN palsy
  • HVA/VMA raised ~90% · MRI/CT, MIBG, bone scan, marrow
  • Excise localised · chemo if not · cervical does better (seen early)

9Thyroid and NPC

  • Child nodule more likely malignant · more nodes and lung mets, survival excellent · prior neck radiation
  • MEN 2 + RET → prophylactic thyroidectomy (timed by mutation) · total thyroidectomy + neck ± RAI (ATA 2015)
  • NPC: adolescents, undifferentiated, EBV · nodes, unilateral effusion · DD: RMS, NHL, angiofibroma (boy) · chemoradiotherapy

10Salivary and others

  • At least two thirds non-neoplastic (mucocele, sialadenitis) · benign = vascular > adenoma
  • Solitary solid mass: malignant in more than half (adults about a quarter)
  • Mucoepidermoid about half, then acinic cell · parotid > submandibular · adult management
  • Rare: LCH, teratoma, Ewing, osteosarcoma, olfactory neuroblastoma

11Team and late effects

  • Paediatric oncology centre, protocols · ENT: diagnose, airway, resection/access, ENT effects
  • RT: bone + tooth growth, asymmetry, glue ear, SNHL, cataract, hypothyroid, pituitary, ORN, second cancers
  • Cisplatin: permanent high-frequency loss, worse young · monitor hearing
  • Surgery: deformity, CN deficits, swallow, speech · psychological, educational · lifelong follow-up
  • NICE NG12: very urgent referral for enlarging, hard, fixed nodes or B symptoms

12Exam traps ✗

  • Repeated antibiotics for a hard node
  • Lymphoma node into formalin
  • GA with stridor or mediastinal mass, no CXR
  • Bleeding aural polyp called COM
  • FNA alone for suspected lymphoma
