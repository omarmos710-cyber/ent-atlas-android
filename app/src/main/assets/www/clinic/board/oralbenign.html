BENIGN ORAL LESIONS & PREMALIGNANT PATCHES

mostly benign lumps · then the white and red patches that may become cancer · biopsy the red ✎

1Three groups

  • Benign: solid (papilloma, fibroma, haemangioma, lymphangioma, torus, pyogenic granuloma, granular cell, minor salivary adenoma) · cystic (mucocele, ranula, dermoid)
  • Premalignant lesions + conditions: leukoplakia, erythroplakia, submucous fibrosis…
  • Malignant: SCC + non-squamous
Lump growing, ulcerated, indurated or fixed · patch red, speckled or nodular → biopsy

4Vascular

  • Haemangioma: children · lip, tongue, cheek · red-blue, compressible, bleeds when bitten · capillary, cavernous, mixed · at birth: observe (many regress) · propranolol, steroids · large: embolisation, laser, excision · sclerotherapy poor in diffuse · older adult: phlebectasia
  • Lymphangioma: anterior two-thirds of tongue → macroglossia · does not regress · excise small, debulk large

5Cysts: above or below mylohyoid

  • Mucocele: minor salivary gland, almost always lower lip, after a bite · bluish, translucent, bursts + refills · excise with the gland
  • Ranula: floor beside the frenulum, lifts the tongue · sublingual gland ducts · plunging: through or behind mylohyoid → submandibular/submental swelling
  • Older texts: excise small, marsupialise large · marsupialisation alone: recurrence common → excise with the sublingual gland through the mouth, guard Wharton's duct + lingual nerve · cures most plunging
  • Dermoid: sublingual = above mylohyoid, midline, white · submental = below, bulges under chin · excise through mouth or neck by its relation to the muscle

6Premalignant

  • Lesion = altered tissue (leukoplakia, erythroplakia) · condition = general state (submucous fibrosis, lichen planus, iron deficiency) · WHO: both = oral potentially malignant disorders
  • Also hyperplastic candida, erosive + atrophic lichen planus, reverse-smoking palate, actinic cheilitis, Plummer–Vinson, pigmented lesions + melanosis, discoid lupus

7Leukoplakia

  • WHO: white patch not characterised as any other disease · clinical term, no histology · exclude lichen planus, candida, leukoedema, white sponge naevus
  • Tobacco, areca, alcohol, teeth/dentures, candida, submucous fibrosis, Plummer–Vinson · India: buccal + commissures where the quid sits · also floor, tongue, gingivobuccal sulcus, lips · mostly men, forties
TypeLook
Homogeneousuniform white · lowest risk
Speckled (nodular)white nodules on a red base

PVLmultifocal, warty, relentlessly progressive · older women without the usual risk factors · very high risk
  • Stop habits, remove sharp teeth → many regress · biopsy the reddest, most nodular part to grade dysplasia
  • Mild/none: review, excise if persists or changes · moderate or worse: excise or laser/cryo
  • Chemoprevention: vitamin A, E, beta-carotene, retinoids (13-cis-retinoic acid) → regression in a quarter to a half, may cut second primaries · relapse when stopped · stopping tobacco is best

9Leukoplakia prognosis

  • A quarter dysplastic · higher grade = higher risk
  • Change < 1% to 17.5% · ~5% older series, ~9.5% recent meta-analysis
  • Highest risk: speckled, erosive, verrucous · floor + ventral tongue · long follow-up

11Erythroplakia

  • Velvety red, sharp edge, slightly depressed · floor, ventral tongue, soft palate, gingivobuccal sulcus
  • Vascular submucosa through thin epithelium · most already severe dysplasia, CIS or cancer · risk many times leukoplakia
  • Excision biopsy + close follow-up
Carcinoma lives in erythroplakia + the red of speckled leukoplakia: biopsy the red

12Pigmented lesions

  • Benign melanotic macules + naevi common · about a quarter of mucosal melanomas look benign at first
  • New, enlarging or irregular on palate or gum → biopsy

14Exam traps ✗

  • "Leukoplakia" used as a histological diagnosis
  • Marsupialising a ranula, leaving the sublingual gland
  • Biopsying the white of a speckled leukoplakia, not the red
  • Excising a pregnancy granuloma during pregnancy
