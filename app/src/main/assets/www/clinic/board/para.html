HYPERPARATHYROIDISM

confirm the biochemistry, localise the gland, remember multigland disease, ectopia and FHH ✎

1Two journeys

  • ~5 × 3 × 1 mm, ~40 mg, caramel/mahogany, glides in fat · ~5% supernumerary · mirror images
  • Superior ~1 cm above RLN–ITA crossing · inferior within 1–2 cm of lower pole, often thyrothymic ligament · both fed by ITA (divide branches on the capsule)
  • Ectopic: retro-oesophageal, thymus, intrathyroid, carotid sheath, undescended

2Calcium physiology

  • PTH: bone resorption · renal Ca reabsorption + phosphate loss · 1α-hydroxylation → gut absorption · calcitonin minor
  • Half-life minutes → intraoperative PTH
  • Correct total Ca +0.8 mg/dL (0.2 mmol/L) per 1 g/dL albumin fall · ionised if albumin abnormal

3Primary hyperparathyroidism

  • ~1 in 500 women, 1 in 2000 men, after menopause · neck RT, lithium
  • Single adenoma ~85% · four-gland hyperplasia 10–15% (MEN 1, 2A, familial) · double 2–5% (elderly) · carcinoma < 1%
  • High Ca + raised or inappropriately normal PTH, low phosphate, high urinary Ca · replace low vitamin D

5Secondary, tertiary, carcinoma

  • Secondary: all glands respond to low Ca · CKD, vitamin D lack · phosphate binders, active vitamin D, cinacalcet · refractory: three-and-a-half gland or total + forearm autograft + thymectomy
  • Tertiary: autonomous after correction (renal transplant) → high Ca
  • Carcinoma: Ca often > 14 mg/dL, neck mass, RLN palsy, bone + renal disease · hard grey adherent · en bloc with thyroid lobe, capsule intact

6Who gets surgery

  • Every symptomatic patient
  • Asymptomatic (2022): Ca > 1 mg/dL (0.25 mmol/L) above normal · under 50 · T-score ≤ −2.5 or vertebral fracture · eGFR < 60, stones, nephrocalcinosis, high urinary Ca · can't be followed
  • Many offer it to all fit patients (2016): the only cure

7Localise, don't diagnose

  • Biochemistry diagnoses · scans plan the operation · essential before reoperation
  • US: hypoechoic oval + polar vessel · poor for retrotracheal, mediastinal
  • Sestamibi: adenoma retains tracer after thyroid washout · 85–90% single adenomas · SPECT-CT
  • 4D CT: arterial enhancement, early washout · fluorocholine PET-CT when negative · venous sampling
Concordant → focused · discordant/negative → multigland or ectopic → bilateral exploration, still operate

8The operation

  • Focused: small incision, control pedicle · IOPTH before + 10 minutes after: fall > 50% into normal (Miami) · cures ~95–98%
  • Bilateral: no localisation, multigland, MEN, familial, PTH didn't fall
  • Missing superior: TE groove, retro-oesophageal · missing inferior: thymus, ligament, carotid sheath · then intrathyroid · no blind sternotomy first time
  • Hyperplasia: subtotal (~50 mg remnant, clipped) or total + autograft · thymectomy in MEN 1
  • Hungry bone: deep, long hypocalcaemia + low PO₄, Mg · permanent hypopara 1–5% · RLN 0.4–4%
  • Persistence: missed/second adenoma, hyperplasia, ectopic, parathyromatosis, carcinoma, FHH

9Hypocalcaemia

  • After thyroidectomy, central neck dissection · DiGeorge, Mg deficiency
  • 24–72 hours: perioral tingling → carpopedal spasm, tetany, laryngospasm, seizures, long QT
  • Chvostek (5–10% normal: record pre-op) · Trousseau: cuff above systolic three minutes
  • Post-op PTH predicts · mild: oral Ca + calcitriol · severe: 10% calcium gluconate 10–20 mL over ten minutes → infusion, monitor, fix Mg
  • > six months = permanent · low-normal target · prevent: capsular dissection, autotransplant, autofluorescence

10Exam traps ✗

  • Operating on FHH
  • Negative scans as a reason not to operate
  • Stopping after one adenoma in MEN 1
  • Rupturing the capsule → parathyromatosis
