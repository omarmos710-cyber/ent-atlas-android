ANAESTHESIA & THE SHARED AIRWAY

surgeon and anaesthetist share the airway · plan it together, before the patient arrives ✎

1Why ENT is high-risk

  • NAP4 (2011): ~two in five major airway complications in head, neck, trachea disease · often no plan for difficulty, failed front-of-neck access
  • CICO ~a quarter of anaesthesia-related deaths
  • Know: the bleeding tonsil, laser fire, retained pack, the patient who can't be reintubated

2Plan together

  • Perioperative medicine: stop smoking (even weeks) · nutrition if weight loss > 10% or BMI < 18.5 · optimise anaemia, diabetes, heart · escalation plans (ReSPECT)
  • Review history, scope, imaging together · primary + backup plans · who does the surgical airway · mark the cricothyroid membrane (US if difficult)
  • Children: district hospitals from ~two years · tertiary for comorbidity, syndromes, young severe OSA, weight extremes · reinforced LMA over ~three · dexamethasone ~0.15 mg/kg for PONV

3The obstructed airway

  • No single proven method
  • Awake fibreoptic (topical, high-flow O₂, light sedation)
  • Inhalational induction, spontaneous breathing
  • IV + paralysis only if intubation judged easy
  • Awake trach under LA if larynx impassable
Surgeon scrubbed, neck prepped before induction in anyone at risk

4Sharing the airway

TechniquePoint
Microlaryngeal tube (5.0–6.0 mm)secure · hides posterior glottis
Laser-resistant tubemetal or wrapped, saline cuff · any laser near airway
Jet (supra- or transglottic)clear view · barotrauma, pneumothorax if outflow blocked

High-flow nasal O₂up to ~70 L/min · longer apnoea · fit patients, short cases

5Jet needs a way out

Laser-resistant transglottic catheter (Hunsaker Mon-Jet) can jet through a tight lesion, with care · THRIVE (2015) allows tubeless laryngeal surgery

6Laser and the bleeding tonsil

  • Laser: safe tube, saline (coloured) cuff, O₂ < ~30%, no N₂O, wet swabs, goggles, door signs · fire: stop laser + gas → tube out → saline → mask with air → reintubate → bronchoscopy
  • Bleeding tonsil: hypovolaemic (children more than they look), stomach of blood · resuscitate, correct clotting first
  • Senior anaesthetist, RSI head-down/lateral/sitting, two suctions, smaller tube · surgeon scrubbed · empty stomach after · extubate awake on the side
  • Paracetamol + ibuprofen · no codeine under 12, or under 18 for OSA surgery

7Nose, packs, extubation

  • Cocaine 4% (200 mg "limit" has no firm basis), Moffett's, adrenaline pledgets, co-phenylcaine · TIVA, head up, no tachycardia · second-generation LMA
  • Retained throat pack = never event · little evidence for routine packs · if used, in the swab count
  • Patent airway ≠ easy reintubation → planned extubation (DAS 2012): awake, suction, exchange catheter, or trach left in
  • Desflurane, N₂O = greenhouse gases → TIVA, low flow

8Exam traps ✗

  • Induction before the surgeon is scrubbed and the plan agreed
  • Jet ventilation with no route for expiration
  • High O₂ during laser surgery
  • Underestimating a bleeding child's blood loss
