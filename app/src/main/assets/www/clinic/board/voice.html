HOARSENESS, VOICE & SPEECH DISORDERS

power, vibrator, resonator · look at every voice wrong for weeks, and listen as carefully as you look ✎

1Why a voice goes hoarse

  • Irregular period or intensity of successive waves · normal voice needs folds that meet, have the right mass + stiffness, and vibrate regularly
  • Fail to meet: paralysis, fixation, mass, sulcus, bowing · mass ↑ oedema, nodules, tumour · stiffness ↑ scar, spasm · irregular: congestion, haemorrhage, edge lesions

2Assess

  • History: use, smoking, reflux, inhalers, thyroid/chest/neck surgery · red flags: pain, otalgia, dysphagia, stridor, haemoptysis, weight loss, neck mass
  • Listen: breathy (gap), rough (irregular), strained (hyperfunction, spasm), diplophonic, tremulous
  • Laryngoscopy: > four weeks (US), three weeks in smokers (UK), at once with red flags · NICE: suspected cancer at 45+
  • No antibiotics, steroids or PPI before the larynx is seen
  • Strobe · neck, chest, neuro exam · MLS · MPT, acoustics, GRBAS, VHI

3Normal larynx, abnormal use

  • Muscle tension dysphonia: strained, tiring, neck ache · supraglottic squeeze, posterior gap · primary or compensatory · therapy + manual therapy
  • Ventricular dysphonia: false folds phonate, rough low voice · secondary (compensating: hard) or functional (counselling)
  • Functional aphonia: sudden, stressed young women fifteen to thirty, whisper · folds close on coughing, loud clear cough · reassure, cough/hum exercises, address stress
  • Puberphonia: high childhood voice in a normal adolescent male · Gutzmann: press thyroid prominence back + down → low voice, then learn it · type III thyroplasty last resort

4Phonasthenia: read the gap

Phonatory muscles tire after misuse or laryngitis · voice rest, hygiene, therapy

5Spasmodic dysphonia

  • Focal dystonia (basal ganglia) · normal larynx · normal breathing, laughing, crying, whispering, often singing
  • Adductor ~90%: TA + LCA · strained, strangled, breaks on vowels · count 80–90
  • Abductor: PCA · breathy breaks after p, t, k · count 60–70 · mixed · tremor
  • Exclude Parkinson, essential tremor, MS, MND, tardive dyskinesia
  • Botox A (choice): TA–LCA via cricothyroid membrane (EMG) or scope · three to four months · breathy, choking on fluids · abductor: PCA, one side at a time
  • Therapy alongside, not alone · psychotherapy ineffective · surgery: denervation–reinnervation, type II thyroplasty, myectomy
  • Now "laryngeal dystonia" (2021) · singer's, adductor respiratory variants

6Other movement disorders

  • Tremor: isolated or essential · rhythmic on sustained phonation · toxin helps partly
  • Parkinson's: quiet, breathy, monotonous, rushes · loudness therapy (Lee Silverman)
  • Inducible laryngeal obstruction: folds adduct on inspiration · exercise, irritants · called asthma · scope during symptoms
  • Refractory cough > eight weeks, 5–10% of adults · upper airway clinic, SLT techniques · gabapentin (RCT 2012) or pregabalin

8Fluency and language

  • Stuttering: repetitions, prolongations, blocks + grimaces · normal dysfluency at two to four years · parents' overreaction fixes it · educate, therapy
  • Dysarthria (motor) vs aphasia (language) → neurology

9Voice care

  • Hydrate, humidify · no smoking, less alcohol + caffeine · treat reflux
  • No shouting, long loud talk, whispering when hoarse, throat clearing
  • Rest after heavy use · warm up · amplification in noisy rooms
  • Voice therapy (SLT): breathing, onset, pitch, resonance, tension · first line for nodules, MTD, functional disorders

10Evidence

  • US guideline (2018): therapy when hoarseness hurts quality of life
  • AAN (2008): botox probably effective for adductor SD
  • NIH panel (2021): "laryngeal dystonia"

11Exam traps ✗

  • Functional aphonia diagnosed without seeing the larynx
  • Spasmodic dysphonia called psychogenic
  • Adenoidectomy over a submucous cleft → hypernasality
