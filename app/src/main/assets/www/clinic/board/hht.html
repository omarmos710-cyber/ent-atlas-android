HEREDITARY HAEMORRHAGIC TELANGIECTASIA

nosebleeds that never stop coming back · screen the lungs and the brain ✎

1A disease of vessels

  • Osler-Weber-Rendu · autosomal dominant disorder of angiogenesis · men = women · penetrance rises with age
  • About 1 in 5,000 to 10,000 · telangiectasias in skin + mucosa, AVMs in organs
  • TGF-β signalling fails → no supporting matrix around new vessels

The three genes explain ~90% · genetic testing mainly screens relatives

2Nose, skin and organs

  • Epistaxis: commonest + earliest · spontaneous, recurrent, both sides, from childhood, worse with age · 80–90% by age 21
  • Telangiectasias: red spots that blanch · lips, tongue, palate, fingertips, face, nasal mucosa · 80–90% by their thirties or forties
  • Iron-deficiency anaemia from nose + gut

3The lesion: no capillary bed

4Curaçao criteria

recurrent spontaneous epistaxismultiple telangiectasiasvisceral AVM / GI lesionfirst-degree relative
three or more = definite · two = possible / suspected · fewer than two = unlikely · genetic testing settles doubt
DDx: von Willebrand, platelet disorders, anticoagulants · one-sided bleed → tumour

5Work-up: screen every patient

  • Head + neck exam, nasal endoscopy · FBC + iron studies
  • Contrast (bubble) echo for lung AVMs → embolise significant ones
  • Contrast MRI brain for cerebral AVMs
  • GI endoscopy if bleeding · Doppler or CT liver · genetic counselling
A lung AVM can cause a stroke or brain abscess in someone who only ever complained of nosebleeds

6Spare the mucosa

  • Goal: fewer, smaller bleeds + a better Hb, not cure · track with the Epistaxis Severity Score
  • Acute bleed: almost always the anterior third → gentle pressure, resorbable pack (Nasopore, Surgicel) or haemostatic matrix · no expanding sponges · antibiotic cover · fails → embolise facial + maxillary feeders, no coils
Repeated packing + wide cautery destroy healthy mucosa, breed new lesions, perforate the septum

8Evidence

  • NOSE (JAMA 2016): topical bevacizumab, oestriol, TXA sprays = placebo
  • PATH-HHT (NEJM 2024, 144 pts): oral pomalidomide cut epistaxis scores over 24 weeks
  • Guidelines 2020: moisture → oral TXA → ablation → graft / closure · IV bevacizumab if severe

9Step up by severity

EUA + KTP / argon laser→septodermoplasty or systemic drugs→nasal closure
  • Laser: green light absorbed by Hb · circle each lesion, then ablate · TXA + antibiotic at induction, no pack · repeat every few months · bipolar better in a bloody field
  • Laser controls it < about three months → step up
  • Septodermoplasty: skin graft replaces anterior septal mucosa · halves later laser sessions
  • Young's procedure (Lund: three vestibular flaps): nostrils closed → bleeding stops, often for good · costs nasal breathing + smell · for the transfusion-dependent
how long each step controls bleeding

10Exam traps ✗

  • Simple epistaxis assumed: no family history, no look at lips + fingers
  • Ribbon gauze, repeated packs, wide cautery
  • Forgetting to screen the lungs
  • Treating the nose but not the haemoglobin
  • Routine embolisation: collaterals return
  • Prescribing a topical bevacizumab spray
