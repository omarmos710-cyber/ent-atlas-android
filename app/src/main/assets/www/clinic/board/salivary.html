SALIVARY INFECTIONS, STONES & SWELLINGS

most swellings aren't tumours · acute or recurrent, painful or not, one gland or all · a firm persistent lump is a tumour until proved otherwise ✎

1Parotid

  • On masseter + ramus, wraps behind mandible · deep lobe reaches prestyloid parapharyngeal space · tight parotidomasseteric fascia → painful parotitis
  • VII divides at pes anserinus · superficial lobe ~80% / deep lobe = surgical plane
  • Retromandibular vein superficial to ECA · ~20 intraparotid nodes (only gland with nodes)
  • Stensen's: over masseter, through buccinator, opposite upper second molar
  • Secretomotor: IX → Jacobson → lesser petrosal → otic ganglion → auriculotemporal · skin: great auricular (C2, C3)

3Submandibular, sublingual, saliva

  • Wraps round back of mylohyoid · Wharton's between mylohyoid + hyoglossus, lingual nerve loops beneath · opens beside frenulum
  • Facial artery grooves it, facial vein superficial, marginal mandibular over capsule, XII deep · chorda tympani
  • Sublingual: ducts of Rivinus, Bartholin's into Wharton's · six hundred to a thousand minor glands (palate most)
  • A litre to a litre and a half a day · rest: submandibular (mucinous, Ca-rich) · stimulated: parotid (serous) · amylase, bicarbonate, IgA, lysozyme

4Assess

  • Which gland, sides, acute/recurrent · swelling with meals = obstruction · pus = infection · dry eyes + mouth = Sjögren · drugs, alcohol, diabetes
  • Red flags: persistent mass, facial weakness, pain, skin fixation, nodes
  • Milk the ducts · bimanual floor of mouth · test VII

5Mumps

  • Paramyxovirus · four to six years (unvaccinated) · incubation two to three weeks
  • Fever, then one parotid, then the other · settles in ~a week
  • Orchitis, oophoritis, pancreatitis, meningitis, unilateral SNHL (sudden), thyroiditis, myocarditis
  • Amylase, IgM, PCR · supportive · MMR

6Bacterial and chronic

  • Acute suppurative: elderly, dehydrated, after abdominal/hip surgery, dry-mouth drugs → stasis → S. aureus (MRSA), strep, anaerobes · pus from Stensen's
  • Rehydrate, IV antibiotics, sialogogues, massage back → front · no response in 48 h: US/CT · drain via parotidectomy incision, radial cuts along VII branches
  • Co-amoxiclav 625 mg tds oral (or flucloxacillin 500 mg–1 g qds + metronidazole 400 mg tds) 7–10 days · IV 1.2 g 8-hourly if unwell · allergy: clindamycin 300–450 mg qds
  • Neonatal (premature, staph) · juvenile recurrent parotitis: three to ten, no pus, punctate sialectasis, remits at puberty · lavage + steroid
  • Chronic recurrent: strictures, stones · punctate/globular/cavitary sialectasis · sialendoscopy → excision · Küttner (IgG4) mimics tumour
  • TB (FNA, AFB) · atypical mycobacteria one to three years, violaceous skin · actinomycosis (sulphur granules) · cat-scratch, toxo

7Stones

  • Hydroxyapatite on mucus nidus · 80–90% submandibular: alkaline, viscous, Ca-rich, long uphill duct · ~10% parotid
  • Salivary colic at meals, settles in an hour or two · pus · felt bimanually
  • Near orifice: hydrate, sialogogues · anterior/middle duct: sialolithotomy (stay suture behind, protect lingual nerve, marsupialise)
  • Sialendoscopy: basket < ~5 mm · laser/lithotripsy larger · combined approaches save the gland (~86% preserved, 2012)
  • Hilar/parenchymal unreachable or destroyed gland: excision

8Sjögren and friends

  • Sjögren: women · primary sicca + parotid swelling · secondary with RA, lupus
  • Schirmer, anti-Ro/La, RF, ANA · lip biopsy · ACR/EULAR 2016: focus score + anti-Ro 3 each, others 1 each, ≥ 4
  • Lip biopsy = gold standard if antibodies negative (focus score ≥ 1) · salivary US · lymphoma risk: cryoglobulins, low C4, monoclonal band
  • Sips, substitutes, 5000 ppm fluoride, dental care, tears · pilocarpine 5 mg tds (max 30 mg/day; not uncontrolled asthma, narrow-angle glaucoma) · cevimeline · hydroxychloroquine (rheumatology)
Big MALT lymphoma risk · new firm mass in a Sjögren gland → biopsy
  • Sarcoid: Heerfordt (parotitis, uveitis, fever, VII palsy)
  • HIV: lymphoepithelial cysts · solid mass → biopsy
  • Sialadenosis: painless, symmetrical · diabetes, alcohol, bulimia
  • Necrotising sialometaplasia: palate ulcer, men in forties, mimics carcinoma · heals five to six weeks

9Dry and wet mouths

  • Xerostomia: drugs, RT (> ~25 Gy irreversible), Sjögren · caries, candida · substitutes, pilocarpine, fluoride · parotid-sparing IMRT
  • Drooling: normal to ~four years · poor control, not excess · submandibular ~70% at rest · anterior vs posterior (aspiration) · therapy → glycopyrronium → botox → surgery (no relocation if aspirating)
  • Glycopyrronium 20 micrograms/kg tds → max 100 micrograms/kg (≤ 1.5–3 mg) tds · hyoscine 1 mg patch every 72 h · incobotulinumtoxin A 100 units (30 each parotid, 20 each submandibular), every 16 weeks

10Exam traps ✗

  • Sialography during acute infection
  • Firm persistent parotid lump called "chronic parotitis"
  • Lingual nerve injured removing a duct stone
