FRONT-OF-NECK AIRWAY & ARTERIAL LIGATION

two operations with no time to look anything up · both depend on landmarks you can feel ✎

1When

  • CICO: failed intubation, failed supraglottic airway, failed mask, falling saturations, fully paralysed
  • Obstruction where intubation is impossible: facial/laryngeal trauma, burns, angio-oedema, tumour, FB, blood or vomit
  • Declare it out loud
Don't keep repeating failed intubation attempts

2Scalpel, bougie, tube

  • Extend the neck · finger down from thyroid notch to the soft dip above the cricoid · can't feel it: 8–10 cm vertical midline incision, blunt finger dissection · US-mark beforehand if planned
  • Stand on the left (right-handed) · laryngeal handshake
  • Transverse stab, size 10 blade, edge towards you → rotate 90° (edge to the feet)
  • Bougie along blade → 6.0 cuffed tube (smallest for prolonged ventilation) → cuff, ventilate, capnography, secure
Stay above the cricoid (stenosis) · not deep (posterior trachea, oesophagus)

3Needles, kits, afterwards

  • 14G cannula + jet or high-flow O₂ · children under ~12 (tiny membrane, no surgical cric) · oxygenates, doesn't ventilate (CO₂ rises after 30–45 minutes) · needs an exit route · kinks
  • Seldinger, guarded-blade kits (Mini-Trach, Melker): slower under stress
  • Convert to tracheostomy within ~24–72 hours · a bridge, not a long-term airway
  • Bleeding, false passage, posterior perforation, emphysema, displacement, subglottic stenosis, voice
  • DAS (2015): scalpel technique · 21 studies (2020): fewer late complications than emergency trach · no kit clearly better (2013)

4The ligation ladder

VesselApproach · point
Sphenopalatineendoscopic, antrostomy + flap · first choice posterior epistaxis
EthmoidalLynch-type or endoscopic · high or traumatic bleed

Embolisation the alternative · ECA also for uncontrollable tonsil or tumour bleeds · nearer the bleed, the better

524-12-6

  • GA, protect the eye · curved incision between medial canthus + dorsum (Z or W: no web)
  • Subperiosteal to anterior lacrimal crest, lift sac laterally, follow suture → AEA tethering periosteum → clip
  • Canthal web, lacrimal sac injury, orbital haematoma, optic nerve (PEA)

6Maxillary artery

  • Caldwell-Luc (or gingivobuccal) · remove posterior antral wall under the microscope · open periosteum
  • Artery in PPF fat, in front of the maxillary nerve · clip trunk + branches
  • Cheek numbness, oroantral fistula, teeth, ophthalmoplegia

7External carotid

  • LA or GA · horizontal incision at greater horn of hyoid · subplatysmal flaps · retract SCM · divide common facial vein
  • Identify ECA by its branches (ICA has none in the neck) + XII crossing · keep vagus
  • Tie twice above superior thyroid or lingual, after seeing two branches
No branches seen = not the ECA · tying the internal carotid → stroke
Rebleed, XII/X/SLN injury, haematoma

8Embolisation

  • 44 studies, 1,664 patients (2023): success 87% · rebleed ~16% · complications ~14% · stroke ~2%, visual loss ~2%

9Exam traps ✗

  • Repeating failed intubation instead of front-of-neck access
  • Cutting through the cricoid
  • Cricothyroidotomy left as a long-term airway
  • Tying the ECA without seeing its branches
