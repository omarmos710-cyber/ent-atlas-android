CANCER OF THE LARYNX

on the fold: hoarse early, no lymphatics, often cured · above or below: silent, nodes, late ✎

1Who and why

  • Second commonest upper aerodigestive cancer, ~1–5% of all · men four to eight times · sixth–seventh decades · UK ~2,400 a year, deprived areas · glottic ~60%, supraglottic ~35–40%, subglottic 1–2%
  • Tobacco (ten- to twentyfold, falls after ten years off) · alcohol (supraglottic; with tobacco ~fifteenfold) · neck RT, papillomas, asbestos, nickel, wood dust, reflux
  • SCC 85–95% · verrucous 1–2%: pushing, no metastases → excise, not RT · others: spindle, basaloid, neuroendocrine, salivary, chondrosarcoma (cricoid), lymphoma

2Regions decide behaviour

  • Glottis to 1 cm below the free edge · conus + quadrangular membrane resist · pre-epiglottic (via epiglottic pits) + paraglottic spaces carry it silently → transglottic
  • Broyles' ligament: no perichondrium at the anterior commissure → into cartilage

3See it, map it

  • Hoarse > two to three weeks at cancer age → laryngoscopy · NBI, office biopsy · strobe: stiff segment · neck: swelling over cricothyroid membrane, tender cartilage, nodes

5Early cancer: one modality

  • Stage I–II: RT or surgery, similar control · choose by voice, swallow, access, fitness, preference · III–IV: combined, preserve organ if safe
  • RT 60–70 Gy over six weeks · T1 glottic ≥ ~90%, T2 ~85% mobile, ~63–70% if impaired · mucositis, chondronecrosis, can't repeat
  • TLM (CO2) / TORS: cordectomy, supraglottic resection · cheaper, quicker, no trach, RT kept in reserve · voice poorer after wide cordectomy · not if fixed, interarytenoid, poor lungs
  • Open (selected, after RT failure): vertical partial (one fold + commissure) · supraglottic (T1–T2, mobile folds, commissure clear, good lungs: all aspirate at first) · supracricoid (selected T2–T3, ≥ one mobile arytenoid; CHP / CHEP)

7Total laryngectomy

  • Whole larynx + hyoid + pre-epiglottic space + upper trachea · pharynx closed · end stoma
  • T3–T4, cartilage invasion, both arytenoids, posterior commissure, transglottic + fixed
  • Failed RT or conservation · non-functioning larynx with aspiration
  • ± neck dissection, post-op RT · not with distant metastases

8Organ preservation + neck

  • VA Larynx (1991): induction cisplatin + fluorouracil → RT = survival of laryngectomy · two-thirds keep larynx
  • RTOG 91-11: concurrent cisplatin best preservation + control · no survival difference at ten years (2013)
  • CRT standard for many T3 + node-positive T2 · cetuximab or carboplatin if unfit for cisplatin
  • But survival fell as practice left surgery (2006) · salvage laryngectomy after CRT: high complications
NICE: offer T3 a choice · T4a: surgery first · also favour surgery: dead larynx, trach, patient wish
  • Node-positive: dissection or RT · PET-CT complete response at ~twelve weeks → watch (PET-NECK, 564 patients, 2016) · ENE or + margin: post-op CRT

9After laryngectomy

  • Voice: tracheo-oesophageal valve (Blom–Singer, Provox), occlude stoma: best, commonest · oesophageal: no device, hard to learn · electrolarynx: easy, mechanical
  • Smell: polite yawning · HME, water protection · hypothyroid a fifth to two-thirds
  • Complications: fistula (more after RT; pectoralis or free flap), stomal stenosis + recurrence, stricture, chondronecrosis, dead larynx after CRT

10Prognosis + follow-up

  • Five-year ~90% T1 glottic · supraglottic + subglottic worse · nodes, ENE, cartilage, subglottic extension adverse
  • Review every one to three months in year one, lifelong · recurrence (oedema, pain, voice, mobility) → PET-CT + biopsy · ~half salvageable, usually total laryngectomy

Impaired mobility = muscle · fixation = paraglottic, joint or nerve: bad sign

12Exam traps ✗

  • Antibiotics + voice rest for a smoker's hoarseness
  • No DL: commissure, ventricle, subglottis missed
  • One-sided neck in supraglottic cancer
  • Organ preservation for T4a through cartilage
