LABYRINTHINE FISTULA & LABYRINTHITIS

a dizzy patient with an infected ear: breached, or inflamed? ✎

1Four meanings

2How infection gets in

  • Middle ear: cholesteatoma eroding the lateral canal (commonest) · oval / round window in AOM or COM · surgery, trauma
  • Meninges: via cochlear aqueduct or IAC · pneumococcus, H. influenzae, meningococcus · pneumococcus: highest deafness risk ~20%
  • Blood: viral, syphilitic

3The eroded lateral canal

7–10% of cholesteatomas · 90% lateral canal · also tumours, trauma, surgery
Brief vertigo on pressure: tragus, cleaning, nose blowing · hearing often kept

4Fistula test & surgery

Fistula test

tragal pressure / Siegle → vertigo + nystagmus · +ve pressure: fast phase usually towards the ear; −ve: away
Specific, insensitive: positive in only 30–50% · negative in a dead labyrinth or when cholesteatoma seals it
Assume a fistula in every cholesteatoma · HRCT shows only ~60% before surgery
Also +ve: Ménière's (Hennebert), SCD, perilymph fistula, congenital syphilis · Rx: small: matrix off at the end, seal (fascia, bone pâté) · large / only hearing ear: leave matrix, exteriorise

5Serous or suppurative?

SerousSuppurative
Causetoxinsbacteria
Nystagmustowards affected ear (irritative)towards healthy ear (failed)
Hearingpartial, recoverstotal, permanent
Patientunwelltoxic, ± meningitis
Outcomerecoverydead ear, ossification
  • Serous Rx: bed rest, affected ear up, full-dose antibiotics, labyrinthine sedatives, myringotomy if bulging · mastoidectomy for a chronic source
  • Suppurative: severe vertigo + vomiting, settles over three to six weeks (compensation) · can spread along the IAC to the meninges · IV antibiotics, remove source, rarely drain labyrinth

6Labyrinthitis ossificans

suppurative (esp. meningitic)→fibrous tissue→new bone, basal turn first
within weeks to months
Deafness after meningitis: test hearing in every patient · bilateral profound → urgent MRI + CT → early cochlear implant, before the cochlea fills with bone · steroids reduce loss in H. influenzae + pneumococcal meningitis

7Perilymph fistula

  • After: stapes surgery, barotrauma, diving, lifting, straining, coughing, head injury · unoperated ear: annular ligament or round window · spontaneous = controversial
  • Fluctuating / sudden loss, roaring tinnitus, disequilibrium, Hennebert's sign
  • Penetrating ear injury + vertigo + loss → stapes subluxation into the vestibule
  • Rx: bed rest 7–10 days, head up, no straining, stool softeners → explore + patch if hearing worsens or symptoms persist

8Viral & syphilitic

  • Mumps: usually one-sided profound loss · measles, rubella
  • CMV = commonest non-syndromic cause of congenital SNHL; may progress after a normal newborn screen
  • Late congenital syphilis at 8–20 years: bilateral sudden / fluctuating loss + vertigo + Hennebert · VDRL / RPR + FTA-ABS · penicillin + steroids

9Evidence

Fistula in ~5–10% of cholesteatomas · CT misses some → expect one with vertigo or +ve test · matrix: remove + seal at once vs leave for second stage is debated; in experienced hands removal keeps hearing in most (steroids often given)

10On call: vertigo in an infected ear

nystagmus + direction→gentle fistula test→audiogram + HRCT→IV antibiotics; myringotomy for AOM→plan cholesteatoma surgery
Hearing loss with meningitis → treat urgently, image for ossification

11Exam traps ✗

  • Reassured by a negative fistula test
  • No hearing test after meningitis
  • Missing late congenital syphilis in a young adult with fluctuating bilateral loss
