TUBERCULOUS & SYPHILITIC OTITIS MEDIA

the chronic ear that refuses to heal ✎

1What and how

  • Mycobacterium tuberculosis in the middle ear cleft · syphilis included: same bone destruction + disproportionate hearing loss
  • Uncommon in low-burden countries; important where TB is endemic · rising with drug resistance + HIV

2Painless, thin, resistant

Drum thickened early, then multiple perforations that merge into one large defect
  • Discharge: painless, thin, cloudy, persists despite treatment
  • Hearing loss out of proportion, often mixed (early ossicular + cochlear involvement)
  • Pale thick mucosa, abundant pale granulation · mastoid sequestra
  • Non-tender cervical + preauricular nodes
  • Facial palsy ~10%, may present it in a child
Suspect it when a chronic ear or a mastoidectomy fails, granulation is pale, or early facial palsy in a painless ear

3Prove it: send the tissue

  • Gold standard: biopsy of granulations → histology (caseating granulomas, Langhans giant cells) + mycobacterial culture with sensitivities (takes weeks)
  • Rapid: PCR / Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra on tissue or discharge → diagnosis + rifampicin resistance in days
  • AFB smear: a negative smear does not exclude it
  • Every patient: chest X-ray (normal in many), HIV test, liver + renal function · IGRA / tuberculin = infection, not active disease
  • HRCT: looks like ordinary COM, more erosion + sequestra
DDx: ordinary CSOM · middle ear carcinoma · GPA · fungal infection

4Drugs first, repair later

  • Surgery only for biopsy, sequestra, abscesses, complications
  • Reconstruct after treatment: healing slow, fistulae common while active; afterwards repair succeeds in most

5Complications

Facial palsy (usually recovers on medical treatment) · sequestrum · postauricular fistula · Bezold abscess · labyrinthitis

6Syphilitic otitis

Rare, destructive · congenital or acquired · temporal bone osteitis + necrosis · labyrinthitis: profound, often fluctuating SNHL + vertigo
  • Tests: treponemal EIA (screen) · RPR / VDRL titre (activity) · TPPA (confirm) · FTA-ABS · LP with GUM + HIV test if otological / neuro signs · audiogram, CT

Treat as neurosyphilis

procaine penicillin 1.8–2.4 million units IM once daily + probenecid 500 mg four times a day × 14 days · alt: benzylpenicillin 1.8–2.4 g IV 4-hourly × 14 days · allergy: doxycycline 200 mg twice a day × 28 days · prednisolone 40–60 mg daily × 3 days, from 24 h before (Jarisch–Herxheimer)

7Viva & traps ✗

Viva: painless discharge, multiple perforations, pale granulation, early facial palsy, disproportionate loss · PCR, histology, culture · treatment is medical; surgery for complications
  • Operating on a chronic ear without sending tissue for TB
  • Reassured by a negative AFB smear
  • Reconstructing during active treatment
