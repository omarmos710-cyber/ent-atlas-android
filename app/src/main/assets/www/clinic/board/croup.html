CROUP, EPIGLOTTITIS & ACUTE LARYNGITIS

the barking child can be soothed · the drooling child must not be upset ✎

1Acute laryngitis

  • Viral after a cold (parainfluenza, influenza, rhino) → bacterial · measles, chickenpox, pertussis · candida (inhaled steroid)
  • Non-infective: shouting, allergy, irritants, burns, reflux, intubation
  • Abrupt hoarseness or aphonia, pain after talking, dry cough at night · early: red supraglottis, white true folds · later red folds, subglottic swelling · abuse → fold haemorrhage
  • Breathlessness is not a feature → think again
  • Voice rest (most important) · no smoking, steam, hydration · antibiotics only for bacterial signs · PPI if reflux · steroid after burns or for performers
  • One to two weeks · > three weeks: laryngoscopy · membranous: swab to exclude diphtheria

4Epiglottitis

  • Supraglottis swells → ball valve · since Hib vaccine: mostly adults (strep, staph)
  • Hours · high fever, septic · child: stridor, tripod, drooling, muffled, no cough · adult: pain out of proportion to a normal oropharynx, hot-potato voice, tender hyoid
✗ tongue depressor ✗ cannula ✗ lying down ✗ radiology alone · stay with parent, oxygen without a struggle → theatre: senior anaesthetist, gas induction, ENT ready to trach
  • Adults: flexible scope usually safe → cherry-red epiglottis · blood cultures beat swabs · CT if abscess
  • Intubate (trach if fails) children + adults with stridor or fast progression · late adults without stridor: HDU
  • Ceftriaxone/cefotaxime at once · steroid, humidified O₂, fluids · rifampicin for Hib contacts
  • Tube for 24–72 hours

5Croup

  • Commonest acute stridor · six months – three years, boys, autumn–winter · parainfluenza 1 + 2, RSV, influenza
  • Subglottis swells inside the rigid cricoid + crusts
  • Cold → hoarse, barking cough, stridor worse at night · not toxic
  • Recession, tachypnoea → restless, rising pulse, pallor, cyanosis, drowsy = late

6Evidence

  • Cochrane (43 trials, 4,565 children, 2018): steroids help within two hours, last ≥ 24 hours · return visits RR 0.52 · ~15 hours shorter stay
  • Nebulised adrenaline: better at 30 minutes, not at two to six hours (2013)
  • Adult epiglottitis (30 studies, 10,148, 2020): ~1 in 9 need an airway · abscess, stridor, diabetes predict

7Croup that isn't croup

  • Bacterial tracheitis (S. aureus, strep): after viral croup · high fever, toxic, thick secretions, fails croup treatment · bronchoscopy to strip membranes (often repeatedly), intubate, IV antibiotics · pneumonia
  • Laryngeal diphtheria: unimmunised under ten · low fever, marked toxaemia · grey membrane bleeds when lifted, bull neck · myocarditis + palsies
treat on suspicion→antitoxin→penicillin→airway
Antitoxin 20,000–100,000 units IV after test dose · penicillin or erythromycin 7–10 days · bed rest two to four weeks, cardiac monitor · isolate, immunise contacts

8Laryngeal oedema

  • Loose mucosa: supraglottis + subglottis, rarely folds
  • Infection (incl. quinsy, Ludwig's) · trauma, intubation, endoscopy, burns, caustics · cancer · allergy, ACE-i, hereditary · RT · nephritis, heart failure, myxoedema
  • Stridor + distress → airway · allergy: IM adrenaline 0.5 mL 1:1000 (repeat after five minutes), antihistamine, steroid · hereditary: C1 inhibitor or icatibant · stop ACE-i

9Exam traps ✗

  • Tongue depressor on a drooling, stridulous child
  • Epiglottitis sent to radiology alone
  • Discharge soon after nebulised adrenaline
  • Missing adult epiglottitis behind a normal oropharynx
