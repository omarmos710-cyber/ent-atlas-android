CSF RHINORRHOEA

a clear drip that gushes on bending forward · prove it, find the hole, close it ✎

1A skull base that leaks

CSF from the subarachnoid space into the nose through a defect in dura + bone · open door to meningitis · ~80% after trauma · spontaneous: often IIH · β2-transferrin confirms, CT + MRI find the hole · most traumatic leaks stop

4A drip you can't sniff back

  • Clear, watery, one side · worse bending, straining, lifting · salty or sweet · can't be sniffed back
  • Reservoir sign: a gush on first bending in the morning (sphenoid)
  • Headache (low pressure, or high in IIH) · anosmia after trauma · past meningitis
  • Endoscopy: fluid from olfactory cleft, middle meatus, sphenoethmoidal recess, ET, or an encephalocele · look in the ear (fluid behind the drum)
  • Halo / double ring / target sign on filter paper after trauma: hint, not proof

7Most traumatic leaks stop

StepTreatment
Conservative (traumatic)bed rest head up, stool softener (lactulose), no nose blowing, sneeze mouth open, no straining · 7–14 days · lumbar drain lowers pressure
Cut CSF productionacetazolamide e.g. 250 mg two to four times a day (adults) · check electrolytes; paraesthesia common
Antibiotic prophylaxisnot recommended (Cochrane 2015: no fall in meningitis)
Every patientpneumococcal vaccination (Green Book risk group)
Meningitistreat as bacterial meningitis, then repair

Repair when

persists beyond one to two weeks · recurs · seen at surgery · spontaneous · meningitis · big defect or encephalocele

8Endoscopic multilayer repair

  • Standard · expose, strip mucosa from the edges, reduce or cauterise an encephalocele, close in layers
  • Inlay fascia, fat or dural substitute between dura + bone · onlay mucosa or fascia · nasoseptal (Hadad-Bassagasteguy) flap on the posterior septal artery for large defects
  • > 90% first time

9Open repair, aftercare, evidence

  • Craniotomy for very large or multiple defects, brain injury · external for high frontal leaks
  • Head up, no straining, stool softeners, no nose blowing for weeks
  • Spontaneous leak: treat raised pressure (weight loss, acetazolamide, sometimes a shunt) or it recurs
  • 55 studies (2012): ~90% sealed first time, ~97% after revision, any graft · nasoseptal flap (2006): extended skull base leaks down to ~5% · little support for routine lumbar drains or long antibiotics after repair

10Complications & traps ✗

  • Meningitis (often pneumococcal, recurrent) · pneumocephalus
  • One-sided clear drip called rhinitis · relying on glucose
  • Repairing a spontaneous leak and ignoring raised pressure
  • Forgetting the ear: CSF otorhinorrhoea down the tube
