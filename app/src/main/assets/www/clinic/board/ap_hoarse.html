APPROACH TO THE HOARSE VOICE

any adult hoarse for more than three weeks gets the larynx examined · well enough to see the folds vibrate ✎

1Body and cover

  • Voice fails if folds can't close (palsy, atrophy, gap) · cover stiff or heavy (scar, sulcus, Reinke's, cancer) · lesion blocks closure · muscles fight it (MTD, dystonia)

2Red flags

  • > three weeks in a smoker/drinker, or persistent over 45 → urgent laryngoscopy (NG12)
  • Stridor, breathless → airway first
  • + dysphagia, odynophagia, otalgia, haemoptysis, weight loss, neck lump
  • Breathy + weak cough after thyroid, chest, neck, cardiac surgery or unexplained → palsy: image the nerve
  • Child + breathing difficulty → RRP

3History

  • Sudden after shouting → haemorrhage, polyp · after a cold → laryngitis · after surgery/intubation → nerve, arytenoid dislocation · gradual → lesion, cancer
  • Fluctuates? worse with use, better with rest?
  • Voice demands (teacher, singer, call centre) · smoking, alcohol · reflux · inhaled steroids · thyroid · tremor, weakness, swallow · breathing, pain, weight
  • VHI-10

4Examine

  • Listen: breathy (gap), rough, strained (MTD, dystonia), tremor, breaks
  • Neck nodes, thyroid, laryngeal tenderness
  • Flexible scope (distal chip): mucosa, lesion, movement of each fold and arytenoid, closure · sniff, "ee", cough
  • Stroboscopy: mucosal wave · nodule vs cyst, scar, sulcus, early cancer · GRBAS, acoustics

6Key lesions

  • Nodules: bilateral, symmetrical, overuse → voice therapy
  • Polyp: one side, after haemorrhage → microlaryngoscopy if therapy fails
  • Reinke's: smokers, low pitch (women) · stop smoking · reduce, preserve cover
  • Leukoplakia: risk by dysplasia grade → excise for histology

7Paralysis and function

  • Left more often (RLN round the aortic arch)
  • Surgery (thyroid, cardiothoracic, anterior spine) · cancer (lung, oesophagus, thyroid, skull base) · idiopathic
  • Unexplained → CT skull base to aortic arch
  • MTD: commonest functional, strained, supraglottic squeeze → voice therapy
  • Spasmodic dystonia: strangled breaks → botulinum

8Treat

  • Voice therapy + hygiene central · stop smoking, hydrate, reflux if features, inhaler technique
  • Phonosurgery: cold steel or laser, preserve superficial lamina propria · biopsy anything suspicious
  • Palsy not recovering: injection medialisation, thyroplasty, arytenoid adduction, reinnervation
  • Cancer → head and neck pathway

9Pathway

Red flag or > three weeks → laryngoscopy (urgent if smoker, over 45, stridor) · recent cold improving → review Scope: suspicious → MLB + biopsy, stage · benign → therapy ± surgery · inflamed → smoking, reflux, inhaler Mobility: palsy → CT, medialise · strained, normal folds → MTD · breaks → dystonia
US guideline (2018): four weeks, sooner if serious · no antibiotics, steroids or antireflux drugs before laryngoscopy

10Exam traps ✗

  • Months of antibiotics or PPI, larynx never seen
  • Lung cancer missed behind a left palsy
  • Surgery for nodules before voice therapy
  • Stripping the fold → scar
