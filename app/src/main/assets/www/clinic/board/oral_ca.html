ORAL PREMALIGNANCY & ORAL CANCER

a cancer you can see, still found late · early 80–90% cured, advanced 30–40% · depth, the neck, the pathology report ✎

1Potentially malignant disorders

  • WHO 2005: "precancer" → OPMD: risk of cancer, not always before it
  • Leukoplakia: white patch not any other lesion · commonest
  • Erythroplakia: rare, highest risk · most already severe dysplasia or cancer
  • PVL: multifocal, spreading, older non-smoking women · very high lifetime risk
  • Submucous fibrosis (areca) · lichen planus, esp. erosive

Higher risk if

  • red or red-and-white · PVL
  • lateral/ventral tongue, floor of mouth
  • multiple, large, long-standing
  • non-smoker, woman
  • dysplasia: ~5–18% transform

2A white or red patch

  • Inspect + palpate whole mouth, neck · remove sharp tooth, denture · treat candida · review in two to three weeks
  • Persists → biopsy the reddest, most indurated, irregular part · mild, moderate, severe dysplasia
  • Toluidine blue, Lugol, autofluorescence, NBI: choose sites, map margins · never replace biopsy
  • Stop tobacco, alcohol, areca · excise moderate/severe dysplasia, erythroplakia · follow for life (field change)

3The pathway

biopsy→stage→primary→neck→pathology
  • Persistent patch or ulcer → biopsy most suspicious area · dysplasia: stop habits, excise moderate/severe
  • MRI/CT, OPG, chest imaging or PET-CT, dental check
  • Excise with 5 mm margins ± mandibulectomy ± free flap
  • N0 + DOI > 3–4 mm: elective I–III or sentinel · N+: comprehensive
  • Read the report → RT or CRT

4Oral SCC

  • > 90% SCC · tobacco (smoked + chewed), alcohol multiplies it, betel quid + areca (South Asia) · HPV small role (unlike oropharynx)
  • Young non-smokers (often women) with tongue cancer · Fanconi anaemia
  • Ulcer, lump, patch, referred otalgia, bleeding, loose teeth, ill-fitting denture, trismus, numb lip (mental nerve), neck lump
Single ulcer not healed in three weeks: biopsy · not repeated topical steroid

6Investigation

  • EUA + biopsy · MRI: soft tissue, depth, marrow, perineural · CT: cortex · OPG: teeth + mandible
  • US + FNA neck · PET-CT: advanced, low nodes, distant, second primary, recurrence
  • Dental assessment before RT (osteoradionecrosis)

7Depth ≠ thickness

DOI: from the level of the adjacent normal basement membrane to the deepest invasion · predicts nodes better than surface size · thickness also counts exophytic growth

9The primary

  • Surgery + risk-based adjuvant: best control at every stage
  • 5 mm margin in three dimensions · specimen: ≥ 5 mm clear, 1–5 mm close, < 1 mm involved
  • Marginal mandibulectomy: abutting, superficial erosion (keeps continuity) · segmental: medullary invasion, thin edentulous jaw
  • RT/brachytherapy for some lip + palate where surgery costs too much function

10The neck

  • D'Cruz 2015 (500, early N0): elective dissection I–III beat watchful waiting · three-year survival 80.0% vs 67.5%
  • Elective when DOI > ~3–4 mm, lower for high-risk sites
  • Sentinel node: three trials, 608 pts (2022): similar survival, better shoulder
  • N+: levels I–V (or I–IV) · opposite side for midline or extensive disease

11Rebuild and add

  • Like with like · small: secondary intention, graft, local flap · tongue/floor: radial forearm, ALT · segmental jaw: fibula (implants later)

12After treatment

  • Bleeding, infection, seroma, chyle, VII/XI weakness · carotid blowout (irradiated)
  • RT: dry mouth, caries, trismus, osteoradionecrosis → lifelong dental care, jaw exercises
  • Close review first two years, at least five · second primaries · stopping tobacco + alcohol matters most

13Exam traps ✗

  • Watching the N0 neck in a deep tongue cancer
  • Calling tumour thickness "depth of invasion"
  • No dental assessment before RT
