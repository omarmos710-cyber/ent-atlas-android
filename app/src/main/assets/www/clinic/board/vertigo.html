VERTIGO

BPPV, neuritis and the central minority not to miss ✎

1A symptom, not a diagnosis

  • Vertigo: false sense of rotation / movement, from vestibular asymmetry · peripheral ~85%
  • Disequilibrium (unsteady, no spin) · oscillopsia (bilateral loss) · presyncope (cardiac, orthostatic, glucose) · physiological (motion, height)
Causes
PeripheralBPPV, neuritis, labyrinthitis, Ménière's, fistula, SCD, ototoxicity, trauma, syphilis
Centralposterior circulation stroke, VBI, Wallenberg, vestibular migraine, MS, tumours, epilepsy
Othercervical, ocular, psychogenic, cardiovascular

2Timing is everything

  • Min–hours also recurrent vestibulopathy · days–weeks also labyrinthitis · variable: perilymph fistula, SCD
  • Triggers: head position (BPPV) · sound / pressure (SCD, fistula) · straining · neck · standing (orthostatic) · migraine triggers
  • Hearing affected → labyrinth, CPA or AICA stroke
  • Diplopia, dysarthria, numbness, weakness, dysphagia, new severe headache → central until proven otherwise

3Eyes, head and gait

  • Peripheral nystagmus: horizontal-torsional, beats away from the bad side, suppressed by fixation (Frenzel), stronger looking towards the fast phase (Alexander's law)
  • Head impulse: catch-up saccade = that side's VOR failed (peripheral)
  • Dix-Hallpike (posterior canal) · supine roll (lateral) · Romberg, tandem, head shake, dynamic visual acuity (> five lines = bilateral loss), fistula test, neuro + cranial nerves
  • Positive fistula test: perilymph fistula, cholesteatoma with fistula, Ménière's, SCD, syphilis
HINTS in continuous vertigo + nystagmus: normal head impulse, direction-changing nystagmus or skew = stroke · + new hearing loss = AICA ("HINTS plus") · beats early MRI (misses up to 1 in 5 in the first 48 h) but only in trained hands (Krishnan 2019: sens 95.5%, spec 71%)

5Principles

On call: timing, triggers, hearing, neuro symptoms → nystagmus, head impulse, skew, gait · focal sign, new severe headache, can't stand, or normal head impulse with nystagmus = stroke until proven otherwise · Dix-Hallpike only when neck + condition allow
  • Antiemetics / vestibular sedatives a few days only: long-term sedatives slow compensation
  • Vestibular rehabilitation (head + eye exercises) = mainstay after any peripheral loss (Cochrane: safe, effective; McDonnell 2015)

6BPPV: seconds, lying down

Utricular otoconia fall into a canal (canalolithiasis) · after head injury, ear infection, or old age · brief vertigo (< a minute) lying down, rolling, looking up · no hearing loss
Dix-Hallpike: rotatory, geotropic, latency of seconds, crescendo then fades within a minute, reverses on sitting, fatigues

Peripheral: latency, < a minute, fatigues, fixed torsional, marked vertigo · central: often no latency, persists, no fatigue, changing / vertical, often mild

7BPPV: treatment

Epley repositions debris into the utricle · most resolve in months anyway · supine-roll side + type guide the horizontal-canal roll
  • No routine imaging / vestibular tests · no post-manoeuvre restrictions · recheck at 1 month · home Epley self-treatment · recurs in up to a third within four years
  • No sedatives (betahistine, prochlorperazine, cinnarizine, benzodiazepines): slow compensation, falls · at most one antiemetic dose for nausea during the manoeuvre
  • Intractable (rare): posterior canal occlusion / singular neurectomy after a year of failed manoeuvres · Cochrane: Epley 56% vs sham 21%
  • Persistent down-beating, atypical, failed manoeuvres, frequent recurrence → image

8Vestibular neuritis

  • Probably viral (nerve / ganglion) · sudden severe continuous vertigo + vomiting for days, settles over two to three weeks, no hearing loss
  • Nystagmus away, head impulse abnormal towards, calorics ↓ · HINTS: abnormal impulse, unidirectional nystagmus, no skew · vHIT / calorics confirm · audiogram normal (loss = labyrinthitis or AICA stroke)
  • Central sign, or not better at two to three weeks → MRI with diffusion (cerebellar infarct)
  • Short sedatives → early mobilisation + rehab · BPPV follows in up to 15%

9Other peripheral

  • Labyrinthitis = neuritis + hearing loss
  • Recurrent vestibulopathy: Ménière-like, no hearing symptoms · ~60% remit; a minority → Ménière's or BPPV
  • Perilymph fistula: episodic vertigo + fluctuating hearing after stapes surgery, barotrauma, lifting, coughing → bed rest head up, laxatives, explore if worse
  • SCD, Ménière's (own boards) · schwannoma: unsteadiness, not severe vertigo · bilateral loss: oscillopsia, unsteady in the dark (aminoglycosides) · CABV / CANVAS

11Migraine + other causes

Vestibular migraine: vertigo minutes–hours with / near migraine, young women, family history · response to treatment supports it · commonest misdiagnosis of Ménière's
StepDrug + dose
Allregular sleep, meals, fluids, trigger diary, no medication overuse · rehab
Attackprochlorperazine 5 mg or metoclopramide 10 mg + simple analgesia · sumatriptan 50–100 mg if headache dominates (not in vascular disease)
Preventionpropranolol 80–160 mg a day · amitriptyline 10 mg at night → 25–75 mg · topiramate 25 → 50 mg 2×/day (pregnancy prevention) · trial each 2–3 months

  • Cervical (over-diagnosed) · presyncope (arrhythmia, orthostatic, glucose) · psychogenic, PPPD after an acute event
