Mucus = middle ear open

The canal has no mucous glands

2Red flags

  • Diabetic, immunosuppressed, old + pain out of proportion + granulation on canal floor → necrotising OE
  • Facial weakness, vertigo, SNHL, headache, fever, neck stiffness, drowsy → complication
  • Blood, persistent polyp/granulation, night pain → carcinoma until biopsy
  • Clear watery after injury or surgery → CSF
  • Postauricular swelling, pinna forward → mastoid abscess

3Where is it coming from?

4History

  • How long, first or latest of many · short painful after swim/scratch/cold → OE or AOM · long painless with hearing loss → COM
  • Pain relieved by discharge = perforated AOM · pain persists = OE, furuncle or deeper
  • Hearing, tinnitus, vertigo, facial weakness · surgery, grommets · buds, aids, plugs, water
  • Diabetes, immunosuppression, chemo, steroids · headache, fever, drowsy · smoking

5Causes by site

  • Canal: OE (Pseudomonas, S. aureus) · otomycosis · furuncle · chronic OE (> three months or four a year, itchy, allergy) · necrotising OE · keratosis obturans, canal cholesteatoma, carcinoma
  • Drum/middle ear: perforated AOM · grommet · mucosal COM · cholesteatoma · granular myringitis · TB (painless, multiple holes, no response)
  • Beyond: CSF (beta-2 transferrin) · mastoid abscess, parotid, skin

6Examine: clean, then look

  • Pinna, sulcus, mastoid first · tragal/pinna tenderness = OE, furuncle · lobule spared = perichondritis
  • Microsuction: key to diagnosis and treatment · canal: oedema, fungus, floor granulation, bare bone · drum: intact, central, marginal/attic, retracted, polyp
  • Facial nerve every time · fistula test for attic lesions · forks, audiometry
  • Malignancy possible: neck, mouth, pharynx, scope · nose, nasopharynx in recurrent OM
Too swollen to clean → drops + wick, bring them back · too many cholesteatomas treated for months as "OE"

7Pathway

Clean · can't: wick, review in days Red flag? NOE: CT, ESR/CRP, biopsy · palsy: CT, admit · watery: beta-2 transferrin Intact drum: OE/fungus · granular myringitis · canal polyp → biopsy Perforated: central mucoid → COM/AOM · attic foul → CT · grommet Two courses failed: swab + fungi, biopsy, CT, think TB

8Treat

  • OE: toilet, antibiotic + steroid drops 7–10 days, dry · wick with aqueous drops, change in two to seven days · acetic acid mild
  • Fungal: debride + clotrimazole two to three weeks
  • Systemic only if spreading: furuncle, cellulitis, perichondritis, NOE
  • Perforated AOM (adult): analgesia, often amoxicillin · grommet, mucosal COM: suction + drops · dry hole: tympanoplasty · cholesteatoma: CT + surgery
  • NOE: admit, glucose, culture first, biopsy, CT + MRI, long antipseudomonal
  • Child: grommet → quinolone · mastoiditis → IV, myringotomy ± cortical

9Drops through a hole

  • Dhingra: antibiotic + steroid drops with toilet
  • Lee: non-ototoxic quinolones
  • Oxford (ENT-UK): untreated infection risks more than a short aminoglycoside course · resistant Pseudomonas rising
  • In practice: quinolone · aminoglycoside short, stop if dizzy
  • 2014: drops first; grommet drops beat oral · Cochrane 2020: quinolones probably best

10Exam traps ✗

  • Drops into pus, drum never seen
  • Months of drops for cholesteatoma or carcinoma
  • NOE called "severe OE"
  • Oil-based drops with a wick
