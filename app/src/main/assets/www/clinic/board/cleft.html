CLEFT LIP & PALATE

commonest head and neck malformation · a team, a timetable, and ears in nearly every child ✎

1Anatomy to restore

  • Lip: orbicularis oris (VII), superior labial artery deep · white roll (a 1 mm step shows), Cupid's bow, philtral columns
  • Incisive foramen splits palate: primary (premaxilla, four incisors, alveolus, lip) · secondary (hard + soft)
  • Levator sling lifts velum · tensor opens the tube, only one by V3 · rest by X (pharyngeal plexus)
  • Greater palatine artery = pedicle of every palatal flap · soft palate: lesser palatine, ascending palatine, ascending pharyngeal

3Embryology

  • Lip + primary palate, weeks four to seven: maxillary prominences (first arch) fuse with medial nasal prominences, which join as the intermaxillary segment (→ philtrum, premaxilla, four incisors) · failure = cleft lip
  • Secondary palate, weeks six to twelve: shelves grow down beside the tongue, rise as it drops, fuse front to back (incisive foramen → uvula) · failure = cleft palate
  • Small jaw, tongue can't drop → wide U-shaped cleft (Robin)

6Describing a cleft

  • Lip: complete (through sill) · incomplete (± Simonart band) · microform (notch, muscle gap)
  • Palate: unilateral (shelf on vomer) · bilateral (vomer free)
  • Veau: I soft · II soft + hard to foramen · III complete unilateral · IV complete bilateral
  • Submucous: mucosa intact, muscle split · Calnan: bifid uvula, zona pellucida, palpable notch · occult: only on nasendoscopy
Bifid uvula, notch or hypernasality → full adenoidectomy can unmask VPI · avoid, or limited superior, + cleft team

7Newborn and ears

  • Antenatal scan → counselling · no suction with a palate cleft → squeezy bottle, Haberman/pigeon teat, upright · watch weight
  • Robin airway: prone/side, nasopharyngeal airway → tongue–lip adhesion, mandibular distraction, tracheostomy · sleep studies
  • Heart, genetics, hearing screen · lip taping, nasoalveolar moulding
  • OME in > 90%: tensor + levator misinserted · test hearing throughout · tubes (often at repair) or aids (NICE NG233, 2023: via cleft team, more otorrhoea) · risk: CSOM, retraction, cholesteatoma

9Lip repair

  • Aims: orbicularis sphincter · white roll · philtrum, sill, alar base · nose
  • Cleft nose: alar cartilage down, back, out · wide base · columella to the non-cleft side · small maxilla
  • Millard: non-cleft (medial) side rotates down, cleft (lateral) side advances · muscle repaired · Fisher, triangular flaps
  • Bilateral: protruding premaxilla, prolabium without muscle, short columella
  • Dehiscence, whistle deformity, white-roll mismatch, scar, nasal asymmetry

10Palate repair

  • 6–12 months (often 9–12), before 18 · early = speech, late = maxillary growth
  • Separate mouth from nose · rebuild the levator sling (intravelar veloplasty): it runs front–back into hard palate
  • Bardach two-flap · von Langenbeck · Furlow double-opposing Z: lengthens + realigns (soft, submucous, VPI) · V–Y pushback stunts growth
  • Fistula up to ~10% (hard–soft junction) · VPI 20–25% · maxillary hypoplasia
  • Submucous: repair only if VPI, feeding or ears
  • TOPS (558 infants, 2023): 6 vs 12 months → VPI at five 8.9% vs 15.0%

11Velopharyngeal dysfunction

  • Insufficiency (structure) · incompetence (nerve, muscle) · mislearning (therapy)
  • Hypernasal vowels, /m n ng/ normal · nasal emission on p b t d s · glottal stops · nasal regurgitation
  • Nasendoscopy + videofluoroscopy · coronal (commonest), sagittal, circular · MRA for carotids, check OSA

12Exam traps ✗

  • Adenoidectomy over an unrecognised submucous cleft
  • Missing Stickler (retina) in Robin sequence
  • Pharyngeal surgery in 22q11 without imaging carotids
  • Stopping hearing surveillance after palate repair
