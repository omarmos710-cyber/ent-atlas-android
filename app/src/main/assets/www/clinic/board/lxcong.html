2Laryngomalacia

  • Floppy supraglottis sucked in · inspiratory fluttering stridor from birth/weeks · worse crying, feeding, supine · better prone · normal cry
  • Peaks six to eight months, gone by 12–24 months · reflux aggravates · types 1 (short AE folds), 2 (redundant arytenoids), 3 (epiglottis falls back, neuromuscular) · can't judge under paralysis

4Paralysed folds

  • Birth trauma (breech, forceps) · Arnold–Chiari, hydrocephalus (bilateral) · cardiac surgery, ductus ligation, cardiomegaly, mediastinal (left) · idiopathic
  • Unilateral: weak breathy cry, mild stridor, chokes with feeds → observe, feeding support
  • Bilateral: good cry, marked stridor, distress → often trach · defer definitive surgery (many recover in a year or two, larynx grows) · shunt Chiari
  • Awake scope · brain, neck, chest imaging · swallow assessment

5Subglottic stenosis

  • Congenital: thick cricoid or soft tissue · < 4 mm term (normal 4.5–5.5), < 3 mm premature · well until a cold → recurrent croup · normal cry
  • Acquired far commoner: neonatal intubation, oversized tubes → failed extubation
  • Cotton–Myer: I ≤ 50%, II 51–70%, III 71–99%, IV no lumen · largest tube with a leak
  • Mild congenital improves with growth · balloon, radial incisions · anterior cricoid split (failed extubation) · LTR with rib graft · cricotracheal resection · trach
  • Soft low-grade acquired: balloon often works · III–IV: open surgery

6Web and atresia

  • Failed recanalisation (tenth week) · usually anterior glottic, concave back edge · test for 22q11
  • Weak or absent cry · thin: divide (knife, CO2) · thick (subglottic): laryngofissure + keel, dilatations
  • Atresia: complete obstruction at birth → emergency airway (or a TOF provides one)

7Subglottic haemangioma

  • Silent at birth → stridor six weeks – six months as it grows · "recurrent croup", normal cry, worse crying
  • Half with skin haemangioma, beard distribution · some mediastinal
  • Soft compressible red-blue, posterolateral subglottis · don't biopsy (bleeds) · MRI/CT
StepDrug and dose
Beforeheart rate, blood pressure, glucose risk · ECG if bradycardia, heart disease, family history · in hospital if under ~5 weeks corrected age or comorbid
First linepropranolol oral, two doses with feeds: 1 mg/kg/day week 1 → 2 week 2 → 3 mg/kg/day · ~6 months (often 12 for airway) · stop if ill, not feeding (hypoglycaemia)
Second lineprednisolone 2–3 mg/kg/day, intralesional steroid, CO2/KTP laser, excision
Airway at risktrach while it involutes (rare now)
RCT (2015): 3 mg/kg/day × six months → cleared 60% vs 4%

8Cysts and laryngocele

  • Saccular + ductal cysts (AE fold, vallecula, subglottis): smooth bluish · aspirate for emergency airway · laser deroof/excise · subglottic cysts after premature intubation
  • Laryngocele: air in saccule · internal, external (thyrohyoid membrane, Valsalva), combined · rare in infants · infected = laryngopyocele · adult: exclude ventricular cancer · endoscopic (internal), external excision (external, combined)

9Laryngeal cleft

TypeExtent (Benjamin–Inglis)
Iinterarytenoid, above folds
IIpartly into cricoid lamina
IIIwhole cricoid → cervical trachea
IVthoracic trachea
  • Chokes, coughs, cyanosis with feeds, aspiration pneumonia, hoarse cry, stridor
  • Palpate the interarytenoid area with a probe at MLS · I: thickened feeds, injection, endoscopic repair · deeper: endoscopic or open

10Beyond the larynx

  • Tracheomalacia: expiratory stridor, barking cough · most improve · aortopexy, stent, trach
  • Vascular rings/slings: double arch, right arch, innominate, PA sling → stridor + dysphagia · CT/MRI, swallow · divide · also TOF + atresia
  • Cri-du-chat (5p deletion): cat-like cry, diamond glottis

11Exam traps ✗

  • Severe noisy baby called malacia without a whole-airway look
  • Biopsying a subglottic haemangioma
  • Missing a cleft: interarytenoid not palpated
