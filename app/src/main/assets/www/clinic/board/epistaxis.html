EPISTAXIS

a sign, not a diagnosis · resuscitate, find the point, ask why ✎

1Two carotids meet in the nose

schematic · blue = internal carotid · green = external carotid
  • Kiesselbach's plexus in Little's area: anteroinferior septum · dry air, thin bound mucosa, fingernail reach → about nine in ten bleeds
  • Sphenopalatine (maxillary): main supply, dominant in ~85% · enters just behind the crista ethmoidalis → classic posterior bleed
  • Woodruff's plexus: lateral wall below the back of the inferior turbinate
  • Bleeding above the middle turbinate = ethmoidal = internal carotid
  • Young: retrocolumellar vein (venous)

4Resuscitate first

sit up, lean forward→pulse, BP, sats · IV, FBC, G&S ± clotting→pinch soft nose ≥5 min (10–15), no peeking→suction, vasoconstrictor, look
  • Trotter's method: leaning over a basin, mouth breathing · ice in the mouth helps a little; ice on the bridge: no proof
  • Swallowed blood hides loss · dark vomit ≠ haematemesis
  • Endoscopy: recurrent, not localised, or suspicious unilateral bleed
  • Bloods only for significant bleeds · CT / MRI if tumour · angiography: pseudoaneurysm, vascular tumour, before embolisation

5The ladder, one rung at a time

first aid + topical→cautery of the point→anterior pack→posterior pack→endoscopic cautery→ligation / embolisation
  • Anaesthetise first · treat only the bleeding point
  • Silver nitrate: ring around the vessel, edge first, then inwards · wipe the vestibule after
  • Bipolar (endoscopic) fails less: fewer packs, admissions, less pain
  • Ribbon gauze 2.5 cm (child 12 mm), paraffin or BIPP, in layers · out at 24 h
  • Sponge / balloon = ED standard · resorbable if anticoagulated or bleeding disorder
Never cauterise both sides of the septum at one sitting → perforation

6Posterior pack, packed patient

  • Postnasal gauze: three ties · two over a dental roll at the columella · third taped to the cheek to pull it out
  • Foley 12–14 F, balloon in the nasopharynx with saline, not air · protect ala + columella · or a dual balloon
  • Admit: posterior packs fail in about a third
  • Oxygen: nasopulmonary reflex → hypoxia in the elderly
  • Watch: alar necrosis, sinusitis, otitis media, aspiration, toxic shock
Hypertension: treat pain + anxiety first, no sharp drops · topical TXA: NoPAC (496 pts) 43.7% vs 41.3% packed = no benefit · oral TXA helps in HHT
US guideline (Tunkel 2020) · UK audit 1,826 cases: 1 in 7 back within 30 days, 3.4% dead at 30 days

7The vessel follows the site

BleedOperation

septal deviationseptal surgery to reach it
  • TESPAL: flap back to the crista, clip every branch · control 95–98% · fails → accessory branch or ethmoidal source
  • Ethmoidal via Lynch incision · 24-12-6 mm: lacrimal crest → anterior → posterior foramen → optic canal
  • Embolisation (internal maxillary) ~88% · unfit, tumours, pseudoaneurysm · risks stroke, blindness
  • Historical: maxillary ligation (fails 10–15%), external carotid ligation

8Special patients

  • Child: antiseptic cream twice daily × four weeks (no arachis oil in peanut allergy) · bruising or family history → clotting + von Willebrand
  • Anticoagulated: local control first, resorbable pack, INR · reverse only if life-threatening, with the prescriber
  • HHT: repeated packing + cautery make it worse

9Exam traps ✗

  • Underestimating loss in an elderly patient swallowing blood
  • Blind packing before suction and a look
  • Stopping anticoagulants for a bleed a pack would stop
  • Embolising an ethmoidal bleed
  • Repacking recurrent bilateral bleeds that are really HHT
