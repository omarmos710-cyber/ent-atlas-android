THE TYMPANIC MEMBRANE

trauma, retraction, barotrauma and sequelae ✎

1The drum records what happens

Lose the fibrous layer on healing → thin, floppy drum · normal right drum: malleus handle, lateral process, cone of light at five o'clock
→ in every case: vertigo? facial weakness? SNHL?

2Traumatic perforation

CauseExamples
Directcotton buds, hairpins, matchsticks, careless syringing / instruments
Pressureslap, blast, fall onto water, kiss on the ear
Fracturetemporal bone
Thermalwelding sparks, hot slag: heal poorly
Sudden pain, a little bleeding, deafness, tinnitus · hole irregular or slit-like, clot, edges turned in
Look deeper: vertigo, SNHL or facial weakness → stapes subluxation, perilymph fistula or fracture → urgent assessment, consider exploration

3Most heal on their own

assess→keep dry, keep out→wait→repair if persists
  • Assess: forks, nystagmus, facial nerve, audiogram when practical · CT if fracture suspected
  • No drops, no syringing, no water · antibiotics only if infected
  • Most heal within weeks · large with in-turned edges → reposition + paper patch under microscope
  • Persists beyond ~three months → myringoplasty

Complications

persistent hole · infection · ossicular dislocation · perilymph fistula · implantation cholesteatoma (squamous driven inwards)

4Retraction & atelectasis

Tube fails → gas absorbed → negative pressure draws drum in · healed / atrophic areas (no fibrous layer) retract first
  • Dull drum, broken / absent light reflex
  • Prominent lateral process of malleus
  • Foreshortened handle of malleus
  • Prominent anterior + posterior malleolar folds

  • Deep pocket collecting keratin / can't be cleaned → excise + cartilage before it becomes cholesteatoma

6Scars of past disease

  • Atrophic drum: thin transparent segment, no fibrous layer (healed hole, long effusion) · moves too much, retracts easily
  • Tympanosclerosis: chalky hyaline / calcified plaques in fibrous layer ± around ossicles, after recurrent OM or grommets · drum plaques usually silent · ossicular deposits → fixation, conductive loss hard to correct

7Barotrauma (aero-otitis media)

descent / diving: outside pressure ↑→tube doesn't open→difference ~90 mm Hg→tube locks
Swallowing / Valsalva can't open it · worse with a cold, allergy, sleeping through descent
  • Severe pain, deafness, tinnitus, ± vertigo · drum retracted, congested, haemorrhage → effusion / haemotympanum / perforation
  • Inner ear: forceful Valsalva against a locked tube → round / oval window rupture → SNHL + vertigo (perilymph fistula) → may need exploration
  • Rx: decongestant, analgesia, gentle Valsalva → politzerisation / myringotomy → grommet if recurrent

Prevention

don't fly with a cold · decongestant spray before descent · stay awake on descent · swallow, yawn, Valsalva repeatedly

8Evidence

Lou 2011: most heal spontaneously within three months; small and dry heal faster · inverted / everted edges: observation, gelfoam patch and approximation + patch heal equally; intervention is faster · retraction pockets: grommets, cartilage, excision used, but no good randomised trials

9Exam traps ✗

  • Syringing or drops into a fresh traumatic perforation
  • Missing a perilymph fistula: vertigo after a slap or dive
  • Ignoring a deep pocket until it's a cholesteatoma
