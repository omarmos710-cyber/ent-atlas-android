RADIOTHERAPY & CHEMOTHERAPY FOR HEAD AND NECK CANCER

teeth first · cisplatin is the partner · know when a stiff jaw or exposed bone is the radiotherapy and when it's the cancer ✎

1Physics

  • Ionising photons (linac X-rays, cobalt-60 gamma), electrons, protons, neutrons, carbon · 1 Gy = 1 J/kg = 100 rad
  • Megavoltage 4–25 MV: dose builds up below skin → skin sparing
  • Linear accelerator photons for most treatment · electrons: skin, lip, nodes over cord · protons: skull base, orbit, re-RT, children · neutrons, carbon: salivary, radioresistant

2Biology

  • Mostly indirect: water ionised → free radicals → double-strand breaks · cells die when they divide, or by apoptosis
  • Oxygen fixes damage · hypoxic cells two to three times resistant → Hb ~12 g/dL, stop smoking, nimorazole
  • Four R's: repair (normal tissue better), reoxygenation, redistribution · repopulation after ~four weeks → gaps are harmful
  • BED = nd(1 + d/(α/β)) · late tissues α/β ~3 (spared by small fractions) · tumour ~10

3Delivery

  • Mask + mouth bite → planning CT + MRI/PET → GTV → CTV → PTV + organs at risk (cord, brainstem, parotids, optic, cochlea, larynx, mandible, constrictors) → plan → daily imaging
  • 2D → 3D conformal → IMRT/VMAT (standard): dose curved round cord + parotids, simultaneous integrated boost · PARSPORT (2011): halved severe xerostomia
  • IGRT (cone-beam CT), adaptive re-planning
  • Stereotactic: one to five sessions · VS 12–13 Gy single · paraganglioma, small recurrences, re-RT
  • Brachytherapy: iridium-192 HDR afterloading · lip, tongue, floor, nasopharynx · boost or recurrence

5When

  • Definitive: early T1–T2 N0 larynx (T1 glottic ~90%), oropharynx, NPC, hypopharynx · + chemo for advanced
  • Postoperative: start four to six weeks, surgery → end < ~11 weeks · margins < 5 mm/+, ENE, ≥ two nodes or > 3 cm, pT3–4, PNI, LVI, IV–V nodes, poorly differentiated
  • + margin or ENE → add cisplatin (EORTC 22931/RTOG 9501)
  • Pre-op rare (fistulae, flap failure, blow-out) · palliative short courses
  • Larynx: advanced → cisplatin CRT (RTOG 91-11) · T4a, dead larynx → laryngectomy · NPC: RT primary (II, V, retropharyngeal), EBV DNA follow-up
  • HPV+ oropharynx: 3-year survival 82% vs 57% (RTOG 0129), de-escalate only in trials · oral cavity: surgery first · adenoid cystic: nerve to skull base
  • After CRT: PET-CT at 12 weeks (PET-NECK) · recurrence: salvage surgery > re-RT

6Chemotherapy

  • Sensitise, micrometastases, organ preservation, palliation · never curative alone
  • Concurrent = standard: MACH-NC ~6.5% at five years, all from concurrent · cisplatin 100 mg/m² three-weekly or 40 weekly (JCOG1008: weekly non-inferior post-op, 2022)
  • Induction TPF (> PF): larynx selection, bulky nodes · not better than CRT (DeCIDE, PARADIGM)
  • Adjuvant chemo: no benefit except NPC
  • Cetuximab (EGFR, > 90%): Bonner (2006) + RT · cisplatin-unfit · EXTREME · rash
  • Pembrolizumab first-line R/M (KEYNOTE-048) · nivolumab after platinum (CheckMate 141) · thyroiditis, pneumonitis, colitis, adrenal
HPV+ oropharynx: RT + cetuximab worse than cisplatin, no less toxic (RTOG 1016, De-ESCALaTE, 2019)

8Beyond SCC

  • Radiosurgery 90–95% small VS · ~90% paragangliomas · JNA residue · NK/T lymphoma · protons for chordoma, chondrosarcoma · olfactory neuroblastoma

10Osteoradionecrosis

  • Exposed irradiated bone not healed in three months, no tumour · posterior mandible · hypovascular, hypocellular, hypoxic
  • > 60 Gy, extraction after RT, bad teeth, surgery, smoking · pain, fistula, fracture → biopsy to exclude recurrence
  • Dental check before RT, extractions ≥ two weeks before, fluoride trays
  • Irrigate, antibiotics, sequestrectomy · PENTOCLO · HBO selected · advanced: resect + fibula

11Exam traps ✗

  • RT before the teeth are assessed
  • Extractions in an irradiated mandible without precautions
  • Long breaks in radiotherapy
  • Post-op RT starting > six weeks after surgery
  • No TSH or audiometry in follow-up
