Two ears are better

head shadow · summation (~3 dB louder) · squelch (better in noise) · localisation

2Hearing aids

Microphone, amplifier, receiver, battery · digital gain shaping + compression
  • BTE: most versatile, mild → profound · children (mould changes as the ear grows)
  • RIC / open fit: high-frequency loss, good low tones, no occlusion
  • ITE / ITC / CIC: cosmetic, mild–moderate adults with good dexterity · body-worn now rare
  • Do well: discrimination ≥ ~50%, wide dynamic range
  • Recruitment narrows the dynamic range → compression · occlusion → venting (loses low gain) · feedback → moulds, cancellation
Cochrane 2017: aids improve hearing-specific QoL (high certainty)

3Bone-conduction devices

Vibrate the skull → both cochleas, bypassing canal + middle ear
TypeExamples
conventionalheadband, spectacles
skin-drive (passive transcutaneous)Baha Attract, Adhear · lose energy, being superseded

active transcutaneousBonebridge, Osia
  • Chronic discharging ears · atresia, microtia · conductive / mixed when surgery unsuitable · SSD with other ear ~20 dB or better
  • Osseointegration ~three months (longer in children) · failure, skin reactions · no localisation
  • UK criteria: bone conduction in range + stable (≤15 dB change at ≥2 frequencies over two years) · aid / CROS failed after ≥ four weeks · headband trial ≥14 days
  • CROS: deaf-side mic → better ear · BiCROS adds gain for a weaker better ear · Dhingra: bone devices replaced CROS; Lee: CROS still an option

4Middle ear implants

Transducer on an ossicle or the round window (Vibrant Soundbridge floating mass) · adults, moderate–severe SN or mixed, unhappy with aids; some conductive · canal left open

5Cochlear implant

mic + processor→across skin to receiver→array in scala tympani→spiral ganglion
place coding: basal electrodes high pitch, apical low
  • Adults: severe–profound bilateral, little aid benefit · Lee: aided sentence ≤50% implant ear, ≤60% best-aided · NICE TA566: 80 dB HL at ≥2 frequencies, AB words ≤50%
  • Children: from ~twelve months (ideally 9–12), three to six month aid trial · trial waived after meningitis · bilateral simultaneous
  • Outcome set by duration of deafness + pre-op speech · postlingual adults high scores · early children mainstream school · prelingual adults mostly sound awareness (language window to ~two years)
  • Work-up: aid trial · MRI (nerve, fluid-filled cochlea) · HRCT (malformation, IAC <3 mm, hazards) · psychology, speech
Contraindications: Michel aplasia · complete ossification · absent cochlear nerve · (retrocochlear disease relative)

6The operation & after

cortical mastoidectomy→posterior tympanotomy (facial recess)→round window / cochleostomy→slow insertion, seal, test
Soft surgery (flexible array, round window, slow, no perilymph suction, steroids) keeps residual hearing · ossified → drill basal turn / split array · Dhingra cochleostomy, Lee round window
Switch on at two to four weeks → mapping + auditory therapy; better over three to six months

7Complications & vaccines

  • Flap breakdown, extrusion · device failure ~4% lifetime (revise same track) · facial injury <1%, facial stimulation (switch off electrodes) · taste, vertigo, tinnitus · gusher (malformations) · meningitis
  • Pneumococcal vaccine: PCV13, then PPSV23 ≥ eight weeks later
  • Malformations → anomalous facial nerve, gusher, meningitis · auditory neuropathy can do well · treat AOM promptly (IV if severe)

8Brainstem implant & more

  • ABI on the cochlear nucleus via the lateral recess of the fourth ventricle · NF2, nerve aplasia, complete ossification · more modest results
  • Atresia: headband bone aid by ~six months → implant at ~four to five years
  • SSD: cochlear implant beats CROS / bone devices for localisation + speech in noise
  • ACHIEVE (Lancet 2023): hearing intervention slowed cognitive decline in higher-risk older adults

9Exam traps ✗

  • Aiding an asymmetric loss without excluding retrocochlear disease
  • Delaying implantation after meningitis
  • Forgetting pneumococcal vaccination
