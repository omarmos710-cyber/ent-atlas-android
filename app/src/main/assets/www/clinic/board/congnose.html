Newborns breathe preferentially through the nose → bilateral block = distress at birth

2Choanal atresia

  • Persistent bucconasal membrane · one-sided commoner, found later · bony ~90% in older texts, modern series mostly mixed
  • CHARGE: coloboma, heart, atresia, retarded growth, genital, ear · about half of CHARGE have bilateral atresia · also Treacher Collins, Crouzon, Apert
BilateralUnilateral
distress at birth · cyclical cyanosis: blue at rest or feeding, pink when crying · can't feedone-sided block + thick discharge, later childhood

3Prove it

  • Mucoid discharge without air bubbles
  • 6–8 F catheter won't pass more than ~3 cm
  • Methylene blue doesn't reach the pharynx · cotton wisp doesn't move
  • Flexible endoscopy · axial CT: atretic plate, narrow back of nose, thick vomer, bony vs membranous (keep for planning)

4Bilateral: airway first

oral airway or McGovern nipple (cut-end teat taped in)→try a catheter each side→CT after suction + decongestion→look for CHARGE: heart, eyes, ears, genitals
Orogastric feeding · intubate if needed · avoids tracheostomy

5Repair

  • Endoscopic transnasal with a drill: plate + back of vomer out (posterior septectomy) ± mucosal flaps = standard
  • Transpalatal: better view, historically ~18 months · risks palatal growth
  • Unilateral: elective · restenosis common → dilate, revise · saline, steroid · mitomycin C unproven

Stents?

Two to six weeks of cut ET tubes: meta-analysis (2016) 65% vs 64% success with vs without · alar injury, vestibular stenosis, blockage → routine stenting no longer justified · unilateral: not worthwhile

6Pyriform aperture stenosis

  • Overgrown nasal processes of the maxilla → narrow front door, mimics bilateral atresia
  • Single central incisor → holoprosencephaly: pituitary + brain work-up
  • Endoscopy + CT tell them apart · medical, or sublabial drilling if distress, sleep, growth, feeding suffer

7Where midline masses come from

schematic sagittal · also via the fonticulus frontalis (frontal–nasal bones)
About 1 in 20,000–40,000 births · may keep an intracranial connection · skin elements = dermoid, glial tissue = glioma, brain + meninges = encephalocele

9The three masses

  • Dermoid (commonest): glabella → columella, midline pit ± hair, infects · simple / sinus under nasal bones / tract to dura · excise cyst, tract, involved bone (open rhinoplasty) ± neurosurgery
  • Glioma = ectopic glia, not a tumour · 60% extranasal (red, telangiectatic), 30% intranasal (pale "polyp"), 10% both · external excision, craniotomy if connected
  • Encephalocele: meningocele or meningoencephalocele, opens into the subarachnoid space · may transilluminate
  • Sincipital (outside: nasofrontal, nasoethmoidal, naso-orbital) vs basal (inside nose / nasopharynx, transethmoidal, sphenoethmoidal, transsphenoidal, sphenomaxillary: looks like a polyp) · divide stalk, repair dura + bone

10Others & current practice

  • Nasolacrimal duct cyst: Hasner's valve shut → bluish swelling under the inferior turbinate · bilateral can block a newborn · marsupialise
  • Haemangioma of the tip: grows year one, regresses · propranolol
  • Arhinia, proboscis lateralis, bifid nose (rare)
  • Conservative septoplasty in children appears safe · adenoids: biggest at 6–10, most obstructive at 2–6

11Exam traps ✗

  • Missing unilateral atresia in a child with one-sided discharge
  • A nasal mass in a child taken for a polyp and biopsied
  • Routine stents, and any stent after unilateral repair
  • Forgetting the CHARGE anomalies
