CARCINOMA OF THE HYPOPHARYNX

the quiet killer · grows silent in a hidden gutter, spreads early, presents with a neck lump ✎

1Who and why

  • > 90% SCC · common in India · piriform dominates in N America, postcricoid in parts of Europe
  • Tobacco + alcohol
  • Piriform: pharyngoepiglottic fold → oesophagus · postcricoid: behind cricoid lamina · posterior wall: hyoid → cricoarytenoid joints
  • Plummer–Vinson (Paterson–Brown–Kelly): iron deficiency, dysphagia, upper oesophageal webs, glossitis, koilonychia · women thirty to fifty → postcricoid
  • Benign: exceptionally rare (papilloma, adenoma, lipoma, fibroma, leiomyoma)

2Three subsites

viewed from above and behind, schematic · one large Indian series

3Why it presents late

  • Silent: roomy sinus, large before it obstructs
  • Submucosal spread past the visible edge → generous margins
  • Nodes early: 60–75% at presentation, often both sides, retropharyngeal + paratracheal
  • Second primary in the oesophagus in a significant minority · distant spread ~a fifth

4Piriform sinus

  • Men over forty · something sticking, pricking on swallowing, one side → odynophagia, referred otalgia (internal laryngeal, X), dysphagia · high neck lump may be first
  • Hoarseness, stridor: larynx invaded, hemilarynx fixed
  • Spread: up (vallecula, tongue base) · down (postcricoid, oesophagus) · medial (paraglottic → fixed cord) · lateral (thyroid cartilage, membrane → thyroid, neck)
  • Nodes ~three-quarters, half bilateral · may appear long after the primary is controlled
Saliva pooling in one sinus · puff the cheeks to open it and show the tumour

5Postcricoid: the woman's cancer

  • Women, even twenties + thirties · Plummer–Vinson in about a third
  • Dysphagia solids first, weight loss · voice change: RLN, posterior cricoarytenoid, joint
  • Annular → tight stenosis · down into oesophagus · paratracheal nodes (impalpable, both sides)
  • Hidden on mirror · arytenoid oedema, pooling, loss of laryngeal crepitus · wide prevertebral shadow
  • Small: RT/CRT · most: laryngopharyngo-oesophagectomy + gastric pull-up or colon · free jejunum if oesophagus spared

6Posterior wall

  • Least common · men over fifty · exophytic · stays local, then prevertebral fascia + vertebrae
  • Nodes ~half, bilateral, retropharyngeal · dysphagia, blood, neck mass
  • Early: RT or lateral pharyngotomy / transhyoid / transoral, larynx kept · late: laryngopharyngectomy
  • Fixed to prevertebral fascia = T4b, unresectable

7Mapping a hidden tumour

  • Flexible laryngoscopy: sinuses, postcricoid, posterior wall · cord mobility · palpate crepitus, neck, thyroid, central compartment
  • Barium swallow: lower limit, webs, second oesophageal tumour
  • CT neck + chest (cartilage, prevertebral, nodes) · MRI soft tissue · PET-CT distant + second primaries
  • Panendoscopy: biopsy, map submucosal spread, oesophagoscopy
  • Thyroid function, Hb + iron, nutrition, swallow

10Evidence and outcome

  • EORTC 24891: induction cisplatin + fluorouracil → RT in responders = survival of laryngectomy · larynx kept in ~a third of survivors at ten years
  • GORTEC 2000-01: adding docetaxel (TPF) beat PF for larynx preservation
  • Guidelines: larynx preservation for T2–T3 who'd need laryngectomy · T4a with cartilage invasion or dead larynx: primary laryngopharyngectomy

11Treating the piriform sinus

  • Early N0: RT/CRT, transoral laser or robotic, Ogura's partial laryngopharyngectomy
  • Advanced, confined: total laryngectomy + partial pharyngectomy · to postcricoid: laryngopharyngectomy · CRT for positive margin or extranodal spread
  • Dhingra: laryngectomy + partial pharyngectomy, routine post-op RT · RT for early
  • Lee (NCCN): induction, CRT, transoral, Ogura · many prefer non-surgical larynx preservation
  • In practice: larynx preservation for T2–T3 · primary surgery for T4a

12Exam traps ✗

  • One-sided pricking on swallowing dismissed as globus
  • Missing a synchronous oesophageal cancer
  • Narrow margins in a tumour that spreads submucosally
