CARCINOMA OF THE HYPOPHARYNX

the quiet killer · grows silent in a hidden gutter, spreads early, presents with a neck lump ✎

1Who and why

  • > 90% SCC · common in India · piriform dominates in N America, postcricoid in parts of Europe
  • Tobacco + alcohol · Plummer–Vinson (Paterson–Brown–Kelly): iron deficiency anaemia, dysphagia, upper oesophageal webs, glossitis, koilonychia · women thirty to fifty → postcricoid

Why it presents late

  • Silent: roomy sinus, large before it obstructs
  • Submucosal spread past the visible edge → generous margins
  • Nodes early: 60–75% at presentation, often both sides, retropharyngeal + paratracheal
  • Second primary in the oesophagus in a significant minority · distant spread ~a fifth

2Three subsites

Piriform: pharyngoepiglottic fold → oesophagus · postcricoid: behind cricoid lamina · posterior wall: hyoid → cricoarytenoid joints

8Rebuild · evidence · debate

  • Partial defect: primary, pectoralis, radial forearm, ALT patch · circumferential: tubed free flap or free jejunum · with oesophagectomy: gastric pull-up (colon) · voice: tracheoesophageal puncture + valve
  • EORTC 24891 (piriform, AE fold): induction PF → RT in responders = survival of laryngectomy · larynx kept in ~a third of survivors at ten years
  • GORTEC 2000-01: adding docetaxel (TPF) beat PF for larynx preservation

Surgery or larynx preservation?

  • Dhingra: laryngectomy + partial pharyngectomy (piriform), laryngopharyngectomy if postcricoid, routine post-op RT · RT for early
  • Lee (NCCN): induction, CRT, transoral, Ogura · many prefer non-surgical larynx preservation

10Piriform sinus · the commonest

  • Spread: up (vallecula, tongue base) · down (postcricoid, oesophagus) · medial (AE fold, ventricle, paraglottic → fixed cord) · lateral (thyroid cartilage, thyrohyoid membrane → thyroid, neck)
  • Nodes ~three-quarters, half bilateral, upper + middle jugular · may appear long after the primary is controlled
  • Men over forty · something sticking, pricking, one side → odynophagia, referred otalgia (internal laryngeal, X), dysphagia · high neck lump may be first · hoarseness, stridor: larynx, hemilarynx fixed
  • Early N0: RT/CRT, transoral laser/robotic, Ogura's · advanced, confined: total laryngectomy + partial pharyngectomy · to postcricoid: laryngopharyngectomy · post-op RT, CRT if margin/ENE · many: CRT or induction to save larynx, surgery for salvage
Saliva pooling in one sinus · puff the cheeks to open it and show the tumour

11Postcricoid: the woman's cancer

  • Annular → tight stenosis · down into oesophagus · forward to arytenoids, cricoid · paratracheal nodes (impalpable, both sides)
  • Women, even twenties + thirties · Plummer–Vinson in about a third · dysphagia solids first, weight loss · voice: RLN, posterior cricoarytenoid, joint
  • Hidden on mirror · arytenoid oedema, pooling, loss of laryngeal crepitus · wide prevertebral shadow
  • Small: RT/CRT · most: laryngopharyngo-oesophagectomy + gastric pull-up or colon · free jejunum if oesophagus spared

12Posterior wall · benign

  • Least common · exophytic · stays local, then prevertebral fascia, muscles, vertebrae · nodes ~half, bilateral, retropharyngeal
  • Men over fifty · dysphagia, spitting blood, neck mass
  • Early: RT or lateral pharyngotomy / transhyoid / transoral, larynx kept · late: laryngopharyngectomy · fixed to prevertebral fascia = T4b, unresectable
  • Benign: exceptionally rare (papilloma, adenoma, lipoma, fibroma, leiomyoma)

13Exam traps ✗

  • One-sided pricking on swallowing dismissed as globus
  • Missing a synchronous oesophageal cancer
  • Narrow margins in a tumour that spreads submucosally
