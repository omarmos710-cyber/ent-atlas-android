APPROACH TO THE WATERING EYE

too many tears or too little drainage? · confirm the block, find its level, look in the nose · DCR for blocks at or below the sac ✎

1Oversupply or poor drainage?

  • Hypersecretion: dry eye with reflex tearing (up to 40% of referrals), blepharitis, trichiasis, entropion, ectropion, allergy, FB, crocodile tears after VII regeneration
  • Poor drainage: lid pump failure (facial palsy, lax lid), punctum, canaliculus, sac or duct
  • NLD obstruction: older adults, women, rises with age

2The drainage system

Lower canaliculus carries most flow · sac lies under the frontal process of maxilla, in front of middle turbinate and uncinate

3Red flags

  • Firm mass above the medial canthal tendon, bloody tears or blood on syringing → sac tumour
  • Watering + nasal block, bleeding, numbness → sinonasal tumour
  • Dacryocystitis + orbital cellulitis signs → urgent
  • Neonate, blue medial canthal swelling → dacryocystocele (can block the nose)

4Assess

  • Constant or intermittent, sticky, swelling, pain, red eye · trauma, sinus surgery, RT, sarcoid, GPA, glaucoma drops, docetaxel
  • Lid position, laxity, puncta, ocular surface · press the sac: mucopus from puncta = mucocoele/dacryocystitis
  • Endoscope: septum, turbinates, polyps, surgery, tumour, access

5Tests

  • Dye disappearance: 2% fluorescein, retained at five minutes = poor drainage
  • Jones I: dye under turbinate = patent · Jones II: syringe after failed I → dye = partial, none = complete or pump failure
  • DCG anatomy · scintigraphy function · CT/MRI after trauma, surgery, tumour, sinus disease

7Endonasal DCR

  • Mucosal flap over frontal process + lacrimal bone → drill/punch → expose the whole sac → open widely, flaps to nasal mucosa
  • No scar, pump preserved, nasal disease treated together · stents selectively (canalicular)
  • ~90% for NLD block if sac fully exposed · 85–95% reported · mitomycin C helps (2014)
  • Failure: opening too small or too high, scar, synechiae to turbinate/septum
  • Dry eye: lubricants, plugs · dacryocystitis: antibiotics ± drain → DCR later

8Primary and congenital

  • Primary acquired: idiopathic fibrosis, middle-aged/older women, mucocoele, dacryocystitis
  • Congenital: ~20% of newborns · most clear by one year with massage ± drops · then probing → intubation, balloon · dacryocystocele with breathing trouble → endoscopic marsupialisation

9Pathway

Surface + lids: dry eye, blepharitis, malposition → ophthalmic · normal → syringe Syringe: free → Jones · same punctum → canalicular · opposite/mucopus → sac/duct Sac/duct: scope · mass/blood → CT/MRI, biopsy · primary → DCR Infant: massage to one year → probe → intubate · cyst + breathing → marsupialise

10Exam traps ✗

  • DCR for a dry eye or lid problem
  • DCR alone for a canalicular block
  • Sac opening too small or too high
  • Sac tumour missed when syringing bleeds
