PINNA HAEMATOMA & PREAURICULAR SINUS

two small operations that fail by leaving something behind ✎

1Pinna haematoma

  • Shearing blow (wrestling, rugby, boxing, judo) → blood between cartilage and perichondrium
  • Cartilage fed by the perichondrium → lifted off → starves, infects (perichondritis) or makes disorganised new cartilage = cauliflower ear
Look beyond the ear: head injury first · haematoma in a baby not yet walking → non-accidental injury · red, hot, very painful → perichondritis: anti-pseudomonal antibiotics + drainage

3Drain and close the space

schematic cross-section: bolster holds skin + perichondrium on the cartilage, both sides
  • Ring block (plain LA traditional; adrenaline is safe) or GA in children
  • Incise along the inner curve of the helix or a natural fold · milk / wash out clot, curette organised clot · leave open or loosely closed
  • Close dead space: bolster sutures, splints, or every concavity packed under a head bandage
Recurrence comes from the dead space, not the method
Aftercare: review 4–7 days · bolster ~a week (Lee 7–14 days) · antibiotics usually · headgear for sport · risks: refill, perichondritis, necrosis, cauliflower ear, pressure necrosis from a tight bolster · fewer recurrences with ENT-led drainage + bolster (87 cases, 2020)

4Preauricular sinus

  • Blind tract opening in front of the ascending helical crus · incomplete fusion of the hillocks of His
  • ≠ first branchial cleft anomaly (which runs to the canal and facial nerve)
  • Fine, branching tract, close to / through helical cartilage, ends on the temporalis fascia above the parotid
  • Asymptomatic pit → nothing · infection → co-amoxiclav, aspirate / small incision for abscess · excise after recurrent infection, once settled

Pits + kidneys

ask about hearing + family · pits with branchial cysts / fistulae + hearing loss → branchio-oto-renal: examine the neck, renal ultrasound

5Excise the block, don't chase

Supra-auricularClassic sinectomy
Howellipse extended up into postauricular / temporal skin · find temporalis fascia above helical root · take all tissue between fascia and skin as one block + sliver of helical cartilageellipse + dissect the tract (probe, methylene blue, microscope)
Recurrence2.2%5.5% (≈1.9% with microscope)
Dye can mislead: probing + dye make false passages and miss branches → wide excision to fascia + helical root
  • Undermine, absorbable deep layer, fine skin sutures, pressure dressing · drains: more infection, no clear benefit
  • Risks: recurrence (main), infection, haematoma, scar, helical cartilage, rarely frontal branch of VII (too far forward and low over the zygoma)

6Exam traps ✗

  • Draining a haematoma without a bolster
  • Missing a head injury or non-accidental injury
  • Operating on an acutely infected sinus
  • Chasing the tract instead of excising the block
