2Joints and membranes

  • Cricoarytenoid: rotation (ab/adduct) + glide · fixed by rheumatoid, intubation
  • Cricothyroid: cricoid tilts → lengthens + tenses folds
  • Thyrohyoid membrane: pierced by superior laryngeal vessels + internal laryngeal nerve
  • Conus elasticus: free edge = vocal ligament · funnels up from cricoid (subglottic FBs lodge)
  • Quadrangular membrane: lower edge = vestibular ligament

5The vocal fold

  • Vestibule → false folds → ventricle (saccule → laryngocele) → true folds → subglottis to lower cricoid
  • Glottis ~24 mm men, 16 mm women · narrowest adult airway · anterior two-thirds phonatory, posterior third respiratory

6Spaces and mucosa

  • Pre-epiglottic (Boyer): fat before infrahyoid epiglottis, below hyoepiglottic ligament · supraglottic + tongue-base cancer
  • Paraglottic: thyroid lamina ↔ ventricle, quadrangular membrane, conus · transglottic spread, exits via cricothyroid membrane
  • Ciliated except vocal folds + upper vestibule · loose (swells) except posterior epiglottis, folds, small cartilages · no glands on folds

8Two nerves

  • SLN (nodose ganglion), divides at greater horn of hyoid · internal: sensory above folds, under piriform mucosa · external: cricothyroid, with superior thyroid artery → thyroidectomy: lost high pitch, stamina
  • RLN: all other intrinsic muscles (interarytenoid both sides) · sensory below folds · left hooks aorta at ligamentum, right subclavian · enter behind cricothyroid joint · left palsy far commoner
  • Non-recurrent right (~0.5–1%) with retro-oesophageal subclavian
  • Fourth arch → SLN · sixth arch → RLN · lumen closes sixth week, reopens by tenth → webs, atresia
  • Nerve monitoring guideline (2011): check signals before + after · consider staging if first side lost

9The child's larynx

  • High: C3–C4 (C2–C3 in some texts), C1–C2 swallowing · epiglottis meets palate → nasal breathing while feeding · down to C5–C6 (C6–C7) by adulthood
  • Soft, omega epiglottis, big arytenoids · overlapping cartilages → no cricothyroidotomy in young children
  • Subglottis narrowest: 4–5 mm newborn · 1 mm oedema halves it · < 4 mm at term = stenosis
  • Fast growth first three years · folds 6–8 mm child, 15–23 mm adult

10Four functions

  • Protection: inlet → false folds → true folds close · breathing stops · larynx up + forward · cough
  • Phonation: myoelastic–aerodynamic + Bernoulli · loudness = subglottic pressure · pitch = length, tension, mass
  • Respiration: abduct in, partly adduct out · fixation of chest for lifting, labour

11Exam traps ✗

  • Cricothyroid is the external laryngeal nerve's
  • Infant's narrowest part is the subglottis, not glottis
  • Ossified cartilage read as a foreign body
