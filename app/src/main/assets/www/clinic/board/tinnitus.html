APPROACH TO TINNITUS

a symptom, not a disease · image the one-sided and the pulsatile · treat the cause, then the reaction ✎

1What it is

  • Sound with no outside source: ringing, hissing, buzzing, roaring
  • 10–15% of adults · about one in thirty moderately annoyed · about one in two hundred severe
  • Most have some hearing loss · about half of troublesome cases have reduced sound tolerance
  • May be the first sign of schwannoma, glomus, dural AVF · hits sleep, mood

2Who can hear it?

  • Subjective: patient only, by far commonest
  • Objective (stethoscope, Toynbee tube): vascular pulsatile · muscular clicks (palatal myoclonus, stapedius, tensor) · patulous tube blows with breathing · TMJ clicks
  • Brief one-sided tapering ringing with fullness: normal, no tests

3Causes

  • Ear: wax, fluid, OM, otosclerosis, Ménière's, presbycusis, noise, sudden loss, ototoxicity, schwannoma
  • General: anaemia, hypertension, thyroid, lipids, B12, head injury, MS
  • Drugs: salicylates, quinine, aminoglycosides, loop diuretics, chemo · mostly reversible

4Why it bothers some

cochlear loss→central gain ↑→brain labels it a threat→limbic + autonomic→attention, anxiety, louder↺
  • Loudness predicts distress poorly → treat the reaction, not the sound
  • Hyperacusis: everyday sounds intolerable · misophonia: specific sounds (chewing) · phonophobia: fear · recruitment: cochlear loudness growth · facial palsy loses stapedial reflex
  • Explanation, graded sound, CBT · constant ear plugs make it worse

5Assess

  • One ear or both? Pulsatile? Hearing, vertigo, fullness · noise, drugs · sleep, work, mood
  • Microscope (red mass, blue drum) · forks · cranial nerves · auscultate ear, orbit, neck
  • Carotid pressure stops arterial · jugular pressure or head turn stops venous hum · breathing rhythm = patulous tube · palatal clicks = myoclonus
  • PTA, speech, tympanometry, pitch/loudness match · long-duration tympanometry for myoclonus or patulous tube
  • FBC, thyroid, lipids, glucose, B12 as indicated
Ask directly how they are coping, including thoughts of self-harm · refer immediately if at risk

6Pathway

Young obese woman with headache: fundi (IIH) · red mass behind drum: glomus · sudden loss with it: treat as SSNHL · palatal myoclonus: MRI posterior fossa

7Treat

  • Cause: wax, OM, otosclerosis, stop ototoxics, anaemia, thyroid
  • Hearing aids first if any loss · explanation: common, real, not a tumour
  • Masking: background sound, generator, wearable
  • CBT: best evidence · Cochrane 28 trials (2020): less impact, not loudness · NICE NG155: digital → group → individual
  • Dural AVF: embolise (cortical drainage bleeds) · myoclonus: relaxants, botulinum · tendon spasm: divide
  • No drug works · betahistine not recommended · TMS, implants investigational · cochlear implant often helps

8Retraining: books differ

TRT = directive counselling + low-level broadband sound → habituation

Dhingra

At least 8 hours a day for 18–24 months

Lee

Up to 16 hours a day for about a year
In practice: TRT trial (2019) no better than standard counselling → sound therapy within stepped care

9Stepped care, outlook

  • Measure: Tinnitus Handicap Inventory, Tinnitus Functional Index
  • Explanation, hearing care, self-help → audiologist support + sound → psychologist CBT → mental health
  • NICE NG155 (2020) · US 2014: no imaging for symmetrical non-pulsatile, no drugs · Europe 2019 agrees · bimodal 2020 unproven
  • A quarter nearly resolve, half improve, a quarter same · goal: habituation · first consultation shapes outcome

10Exam traps ✗

  • "Nothing can be done"
  • Pulsatile tinnitus missed: nobody asked
  • One-sided tinnitus not imaged
  • Mood ignored
