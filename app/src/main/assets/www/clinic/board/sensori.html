ACQUIRED SENSORINEURAL HEARING LOSS

ototoxicity, noise, presbycusis and immune inner ear disease ✎

1Four causes

Slowly progressive bilateral SNHL = mostly age, noise or drugs → ask about each, stop what still harms · don't miss autoimmune: it can respond to steroids · much is preventable, some reversible
Also exclude: vestibular schwannoma · Ménière's · syphilis · multiple sclerosis · familial progressive SNHL

4Noise

schematic · symmetrical in both ears
  • Outer hair cells, basal turn (~4 kHz) fail first · TTS recovers (Dhingra up to two weeks, Lee within 24 hours) → repeated → PTS
  • High-pitched tinnitus, trouble hearing in noise · notch deepens + widens into speech frequencies
  • Acoustic trauma (~140 dB or more): drum, ossicles + cochlea at once, no preceding TTS → treat like sudden SNHL, early steroids
  • Hidden loss: synaptopathy (Kujawa & Liberman, mice, 2009); human effect debated · recreational noise rising in the young (WHO: >1 billion at risk)
  • Tests: PTA after ≥16 h away from noise · asymmetry >~15 dB or unilateral tinnitus → MRI IACs · workplace dosimetry + surveillance audiometry

7Immune inner ear disease

  • Primary: rapidly progressive bilateral, often asymmetric SNHL over weeks–months · age 40–50 · vestibular in ~half · ~15% another autoimmune disease
  • Secondary: Cogan (interstitial keratitis + audiovestibular loss), GPA, SLE, antiphospholipid
  • Dx: bilateral ≥30 dB, progressing on serial pure-tone + speech audiograms within three months · MRI IACs every patient · ESR, CRP, ANA, RF, ANCA, complement, syphilis, thyroid, glucose · anti-Hsp70 (activity, not specific) · eye symptoms → slit-lamp (Cogan) · steroid response = best practical test

Treatment

Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day (≤60 mg) for four weeks · response = 15 dB at one / 10 dB at consecutive frequencies, or better discrimination → continue to plateau, taper slowly to lowest dose; non-responders taper over ~2 weeks · check glucose, blood pressure, bone + gastric protection · secondary disease → rheumatology · steroid-sparing (methotrexate, cyclophosphamide, biologics, IT steroid) on specialist advice: methotrexate = placebo for maintenance (Harris, JAMA 2003) · implant if lost

8Exam traps ✗

  • Not asking about drugs and noise
  • Missing a mitochondrial aminoglycoside susceptibility in the family history
  • Calling rapidly progressive bilateral loss presbycusis
