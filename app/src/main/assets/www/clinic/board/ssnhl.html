SUDDEN SENSORINEURAL HEARING LOSS

deafness overnight: an otological emergency ✎

1Definition & why it matters

≥30 dB SNHL+≥ three contiguous frequencies+within three days
  • ~5–27 per 100,000 a year · rises with age, middle / later life · men = women
  • Almost always one-sided: both ears at once → systemic or central cause
  • Treatment works best in the first two weeks
  • A minority hide vestibular schwannoma or posterior circulation stroke

Idiopathic (most): three theories

Viral

cochleitis / neuritis · seasonal clusters, serology

Vascular

labyrinthine artery = end artery: thrombosis, embolism, spasm, bleed

Membrane rupture

cochlear membranes or round window → perilymph leak

3One ear, often on waking

  • Sudden, one-sided, on waking or over hours · feels like a blocked ear
  • Tinnitus + fullness before or with it · vertigo in some = worse prognosis
  • Canal + drum normal
Forks = key bedside test: Weber → good ear · Rinne positive in the bad ear
Not wax until proven otherwise: a blocked ear after a cold, swim or flight may be SSNHL; assume wax without forks and the treatment window closes

4Confirm, then search

  • Forks first, before steroids · PTA with masking + speech discrimination, same / next day, repeated · pattern (low, high, flat, total) guides prognosis
  • Tympanometry normal → no fluid
  • MRI IACs + CPA with gadolinium, every patient → schwannoma (a few per cent), stroke, demyelination · can't have MRI → ABR (less sensitive for small tumours) · no routine CT
  • Bloods not routine (US guideline): by history: glucose (before steroids), FBC + ESR, syphilis, Lyme, autoimmune if bilateral / fluctuating
  • Exploratory tympanotomy only for strongly suspected fistula

5On the day

forks→exclude wax→audiogram same / next day→steroids early
Facial weakness, vertigo + ataxia, vascular risk → treat as posterior circulation stroke · both ears at once → urgent systemic / infective / autoimmune / central work-up

6Steroids

Oral

Prednisolone 1 mg/kg once each morning (usual max 60 mg) for 7–14 days, then taper over a similar period (STARFISH trial: 7 days) · or methylprednisolone 48 mg / dexamethasone 10 mg daily · as early as possible, ideally within two weeks; unlikely to help after six weeks · check glucose + blood pressure; caution diabetes, peptic ulcer, glaucoma, psychiatric history; gastric protection; warn mood, sleep, stomach
Alternative: 40–60 mg each morning for a week, taper over three weeks
Higher doses don't help: HODOKORT (325 patients): no better at 30 days, more hyperglycaemia + hypertension

Intratympanic

dexamethasone 10 mg/ml (or 16–24 mg/ml compounded) or methylprednisolone 30–40 mg/ml, 0.4–0.8 ml every 3–7 days × 3–4 injections
• first line = as good as oral → when systemic steroids unsafe (poorly controlled diabetes)
• salvage within two to six weeks after oral failure: modest extra gain
Risks: pain, transient vertigo, persistent perforation (few)

7Everything else

  • No proven benefit: antivirals, vasodilators, vasoactive drugs, antioxidants · old: bed rest, carbogen, dextran, low salt
  • Hyperbaric oxygen: option with steroids within two weeks (a month as salvage) · modest gain · access, cost
  • US guideline 2019: steroids within two weeks · IT salvage two to six weeks · HBO option · no antivirals, vasodilators, routine CT or labs · Dhingra lists carbogen, low salt, HBO; Lee: steroids, no antivirals
  • Evidence: Cochrane (Wei 2013): systemic steroid evidence weak · IT = systemic first line (Mirian 2020, seven trials) · HBO + medical ≈ +10 dB (Joshua 2022, three trials) · HODOKORT 2024 (Plontke)

8Rehabilitation

Partial loss → hearing aid · single-sided deafness → CROS, bone-conduction device or cochlear implant · tinnitus counselling · repeat audiogram during + after treatment · protect the other ear from noise and ototoxics

9Prognosis

A third to two-thirds recover partly or fully, much of it spontaneously, mostly in the first two weeks · after three months unusual

10Exam traps ✗

  • Calling it wax or a cold without forks
  • Delaying steroids while waiting for an audiogram slot
  • Forgetting the MRI after the acute phase
  • Missing a stroke with vertigo + ataxia
