APPROACH TO FACIAL WEAKNESS

Bell's palsy is a diagnosis of exclusion · find the patient who does not fit · whatever the cause, the eye comes first ✎

1The forehead decides

  • Upper face: both hemispheres · lower face: opposite only
  • Sudden forehead-sparing palsy = stroke pathway now · emotional smile may survive
  • Pons: face + VI (fibres loop the abducens nucleus)

2Doesn't fit Bell's

  • Forehead sparing, limb, speech, brainstem signs → stroke
  • Slow over weeks, twitching first, recurs same side → tumour until imaged
  • No recovery by three to four months, or worse than HB III
  • Discharging ear, cholesteatoma, AOM · parotid mass, facial skin cancer
  • Bilateral: Lyme, GBS, sarcoid, leukaemia, HIV

3History

  • Speed: Bell's worst within 72 hours (often 24–48) · postauricular pain first
  • Taste, hyperacusis (common in Bell's) · ear pain, vesicles · hearing, tinnitus, vertigo · discharge, surgery, head injury, parotid operations
  • Ticks, travel (Lyme) · ascending weakness (GBS) · diabetes, BP, pregnancy · Melkersson–Rosenthal: recurrent alternating + swelling + fissured tongue

5Find the cause

  • Microscope: cholesteatoma, OM, mass, blood · vesicles in concha, canal, palate, tongue
  • Forks · palpate parotid and neck · skin and scalp cancer · other cranial nerves, limbs, gait

6Causes

  • Bell's: ~70% · ~30 per 100,000 a year · peak ~40 · recurs ~7%
  • Ramsay Hunt: second · geniculate zoster · only ~20% full recovery · zoster sine herpete: pain, hearing loss, vertigo → antiviral
  • AOM child (dehiscent canal, excellent) · cholesteatoma under 1% (up to 50% petrous apex) · NOE · fracture (otic capsule), surgery
  • Tumour: schwannoma, haemangioma · paraganglioma, meningioma, VS · parotid cancer, perineural cSCC
  • Stroke, GBS, Lyme, sarcoid · Möbius, CHARGE

7Tests

  • Typical Bell's: none needed · PTA useful in all · glucose, Lyme, vesicle PCR
  • Gadolinium MRI + CT for any atypical feature
  • ENoG: complete palsy, day 3–21 · > 90% degeneration = poor (trauma exploration)
  • EMG after three weeks: fibrillation, polyphasic · silence = muscle gone

8Pathway

Forehead? spared → stroke · paralysed → LMN Eye now: drops by day, ointment + tape at night, ophthalmology Ear, parotid, nerves: vesicles → steroid + antiviral · cholesteatoma → CT, surgery · mass → imaging, FNA · bilateral → neurology Typical within 72 hours → prednisolone, eye care, review Not recovering by three to four months → MRI + CT, facial clinic

9Treat

  • Bell's: prednisolone within 72 hours, 50–60 mg for 5–10 days · full recovery 64% → 83% (2007) · antivirals: severe or zoster only
  • ~85% start recovering within three weeks · worse: complete, over 60, pain, diabetes, BP · labyrinthine decompression (~0.6 mm) controversial
  • Hunt: steroid + aciclovir/valaciclovir, eye, pain, early therapy
  • Fracture: immediate complete + disrupted canal or > 90% → explore · delayed recovers · cut at surgery → repair now
  • Late: therapy, gold weight, lid tightening, botulinum · reanimation by 18–24 months (2013 guideline: refer if incomplete at three months)

10Exam traps ✗

  • Slow or twitching palsy called Bell's
  • Eye forgotten: cornea lost while face recovers
  • Ear never examined, parotid never felt
  • No review: no recovery by three to four months → image
