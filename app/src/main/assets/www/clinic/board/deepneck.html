DEEP NECK SPACE INFECTION

airway first · image the neck · right antibiotics · drain the pus ✎

1Fascia and the spaces behind

  • Superficial (investing): SCM, trapezius, glands · middle (visceral): airway, gullet, thyroid · deep: prevertebral + alar · all three → carotid sheath
front to back: retropharyngeal · (alar) · danger · (prevertebral) · prevertebral
Parapharyngeal = crossroads (retropharyngeal, submandibular, parotid, carotid, visceral) · carotid sheath = "Lincoln highway" to the root of the neck

3Assess

  • Pain, fever, drooling, voice change, trismus, breathing · recent dental work, sore throat, intubation · diabetes, immunity
  • Teeth, floor of mouth, tonsils, posterior wall · crepitus (gas) · cranial nerves
  • Bloods: count, glucose, renal function, cultures
  • Flexible endoscopy if voice change, dyspnoea, stridor · sats can be normal with a critical airway
  • Contrast CT = key: which spaces, abscess vs phlegmon, mediastinum · lateral film: prevertebral > ~7 mm at C2 (adult), > half a vertebra (child) · ultrasound in children
Adult "abscess" may be a necrotic HPV node or a branchial cyst hiding a primary → examine the upper aerodigestive tract

4Treat

airway: sit up, O₂, steroid, adrenaline nebs→narrowed > half: awake fibreoptic or tracheostomy under LA→fluids→IV antibiotics + anaerobic cover→drain→remove the tooth / FB · control glucose
  • Co-amoxiclav / ampicillin-sulbactam, or 3rd-gen cephalosporin + metronidazole · clindamycin (allergy, small children) · vancomycin (MRSA)
  • Drain: abscess in a space, gas, threatened airway, no better in 48–72 h · small phlegmon (children) often settles
  • Single unilocular abscess, stable: image-guided aspiration
Never paralyse a patient whose airway you can't see · never send them to CT alone
60 studies: CT in 78%, open drainage usual · diabetes doubles multi-space spread (RR 1.96) + complications (2.42)

5Ludwig's angina

  • Sublingual (above mylohyoid) · submandibular (below) · submental · premolar + first molar roots above the mylohyoid line, second + third below
  • Bilateral cellulitis, usually no discrete abscess · teeth ~80%
  • Drooling, trismus, raised floor of mouth pushing the tongue up + back, woody-hard swelling under both sides of the jaw, sits forward
  • Awake fibreoptic / tracheostomy · antibiotics + steroid · drain intraorally or below the jaw through mylohyoid (often serous) · pull the tooth
Laid flat or sedated, the drooling patient can obstruct completely → theatre with the airway team

6Retropharyngeal abscess

  • Gillette nodes (regress ~4–5 years) · child under 3–5 · adult: fish bone, instruments
  • Feeding refused, drooling, stridor, croupy cough · torticollis (best sign, Lee), neck extended · bulge off the midline
  • Lateral film: wide prevertebral shadow ± gas · CT
  • Antibiotics · transoral drainage, supine head-low, aspirate then vertical incision (carotid lateral) · neck if low
Chronic (TB)Drain
spinal caries: midline · TB nodes: to one sidethrough the neck · ATT, spinal team

7Parotid & masticator

  • Parotid: dehydrated, post-op · S. aureus up the duct · hot, hard gland · rehydrate, sialogogues, antibiotics, drain via parotidectomy incision
  • Masticator: second + third molars · marked trismus · drain lateral to the retromolar trigone

10Exam traps ✗

  • Threatened airway sent to the scanner unaccompanied
  • Parapharyngeal swelling incised through the mouth
  • TB prevertebral abscess drained transorally
  • Adult cystic neck mass called an abscess, no primary sought
