APPROACH TO STRIDOR AND THE THREATENED AIRWAY

timing tells where · history tells what · work of breathing tells how long you have · a safe airway before any scan ✎

1Phase tells the site

  • Stertor: nose, nasopharynx, oropharynx · wheeze: small airways
  • Snoring → nose · gurgle, muffled → supraglottis · hoarse, weak cry → folds (cry normal in laryngomalacia, SGS) · bark → subglottis

2How bad?

  • Mild: only when active or crying, no recession
  • Moderate: at rest, recession, tug, flaring, fast breathing, tachycardia
  • Severe: marked recession, agitation (hypoxia), drooling, can't speak or feed
  • Impending arrest: fatigue, slowing rate, quieter stridor, cyanosis, drowsy
Quieter stridor in a tiring child is failure, not improvement · oximetry stays normal until late

4History and exam

  • Onset (birth or later) · sudden (FB, oedema, epiglottitis) or progressive · fever, toxic · feeding, choking, cyanotic spells · witnessed choking · intubation, surgery, trauma · voice
  • Watch from a distance · phase · prone helps laryngomalacia, micrognathia, macroglossia · auscultate nose, mouth, neck, chest
  • Awake flexible scope: dynamic lesions need a breathing patient

5Tests and MLB

  • Only when the airway allows · lateral/AP neck: steeple (croup), thumb (epiglottitis), prevertebral · CXR with insp/exp for radiolucent FB · CT/MRI · contrast swallow · ABG
  • MLB: theatre, experienced anaesthetist, spontaneous breathing · whole airway · a second lesion is common

6Common in children

  • Laryngomalacia: well over half of congenital · inspiratory, first weeks, worse feeding/crying/supine · settles by 18–24 months · omega epiglottis, short AE folds · most: reassurance + reflux treatment · supraglottoplasty for poor weight, apnoeas, cyanosis, pectus
  • Cord palsy: second · bilateral with Arnold–Chiari
  • SGS (often post-intubation) · haemangioma: weeks old, biphasic, beard haemangioma → propranolol
  • Croup: bark, six months to three years · dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg ± nebulised adrenaline · recurrent, atypical, under six months → scope

7Airway first

  • Calm, upright (parent's arms) · humidified O₂ without distress · senior anaesthetist + ENT early
  • Buy time: nebulised adrenaline, dexamethasone, heliox · treat cause
  • Intubate in theatre, ENT scrubbed · tracheostomy if impossible, prolonged, trauma, long-term
  • CICO: scalpel–bougie–tube cricothyroidotomy (DAS 2015) · small child: needle + jet or surgical trach
  • Known tumour: awake tracheostomy under LA or awake fibreoptic, planned before sedation · HFNO extends apnoea time
  • NAP4 (2011): head and neck over-represented, needle FONA often failed

8Pathway

Silent chest, cyanosis, exhausted → airway now, theatre · severe → O₂, adrenaline, dex, seniors Infant inspiratory → laryngomalacia · biphasic, worsening → haemangioma/SGS · bark → croup · sudden while eating → FB, bronchoscopy Febrile, toxic: drooling → supraglottitis · poor croup response → tracheitis Adult: tumour → awake trach · post-thyroid palsy → reintubate/trach · angioedema → adrenaline, stop ACE-i

9Exam traps ✗

  • Child with stridor sent alone to X-ray
  • Quieter stridor taken as improvement
  • Stopping at the first lesion
