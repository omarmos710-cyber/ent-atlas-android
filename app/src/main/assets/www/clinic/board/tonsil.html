TONSILLITIS & PERITONSILLAR ABSCESS

most sore throats need no antibiotic · the one who can't swallow his saliva has a quinsy ✎

1The palatine tonsil

  • In the fossa between anterior (palatoglossal) + posterior (palatopharyngeal) pillars · bigger than it looks
  • Medial: 12–15 crypts · crypta magna = second pouch remnant · lateral: capsule + loose tissue = dissection plane + quinsy space
  • Bed: superior constrictor + styloglossus · IX and an elongated styloid low in the fossa · ICA ~2.5 cm behind + lateral
  • Five arteries: tonsillar branch of facial (main, lower pole), ascending palatine, ascending pharyngeal, dorsal lingual, descending palatine
  • Paratonsillar vein across the capsule = usual bleeder · nodes: jugulodigastric · IX → earache
  • Most active 4–10 years · removal hasn't been shown to harm immunity

2Acute tonsillitis

catarrhalfollicular (spots)parenchymatousmembranous
  • Children + young adults · mostly viral · bacterial: group A strep (also staph, pneumococcus, H. influenzae)
  • Sore throat, odynophagia, fever 38–40°C, earache, abdominal pain (mesenteric adenitis) · tender jugulodigastric nodes
  • Rest, fluids, paracetamol / ibuprofen · penicillin five to ten days, ten where rheumatic fever is common (erythromycin) · no amoxicillin if glandular fever possible
  • → chronic, quinsy, parapharyngeal / neck abscess, otitis media, rheumatic fever, glomerulonephritis

4Faucial diphtheria

C. diphtheriae, incubation two to six days · toxin → myocarditis, later palatal, ocular, diaphragm palsies · airway membrane · antitoxin without waiting: 20,000–40,000 units (< 48 h, tonsil only) or 80,000–120,000 IV after a test dose · penicillin / erythromycin · isolate until three negative swabs · booster · contacts: immunise + erythromycin

5Chronic tonsillitis

  • Follicular (cheesy crypts) · parenchymatous (huge: OSA, cor pulmonale) · fibroid (small, infected)
  • Halitosis, bad taste, cough · flushed anterior pillars + persistent jugulodigastric nodes
  • Size grade 0 in the fossa · 1 < 25% · 2 25–50% · 3 50–75% · 4 > 75% (kissing)
  • Intratonsillar abscess, tonsillolith, cyst · lingual tonsil hypertrophy / abscess
  • PFAPA: fever, aphthae, pharyngitis, adenitis every 3–8 weeks · exclude cyclic neutropenia · one steroid dose aborts · tonsillectomy cures most

6Quinsy

Pus between capsule + superior constrictor · blocked crypt (crypta magna) or Weber glands · mixed aerobes + anaerobes · young adults, one side
mouth open, patient's left quinsy · ✕ = incision: base-of-uvula line meets anterior pillar line, just lateral
  • Fever 40°C, one-sided pain, drooling, hot-potato voice, earache, trismus (best single sign of pus), torticollis
  • Palate + pillar bulge above the tonsil · uvula pushed across · tonsil down + in
  • Unsure → aspirate · CT if trismus prevents a look or parapharyngeal spread

7Treat the quinsy

admit, IV fluids→IV penicillin + metronidazole (or co-amoxiclav) ± one steroid dose→drain: needle or guarded-blade stab + sinus forceps
  • Interval tonsillectomy at four to six weeks after a second adult quinsy (recurs ~17%), sooner in children · hot tonsillectomy: more bleeding
  • Parapharyngeal abscess, laryngeal oedema, aspiration, sepsis, Lemierre, carotid erosion

8Tonsillectomy: who

Paradise / SIGN

7 in one year · 5 a year × two · 3 a year × three · disabling · below: watch
  • OSA = commonest indication in children · second adult quinsy · febrile seizures · one-sided big tonsil: lymphoma (child) / SCC (adult) · unknown primary
  • Relative: carriers, halitosis, valvular heart disease · part of UPPP, styloidectomy
  • Not: Hb < 10 g/dL, acute infection, under three (relative), (submucous) cleft, bleeding disorders, uncontrolled disease
  • NATTINA (Lancet 2023, 453 adults): sore-throat days 23 vs 30, rate ratio 0.53

9The operation

  • Rose's position, Boyle-Davis gag on Draffin's bipods · incise at the anterior pillar, upper pole first, outside the capsule · snare the lower pole
  • Hot (diathermy, coblation, laser): less bleeding on the day, more pain + secondary haemorrhage · lowest power · intracapsular (tonsillotomy): less pain + bleeding, may regrow
  • Dexamethasone once · no routine antibiotics · paracetamol + ibuprofen · no codeine under 12 (or under 18 for OSA) · pain peaks days 5–7

11Exam traps ✗

  • Draining a "quinsy" without trismus or bulge: aspirate first
  • Amoxicillin in glandular fever
  • Ignoring a one-sided big tonsil in an adult
  • Sending home a warning bleed
