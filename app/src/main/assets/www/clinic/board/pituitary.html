PITUITARY TUMOURS & SELLAR LESIONS

endocrine disease, neurosurgical tumour, rhinological door ✎

1The sella and its neighbours

coronal schematic · III, IV, V1, V2 in the lateral wall · VI free beside the carotid
  • Roof: diaphragma sellae · chiasm above → bitemporal hemianopia · lateral spread: VI fails first

2The sphenoid is the corridor

  • Sellar (most adults) · presellar · conchal (children: drill)
  • Lateral wall: carotid + optic bulges, opticocarotid recess between
  • Intersinus septum off midline, may insert on the carotid → never twist it
  • Onodi cell above the sphenoid can carry the optic nerve

Four questions on CT

pneumatisation? · where do the septa insert? · carotid or optic canal dehiscent? · Onodi cell?

3Hormones, fields, MRI

  • Prolactin, morning cortisol + ACTH, GH + IGF-1, TSH + T4, LH, FSH, sex steroids, sodium · osmolalities if thirst
  • Acuity, colour, formal perimetry (baseline, commonest reason to operate)
  • Gadolinium MRI: micro = enhances less than gland · macro: dumbbell at the diaphragma · blood bright on T1
  • CT: septa, dehiscence, calcification · CTA / MRA: aneurysm, kissing carotids

4Pituitary adenoma

  • ~90% of sellar tumours, 10–15% of intracranial · benign · micro < 10 mm, macro ≥ 10 mm, giant > 4 cm
  • Most secrete: prolactin > GH > ACTH ≫ TSH · non-functioning present late as macroadenomas · MEN1
  • Stalk effect: prolactin is the only hormone held down by dopamine → stalk compression raises it modestly (usually < ~200 ng/ml), others fall

5Pituitary apoplexy

Bleed or infarct in an adenoma: thunderclap headache, vomiting, visual loss, ophthalmoplegia, drowsy, acute hypopituitarism · mimics SAH
IV hydrocortisone now→Na, glucose, cortisol, hormones→urgent MRI (or CT)→acuity, fields, movements→decompress if vision or consciousness falls
Can look like a subarachnoid haemorrhage · eye movement palsies alone often recover without surgery (UK guideline 2011) · an adrenal crisis kills

8Endoscopic endonasal surgery

Two surgeons, four hands: ENT opens + closes, neurosurgeon removes · replaced sublabial / transseptal microscope
nasoseptal flap (or "rescue")→posterior septectomy, both ostia wide→drill septa, don't twist→sella, carotid, optic, OCR→open floor + dura, remove→reconstruct: graft or flap
Transplanum / transtuberculum for suprasellar + giant · endoscope: fuller removal, fewer perforations

10Exam traps ✗

  • Operating on a prolactinoma tablets would shrink
  • Twisting off a septum that inserts on the carotid
  • Calling a carotid aneurysm a tumour
  • No steroid cover or sodium checks after discharge
