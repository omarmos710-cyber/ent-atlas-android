CHRONIC SUPPURATIVE OTITIS MEDIA

mucosal (safe) type, without cholesteatoma ✎

1A hole that keeps running

Long-standing infection of the middle ear cleft with discharge + permanent perforation.

Tubotympanic

safe · mucosa, anteroinferior cleft · this board

Atticoantral

unsafe · bone, usually cholesteatoma
active: discharge via pars tensa hole⇄inactive: dry hole; flares with cold / water
Healed: atrophic drum, tympanosclerosis, some conductive loss · common cause of preventable hearing loss worldwide (crowding, poverty, poor access)

2Why it won't heal, why it recurs

After repeated childhood AOM, acute necrotising OM or trauma · effusion + tube dysfunction keep mucosa inflamed
Squamous epithelium grows round the edge to meet the mucosa → behaves like a fistula, won't close
  • Via the tube: nose, sinuses, adenoids, tonsils; nose blowing
  • Via the canal: water when bathing / swimming
  • Biofilms (esp. Pseudomonas) shield from antibiotics → relapse
Bugs: Pseudomonas, Proteus, E. coli, S. aureus + anaerobes (Bacteroides) · fungi after repeated drops

3The safe ear

  • Discharge: profuse, mucoid / mucopurulent, not foul, intermittent (colds, water), dries between
  • Hearing: conductive, rarely >50 dB · may hear better when wet (discharge shields round window, restores phase difference)
  • Perforation: central, rim all round · anterior, posterior, inferior to malleus or subtotal · may expose ossicles, promontory
  • Mucosa: pale (inactive), red oedematous (active) · pale polyp may fill hole
Never avulse a polyp: may arise from stapes, facial nerve or lateral canal → facial palsy / dead ear · remove under microscope or at surgery; histology

4Investigations

  • Microscope for every chronic ear: a "dry" ear may hide pus; rarely cholesteatoma with a central hole
  • Swab if not responding
  • Audiogram: air-bone gap >~40 dB → ossicular discontinuity (incus long process)
  • CT not routine (no erosion): only if cholesteatoma / complication suspected, or before revision · MRI for intracranial
  • Biopsy persistent granulation → TB, malignancy

6Dry the ear

aural toilet→topical drops→keep water out→dry ear: decide repair
  • Toilet under microscope: suction, dry mop, gentle saline · never forceful syringing · drops can't work through pus
  • Quinolone drops (cipro / ofloxacin ± steroid) = first choice with a hole: not ototoxic · one to two weeks after debridement, longer if active · tragal pumping
  • Acetic acid 1.5% against Pseudomonas
  • Topical > oral; systemic only for spread, complications, topical failure (by culture)
  • Treat tonsils, adenoids, sinuses, allergy · no hard nose blowing
  • Cotton wool + petroleum jelly / moulded plug · no swimming while active

Aminoglycoside drops?

Ototoxic in animals; makers advise against with open middle ear · ENT UK: infection risk > drop risk → active infection only, up to ~two weeks, warn to stop if hearing worse / dizzy, hearing test first · still needed: quinolone-resistant Pseudomonas · Dhingra: avoid

7Surgery

  • Tympanoplasty once dry for some weeks (myringoplasty ± ossicular reconstruction) → closed, protected ear · success 60–90%, better if air passes on Valsalva (working tube)
  • Mastoidectomy for discharge persisting despite proper medical treatment · cortical + tympanoplasty; alone it doesn't improve drum closure
  • Hearing aid if unfit / unwilling, ideally once dry
The other ear predicts tube function · no tube treatment has proven benefit

8Complications

  • Incus long process = vascular watershed, erodes first → bigger conductive loss
  • SNHL: toxins via round window → basal turn → high-frequency loss
  • Tympanosclerosis: chalky hyaline plaques; may fix ossicles
  • Rarely in a flare: mastoiditis, facial palsy, labyrinthitis, intracranial

9Evidence

Cochrane 2020 & 2021: evidence limited and old · topical antibiotics improve resolution vs placebo, probably > systemic · quinolone ≈ aminoglycoside · drops may beat antiseptics (boric acid)

10Exam traps ✗

  • Drops into an ear full of pus
  • Aminoglycoside drops through a hole without need
  • Calling an ear safe without the microscope
  • Missing TB / malignancy in granulation that won't settle
