ADENOIDS & NASOPHARYNGITIS

mouth breathing, snoring, wet nose, dull look · judge the airway, not the adenoid ✎

1The nasopharyngeal tonsil

  • Roof–posterior wall junction · vertical ridges + deep clefts · ciliated, squamous + transitional epithelium
  • Unlike the palatine tonsil: no crypts, no capsule
  • Arteries: ascending palatine, ascending pharyngeal, pharyngeal branch of maxillary, ascending cervical (inferior thyroid)
  • Lymph → upper jugular (direct or via retro- + parapharyngeal)
  • Nerves IX + X → earache of adenoiditis

2Size with age

Present at birth · physiological peak three to seven · regresses from puberty, largely gone by twenty
Stays big: physiological, lymphoid hyperplasia, recurrent rhinitis, sinusitis, tonsillitis, allergy
Big adenoid in a big nasopharynx: no symptoms · modest one in a small space: total block → judge the airway

3Nose

  • Obstruction (commonest), mouth breathing · infants can't breathe + suck → poor feeding, failure to thrive
  • Discharge: blocked choanae + chronic rhinitis → "wet, bubbly nose"
  • Chronic maxillary sinusitis and adenoid infection feed each other · epistaxis when inflamed
  • Hyponasal voice (rhinolalia clausa): nasal consonants lost

4Ears

  • Tube block → retracted drum, conductive loss · recurrent AOM up the tube
  • OME: adenoid an important cause · fluctuating size → fluctuating hearing
  • CSOM that won't settle while the adenoid is infected

5General

  • Adenoid facies: long face, dull look, open mouth, crowded prominent upper teeth, short upper lip, pinched nose, high-arched palate
  • Snoring, OSA, daytime tiredness, poor concentration (aprosexia)

6Diagnosis

  • Flexible nasendoscopy = first line: best view of adenoid (grade by choanal block), choanae, tube openings · no radiation
  • Lateral soft-tissue X-ray if no scope tolerated: adenoid–nasopharynx ratio · crying lifts the palate → exaggerates size · mirror in older children
  • Whole nose for other causes · tympanometry + audiometry (OME) · oximetry / PSG if SDB severity uncertain or high risk
  • Before surgery: palate check → submucous cleft
Adult with unilateral OME, nasopharyngeal mass or neck node: examine ± biopsy for NPC · adult adenoid tissue: think HIV, lymphoma

8Who gets the operation?

Marked symptoms, SDB, OME with adenoid

Indications

  • Snoring, mouth breathing, OSA, hyponasal speech
  • Recurrent rhinosinusitis
  • OME: consider with grommets (NICE)
  • Ear discharge with adenoiditis
  • Malocclusion: prevents relapse after orthodontics

Against

  • Cleft or submucous cleft → VPI · bifid uvula, palatal notch, zona pellucida
  • Bleeding disorder
  • Acute URTI

9Adenoidectomy

  • GA, oral tube, tonsil position · never hyperextend · Boyle–Davis gag
  • Retract palate + palpate (feel for submucous cleft)
  • Guarded curette to back of septum, press back, sweep down, head slightly flexed (protects atlas) · lateral tags with punch forceps, spare tube openings
  • Pack · diathermy · rarely postnasal pack for 24 hours · with tonsils: adenoid first
  • Endoscopic suction diathermy, microdebrider, coblation: more complete around tubes + choanae

10Evidence

  • IPD meta-analysis (ten trials, 2014): small benefit for recurrent AOM or glue ear, mainly two years and over · not a first operation in younger children
  • NICE NG233 (2023): consider adenoidectomy with grommets for glue ear (unless palatal abnormality) · fewer repeat operations
  • OSA: adenotonsillectomy if big tonsils · adenoidectomy alone if small · intracapsular tonsillectomy rising in young children

12Nasopharyngitis + Thornwaldt

  • Acute: cold, flu, strep, pneumococcus, Haemophilus · burning behind the nose → painful swallow · analgesia, antibiotics if severe
  • Chronic: adult, smoker, drinker, dust · postnasal crusts, hawking · treat source, alkaline douche, steam
  • Thornwaldt: infected pharyngeal bursa (notochord) · postnasal discharge, OME, occipital headache · midline cystic swelling · antibiotics → marsupialise · incidental MRI cyst: leave
