  • Mild: discomfort, low fever, no nodes · moderate–severe: odynophagia, high fever, exudate, follicles · very severe: palate + uvula oedema, tender nodes
  • Virus vs bacteria: can't tell by looking

4Test and treat

  • Swab culture finds ~90% of GAS · rapid antigen in minutes · diphtheria: special media · gonococcus: plate at once · no growth → viral · Monospot, EBV serology, film
  • Strep: penicillin V ten days (US) or five to ten (NICE), or benzathine IM · macrolide if allergic
  • Rheumatic fever common (Egypt, developing world): full ten days or benzathine
  • General: rest, fluids, gargles, analgesia, flurbiprofen 8.75 mg lozenge (adults), viscous lidocaine · diphtheria: antitoxin + penicillin or erythromycin

5Glandular fever

  • EBV, older child, young adult · grey-white membrane, palatal petechiae, posterior + anterior nodes, spleen, liver
  • FBC + film: atypical lymphocytes · Monospot (often negative in week one + young children) · EBV serology (VCA IgM, EBNA IgG) · LFT · nasendoscopy if airway concern
  • Amoxicillin / ampicillin → rash in most: avoid · antivirals: no benefit · paracetamol / ibuprofen, fluids, rest · fatigue may last weeks
  • Airway / severe swelling: admit, short steroid course (not routine), rarely nasopharyngeal airway or tonsillectomy · no contact sport ≥ 3–4 weeks (longer if spleen big)

6Other viral patterns

  • CMV: mono-like, immunosuppressed, Monospot negative · acute HIV: mono-like + rash + mouth ulcers
  • Herpangina (coxsackie A): vesicles, red halo, palate + pillars · lymphonodular (coxsackie): white-yellow nodules, posterior wall
  • Adenovirus: throat + fever + conjunctivitis · measles: Koplik spots three or four days before rash

7Candida · Quincke

  • Candida spreading from the mouth: immunosuppressed, diabetic, steroids (incl. inhaled), antibiotics · pain, dysphagia, white plaques, hyphae
  • Mild: miconazole gel 2.5 ml QDS after food (warfarin interaction) or nystatin 100,000 units/ml 1 ml QDS · 7 days + 2 after healing · extensive: fluconazole 50–100 mg daily (200–400 severe) 7–14 days · inhaler: spacer + rinse
  • Quincke: swollen uvula · tonsillitis, burns, heavy snoring

8Angioedema

  • Rapid, painless, watery swelling, lips to larynx · allergic = histamine · ACE inhibitor + hereditary (C1 esterase) = bradykinin, poor antihistamine response
  • Allergic: adrenaline 1:1000 IM thigh: 500 micrograms (> 12 y), 300 (6–12), 150 (6 months–6 y), repeat after 5 min · then antihistamine
  • ACE-i: stop for life · hereditary: C1 inhibitor 20 units/kg IV or icatibant 30 mg SC (repeat after 6 h) · awake fibreoptic or tracheostomy
Drooling, can't swallow saliva, muffled voice, stridor, leaning forward: don't lie flat, no tongue depressor, senior airway help

9Chronic pharyngitis

  • Catarrhal: congested wall, engorged vessels, mucus · hypertrophic (granular): thick wall, red lymphoid granules, lateral bands, long uvula · atrophic: dry, glazed, crusts, with atrophic rhinitis
  • Causes: postnasal drip, dental + tonsil sepsis · mouth breathing (dry unfiltered air) · smoking, pan, alcohol, spice, fumes · LPR · voice misuse, throat clearing · worse in the morning, lump feeling, tiring voice, cough, gagging
  • Remove cause → voice rest, speech therapy, stop hawking → saline gargles → Mandl's paint → cautery of granules (silver nitrate, diathermy)
  • Atrophic: alkaline sprays + irrigation, Mandl's, oral potassium iodide, treat the atrophic rhinitis
One-sided pain, earache, dysphagia, weight loss, haemoptysis, neck lump: not chronic pharyngitis until endoscopy + neck palpation

10Keratosis pharyngis

  • White-yellow horny spikes, firmly adherent · tonsils, posterior wall, lingual tonsil
  • Hypertrophy + keratinisation of epithelium
  • No inflammation, no fever → not follicular tonsillitis · harmless, often regresses · reassure

11Pain without inflammation

  • Eagle (styalgia): long styloid or calcified stylohyoid ligament · pain in tonsillar fossa + upper neck → ear · often after tonsillectomy · tip felt in the fossa · open-mouth / lateral X-ray or 3D CT · many long styloids are silent · shorten via fossa or neck

12Neuralgia · globus

  • Glossopharyngeal neuralgia: electric pain tonsil, tongue base, ear · swallowing, yawning · MRI posterior fossa · carbamazepine 100 mg once / twice daily → usually 200 mg TDS–QDS (HLA-B*1502 if Han Chinese / Thai; FBC, sodium, LFT) · oxcarbazepine, gabapentin, pregabalin · microvascular decompression
  • Globus: persistent lump between meals, food goes down normally · examine fully + endoscopically · reflux, anxiety · red flags (pain, dysphagia, weight loss, voice change, neck mass) → investigate

13Exam traps ✗

  • Amoxicillin for exudative throat + big posterior nodes
  • Stopping penicillin at three days because it feels better
  • Smoker's persistent pain called chronic pharyngitis, no endoscopy
  • ACE inhibitor angioedema treated with antihistamines alone
  • Lying down or using a tongue depressor in a drooling, stridulous patient
