INTRACRANIAL COMPLICATIONS OF OTITIS MEDIA

a discharging ear + headache, fever, fits or drowsiness ✎

1When OM crosses the dura

Six: extradural abscess · subdural empyema · meningitis (commonest) · brain abscess (next) · lateral sinus thrombophlebitis · otitic hydrocephalus · (perforation, ossicular erosion, tympanosclerosis, atelectasis = sequelae)
  • Otogenic: half of adult brain abscesses, a quarter in children · temporal ≈ twice as common as cerebellar
  • Infant + AOM → meningitis (blood-borne) · adult + chronic OM / cholesteatoma → abscess, sinus thrombosis (erosion)
Often more than one: another lesion in a third (Dhingra) to a half (Lee) of otogenic meningitis → look for a second in every patient

2Three routes into the skull

  • Erosion by cholesteatoma, osteitis, granulation · thrombophlebitis → abscess without visible erosion
  • Preformed: dehiscence, petrosquamous suture / Hyrtl's fissure (child), inner ear malformation, fracture, surgery, round + oval windows
Risk: extremes of age, poverty, virulent / resistant bugs, inadequate antibiotics, immunosuppression, cholesteatoma · Bugs: S. aureus, strep, Proteus, Pseudomonas, E. coli + anaerobes (Bacteroides) esp. abscess

3Extradural & subdural

Extradural abscess

pus between bone and dura · often silent, found at mastoidectomy · deep one-sided headache, eases when the ear runs
Rx: remove bone to healthy dura

Subdural empyema

spreads fast: headache, fever, meningism, fits, aphasia, rapid hemiplegia
Rx: neurosurgical emergency: burr holes / craniotomy

4Meningitis: the commonest

  • Headache, fever, photophobia, vomiting, neck stiffness, Kernig, Brudzinski → drowsiness, fits, coma
  • CSF: turbid, raised pressure, polymorphs, high protein, low sugar
  • IV antibiotics with CSF penetration + steroids · ear: myringotomy (AOM), mastoidectomy (chronic) once fit
After meningitis → hearing test early: labyrinthitis ossificans can close the cochlea within weeks; implant before it does

5Brain abscess

invasion (encephalitis)→localisation: quiet→enlargement→rupture
Dhingra four stages · Lee three · the quiet stage is the trap: seems better while the abscess forms
Raised ICP: morning headache, projectile vomiting, papilloedema, slow pulse, subnormal temperature, drowsiness

sinus wall inflamed→mural thrombus→infected clot
Picket-fence fever: high, swinging, rigors, well between (like malaria, but irregular)

7Otitic hydrocephalus

  • Raised ICP, normal CSF, normal ventricles · usually after sinus thrombosis blocking venous outflow
  • Headache, vomiting, blurred vision, papilloedema, sixth nerve palsy · CSF pressure >300 mm H₂O
  • Treat ear + sinus · acetazolamide, steroids, mannitol · repeated LP / shunt
Serial eye checks: the danger is blindness from optic atrophy

8Image before you puncture

  • Contrast CT brain + temporal bone first: abscess, empyema, erosion, delta sign
  • MRI + MRV: early cerebritis, subdural, sinus thrombosis
  • LP only once mass effect + raised pressure excluded

9Patient first, ear last

ABC, sepsis→neuro + fundi→CT → MRI/MRV→IV antibiotics→neurosurgery early→then the ear
Ear: AOM → myringotomy + grommet · chronic / cholesteatoma → mastoidectomy once controlled + fit · abscess: antibiotics six to eight weeks
Outcomes: deafness, epilepsy, focal deficit, blindness, death · abscess rupture into ventricle ≈ 80% mortality

10Exam traps ✗

  • LP before imaging
  • Reassured by the quiet stage of an abscess
  • Swinging fever called malaria without examining the ear
  • Stopping at one complication
  • No hearing test after meningitis
