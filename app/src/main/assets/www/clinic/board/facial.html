FACIAL NERVE PALSY

forehead? ear? parotid? protect the eye today ✎

1Upper or lower? Think beyond Bell's

  • Forehead nucleus gets fibres from both hemispheres; lower face only from the opposite side
  • UMN (supranuclear) lesion spares the forehead · emotional movement travels separately → a smile may survive a stroke
  • Eye at risk within hours · palsy may be the first sign of tumour, cholesteatoma, fracture

5Eye first, then the nerve

Eye: drops by day, ointment + taping at night, sunglasses / moisture chamber · poor closure → gold / platinum lid weight, lateral tarsal strip, tarsorrhaphy

Repair & reanimation

tension-free anastomosis or cable graft (great auricular — not with neck metastases; sural, medial antebrachial) · transfer ideally within 18 months: masseteric (most used), hypoglossal (costs tongue), cross-face (spontaneous smile) · gracilis: masseteric (one stage, ~92%, not spontaneous) or cross-face (two stages, ~80%, spontaneous) · temporalis · static: brow lift, fascia lata sling, lid

6Incomplete recovery

exposure keratitis · synkinesis (eye closes on smiling; physio, botox) · contracture, hemifacial spasm · crocodile tears (Bogorad: tearing on eating) · psychological distress

7Bell's palsy

acute idiopathic LMN palsy = 60–75% · likely HSV reactivation; swelling in the labyrinthine segment · ↑ in diabetes, pregnancy · peaks within 72 h, often after pain behind the ear · a diagnosis of exclusion · full recovery 85–90% (95% if never complete)
Tests: not routine labs / imaging at presentation, no electrodiagnosis in incomplete palsy · complete → ENoG day 3–14 + EMG · atypical → contrast MRI ± CT · bilateral / recurrent → glucose, Lyme serology, ACE + chest film, HIV

8Ramsay Hunt · otitis media

zoster: vesicles (concha, canal, palate), severe pain, palsy ± SNHL, vertigo · face outlook worse than Bell's
prednisolone 50–60 mg daily (1 mg/kg up to 60 mg) × 10 days + aciclovir 800 mg five times a day / valaciclovir 1 g / famciclovir 500 mg three times a day × 7 days (within 72 h if possible; ↓ in renal impairment) · analgesia ± neuropathic agents · eye care · short prochlorperazine for vertigo
AOM inflames a dehiscent nerve → antibiotics + myringotomy · cholesteatoma erodes (tympanic segment) → urgent exploration; leave granulation that has invaded the nerve

10Tumour, both sides, spasm

Tumour clues: slow progression, recurrence same side, twitching · palsy + parotid lump = almost always malignant → contrast MRI
Bilateral: Lyme, GBS, sarcoid, leukaemia, Möbius, HIV, NF2, fractures · recurrent same side: tumour in ~a third · Melkersson-Rosenthal: recurrent palsy + lip swelling + fissured tongue · hemifacial spasm: all muscles + platysma; vascular loop → botox or microvascular decompression

11Exam traps ✗

Bell's without looking in the ear or feeling the parotid · forgetting eye care · missing a slow or recurrent palsy that needs imaging · not recording immediate vs delayed after trauma
