1Two diseases of one cleft

TermMeans
Acute OMrapid onset (<48 h) of inflammation + effusion: pain, fever, red bulging drum
Recurrent AOM≥3 in 6 months or ≥4 in a year, well in between
OM with effusionfluid behind an intact drum, no acute signs (secretory OM, glue ear); chronic after 3 months
Acute necrotising OMrare; β-haemolytic strep after measles / scarlet fever → destroys drum, annulus, ossicles
  • AOM peaks 6 months – 2 years
  • Effusion follows most AOM = commonest cause of childhood hearing loss (peaks ~2 and ~5 yrs)
  • Acute = pain + rare serious complication · Effusion = hearing, speech, behaviour, school in a child who does not complain

2Why children, why after a cold

  • Child's tube opens less efficiently: nasopharynx secretions reach the ear, ear drains poorly
  • Adenoid = bacterial reservoir + tubal block
viral cold→tube + adenoid inflamed→gas absorbed, −ve pressure→transudate→bacteria infect it

Bacteria

S. pneumoniae > non-typeable H. influenzae > M. catarrhalis
β-lactamase: ~½ of H. flu, almost all Moraxella
Viruses: RSV, rhino, adeno

Risk factors

daycare, siblings, smoke, dummy, bottle lying flat, no breastfeeding · cleft palate, Down, craniofacial · prematurity, immunodeficiency, low SES · adenoid

4Managing AOM

Analgesia for everyone (paracetamol / ibuprofen) · most settle in 3 days – 1 week · antibiotics help pain in ~1 in 20 at 2–3 days, harm ~1 in 14 · do not meaningfully prevent mastoiditis

5Which antibiotic

amoxicillin 5–7 d→2–3 d no better: co-amoxiclav→fails: specialist, culture, IV ceftriaxone
Allergy → clarithromycin · AAP 2013: treat severe (≥39°C, pain >48 h) or non-severe bilateral <2 yrs; may observe non-severe unilateral ≥6 months, any non-severe ≥2 yrs · dose: Dhingra 40 mg/kg/day ≥10 d · Lee 90 · AAP 80–90 (resistant pneumococci)
Myringotomy: bulging + severe pain · incomplete resolution with loss · effusion >12 weeks · second-line failure (culture)

6Recurrent AOM

Cut risk factors, breastfeed, check pneumococcal + Hib vaccines (infections despite them → poor antibody response?)
  • Watchful: treat each episode promptly; many outgrow it
  • Grommets: drops not oral; fewer episodes first 6 months but not over 2 years (Hoberman 2021: 1.48 vs 1.56/yr) · usual if effusion persists between attacks · Dhingra: 4 in 6 months or 6 in a year
  • Prophylaxis (½-dose amoxicillin or azithromycin 3×/week, 2–3 months): halves episodes only while taken; resistance → rare in UK

7OM with effusion: the quiet ear

Tube dysfunction → −ve pressure + fluid → ↑ goblet cells + mucous glands → thick glue · + adenoid infection, allergy, unresolved AOM

Symptoms

often none: inattention, poor speech, loud TV · adults: fullness, muffled hearing

Drum

dull, opaque, amber/grey/bluish, fine radial vessels, retracted, air-fluid level / bubbles, ↓ mobility
  • Tympanogram: flat type B (or far-negative type C)
  • Audiogram: 20–40 dB conductive; needed at 3 months, or sooner if speech/development concern
Unilateral effusion in an adult → examine the nasopharynx (nasopharyngeal carcinoma), biopsy if abnormal · lowest threshold: smokers, endemic regions

Natural history

screen-detected: clears ~63% by 3 months, 88% by a year · ~90% after treated AOM resolve · but bilateral >3 months → only ~¼ clear by 6 months · in 60–85% of Down syndrome or cleft palate

8Managing effusion (NICE NG233, 2023)

no hearing loss: reassure→loss >20 dB HL: re-test at 3 months→shared decision
  • No lasting benefit: antibiotics, steroids, antihistamines, decongestants, anti-reflux
  • Autoinflation (nasal balloon) if old enough
  • Air-conduction aids = equal alternative to surgery when loss stable · bone-conduction (soft band) if fluctuating / narrow canals / otorrhoea; preferred in many with Down
  • Grommets: persistent effusion + loss, esp. bilateral affecting speech, learning, behaviour; at-risk children · thick glue → two incisions
  • Adenoidectomy: consider alongside grommets unless cleft / submucous cleft (VPI) · AAP: tubes alone <4 yrs

9Complications

Of AOM (0.05–0.25%)

perforation ~5% (~90% heal) · mastoiditis <0.4%, mostly <2 yrs; IV alone works in ~¾ · facial palsy, labyrinthitis · meningitis, intracranial

Of chronic effusion

atrophy, atelectasis · incus long process necrosis · tympanosclerosis · retraction pocket → cholesteatoma · cholesterol granuloma
Short antibiotic courses → masked mastoiditis / persistent effusion

10Exam traps ✗

  • Calling AOM from a red drum in a crying child
  • Withholding analgesia while deciding on antibiotics
  • Decongestants / antihistamines for glue ear
  • Grommet otorrhoea → oral antibiotics (use drops)
  • Missing NPC in an adult with one-sided glue
