SNORING & OBSTRUCTIVE SLEEP APNOEA

every apnoeic snores, not every snorer is apnoeic · CPAP first, surgery by the level of collapse ✎

1Why the throat closes

  • Snoring: ~a quarter of men, 15% of women, ↑ with age · children 10–15% snore, 1–4% OSA
  • Muscles relax → narrowing → palate, uvula, pillars, tongue base vibrate (up to 90 dB)
  • No bony support palate → larynx · tone falls most in REM + stage 2 · each apnoea ends in an arousal → fragmented sleep
  • Sleep: 3–5 cycles of 90–120 min · NREM 75–80% · REM 20–25% (muscle near-paralysis → worst obstruction)
  • Often more than one level, and it can change from night to night

2Night and day

  • Loud snoring (snoring spouse syndrome), witnessed choking + gasping, restless unrefreshing sleep
  • Daytime sleepiness, morning headache, fatigue, memory + concentration, irritability, depression, libido ↓, nocturia

3Words of the sleep study

  • Apnoea: no airflow ≥ 10 seconds · hypopnoea: flow ↓ (commonly 30–50%) + sats ↓ 3–4% or arousal
  • AHI = events per hour of sleep (= RDI) · arousal = brief EEG waking from effort · sleep efficiency = asleep ÷ in bed · MSLT: timed naps (sleepiness, narcolepsy)
Under 5 normal · mild 5–14 · moderate 15–29 · severe 30 or more

4History & examination

  • From the bed partner: snoring, pauses, gasping, sweating · alcohol, sedatives, caffeine, shift work, position, weight, menopause, nose, driving
  • Epworth: eight situations scored 0–3, total out of 24 · > 10 = excessive sleepiness · STOP-Bang, Berlin screen
  • BMI ≥ 30 · neck > 42 cm (men), 37.5 cm (women) · BP, heart failure, hypothyroidism
  • Nose + endoscopy · tonsils, palate, uvula, tongue scalloping, bite, retrognathia · tongue base, lingual tonsil, epiglottis
TonsilsFriedman palate (tongue in the mouth)

5Find the level, prove the apnoea

  • Müller: forced inhale, nose + mouth shut · poorly reproducible
  • DISE (propofol / midazolam) · VOTE: velum, oropharynx, tongue base, epiglottis · AP, lateral or concentric · before / after failed surgery, study ≠ findings
  • Polysomnography = gold standard (EEG, EOG, EMG, ECG, flow, effort, sats, position): obstructive vs central, snoring vs apnoea · split-night under-counts
  • Cephalometry for the skeleton · lateral neck film: adenoids (child)

6Types · children

  • Obstructive: effort continues · central: no effort · mixed: central then obstructive
  • Spectrum: primary snoring (AHI < 5, no arousals, no sleepiness) → upper airway resistance (arousals, sleepy) → OSA

Children are different

Adenotonsillar hypertrophy (two to six years, Brodsky ≥ 3) · more in obese, Black, premature · snoring, pauses, neck hyperextended, sweating, bedwetting · by day hyperactive, poor school, failure to thrive (not sleepy) · AHI > 1 abnormal
  • History + exam, parental video · oximetry triage (normal ≠ excluded) · PSG for < 2, obese, Down, craniofacial, neuromuscular, cardiac
  • Adenotonsillectomy first · mild: wait, steroid spray, montelukast · obese: weight loss · persistent / no big tonsils: DISE / MRI · overnight watch if < 3, severe, comorbid
  • CHAT 2013 + PATS 2023: surgery didn't improve attention or executive function, but symptoms, behaviour, QoL did · 46% of waiters normalised

7Non-surgical first

  • Every patient: weight loss (bariatric if morbid) · no evening alcohol, sedatives · stop smoking · side sleeping · treat the nose · DVLA advice if sleepy
  • Tirzepatide: AHI ↓ ~half (SURMOUNT-OSA, 2024) · licensed in the United States (2024) for moderate–severe OSA + obesity
  • CPAP 5–20 cm H₂O = pneumatic splint · bilevel, auto-titrating · only ~half use it ≥ 4 h × 5 nights · coaching helps
  • Mandibular advancement: snoring, mild–moderate, retrognathia · jaw pain, tooth movement

Bypasstracheostomy: only sure cure, last resort
  • UPPP risks: pain, bleed, VPI, nasopharyngeal stenosis, voice · palatal surgery: snoring ~80%, apnoea ~half
  • After surgery: overnight sats if severe, cardiac, tongue base, small child · opioid-sensitive · restart CPAP

9Evidence · Dhingra vs Lee

  • SAVE (2016, 2,717): CPAP at 3.3 h a night didn't prevent CV events, but helped sleepiness, mood, QoL
  • SAMS (JAMA 2020): multilevel surgery AHI 47.9 → 20.8 vs 45.3 → 34.5 · hypoglossal (2014): 29.3 → 9.0 (−68%)
  • Dhingra: tracheostomy = surgical gold standard · Lee: CPAP = gold standard, UPPP > 85% if favourable

10More than noise

  • Hypertension (independent risk; treatment lowers even resistant BP) · arrhythmia, ischaemia, heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, cor pulmonale · untreated severe ↑ mortality · stroke · insulin resistance, polycythaemia
  • Difficult airway + opioid sensitivity · road accidents: ask about driving, advise on licensing
