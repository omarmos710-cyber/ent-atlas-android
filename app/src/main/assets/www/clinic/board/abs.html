ACUTE RHINOSINUSITIS

a cold that reached the sinuses · a swollen eye in a child with a cold is an emergency ✎

1By the clock

TypeDuration
Acute< 12 weeks, full resolution (old US: < 4)
Chronic12 weeks or more
Recurrent acute4+ a year, clear in between
Acute on chronicflare, back to baseline
Symptoms: block, discharge (front or back), facial pain / pressure, ↓ smell · cough, fever, dental pain, halitosis, ear fullness
EPOS 2020 (no "subacute") splits acute into:
cold: < 10 days→post-viral: worse after 5 or > 10 days→bacterial

Bacterial = 3 or more of

discoloured discharge (one side) · severe local pain (one side) · fever > 38 °C · ↑ ESR / CRP · double sickening
Only 0.5–2% of colds turn bacterial · coloured mucus alone ≠ bacteria

2Why the eye and the brain

coronal schematic · red = the paper-thin wall infection crosses
  • Frontal, anterior ethmoid, maxillary → middle meatus (OMC) · posterior ethmoid → superior meatus · sphenoid → sphenoethmoidal recess
  • At birth: maxillary + ethmoid · frontal ~ sixth year → ethmoiditis = infants, frontal = adults

3Virus first, then bacteria

rhinovirus, flu · 1–4 day incubation→cilia damaged, ostia swell shut→secretions stagnate→bacteria
  • S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis, S. aureus · anaerobes with teeth
  • Suspect bacteria: > 10 days no better · double worsening · severe from the start
  • Predisposed: swimming, teeth, septum, concha bullosa, polyps, allergy, adenoids, immune deficiency, CF

5Most need no antibiotic

  • Viral: saline, analgesia, short decongestant, fluids · post-viral > 10 days → nasal steroid
  • Bacterial: amoxicillin ± clavulanate, or phenoxymethylpenicillin × five days in the UK (allergy: doxycycline, quinolone) · watchful waiting with a safety net in mild disease · saline, steroid spray · antihistamine only with allergy
  • Early antibiotics don't prevent complications · given to > 90% in some UK series
  • S. anginosus (milleri): complicated disease in older children, intracranial abscess

7Look, image, drain

Endoscopy: pus in the middle meatus, swab it · no imaging unless a complication or failure → contrast CT of sinuses, orbits, brain · Waters' view = history
  • Frontal trephine if worse despite 48 hours: incision below the medial eyebrow, culture, irrigate until methylene blue reaches the nose · or endoscopic
  • Maxillary: washout rarely · endoscopic middle meatal antrostomy
  • Ethmoid: drain orbital abscess + ethmoids, endoscopic or external

8Local complications

  • Mucocele: blocked ostium, expands, erodes bone · infected = pyocele · frontal > ethmoid > maxillary > sphenoid
  • Frontal: swelling above medial canthus (90%), eye forward, down, out, egg-shell crackle · sphenoid: SOF or apex syndrome
  • Endoscopic marsupialisation has replaced external surgery
  • Retention cyst: incidental, no bone erosion, leave it
  • Osteomyelitis: maxilla in infants (tooth buds) · frontal in adults → Pott's puffy tumour + extradural abscess beneath

11Exam traps ✗

  • Antibiotics for every cold
  • "Preseptal" without checking vision + movement
  • Missing the extradural abscess under a Pott's puffy tumour
  • Drowsy or meningism → brain imaging + neurosurgery
