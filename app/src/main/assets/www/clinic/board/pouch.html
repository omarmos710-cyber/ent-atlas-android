6Pharyngeal pouch (Zenker)

posterior view, schematic · two parts of the inferior constrictor
  • Pulsion diverticulum (hypopharyngeal) through Killian's dehiscence · starts midline behind the gullet, grows down + left · its mouth lines up with the pharynx → food enters the sac

7The pouch patient

  • Cause not fully known: pharynx–cricopharyngeus incoordination, sphincter fails to open, raised pressure above · reflux may add · elderly, usually over sixty, men > women
  • Dysphagia after a few swallows, progressive · gurgling · left neck swelling that empties · regurgitates old food, night cough, aspiration pneumonia · halitosis, weight loss · hiatus hernia · rarely cancer in the sac

8Treating the pouch

  • Small, silent: watch · endoscopic stapling (Weerda scope): divide the party wall (cricopharyngeus) → sac joins the gullet · commonest, quick, eats early · first line: medium + large, fit
  • Dohlman / CO₂ laser: poor-risk, elderly · small (< ~2–3 cm, too little bar) or stiff neck / small mouth: laser or open
  • External (left neck, sternomastoid border): excise, invert or suspend + cricopharyngeal myotomy
  • Flexible septotomy, Z-POEM (gastroenterologists): frail, can't extend the neck · good early series, little long-term data
Always divide cricopharyngeus: it treats the cause · excision without myotomy → recurrence, fistula
903 patients (2021): open beats laser for persistent symptoms, laser = stapling · stapling quicker, more recurrences
Risks: mediastinitis, fistula, RLN palsy, recurrence, incomplete division

9Cricopharyngeal dysfunction

UOS = lower inferior constrictor + cricopharyngeus + upper oesophagus · opens too little · VF bar at C5–C6 seen in up to a third of elderly people without symptoms → match it to the complaint · high-resolution manometry + VF: normal pharyngeal push vs high residual pressure predicts benefit
