2Acute diffuse OE: swimmer's ear

Causes: moisture, cotton buds / nails, hearing aids, earbuds, eczema, lost wax · Pseudomonas, S. aureus
  • Pain over a day or two, itch, blockage, scanty discharge · pain on moving tragus / pinna · red swollen canal + debris · tender nodes
  • Drum (if seen) moves normally on pneumatic otoscopy ≠ OM
clean (anteroinferior recess!)→closed? wick→antibiotic-steroid drops→keep dry
  • Wick soaked in drops, or aluminium acetate 8% / silver nitrate 3% · review at ~two to three days (Dhingra daily, Lee every three to five)
  • Drops a week to ten days · dilute vinegar for mild cases + prevention · no swimming until healed; cotton wool + petroleum jelly when bathing
  • Oral antibiotics only for spreading cellulitis, diabetic, immunocompromised
Drops fail? neomycin contact allergy · fungal superinfection · necrotising infection

3Furuncle

  • Staph hair follicle → outer cartilaginous canal only
  • Pain > size, worse chewing; may swell postauricular groove and mimic mastoiditis
  • vs mastoiditis: normal drum, no OM, no mucoid pus, pinna movement painful
  • Analgesia, oral anti-staph, heat, ichthammol-glycerine pack, incise when pointing
  • Recurrent → test for diabetes, treat nasal carriage

5Chronic & reactive

  • Chronic OE: itch, thick dry scaling, narrowing → treat eczema / seborrhoea, steroid drops or ointment, stop scratching
  • Reactive: eczematous (drops, moulds), seborrhoeic (scalp), neurodermatitis
  • Haemorrhagica: blood blisters on deep canal + drum, with influenza
  • Stenosing: fibrous deep-canal narrowing → excise skin, widen bony canal, graft

6Necrotising OE

canal floor→fissures of Santorini→infratemporal fossa→skull base bone
  • Older + diabetic; ~1 in 10 no obvious immunosuppression
  • Pseudomonas ~60% (rising resistance) · Aspergillus in a minority (HIV, low CD4) · some grow nothing
  • Severe deep night pain out of proportion · discharge despite treatment
  • Granulation on canal floor at bony-cartilaginous junction
VII stylomastoid→IX, X, XI jugular foramen→XII
multiple palsies = worse prognosis

  • Normal early CT is falsely reassuring · FDG PET-CT increasingly to diagnose + monitor
  • CT / MRI changes persist up to a year → can't decide when to stop
  • ESR / CRP (follow) · glucose, HbA1c · swab + granulation biopsy (bacteria, fungi, histology)
Key differential: squamous carcinoma of the temporal bone → always biopsy

8Treating NOE

  • Admit · MDT (otology, diabetes, micro, radiology, elderly care) · control diabetes, regular cleaning
  • Empirical IV anti-pseudomonal, often dual: pip-tazo or ceftazidime + fluoroquinolone → by culture
  • Oral ciprofloxacin alone: early, limited only · voriconazole for Aspergillus
  • No agreed duration: ≥ six weeks (often at home), by pain, CRP, MRI / PET-CT
  • Surgery: biopsy, deep sampling, drain abscess / sequestra · debridement, nerve decompression, hyperbaric O₂ unproven
Suspect it: diabetic / immunocompromised + severe pain + canal granulation · OE not better after a week of proper treatment · cranial palsy with ear pain

9Outcome & evidence

  • Mortality ~5–20%: sepsis, sinus thrombosis, cranial neuropathies · quinolone resistance rising
  • Takata 2023 (70 studies, 2274 patients): 84% diabetic, Pseudomonas 62%, 14% no growth, median 7.2 weeks; no evidence for best scan, regimen or duration
  • Cure rates falling, culture-negative cases rising (Byun 2020) · UK MDT guideline flags high-risk patients (Hopkins 2020)
  • AOE: topical drops first line, no oral unless spreading or at risk (Rosenfeld 2014)

10Exam traps ✗

  • Oral antibiotics instead of cleaning + drops
  • Drops into a canal full of debris
  • Neomycin allergy taken for treatment failure
  • Missing necrotising OE in a diabetic
