@stand Betahistine is a histamine analogue prescribed widely for Ménière's disease to reduce attacks of vertigo. It is thought to improve cochlear and vestibular blood flow and to modulate vestibular nuclei activity. The best trial to date (BEMED, 2016) found no difference from placebo in attack frequency at either standard or high dose, so it should be offered honestly, as a low-risk option with uncertain benefit.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Betahistine
 | Summary
Class | Histamine analogue: weak H1 agonist and potent H3 antagonist
How it works | Proposed: increased inner ear microcirculation via H1 vasodilatation and H3-mediated increase in histamine release; reduced firing of vestibular nuclei
ENT uses | Ménière's disease (reduction of vertigo attacks); widely used, without good evidence, for other vertigo
Adult dose | 16 mg three times daily or 24 mg twice daily with food; some use higher doses (up to 48 mg three times daily) on specialist advice
Child dose | Not recommended under 18 years
Avoid | Phaeochromocytoma; caution in asthma, peptic ulcer disease and with antihistamines
Pregnancy | Avoid unless essential
!!

# Using it in ENT | Indications.
## Ménière's disease
The first steps are explanation, salt restriction, attention to caffeine and stress, vestibular rehabilitation, and acute vestibular sedatives for attacks. Betahistine may be offered as a preventive; review it at 3 to 6 months and stop it if attacks have not reduced. Treatment escalates to intratympanic steroids, then intratympanic gentamicin or surgery for disabling, frequent attacks.
## Evidence
In the BEMED trial, neither 24 mg twice daily nor 48 mg three times daily reduced attacks more than placebo over 9 months; attacks fell in all groups, illustrating the natural fluctuation of the disease.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Nausea, dyspepsia, headache, and rarely rash or pruritus. It does not sedate.
## Interactions
Antihistamines may in theory reduce its effect; MAO-B inhibitors raise its levels.
