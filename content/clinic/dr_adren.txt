@stand Adrenaline (epinephrine) appears in ENT in three forms. As a vasoconstrictor, it is mixed with local anaesthetic for infiltration and soaked onto pledgets to dry the surgical field. Nebulised, it shrinks oedematous airway mucosa in croup and post-extubation stridor. Injected intramuscularly, it is the life-saving first treatment for anaphylaxis. The concentrations differ by a factor of a thousand, and confusion between them causes harm.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Adrenaline
 | Summary
Class | Catecholamine: alpha and beta adrenoceptor agonist
Concentrations | 1:1,000 = 1 mg/ml (intramuscular anaphylaxis, nebulised, topical pledgets); 1:10,000 = 100 micrograms/ml (intravenous in cardiac arrest, some topical use); 1:80,000 to 1:200,000 = 12.5 to 5 micrograms/ml (with local anaesthetic infiltration)
ENT uses | Haemostasis in surgery (infiltration and pledgets); epistaxis (pledgets); croup and post-extubation stridor (nebulised); anaphylaxis and allergic angioedema (intramuscular)
Anaphylaxis dose (intramuscular, 1:1,000, anterolateral thigh) | Adult and child over 12: 500 micrograms (0.5 ml); 6 to 12 years: 300 micrograms (0.3 ml); 6 months to 6 years: 150 micrograms (0.15 ml); under 6 months: 100 to 150 micrograms; repeat after 5 minutes if no better
Croup (nebulised) | 400 micrograms/kg of 1:1,000 (maximum 5 mg), repeated after 30 minutes if needed
Topical | Pledgets soaked in 1:1,000 (or 1:10,000) in endoscopic sinus surgery
!!

# Using it in ENT | Practice.
## Surgical haemostasis
Infiltrated with lidocaine, adrenaline constricts arterioles; wait 7 to 10 minutes. Cotton pledgets with 1:1,000 adrenaline in endoscopic sinus surgery reduce bleeding and improve the view; count and remove them. Absorption from the nasal mucosa can cause tachycardia and hypertension, especially with high-concentration solutions.
## Croup and stridor
Nebulised adrenaline reduces mucosal oedema within minutes in **severe croup** or post-extubation stridor; it wears off in about 2 hours, so observe the child for at least 2 to 4 hours afterwards; dexamethasone treats the underlying swelling.
## Anaphylaxis
Recognise airway, breathing or circulation problems with skin or mucosal changes after exposure to a trigger (drugs, latex, chlorhexidine in theatre). Give **intramuscular adrenaline immediately**, lie the patient flat (or sit if breathing is difficult), give oxygen and fluids; repeat after 5 minutes; start an adrenaline infusion for refractory anaphylaxis under critical care. Antihistamines and steroids are not first-line. Take mast-cell tryptase samples and refer to allergy services.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Tachycardia, arrhythmia, hypertension, anxiety, tremor and pallor; myocardial ischaemia in patients with coronary disease; tissue necrosis if injected into compromised tissue or flaps. **Never give 1:1,000 adrenaline intravenously** in anaphylaxis outside critical care.
## Interactions
Non-selective beta blockers (severe hypertension with reflex bradycardia, and poor response in anaphylaxis: glucagon may help), tricyclics and MAOIs (enhanced pressor response), halothane (arrhythmias).

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- The **concentrations** and what each is used for.
- **Anaphylaxis** doses by age and route.
- Nebulised adrenaline in croup: dose, duration and observation.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Intravenous instead of intramuscular adrenaline in anaphylaxis.
- Delaying adrenaline to give antihistamines.
- Discharging a child soon after nebulised adrenaline.
!!
