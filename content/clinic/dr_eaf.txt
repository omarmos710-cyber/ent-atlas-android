@stand Otomycosis is a fungal otitis externa caused mainly by Aspergillus (black or grey spores on a fluffy mat) and Candida (white, creamy debris). It follows prolonged antibiotic drops, humidity, hearing aids and immunosuppression. Cleaning the canal is as important as the antifungal; clotrimazole solution is the usual first-line drug.

# At a glance | The drugs on one page.
## Summary
!table Antifungal ear preparations
 | Summary
Preparations | Clotrimazole 1% solution (first-line); miconazole or econazole creams; boric acid powder or solution; ciclopirox (where available); systemic itraconazole or voriconazole for invasive disease
How they work | Azoles block ergosterol synthesis in the fungal membrane (lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase); boric acid acidifies and is fungistatic
ENT uses | Otomycosis; fungal infection in mastoid cavities; prophylaxis in patients prone to recurrence
Dose | Clotrimazole 2 to 3 drops two or three times daily, continued for at least 14 days after clinical resolution
Pregnancy | Topical use acceptable

# How they work | Mechanism and handling.
## Mechanism
Azoles deplete ergosterol and accumulate toxic sterols in the fungal membrane. They are active against Candida and Aspergillus, although Aspergillus is less sensitive to clotrimazole than to the newer triazoles.

# Using them in ENT | Indications and doses.
## Treating otomycosis
- **Clean the canal** under the microscope: remove the fungal debris and spores by microsuction, repeated at intervals. Drops alone into a canal full of hyphae fail.
- **Clotrimazole 1%** drops for at least two weeks after the canal looks clean; stop the antibacterial drops that caused it.
- Keep the ear **dry**; treat underlying eczema; control diabetes.
- **With a perforation**, clotrimazole has not been shown to be ototoxic and is commonly used; avoid gentian violet and alcohol-based preparations, which are irritant and potentially ototoxic.
## When to think further
Persistent or recurrent fungal infection in a diabetic or immunocompromised patient, with pain and granulation, may be **invasive fungal necrotising otitis externa** (usually Aspergillus): biopsy and treat systemically with voriconazole.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Local irritation and burning; rarely contact sensitivity.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- Organisms and appearance (Aspergillus niger, Candida).
- The essential role of **aural toilet**.
- Duration of treatment and what to do with a perforation.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Treating otomycosis with more antibiotic drops.
- Stopping clotrimazole as soon as symptoms settle.
!!
