@stand A painful ear that looks completely normal means the pain is coming from somewhere else. The ear shares its sensory nerves with the teeth, jaw, throat, larynx and neck. In an adult smoker, that referred pain can be the first sign of a hidden cancer.

# Overview | Pain in the ear, cause elsewhere.
## Definition
*Otalgia* is ear pain. It is *primary* when the cause is in the ear and *referred* when it arises in another structure that shares the ear's sensory supply.
## The rule
If the canal, drum and hearing are normal, examine everything that shares the ear's nerves before calling the pain psychogenic.

# Nerve supply | Why pain travels to the ear.
## Five nerves
!table Nerves of the ear and where their pain comes from
Nerve | Supplies in the ear | Referred from
Trigeminal (auriculotemporal) | Tragus, anterior canal and drum | Teeth, jaw joint, mouth, tongue, sinuses, parotid
Facial | Concha and posterior canal (sensory branch) | Geniculate ganglion, herpes zoster
Glossopharyngeal (tympanic) | Middle ear | Tonsil, base of tongue, pharynx, styloid, after tonsillectomy
Vagus (auricular, Arnold's) | Concha, posterior canal | Larynx, hypopharynx, vallecula, oesophagus
C2 and C3 | Back of the pinna and mastoid skin | Cervical spine, neck injury

# Causes | Local, referred and psychogenic.
## Local causes in the ear
Furuncle, impacted wax, otitis externa and otomycosis, bullous myringitis, herpes zoster oticus, acute otitis media, tubal obstruction and barotrauma, mastoiditis, extradural abscess, perichondritis, and carcinoma of the canal or middle ear.
## Referred causes
### Through the trigeminal nerve
Dental caries and abscess, impacted third molars, ulcers of the mouth and tongue, temporomandibular joint disorders, bruxism and poorly fitting dentures, and sinusitis.
### Through the glossopharyngeal nerve
Tonsillitis, quinsy, the days after tonsillectomy, ulcers or cancer of the tonsil and base of tongue, and an elongated styloid process (*Eagle syndrome*).
### Through the vagus
Cancer or inflammation of the vallecula, epiglottis, larynx, piriform fossa and oesophagus.
### Through C2 and C3
Cervical spondylosis, neck injury and spinal disease.
## Temporomandibular joint disorders
Pain with chewing, clicking, limited opening and tenderness over the joint. *Costen syndrome* links a faulty bite with earache, a blocked feeling, tinnitus, vertigo and headache.
## Psychogenic
Only after a full search, and kept under review.

# Assessment | Look everywhere.
## History
Character and timing, relation to chewing or swallowing, voice change, weight loss, smoking and alcohol, dental problems, neck pain.
## Examination
After otoscopy and hearing: the teeth and bite, the temporomandibular joints, the mouth and tongue, palpation of the tongue base and tonsils, the nasopharynx, and flexible laryngoscopy of the larynx and hypopharynx. Examine the neck for nodes and the cervical spine.
!flag The smoker with a normal ear
An adult smoker or drinker with one-sided ear pain, a normal ear, and any throat symptom (dysphagia, hoarseness, a lump in the neck) needs endoscopy of the upper aerodigestive tract. Referred otalgia is cancer until the base of tongue, piriform fossa and larynx have been seen.
!!
## Investigations
Guided by findings: dental X-rays, imaging of the joint or styloid, CT or MRI of the neck, and biopsy.

# Management | Treat the source.
Treat the cause found. For temporomandibular joint dysfunction: analgesia, warmth, a soft diet, jaw exercises, a bite splint and dental correction. For an elongated styloid: excision if symptoms persist.
!ops
!pearl Pearl
Ear pain after tonsillectomy is almost always referred pain through the glossopharyngeal nerve, not an ear infection.
!!

## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **The five nerves** and the sites that refer pain through each.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Prescribing ear drops for a normal ear.
- Labelling pain psychogenic before examining the throat and larynx.
!!
