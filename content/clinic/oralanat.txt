@stand The mouth is a small room with many tenants: lip, cheek, gum, teeth, tongue, palate and floor, each with its own nerve, vessel and lymphatic route. Know where each subsite begins and ends, and where its lymph goes, and the examination of the mouth, the staging of its cancers and the planning of its neck dissections follow naturally.

# Boundaries | Where the mouth ends and the pharynx begins.
## Extent
The oral cavity runs from the vermilion border of the lips in front to the *oropharyngeal isthmus* behind: the junction of the hard and soft palate above, the line of the circumvallate papillae below, and the anterior tonsillar pillars and retromolar trigones at the sides. Everything behind this ring (soft palate, tonsils, base of tongue, valleculae) is oropharynx, a distinction that matters because the two regions are staged and treated differently.
!anat s165_8 s166_11 | The vestibule and the oral cavity proper, and the boundary between the oral cavity and the oropharynx.
## Subsites
!fig d42-1 | The subsites of the oral cavity: lip, buccal mucosa, gingiva, retromolar trigone, hard palate, oral tongue and floor of mouth.
- **Lips:** the mucosal lip, from the vermilion border to the line where the lips meet.
- **Buccal mucosa:** the lining of the cheeks and inner lips, back to the pterygomandibular raphe.
- **Upper and lower alveolar ridges (gingivae):** the mucosa over the tooth-bearing bone.
- **Retromolar trigone:** the triangle of mucosa over the front of the ascending ramus, its base behind the last molar and its apex at the maxillary tuberosity.
- **Hard palate:** the roof of the mouth.
- **Oral tongue:** the anterior two-thirds, in front of the circumvallate papillae, with its tip, lateral borders, dorsum and undersurface.
- **Floor of mouth:** the crescent between the lower gingiva and the undersurface of the tongue, with the frenulum and the sublingual papillae where the submandibular ducts open.
!anat s166_10 s172_22 | The subsites of the oral cavity, and the retromolar trigone with the pterygomandibular raphe.
!pearl Examining the floor of mouth
The anterior floor is seen best when the patient lifts the tongue tip to the palate; the lateral floor when a tongue depressor pushes the tongue to the other side. Always palpate the floor bimanually: small cancers here are felt before they are seen.
!!
## Functions
Intake and chewing of food, the oral phase of swallowing, the shaping of speech, and protection against noxious substances through saliva, taste and the gag reflex.

# The lips | The gateway.
## Surface anatomy
The *vermilion* is non-keratinised squamous epithelium without hair or sebaceous glands; its colour comes from the capillaries beneath. The vermilion border marks the change from skin to mucosa; the philtral ridges and Cupid's bow shape the upper lip; the two lips meet at the oral commissures.
!anat s167_13 s169_18 | Lip landmarks, and the arterial supply of the lips from the superior and inferior labial arteries.
## Muscles
All are supplied by the facial nerve. The *orbicularis oris* forms the sphincter. At each commissure several muscles meet in a tendinous knot, the *modiolus*: orbicularis oris, buccinator, levator and depressor anguli oris, zygomaticus major and risorius. The lip elevators (levator labii superioris, levator labii superioris alaeque nasi, zygomaticus minor) and depressors (depressor labii inferioris, mentalis, platysma) complete the set.
## Nerves, vessels and lymph
- **Sensation:** upper lip from the infraorbital nerve (V2), lower lip from the mental nerve (V3).
- **Arteries:** the superior and inferior labial branches of the facial artery.
- **Lymph:** the lower lip drains to the submandibular nodes, its midline also to the submental nodes, and it may cross the midline; the upper lip drains to the submandibular, preauricular and infraparotid nodes.

# Teeth, gums and jaws | What the dentist sees.
## The tooth
- **Enamel:** the hardest outer layer, laid down by ameloblasts.
- **Dentine:** beneath it, formed throughout life by odontoblasts, and sensitive.
- **Pulp:** the inner core of vessels and nerves.
- **Cementum:** covers the root and anchors it through the periodontal ligament to the alveolar bone.
## Numbers
Twenty deciduous teeth (no premolars) and thirty-two permanent teeth: in each quadrant two incisors, one canine, two premolars and three molars, the third molar being the wisdom tooth.
## Gingiva
The attached gingiva is bound firmly to the alveolar bone; the looser alveolar mucosa lies beyond it.
!anat s169_16 | Gingiva and alveolar mucosa.
## Muscles of mastication
All supplied by the mandibular nerve (V3):
- **Masseter:** from the zygomatic arch to the angle and lateral ramus; elevates the mandible.
- **Temporalis:** from the temporal fossa to the coronoid process; elevates and retracts.
- **Lateral pterygoid:** from the greater wing of the sphenoid and lateral pterygoid plate to the condyle; opens the mouth, protrudes and moves the jaw from side to side.
- **Medial pterygoid:** from the medial side of the lateral pterygoid plate and the maxillary tuberosity to the medial angle; elevates and moves the jaw from side to side.
Infiltration of these muscles by infection or cancer produces *trismus*.

# The tongue | The busiest muscle in the head.
## Surface
The *sulcus terminalis* and the V of circumvallate papillae divide the oral tongue in front from the base of tongue behind. At the apex of the V lies the *foramen caecum*, from which the thyroid descends in the embryo; a lingual thyroid or thyroglossal cyst begins here. Behind lie the lingual tonsil and the valleculae, both oropharynx.
!anat s174_30 d181_1 | The dorsum of the tongue with its papillae, sulcus terminalis and lingual tonsil, and its undersurface with the floor of mouth.
## Papillae
- **Filiform:** the most numerous; texture, temperature and pain; no taste.
- **Fungiform:** mostly at the tip; taste buds on their upper surface.
- **Foliate:** on the lateral border; taste buds on their sides.
- **Circumvallate:** eight to twelve in a V in front of the sulcus terminalis; taste buds in their walls.
## Muscles
The intrinsic muscles (longitudinal, transverse and vertical) change its shape; the extrinsic muscles move it.
!table Extrinsic muscles of the tongue
Muscle | From | Action | Nerve
Genioglossus | Mental spine of the mandible | Protrudes and depresses; the bulk of the tongue | Hypoglossal
Hyoglossus | Hyoid bone | Depresses and retracts | Hypoglossal
Styloglossus | Styloid process | Retracts and elevates | Hypoglossal
Palatoglossus | Palatine aponeurosis | Raises the back of the tongue, closes the isthmus | Vagus (pharyngeal plexus)

!pearl Testing the hypoglossal nerve
With a lower motor neurone lesion the protruded tongue deviates towards the paralysed side, pushed across by the healthy genioglossus, and the paralysed half wastes and fasciculates.
!!
## Nerves and taste
- **Oral tongue:** touch, pain and temperature by the lingual nerve (V3); taste by the chorda tympani (VII) to the geniculate ganglion and nucleus solitarius.
- **Base of tongue:** both general sensation and taste by the glossopharyngeal nerve (IX).
- **Epiglottis and valleculae:** a little taste by the internal laryngeal branch of the vagus (X).
The five tastes are sweet, sour, salty, bitter and umami; flavour is taste combined with smell.
!anat s175_33 | The nerve supply and taste pathways of the tongue.
!pearl Referred otalgia
The lingual nerve and the auriculotemporal nerve are both branches of V3, and the glossopharyngeal nerve also supplies the middle ear through Jacobson's nerve. A cancer of the tongue or tonsil therefore often presents as earache with a normal ear.
!!
## Vessels and lymph
The lingual artery, second branch of the external carotid, supplies the tongue; the lingual vein and the vein accompanying the hypoglossal nerve drain it. Lymph from the tip goes to the submental and jugulo-omohyoid nodes, from the lateral border to the ipsilateral submandibular and deep cervical nodes (sometimes skipping straight to level IV), and from the central tongue and base to the deep cervical nodes of both sides.

# Palate and floor | Roof and floor.
## Hard palate
The palatine processes of the maxillae in front and the horizontal plates of the palatine bones behind, covered by mucoperiosteum firmly bound to bone.
- **Incisive foramen:** in the midline behind the incisors; nasopalatine nerve and vessels.
- **Greater palatine foramen:** medial to the second or third molar; greater palatine nerve and vessels, the pedicle of palatal flaps.
- **Lesser palatine foramina:** behind it, to the soft palate.
Sensation is from the nasopalatine and greater palatine nerves (V2); blood from the greater palatine artery; lymph to the upper deep cervical and lateral retropharyngeal nodes, the front of the palate also to the submandibular nodes.
!anat s173_27 | The hard palate, soft palate, retromolar trigone and oral tongue.
## Floor of mouth
The mylohyoid muscle forms the diaphragm of the floor. Above it lie the sublingual glands, the submandibular duct, the lingual nerve looping beneath the duct, and the hypoglossal nerve; below it, the submandibular space. The anterior floor drains to the submandibular nodes and crosses the midline freely; the posterior floor drains to the upper deep cervical nodes.
!anat s170_19 d125_84 | The floor of the mouth with the sublingual papillae and folds, and the openings of the ducts.
## Saliva
One to one and a half litres a day. At rest about two-thirds comes from the submandibular glands; when stimulated, more than half comes from the parotids. The parotid (Stensen) duct opens opposite the upper second molar; the submandibular (Wharton) duct beside the frenulum; the sublingual glands by many small ducts into the floor. Minor salivary glands line the lips, cheeks, palate and tongue, and are the source of the palate's salivary tumours.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Boundaries** of the oral cavity and its subsites.
- **Nerve supply of the tongue:** general sensation and taste, front and back.
- **Extrinsic muscles** and the test of the hypoglossal nerve.
- **Lymphatic drainage** of the lip, tongue and floor of mouth.
- **Referred otalgia** from the mouth and pharynx.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Calling the base of tongue or the tonsil "oral cavity".
- Forgetting that the lateral tongue can skip to level IV nodes.
- Inspecting the floor of mouth without palpating it.
!!
