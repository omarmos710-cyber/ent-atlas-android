@stand The pharynx is a shared corridor: air passes through it to the larynx and food through it to the oesophagus, and many times a minute it switches between the two without error. Its walls hold the lymphoid ring that guards both portals, its spaces carry infection down into the chest, and its three parts each give rise to their own cancers. The anatomy explains the symptoms: the earache of a tonsil cancer, the nasal voice of a palatal palsy, the pouch at Killian's dehiscence.

# The pharynx in general | A fibromuscular funnel.
## Extent
A conical fibromuscular tube 12 to 14 cm long, from the base of the skull (basisphenoid and basiocciput) to the lower border of the cricoid cartilage at the level of the sixth cervical vertebra, where it becomes the oesophagus. It is about 3.5 cm wide at the top and narrows to 1.5 cm at the pharyngo-oesophageal junction, the narrowest part of the gut after the appendix, which is why swallowed foreign bodies lodge there.

!anat s195_0 d172_73 | The divisions of the pharynx, and its cavity in sagittal section.
## Layers of the wall
From within outwards:
- **Mucous membrane:** ciliated columnar in the nasopharynx, stratified squamous below; continuous with the lining of the Eustachian tubes, nose, mouth, larynx and oesophagus.
- **Pharyngobasilar fascia (pharyngeal aponeurosis):** thick at the skull base, where it fills the gap above the superior constrictor, thin below.
- **Muscular coat:** an outer circular layer (the three constrictors) and an inner longitudinal layer (stylopharyngeus, salpingopharyngeus, palatopharyngeus).
- **Buccopharyngeal fascia:** covers the constrictors and continues forwards over the buccinator.

# Muscles and gaps | Three constrictors, three elevators.
## Constrictors
Each passes backwards to insert into the midline raphe, the lower overlapping the upper like stacked cups. All are supplied by the pharyngeal plexus (vagus).
- **Superior:** from the pterygoid hamulus, pterygomandibular raphe and mandible.
- **Middle:** from the hyoid bone and stylohyoid ligament.
- **Inferior:** from the thyroid and cricoid cartilages, in two parts: the oblique *thyropharyngeus* and the transverse *cricopharyngeus*, which is the upper oesophageal sphincter, tonically closed at rest and relaxing on swallowing.
!anat s198_3 d170_69 | The pharyngeal constrictors in lateral and posterior views.
## Longitudinal muscles
- **Stylopharyngeus:** from the styloid process; the only pharyngeal muscle supplied by the glossopharyngeal nerve.
- **Salpingopharyngeus:** from the Eustachian tube cartilage; raises the salpingopharyngeal fold.
- **Palatopharyngeus:** from the palate; forms the posterior pillar and, with fibres of the superior constrictor, *Passavant's ridge*.
All elevate the pharynx and larynx during swallowing.
## Gaps and what passes through them
- **Above the superior constrictor (sinus of Morgagni):** the Eustachian tube, levator and tensor veli palatini, and the ascending palatine artery.
- **Between superior and middle constrictors:** stylopharyngeus and the glossopharyngeal nerve.
- **Between middle and inferior constrictors:** the internal laryngeal nerve and superior laryngeal vessels.
- **Below the inferior constrictor:** the recurrent laryngeal nerve and inferior laryngeal vessels.
!anat s205_17 d171_72 | The gaps between the constrictors and the structures passing through them.
## Killian's dehiscence
A weak triangle in the posterior wall between thyropharyngeus above and cricopharyngeus below. Mucosa herniates through it to form a *pharyngeal pouch* (Zenker diverticulum), and an oesophagoscope can perforate it, hence its old name, the *gateway of tears*. Below cricopharyngeus lie Laimer's triangle and the lateral Killian–Jamieson area.

# Waldeyer's ring | Sentinels at the portals.
## Components
Lymphoid tissue lies throughout the pharyngeal submucosa and gathers into masses that ring the entrances of the air and food passages:
- **Nasopharyngeal tonsil (adenoids)** at the junction of roof and posterior wall.
- **Palatine tonsils** between the pillars.
- **Lingual tonsil** on the base of tongue.
- **Tubal tonsils** around the Eustachian tube openings.
- **Lateral pharyngeal bands** behind the posterior pillars.
- **Nodules** scattered on the posterior wall.
!fig d47-2 | Waldeyer's ring.
!anat s200_8 s209_23 | Waldeyer's ring seen through the mouth, and the adenoids compared with the palatine tonsils.
## Immunology
B-lymphocytes in the germinal centres produce secretory IgA, T-lymphocytes in the parafollicular areas give cell-mediated immunity, and plasma cells secrete IgM and IgG against organisms that enter the crypts. The tissue is most active in childhood. No lasting immune deficit has been shown after removal of the tonsils and adenoids.

# Nasopharynx | Behind the nose.
## Boundaries
Behind the nasal cavities, from the skull base to the level of the hard palate.
- **Roof and posterior wall:** basisphenoid and basiocciput, curving down onto the arch of the atlas.
- **Floor:** the upper surface of the soft palate in front; behind it the *nasopharyngeal isthmus*, closed by the palate in swallowing and speech.
- **Anterior wall:** the choanae and the posterior ends of the turbinates.
- **Lateral wall:** the Eustachian tube opening, about 1.25 cm behind the inferior turbinate, capped above and behind by the *torus tubarius*; behind the torus, the *fossa of Rosenmüller*, the commonest site of nasopharyngeal carcinoma; and the salpingopharyngeal fold descending from it.
!fig d47-4 d47-5 | First, the nasopharynx in sagittal section with the adenoids, nasopharyngeal bursa and Rathke's pouch. Second, an endoscopic view of the torus tubarius and the fossa of Rosenmüller behind it.
!anat s204_14 s204_15 | The lateral wall of the nasopharynx with the torus tubarius and fossa of Rosenmüller, and an endoscopic view.
## Special features
- **Adenoids:** grow until about six years and then atrophy; the overlying mucosa is thrown into radiating folds.
- **Nasopharyngeal bursa:** a midline recess within the adenoid, marking where the notochord touched the pharyngeal endoderm; infection causes postnasal discharge, and an abscess or cyst is *Thornwaldt disease*.
- **Rathke's pouch:** a dimple above the adenoids, remnant of the invagination that forms the anterior pituitary; a craniopharyngioma can arise along its course.
- **Passavant's ridge:** a ridge around the isthmus against which the soft palate closes.
## Lymphatic drainage
To the retropharyngeal (node of Rouvière) and parapharyngeal nodes, then the upper deep cervical and spinal accessory (posterior triangle) chains, often to both sides.
## Functions
Air conduit; ventilation and pressure equalisation of the middle ear; velopharyngeal closure in swallowing, vomiting and speech; a resonator for the voice (hyponasal with obstruction, hypernasal with velopharyngeal insufficiency); and drainage of nasal mucus.

# Oropharynx | Behind the mouth.
## Boundaries
From the plane of the hard palate to the plane of the hyoid bone, opening forwards into the mouth through the oropharyngeal isthmus (soft palate above, tongue below, anterior pillars at the sides).
- **Anterior wall:** the base of tongue with the lingual tonsil, and the *valleculae*, each between the median and lateral glossoepiglottic folds, a site for retention cysts.
- **Lateral wall:** the palatine tonsil in its fossa between the anterior (palatoglossal) and posterior (palatopharyngeal) pillars.
- **Posterior wall:** in front of C2 and C3, with the retropharyngeal space behind.
- **Superior wall:** the undersurface of the soft palate.
The upper border of the epiglottis and the pharyngoepiglottic folds separate it from the hypopharynx.

## Soft palate
A mobile flap hung from the back of the hard palate. When it rises it seals the nasopharynx from the oropharynx; when it falls against the tongue it seals the mouth, so we can chew and breathe together.
!table Muscles of the soft palate
Muscle | Action | Nerve
Levator veli palatini | Raises the palate | Vagus (pharyngeal plexus)
Tensor veli palatini | Tightens the palate; opens the Eustachian tube | Mandibular nerve (V3)
Palatoglossus | Narrows the isthmus; forms the anterior pillar | Vagus
Palatopharyngeus | Raises the pharynx; forms the posterior pillar | Vagus
Musculus uvulae | Shortens the uvula | Vagus

!pearl The uvula in a vagal palsy
With a unilateral vagal (pharyngeal plexus) palsy the uvula and palate are pulled towards the healthy side on saying "aah". The tensor alone is supplied by the trigeminal nerve.
!!
## The palatine tonsil
Its anatomy, blood supply and bed are described with tonsillitis; it drains to the *jugulodigastric (tonsillar) node*.
## Lymphatic drainage
To the upper jugular chain, especially the jugulodigastric node; the soft palate, pharyngeal walls and base of tongue also drain through the retropharyngeal and parapharyngeal nodes, and the base of tongue drains to both sides.
## Functions
A passage for air and food, the pharyngeal phase of swallowing, shaping of speech sounds, taste (taste buds on the base of tongue, soft palate, pillars and posterior wall), and immune defence through Waldeyer's ring.

# Hypopharynx | Behind and beside the larynx.
## Boundaries and subsites
From the plane of the hyoid bone to the lower border of the cricoid, in front of C3 to C6.
- **Piriform sinus (fossa):** on each side of the larynx, from the pharyngoepiglottic fold to the oesophagus; bounded laterally by the thyrohyoid membrane and thyroid cartilage, medially by the aryepiglottic fold, arytenoid and cricoid. It is the lateral food channel where fish bones lodge. The *internal laryngeal nerve* runs under its mucosa, so it can be anaesthetised there, and through it a piriform cancer refers pain to the ear.
- **Postcricoid region:** the anterior wall behind the cricoid lamina; the site of carcinoma in women with Plummer–Vinson syndrome.
- **Posterior pharyngeal wall:** from the hyoid to the level of the cricoarytenoid joints.
!fig d47-8 d47-9 | First, the right piriform fossa with its lateral and medial walls, apex and the postcricoid area. Second, the pharynx opened from behind, showing the nasopharynx, oropharynx and laryngopharynx.
!anat s221_43 s222_46 | The three subsites of the hypopharynx, and the internal laryngeal nerve under the mucosa of the piriform fossa.
## Lymphatic drainage
The piriform sinus is richly supplied; its lymphatics pierce the thyrohyoid membrane to the upper jugular nodes, which is why its cancers so often present with neck nodes. The posterior wall drains to the retropharyngeal and parapharyngeal nodes; the postcricoid region to the parapharyngeal, paratracheal and supraclavicular nodes.

# Nerves, vessels and spaces | The supply lines.
## Nerve supply
- **Motor:** the pharyngeal plexus (vagus) supplies all pharyngeal and palatal muscles except stylopharyngeus (glossopharyngeal) and tensor veli palatini (mandibular nerve).
- **Sensory:** nasopharynx by the maxillary nerve (V2); oropharynx by the glossopharyngeal nerve, the afferent limb of the gag reflex; hypopharynx by the internal laryngeal branch of the vagus.
!pearl Referred otalgia from the throat
The glossopharyngeal nerve supplies the middle ear through Jacobson's nerve, and the vagus supplies the ear canal through Arnold's nerve. Tonsillitis, a quinsy, a post-tonsillectomy throat and cancers of the tonsil, base of tongue or piriform sinus all present with earache.
!!
## Blood supply
The ascending pharyngeal artery, the tonsillar and ascending palatine branches of the facial artery, branches of the maxillary and lingual arteries, and below, the inferior thyroid artery. Veins form a pharyngeal plexus draining to the pterygoid plexus and internal jugular vein.
## Pharyngeal spaces
- **Retropharyngeal space:** behind the pharynx, in front of the prevertebral fascia, from the skull base to the bifurcation of the trachea; contains the retropharyngeal nodes, which regress after childhood.
- **Parapharyngeal space:** beside the pharynx, an inverted pyramid from the skull base to the hyoid; its post-styloid part contains the carotid vessels, internal jugular vein, the last four cranial nerves and the sympathetic chain.
Their infections are described with deep neck space infection.

# Swallowing | Three phases.
## Oral phase
Voluntary. Food is chewed, mixed with saliva and formed into a bolus that the tongue pushes back. When the bolus touches the anterior pillars, receptors supplied by the glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves trigger the pharyngeal phase.
## Pharyngeal phase
Involuntary and lasting under a second, while breathing stops:
1. The soft palate rises and meets Passavant's ridge, closing off the nasopharynx.
2. The vocal folds, false folds and arytenoids close the glottis, and the epiglottis tilts down.
3. The larynx and hyoid rise and move forwards under the suprahyoid and thyrohyoid muscles.
4. The tongue base drives back and the constrictors contract from above down.
5. Cricopharyngeus relaxes and is pulled open by the forward movement of the larynx, the most important factor in opening the sphincter.
## Oesophageal phase
A primary peristaltic wave carries the bolus to the stomach in three to six seconds, and the lower oesophageal sphincter relaxes.
!note Swallowing nerves
Sensation by V, IX and X; muscles by V (jaw, mylohyoid, tensor), VII (lips, face), IX (stylopharyngeus), X (palate, pharynx, larynx, cricopharyngeus) and XII (tongue). Incoordination between pharyngeal contraction and cricopharyngeal relaxation is thought to underlie the pharyngeal pouch.
!!

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Forgetting that stylopharyngeus (IX) and tensor veli palatini (V3) are the exceptions to vagal supply.
- Placing the valleculae in the hypopharynx; they belong to the oropharynx.
- Looking only at the ear in a patient with otalgia and a normal drum.
!!
