@stand Proton pump inhibitors are among the most prescribed drugs in the world and among the most over-prescribed in ENT. They are effective for gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, oesophagitis and peptic ulcer, and for protecting the stomach during NSAID and steroid treatment. For laryngopharyngeal reflux and persistent throat symptoms, however, randomised trials, including the UK TOPPITS trial, have not shown benefit over placebo, so empirical courses should be limited and reviewed.

# At a glance | The drugs on one page.
## Summary
!table Proton pump inhibitors
 | Summary
Agents | Omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole
How they work | Irreversibly inhibit the H+/K+ ATPase (proton pump) of the gastric parietal cell after activation in acid
ENT uses | Typical reflux symptoms with throat complaints; oesophagitis; after some laryngeal surgery and with laryngeal granulomas; in combination with alginates in suspected laryngopharyngeal reflux (limited evidence); gastroprotection
Adult dose | Omeprazole 20 to 40 mg once daily; lansoprazole 30 mg once daily; twice-daily dosing is often used in suspected laryngopharyngeal reflux, for 8 to 12 weeks, then stopped or reduced
Timing | 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast (and before the evening meal if twice daily)
!!

# Using them in ENT | Practice.
## Throat symptoms
Globus, throat clearing, chronic cough, hoarseness and catarrh have many causes. Examine with nasendoscopy and exclude red flags (dysphagia, weight loss, odynophagia, persistent hoarseness, a neck lump, smoking history) before considering reflux. In the **TOPPITS** trial, lansoprazole 30 mg twice daily for 16 weeks was no better than placebo for persistent throat symptoms. If a trial is given, set a time limit, combine with lifestyle measures and alginates, and stop if there is no benefit.
## Laryngeal granulomas and surgery
Contact granulomas of the vocal process are often treated with anti-reflux therapy, voice therapy and sometimes botulinum toxin; surgery has a high recurrence rate.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Headache, diarrhoea; with long-term use: **C. difficile**, hypomagnesaemia (check magnesium on long-term use, especially with diuretics), fractures, vitamin B12 deficiency, pneumonia, subacute cutaneous lupus, interstitial nephritis; rebound acid hypersecretion on stopping.
## Interactions
Omeprazole and esomeprazole reduce the activation of **clopidogrel** (choose pantoprazole); PPIs reduce absorption of itraconazole capsules, ketoconazole and some tyrosine kinase inhibitors; raise methotrexate levels at high doses.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- The weak evidence in laryngopharyngeal reflux and the **TOPPITS** trial.
- Correct timing of doses.
- Long-term risks and the clopidogrel interaction.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Lifelong PPIs for globus without review.
- Missing a cancer by labelling symptoms as reflux.
