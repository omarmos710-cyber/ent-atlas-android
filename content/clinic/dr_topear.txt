@stand The ear drum and the bony ear canal are among the most sensitive surfaces in the body, and procedures on them in the clinic depend on good topical anaesthesia. Phenol cauterises and anaesthetises a small area of the drum in seconds; EMLA and tetracaine numb the drum and canal over 30 to 60 minutes; and injection of local anaesthetic into the canal (a four-quadrant block) anaesthetises the whole canal and drum.

# At a glance | The agents on one page.
## Summary
!table Topical anaesthesia of the ear
 | Summary
Agents | Phenol (liquefied, about 80% to 90%) applied to a spot; EMLA cream (lidocaine 2.5% and prilocaine 2.5%) filling the canal; tetracaine (amethocaine) 4% gel or solution; lidocaine infiltration of the canal
ENT uses | Myringotomy and grommet in the clinic; intratympanic injections; biopsy of the drum; removal of granulation or small polyps; clinic myringoplasty
Phenol | Apply a tiny amount with a fine applicator to a clean, dry drum at the planned site; the drum blanches white within seconds and the area is anaesthetic for minutes
EMLA | Fill the canal to the drum, leave for 30 to 60 minutes, then suction out completely
Tetracaine | Fill the canal for 15 to 45 minutes, then remove

# Using them in ENT | Practice.
## Choosing
Phenol is quick and precise for a single puncture or a small incision. EMLA and tetracaine cover more of the drum for longer procedures but take time and must be removed fully. Injecting the canal at the bony-cartilaginous junction in four quadrants with lidocaine and adrenaline numbs both canal and drum and reduces bleeding.
## Safety with a perforation
Topical anaesthetics reaching the middle ear can cause **transient vertigo** or facial weakness; avoid filling an ear with a perforation.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Phenol: excess causes a burn and a persistent perforation. EMLA: **methaemoglobinaemia** from prilocaine, mainly in infants and with large areas (not a practical concern in the adult ear). Tetracaine: allergy (ester anaesthetic). All: vertigo if they reach the inner ear.
