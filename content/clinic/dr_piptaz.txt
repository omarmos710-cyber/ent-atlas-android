@stand Piperacillin with tazobactam is a broad-spectrum ureidopenicillin protected by a beta-lactamase inhibitor. It covers Pseudomonas, most Enterobacterales, staphylococci (not MRSA), streptococci and anaerobes, which makes it a standard intravenous choice for necrotising otitis externa, hospital-acquired head and neck infection and sepsis after major surgery. It is a hospital drug, and every use should be reviewed at 48 to 72 hours.

# How it works | Mechanism and handling.
## Mechanism
Piperacillin's side chain lets it penetrate the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacilli including Pseudomonas; tazobactam inhibits many plasmid-mediated beta-lactamases. Extended-spectrum beta-lactamase (ESBL) and AmpC producers are often resistant; use a carbapenem for proven ESBL infection.
## Handling
Given intravenously only; renally excreted, so reduce the dose when the creatinine clearance is below 40 ml/min.

# Using it in ENT | Indications and doses.
## Necrotising otitis externa
An elderly diabetic or immunocompromised patient with severe otalgia out of proportion, granulation at the bony-cartilaginous junction and cranial nerve palsy (VII first). Take deep cultures and biopsy (to exclude carcinoma) **before** antibiotics if possible, control the diabetes, start intravenous anti-pseudomonal therapy (piperacillin-tazobactam or ceftazidime, often with ciprofloxacin), and convert to oral ciprofloxacin for a total of at least 6 weeks guided by clinical response, inflammatory markers and imaging.
## Post-operative and hospital infection
After major head and neck surgery, a wound or neck infection with a pharyngocutaneous fistula, or aspiration pneumonia, is often polymicrobial with Gram-negatives; piperacillin-tazobactam is a common empirical choice, narrowed when cultures return.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- Hypersensitivity; diarrhoea and C. difficile.
- **Acute kidney injury**, especially in combination with vancomycin.
- Hypokalaemia (sodium load), thrombocytopenia and neutropenia with prolonged courses, and hepatitis.
## Contraindications and cautions
- Penicillin allergy.
- Renal impairment (dose reduction).
## Interactions
**Vancomycin** (additive nephrotoxicity: monitor renal function); methotrexate; aminoglycosides (inactivated if mixed in the same infusion: give separately).

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- Organisms of **necrotising otitis externa** (Pseudomonas in most; Aspergillus in some) and the treatment plan.
- Which beta-lactams cover Pseudomonas: piperacillin-tazobactam, ceftazidime, cefepime, carbapenems (not ertapenem).
- Why to culture and biopsy before treatment.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Starting antibiotics in necrotising otitis externa before taking deep cultures and a biopsy.
- Stopping too early on clinical improvement.
- Continuing broad-spectrum therapy without a 48-hour review.
!!
