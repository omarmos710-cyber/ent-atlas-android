@stand Healthy ear canal skin is acidic, with a pH of about 5 to 6, which keeps Pseudomonas and other bacteria in check. Acidifying preparations (acetic acid), astringents (aluminium acetate) and drying powders (boric acid) restore that environment. They are useful for mild otitis externa, for prevention in swimmers and in wet mastoid cavities, and as an alternative to antibiotics.

## Summary

How they work | Lower the canal pH, are bacteriostatic and antifungal, and dry and shrink inflamed skin

Dose | Acetic acid spray, one spray three times daily for up to 7 days (until 2 days after symptoms resolve)
Avoid | Perforated drum for acetic acid and alcohol drops (pain and possible ototoxicity)

# How they work | Mechanism and handling.
## Mechanism
Acid inhibits Pseudomonas and staphylococci, astringents precipitate proteins and reduce oedema and exudate, and boric acid both dries and acidifies.

# Using them in ENT | Indications and doses.
## Mild otitis externa and prevention
Acetic acid spray is an over-the-counter first step in mild otitis externa, with results close to antibiotic-steroid drops in the first week but less effective with longer courses. Swimmers with recurrent infection can use acidifying or alcohol drops after swimming.
## Cavities
Insufflating **boric acid powder** after cleaning a wet mastoid cavity helps keep it dry; repeated sessions may be needed.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Stinging and burning (especially in an inflamed canal), and irritation through a perforation.
