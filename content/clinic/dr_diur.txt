@stand Diuretics have been used in Ménière's disease for decades on the theory that they reduce endolymphatic hydrops. Thiazides (bendroflumethiazide, hydrochlorothiazide, often with a potassium-sparing drug) and acetazolamide are the agents. Evidence of benefit is weak and comes mostly from older, small studies, but many patients and clinicians report fewer attacks. They need electrolyte monitoring.

# At a glance | The drugs on one page.
## Summary
!table Diuretics in Ménière's disease
 | Summary
Agents | Bendroflumethiazide; hydrochlorothiazide with triamterene or amiloride; acetazolamide (carbonic anhydrase inhibitor)
How they work | Thiazides block the Na-Cl cotransporter in the distal tubule; acetazolamide reduces bicarbonate reabsorption and may reduce endolymph production; both proposed to reduce hydrops
ENT uses | Maintenance treatment in Ménière's disease to reduce vertigo attacks (alongside salt restriction); acetazolamide also in idiopathic intracranial hypertension with pulsatile tinnitus, and in some CSF leaks
Adult dose | Bendroflumethiazide 2.5 mg once daily; hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg with triamterene once daily; acetazolamide 250 mg twice daily (higher in intracranial hypertension)
Avoid | Hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, gout, severe renal or hepatic impairment; acetazolamide in sulfonamide allergy, renal stones, metabolic acidosis
!!

# Using them in ENT | Indications.
## Ménière's disease
Offered after lifestyle measures, often with or instead of betahistine; review at 3 to 6 months. Check sodium, potassium and renal function before and periodically. Salt restriction (about 1.5 to 2 g sodium a day) is part of the same strategy.
## Pulsatile tinnitus and intracranial hypertension
Acetazolamide lowers CSF production in **idiopathic intracranial hypertension**, which presents with pulsatile tinnitus, headache, obesity and papilloedema; involve neurology and ophthalmology.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- Thiazides: hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hyperuricaemia and gout, hyperglycaemia, postural hypotension, erectile dysfunction, photosensitivity.
- Acetazolamide: paraesthesia, taste change (carbonated drinks taste flat), fatigue, metabolic acidosis, renal stones, rarely blood dyscrasias and Stevens-Johnson syndrome.
## Interactions
Lithium (toxicity), NSAIDs (reduced effect), digoxin (toxicity if hypokalaemic).
