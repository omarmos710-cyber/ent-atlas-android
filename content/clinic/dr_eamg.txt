@stand Aminoglycoside ear drops combine gentamicin, neomycin or framycetin, often with polymyxin and a steroid. They are effective, cheap and widely used for otitis externa. Their problem is the inner ear: if they reach the middle ear through a perforation or a ventilation tube, they can cause sensorineural loss and vestibular damage. Neomycin also sensitises the skin, and contact dermatitis can perpetuate the otitis externa it was meant to treat.

# How they work | Mechanism and handling.
## Mechanism
Topical aminoglycosides reach very high concentrations in the canal. In the middle ear they diffuse through the **round window membrane** into the perilymph and damage hair cells, as systemic aminoglycosides do. The risk depends on the dose, the duration, and the state of the middle ear mucosa: inflamed, thickened mucosa may protect the round window, which is why toxicity is uncommon in actively discharging ears and more likely in a dry ear with a perforation.

# Using them in ENT | Indications and doses.
## With an intact drum
They are a reasonable choice for **acute otitis externa** when the drum is seen and intact.
## With a perforation or a tube
Guidance from ENT UK accepts that aminoglycoside drops may be used with caution in a **discharging** ear with a perforation when the benefit outweighs the risk, for **no longer than 2 weeks**, and with the patient warned; but quinolone drops avoid the dilemma and are preferred. Do not use them in a **dry** perforation.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- **Ototoxicity**: sensorineural hearing loss, tinnitus and vestibular failure if they reach the inner ear.
- **Contact dermatitis**: neomycin sensitivity is common in chronic otitis externa (patch tests are positive in a substantial proportion); a red, weeping, itchy canal that worsens on treatment suggests it.
- Otomycosis with prolonged use; steroid-related local effects.
## Interactions
Clinically insignificant systemically. Avoid with other ototoxic ear preparations.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Prescribing gentamicin drops for weeks in an ear with a perforation.
- Missing neomycin allergy in an otitis externa that gets worse with treatment.
