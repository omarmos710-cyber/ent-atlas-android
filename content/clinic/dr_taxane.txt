@stand Docetaxel and paclitaxel stabilise microtubules and stop cell division. Docetaxel is the "T" in TPF induction chemotherapy (docetaxel, cisplatin, 5-FU), used in larynx preservation protocols and to shrink advanced tumours before definitive treatment; paclitaxel is used weekly in palliation and with radiotherapy in some regimens. Both need steroid premedication and cause neutropenia and neuropathy.

# At a glance | The drugs on one page.
## Summary
!table Docetaxel and paclitaxel
 | Summary
Class | Taxanes
How they work | Bind beta-tubulin and stabilise microtubules, arresting cells in mitosis
ENT uses | TPF induction chemotherapy for larynx and hypopharynx preservation and for selected locally advanced tumours; palliative treatment of recurrent and metastatic disease (alone, with platinum, or after immunotherapy); some sinonasal and nasopharyngeal regimens
Dose | TPF: docetaxel 75 mg/m2 and cisplatin 75 mg/m2 on day 1, 5-FU 750 mg/m2 daily on days 1 to 5, every 3 weeks for 2 to 3 cycles; paclitaxel 80 mg/m2 weekly in palliation
Premedication | Dexamethasone (docetaxel: fluid retention and hypersensitivity); antihistamines and steroids (paclitaxel: hypersensitivity to the Cremophor vehicle)
!!

# Using them in ENT | Practice.
## Larynx preservation
In advanced laryngeal and hypopharyngeal cancer, responders to TPF induction proceed to radiotherapy or chemoradiotherapy with preservation of the larynx; non-responders proceed to laryngectomy. Patients with a non-functioning larynx (aspiration, airway obstruction needing tracheostomy, cartilage destruction) are usually better served by laryngectomy.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
**Neutropenia** and febrile neutropenia (granulocyte colony-stimulating factor support with TPF), peripheral neuropathy, hypersensitivity reactions, fluid retention (docetaxel), alopecia, nail changes, mucositis.
