@stand Flucloxacillin is a penicillinase-resistant penicillin: its side chain shields the beta-lactam ring from staphylococcal penicillinase. It is the drug for infections caused by Staphylococcus aureus: furuncles of the ear canal and nasal vestibule, cellulitis of the face and pinna, infected wounds and haematomas, and acute suppurative parotitis. It does not cover MRSA, Pseudomonas or Haemophilus.

# How it works | Mechanism and handling.
## Mechanism
An isoxazolyl penicillin whose bulky side chain prevents penicillinase from reaching the beta-lactam bond. **MRSA** resists it through a different penicillin-binding protein (PBP2a, encoded by mecA), so methicillin resistance means flucloxacillin resistance.
## Absorption and handling
Oral absorption is incomplete and reduced by food: take it **an hour before or two hours after meals**. It is highly protein-bound, excreted renally and partly by the liver.

# Using it in ENT | Indications and doses.
## Staphylococcal skin and soft tissue infection
- **Furuncle of the ear canal**: a painful boil in the outer, hair-bearing canal; incise if pointing, give oral flucloxacillin for spreading infection, and a wick with antibiotic-steroid drops if the canal is shut.
- **Nasal vestibulitis and furunculosis**: topical antiseptic or mupirocin for mild disease, oral flucloxacillin for a furuncle, and urgent assessment if there is spreading cellulitis (the danger triangle drains to the cavernous sinus).
- **Cellulitis of the face, pinna and neck**: flucloxacillin, with benzylpenicillin or clindamycin in severe disease.
## Perichondritis of the pinna
Perichondritis after piercing of the upper ear cartilage is often caused by **Pseudomonas**, which flucloxacillin does not cover; ciprofloxacin is the oral choice. Use flucloxacillin for simple cellulitis of the lobule and staphylococcal wound infection.
## Acute suppurative parotitis
Usually S. aureus in a dehydrated, elderly or post-operative patient: rehydrate, massage, sialogogues and intravenous flucloxacillin (with metronidazole or as co-amoxiclav), and look for an abscess on ultrasound if it does not settle.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- **Cholestatic jaundice and hepatitis**, which may appear up to two months after stopping; commoner in the elderly and with courses longer than 14 days.
- Gastrointestinal upset; phlebitis with intravenous use.
- The hypersensitivity of all penicillins.
## Contraindications and cautions
- Penicillin allergy; previous flucloxacillin jaundice.
- Hepatic impairment.
## Interactions
**Paracetamol** with flucloxacillin, in malnourished or septic patients, has been linked to high anion gap **metabolic acidosis** (pyroglutamic acidosis). Methotrexate excretion is reduced.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- Why flucloxacillin resists penicillinase, and why MRSA resists flucloxacillin.
- The organisms of **otitis externa (Pseudomonas, S. aureus)** and of **perichondritis (Pseudomonas)**, and which drug covers which.
- Take on an **empty stomach**; delayed cholestatic jaundice.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Treating a cartilage perichondritis with flucloxacillin alone.
- Oral flucloxacillin taken with food.
- Forgetting to drain a pointing furuncle or an abscess.
!!
