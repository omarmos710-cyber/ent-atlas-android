@stand Cefalexin (first generation) and cefuroxime (second generation) are the oral cephalosporins an ENT doctor uses. They are alternatives to penicillins for patients whose penicillin allergy was a non-severe rash, and cefalexin is a reliable drug for staphylococcal and streptococcal skin and wound infection. Cefuroxime adds better Haemophilus and Moraxella cover. Neither covers Pseudomonas, anaerobes, MRSA or enterococci, and both are associated with C. difficile.

# How they work | Mechanism and handling.
## Mechanism and generations
Cephalosporins share the beta-lactam ring of penicillins but have a six-membered dihydrothiazine ring that resists many beta-lactamases. Moving from the first to the third generation, Gram-negative activity increases and Gram-positive activity falls; cefalexin is the best oral anti-staphylococcal cephalosporin, cefuroxime the best balanced for the respiratory organisms.
## Absorption and handling
Cefalexin is almost completely absorbed; cefuroxime axetil is a prodrug absorbed better with food. Both are excreted by the kidney: reduce the dose when renal function is poor.

# Using them in ENT | Indications and doses.
## Penicillin allergy
Ask what actually happened. A **non-severe delayed rash** is not a contraindication to a cephalosporin; cross-reactivity is low (about 1% to 2%) and is driven mainly by similar side chains (cefalexin shares one with amoxicillin, cefuroxime does not). After **anaphylaxis, angioedema, or a severe cutaneous reaction** (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, DRESS), avoid all beta-lactams and choose a macrolide, doxycycline or clindamycin.
## Skin, wound and soft tissue
Cefalexin covers the staphylococci and streptococci of cellulitis, wound infection and infected epidermoid cysts, and is an option for prophylaxis after ear and facial plastic surgery where cover is wanted.
## Surgical prophylaxis
Intravenous cefazolin or cefuroxime at induction (with metronidazole if the pharynx is opened) is standard for clean-contaminated head and neck surgery; prophylaxis should not run beyond 24 hours.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- Diarrhoea and **C. difficile**, among the highest risks of any antibiotic class.
- Hypersensitivity, including rash, serum sickness-like reactions (cefaclor especially) and anaphylaxis.
- Rarely a positive Coombs test with haemolysis, and interstitial nephritis.
## Interactions
Mild potentiation of warfarin. Probenecid raises their levels.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- The **generations** and how the spectrum shifts.
- Cross-reactivity with penicillin and which allergy histories rule them out.
- That no oral cephalosporin covers **Pseudomonas** or anaerobes.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Prescribing a cephalosporin after documented penicillin anaphylaxis.
- Using them for necrotising otitis externa or a dental abscess without anaerobic cover.
- Prolonged prophylaxis beyond 24 hours.
!!
