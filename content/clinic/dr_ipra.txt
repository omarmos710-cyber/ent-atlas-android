@stand Ipratropium bromide is an antimuscarinic. As a nasal spray, it blocks the parasympathetic supply to the seromucous glands and stops watery rhinorrhoea within minutes, without affecting congestion, sneezing or itch. It is the drug for a "runny tap" nose: vasomotor rhinitis, gustatory rhinitis, rhinitis in the elderly, and the common cold.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Ipratropium nasal spray
 | Summary
Class | Short-acting muscarinic antagonist
How it works | Blocks acetylcholine at muscarinic receptors on nasal glands, reducing secretion
ENT uses | Non-allergic (vasomotor) rhinitis with predominant rhinorrhoea; gustatory rhinitis; elderly rhinitis ("old man's drip"); persistent rhinorrhoea in allergic rhinitis despite a steroid; common cold
Adult dose | 2 sprays (21 micrograms each, 0.03%) into each nostril two or three times daily
Child dose | Licensed from 12 years in the UK
Avoid | Caution in glaucoma (avoid spraying into eyes), prostatic hypertrophy and urinary retention
Pregnancy | Limited data; use if needed
!!

# Using it in ENT | Indications.
## Choosing it
It does not help **congestion**, so it is not a substitute for a nasal steroid where blockage is the main problem; combine the two when both symptoms are present. For gustatory rhinitis, use it 15 to 30 minutes before meals.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Nasal dryness, epistaxis, irritation; rarely anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention).
