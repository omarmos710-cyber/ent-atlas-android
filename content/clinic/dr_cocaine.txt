@stand Cocaine is the only local anaesthetic that also causes intense vasoconstriction, by blocking noradrenaline reuptake. That made it the classic preparation for nasal surgery: Moffett's solution or cocaine paste shrinks and anaesthetises the nasal mucosa in one step. It is also a sympathomimetic with real cardiovascular risks, a controlled drug, and a cause of septal perforation and midline destruction when abused. Many units now use lidocaine with adrenaline or oxymetazoline instead.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Cocaine (topical)
 | Summary
Class | Ester local anaesthetic and sympathomimetic
How it works | Sodium-channel block plus inhibition of noradrenaline, dopamine and serotonin reuptake, causing vasoconstriction and central stimulation
Preparations | Topical solution 4% to 10%; paste 10% to 25%; Moffett's solution (classically 2 ml of 8% cocaine, 2 ml of 1% sodium bicarbonate and 1 ml of 1:1,000 adrenaline, applied on pledgets or as a spray, with the patient in the head-down position)
ENT uses | Topical anaesthesia and decongestion for septal, turbinate, sinus and nasal fracture surgery
Maximum dose | 1.5 mg/kg (many units limit the total to 100 mg in an adult)
Avoid | Ischaemic heart disease, hypertension, arrhythmia, hyperthyroidism, phaeochromocytoma, pregnancy; patients on MAOIs, tricyclics or non-selective beta blockers
Legal status | Schedule 2 controlled drug: record, store and dispose of it as required
!!

# Using it in ENT | Practice.
## Application
Apply on pledgets or as a measured spray, keep within the dose limit, and allow 10 to 15 minutes. With Moffett's solution, adrenaline adds vasoconstriction and the bicarbonate improves absorption of the cocaine base.
## Recognising misuse
Chronic intranasal cocaine use causes septal perforation, loss of the turbinates, palatal perforation and destruction of the midline (cocaine-induced midline destructive lesion), which can mimic granulomatosis with polyangiitis, including a positive ANCA (often PR3 or HNE). Ask about drug use, and be aware that levamisole adulteration causes vasculitis and neutropenia.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Tachycardia, hypertension, arrhythmia, coronary vasospasm, myocardial infarction, stroke, agitation, hyperthermia and seizures; with adrenaline, the risk is additive. Treat toxicity with benzodiazepines, cooling and avoidance of beta blockers (unopposed alpha stimulation).
## Interactions
MAOIs, tricyclics, adrenaline and other sympathomimetics; non-selective beta blockers; halothane (arrhythmias).

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- Mechanism of vasoconstriction (reuptake inhibition).
- Moffett's solution and its components.
- The maximum dose and cardiovascular contraindications.
- **Cocaine-induced midline destructive lesion** versus GPA.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Giving a beta blocker to treat cocaine-induced hypertension.
- Forgetting controlled drug procedures.
