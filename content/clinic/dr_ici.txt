@stand Pembrolizumab and nivolumab are antibodies against programmed death receptor 1 (PD-1). By blocking the brake on cytotoxic T cells, they let the immune system attack the tumour. Pembrolizumab, alone or with chemotherapy, is first-line treatment for recurrent or metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma expressing PD-L1 (KEYNOTE-048); nivolumab improved survival after platinum failure (CheckMate 141); and perioperative pembrolizumab has improved event-free survival in resectable locally advanced disease (KEYNOTE-689). Their side effects are immune attacks on normal organs, which can appear months after starting.

# At a glance | The drugs on one page.
## Summary
!table Pembrolizumab and nivolumab
 | Summary
Class | Immune checkpoint inhibitors (anti-PD-1)
How they work | Block PD-1 on T cells so tumour PD-L1 cannot switch them off
ENT uses | Recurrent or metastatic HNSCC first-line (pembrolizumab alone if PD-L1 combined positive score of 1 or more, or with platinum and 5-FU); after platinum failure (nivolumab, pembrolizumab); perioperative treatment of resectable locally advanced HNSCC (pembrolizumab, approved in some countries); cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cemiplimab); nasopharyngeal carcinoma (toripalimab and others with chemotherapy)
Dose | Pembrolizumab 200 mg every 3 weeks or 400 mg every 6 weeks; nivolumab 240 mg every 2 weeks or 480 mg every 4 weeks
Biomarker | PD-L1 **combined positive score** (CPS) on tumour tissue
!!

# Using them in ENT | Practice.
## Response patterns
Responses can be slow and durable; pseudoprogression (apparent growth from immune infiltration) occurs occasionally, so scans are interpreted in context. About a fifth of patients respond, and some have long-term control.

# Safety | Immune-related adverse events.
## Recognising them
Any organ can be affected:
- **Thyroid**: hypothyroidism (very common, particularly after neck radiotherapy) or thyroiditis with transient thyrotoxicosis; check thyroid function regularly.
- **Skin**: rash, pruritus, vitiligo.
- **Gut**: diarrhoea and colitis.
- **Lung**: pneumonitis (cough, breathlessness).
- **Liver**: hepatitis.
- **Endocrine**: hypophysitis, adrenal insufficiency, type 1 diabetes.
- Rarer: myocarditis (often fatal), myositis, neuropathy, nephritis, sicca.
## Management
Grade the toxicity, withhold the drug for moderate or severe events, and treat with corticosteroids (with other immunosuppressants for refractory cases) in discussion with oncology; replace hormones for endocrine failure, which is usually permanent.
