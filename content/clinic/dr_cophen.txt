@stand Co-phenylcaine is a nasal spray of lidocaine 5% with phenylephrine 0.5%: an anaesthetic and a decongestant in one. It is the standard preparation for nasendoscopy, cautery of Little's area, nasal biopsy and removal of foreign bodies in the clinic, and it shrinks the mucosa to show the middle meatus and nasopharynx.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Co-phenylcaine
 | Summary
Composition | Lidocaine hydrochloride 5% and phenylephrine hydrochloride 0.5% (each spray about 5 mg lidocaine and 0.5 mg phenylephrine)
How it works | Sodium-channel block (lidocaine) and alpha-1 vasoconstriction (phenylephrine)
ENT uses | Rigid and flexible nasendoscopy; cautery for epistaxis; biopsy; nasal foreign body removal in older children and adults; packing removal; preparation for flexible laryngoscopy (with a throat spray)
Dose | Adults and children over 12: up to 8 sprays in total (commonly 2 to 5 sprays into each nostril); children 8 to 12: up to 5 sprays; wait about 5 to 10 minutes
Avoid | Under 8 years; MAOIs within 2 weeks; severe hypertension or ischaemic heart disease (caution); hyperthyroidism
!!

# Using it in ENT | Practice.
## Technique
Spray with the patient's head slightly forward, aiming along the floor and toward the middle meatus; wait at least 5 minutes. Soaked cotton pledgets placed under the inferior and middle turbinates give deeper anaesthesia for cautery or biopsy. Warn the patient about the bitter taste and the numb throat, and advise nothing to eat or drink until sensation returns (about an hour) if the spray has reached the pharynx.
## Alternatives
Lidocaine spray alone with xylometazoline, or for children, topical anaesthetic gel or a short examination without anaesthesia.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Bitter taste, numb throat, occasional palpitations and hypertension; vasovagal reactions during procedures; local anaesthetic toxicity if excess is used, especially in children.
## Interactions
MAOIs, tricyclics and beta blockers (phenylephrine).
