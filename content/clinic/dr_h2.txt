@stand H2 receptor antagonists reduce gastric acid by blocking histamine at the parietal cell. They are less potent than PPIs but act within an hour and are useful at bedtime for nocturnal acid breakthrough, as an alternative when PPIs are not tolerated, and in some regimens for laryngopharyngeal reflux. Ranitidine was withdrawn worldwide in 2020 because of contamination with the carcinogen NDMA; famotidine is the usual choice.

# At a glance | The drugs on one page.
## Summary
!table H2 receptor antagonists
 | Summary
Agents | Famotidine; cimetidine; nizatidine (ranitidine withdrawn)
How they work | Competitive antagonism of histamine H2 receptors on parietal cells, reducing basal and nocturnal acid secretion
ENT uses | Nocturnal reflux symptoms with a PPI; PPI intolerance; premedication to reduce aspiration risk in some anaesthetic protocols
Adult dose | Famotidine 20 to 40 mg once or twice daily (reduce in renal impairment)
Child dose | Famotidine on specialist advice by weight
!!

# Using them in ENT | Practice.
## Place in treatment
Tolerance (tachyphylaxis) develops within weeks of regular use, limiting their long-term effect. At bedtime, a dose with a morning PPI is sometimes used for nocturnal symptoms.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Headache, dizziness, diarrhoea; confusion in the elderly and in renal impairment; gynaecomastia and many interactions with **cimetidine** (a CYP inhibitor raising warfarin, phenytoin and theophylline levels).
