@stand Sodium cromoglicate stabilises mast cells and prevents them releasing histamine and other mediators. It is safe in children and pregnancy but works only when used before exposure and several times a day. In ENT it is now a minor drug: a preventive spray for predictable allergen exposure and an eye drop for allergic conjunctivitis.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Sodium cromoglicate
 | Summary
Class | Mast-cell stabiliser (cromone)
How it works | Inhibits mast-cell degranulation, possibly through chloride channel effects
ENT uses | Prophylaxis of seasonal or exposure-related allergic rhinitis (pollen, animals); allergic conjunctivitis; patients wishing to avoid steroids, especially in pregnancy and children
Dose | Nasal spray 4%: 1 spray into each nostril two to four times daily (up to six times); eye drops 2%: 1 to 2 drops four times daily
Pregnancy | Considered safe
!!

# Using it in ENT | Indications.
## Place in treatment
Less effective than nasal steroids; useful for short, predictable exposures when started a week before the season or taken shortly before contact. It has no effect on symptoms once they have started.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Local irritation, transient sneezing; very rarely bronchospasm.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- Mechanism and why it is preventive only.
- Where it fits (pregnancy, children, predictable exposure).
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
