@stand Tranexamic acid blocks the breakdown of fibrin clot. It is cheap, widely available and safe in most patients, and in ENT it is used for epistaxis, tonsillar and post-tonsillectomy haemorrhage, bleeding in patients with bleeding disorders, and to reduce blood loss in major head and neck and sinus surgery. It can be given by mouth, intravenously or topically on a pledget or as a mouthwash.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Tranexamic acid
 | Summary
Class | Antifibrinolytic (lysine analogue)
How it works | Blocks the lysine-binding sites of plasminogen, so plasmin cannot bind and break down fibrin
ENT uses | Epistaxis (topical or oral adjunct); post-tonsillectomy haemorrhage; bleeding in von Willebrand disease, haemophilia and platelet disorders (with desmopressin or factor); reduction of blood loss in sinus, orthognathic and major head and neck surgery; hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia (oral, long-term in selected patients)
Adult dose | Oral 1 g two or three times daily (up to 1.5 g); intravenous 1 g slowly; topical 500 mg (5 ml of 100 mg/ml) on a pledget or swab, or a 5% mouthwash
Child dose | Oral 15 to 25 mg/kg two or three times daily (maximum 1.5 g); intravenous 10 to 15 mg/kg (maximum 1 g)
Avoid | Active thromboembolic disease; history of convulsions (high doses); severe renal impairment (dose reduction); disseminated intravascular coagulation with active clot formation
Pregnancy | Can be used
!!

# Using it in ENT | Evidence and practice.
## Epistaxis
Topical tranexamic acid on a pledget or nebulised has been used as a first step, but a large UK trial (NoPAC, 2021) found that topical tranexamic acid did not reduce the need for anterior packing in emergency department epistaxis. It remains useful as an adjunct, particularly in patients on antiplatelets, and orally in recurrent bleeding from hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia.
## Tonsillar haemorrhage
Intravenous or oral tranexamic acid is widely given as part of the conservative management of secondary post-tonsillectomy bleeding, alongside admission, intravenous access and assessment for return to theatre; the evidence is limited but harm is rare. A gargled mouthwash is an option in adults.
## Surgery
A dose at induction reduces blood loss in endoscopic sinus surgery (better field), orthognathic and major head and neck procedures, without an increase in thrombosis in trials.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Nausea and diarrhoea; **seizures** with high intravenous doses (cardiac surgery); visual disturbance rarely; thromboembolism in predisposed patients is a theoretical concern not borne out in large trials.
