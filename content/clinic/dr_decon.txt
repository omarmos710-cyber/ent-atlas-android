@stand Xylometazoline and oxymetazoline are alpha-adrenergic agonists that shrink the nasal mucosa within minutes. They relieve congestion in acute colds and sinusitis, open the nose before examination and surgery, help in epistaxis, and are a diagnostic tool when a fixed obstruction is suspected. Used for more than a few days, they cause rebound congestion and rhinitis medicamentosa.

# At a glance | The drugs on one page.
## Summary
!table Topical nasal decongestants
 | Summary
Agents | Xylometazoline 0.1% (adults) and 0.05% (children); oxymetazoline 0.05%; phenylephrine (in combinations)
How they work | Alpha-1 and alpha-2 adrenoceptor agonists: venous sinusoid constriction in the turbinates
ENT uses | Short-term relief of congestion in colds and acute sinusitis; preparation for nasendoscopy and procedures (often with lidocaine); epistaxis (on pledgets); before nasal surgery; Eustachian tube symptoms in air travel; testing whether obstruction is mucosal (shrinks) or structural (does not)
Adult dose | Xylometazoline 0.1%, 1 to 2 sprays into each nostril up to three times daily, for no more than 7 days
Child dose | In the UK, not recommended under 6 years; 6 to 11 years xylometazoline 0.05%, 1 spray up to twice daily for up to 5 days
Avoid | Within 2 weeks of an MAOI; after trans-sphenoidal surgery; dry rhinitis; narrow-angle glaucoma; caution in hypertension, heart disease, hyperthyroidism

# How they work | Mechanism and handling.
## Mechanism and rebound
They constrict the capacitance vessels of the turbinates. With repeated use, receptors downregulate and the mucosa congests again as the effect wears off (**rebound**), prompting more use. After about 5 to 10 days this becomes **rhinitis medicamentosa**: persistent congestion, a red, swollen, granular mucosa, and dependence on the spray.

# Using them in ENT | Indications and doses.
## In the clinic and theatre
Applied with lidocaine as a spray or on cotton pledgets, they improve the view and comfort of nasendoscopy and procedures. In theatre, oxymetazoline or adrenaline pledgets reduce bleeding in endoscopic sinus surgery. In **epistaxis**, a pledget with decongestant before cautery is standard first aid.
## Treating rhinitis medicamentosa
Stop the spray (one nostril at a time if needed), cover the withdrawal with a **nasal corticosteroid** for several weeks, and sometimes a short course of oral prednisolone; treat the original cause of congestion.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- Rebound congestion and rhinitis medicamentosa.
- Local stinging and dryness.
- Systemic absorption: hypertension, palpitations and, in infants and young children, CNS depression and bradycardia (hence the age limits).
## Interactions
MAOIs (hypertensive crisis), tricyclics, beta blockers (reflex hypertension with phenylephrine).
