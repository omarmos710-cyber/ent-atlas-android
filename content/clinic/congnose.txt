@stand Newborns breathe through the nose. A baby who turns blue when quiet or feeding and pink when crying has bilateral choanal atresia until proven otherwise, and needs an oral airway now. A midline mass on a child's nose may connect to the brain: it is imaged, never biopsied or probed, before a plan is made.

## Why it matters
Obligate nasal breathing in the first weeks of life makes bilateral obstruction an emergency; midline masses carry a risk of meningitis and CSF leak.

# Choanal atresia | The closed back door.
## Embryology
Persistence of the *bucconasal membrane* (or abnormal mesoderm) between the nasal cavity and the nasopharynx.
## Types
- One-sided or both; one-sided is commoner and may go unnoticed until adulthood.
- Complete or incomplete.
- Bony (about 90% in Dhingra; modern series show most are mixed bony and membranous) or purely membranous.
## Associations
Associated anomalies are common, especially with bilateral atresia: *CHARGE* syndrome (coloboma, heart defects, choanal atresia, retarded growth and development, genital and ear anomalies), Treacher Collins and Crouzon syndromes.
## Clinical picture
### Bilateral
Respiratory distress at birth: *cyclical cyanosis*, blue at rest or when feeding and pink when crying (the baby breathes through the mouth while crying), with difficulty feeding.
### Unilateral
One-sided nasal obstruction and persistent thick discharge from that side, often discovered in later childhood.
## Diagnosis
- Mucoid discharge without air bubbles.
- Failure to pass a soft catheter (a size 6 to 8 French) more than about 3 cm into the nose.
- Methylene blue in the nose fails to reach the pharynx; a wisp of cotton under the nostril does not move.
- Flexible nasal endoscopy.
- **CT** in the axial plane is the definitive test: it shows the atretic plate, narrowing of the posterior nasal cavity and thickening of the vomer, and separates bony from membranous atresia.
!imaging choanal-atresia piriform-aperture-malformation | Choanal atresia and pyriform aperture stenosis on CT.
## Management
### Emergency airway in bilateral atresia
An oral airway, or a feeding nipple with the end cut off taped in the mouth (*McGovern's technique*), keeps the mouth open and avoids tracheostomy; orogastric feeding. Intubation if needed.
!ward Newborn with cyclical cyanosis
1. Insert an oral airway or McGovern nipple.
2. Try to pass a catheter through each side of the nose.
3. Arrange CT of the nasal cavity after suction and decongestion.
4. Look for CHARGE anomalies: heart, eyes, ears, genitalia.
!!
### Definitive repair
- **Endoscopic transnasal repair** with a drill: removal of the atretic plate and the posterior part of the vomer, with or without mucosal flaps and stents. The standard approach now.
- **Transpalatal repair:** better exposure, historically at about 18 months, but risks palatal growth disturbance.
- Unilateral atresia can be repaired electively in childhood.
### After repair
Restenosis is common, so regular review, dilatation and revision may be needed.
!ops
## Pyriform aperture stenosis
Narrowing of the bony nasal entrance from overgrowth of the nasal processes of the maxilla, presenting like bilateral choanal atresia but at the front of the nose. A single central maxillary incisor suggests holoprosencephaly and pituitary problems. Mild cases are managed medically; severe cases by drilling through a sublabial approach.

# Congenital midline nasal masses | Dermoid, glioma and encephalocele.
## Embryology
In early development a projection of dura passes through the *foramen caecum* (between the frontal and ethmoid bones) into the prenasal space, or between the frontal and nasal bones through the *fonticulus frontalis*. Normally it regresses. If it does not, ectodermal elements (a dermoid), isolated glial tissue (a glioma) or a herniation of brain and meninges (an encephalocele) remain, sometimes with an intracranial connection. Incidence is about 1 in 20,000 to 40,000 births.
## Nasal dermoid
### Picture
The commonest midline nasal mass: a firm, non-tender, non-pulsatile, non-compressible swelling anywhere from the glabella to the columella, often with a *midline pit* that may sprout a hair. It may become infected repeatedly.
### Types
- Simple dermoid beneath the skin, in front of the nasal bones, without an opening.
- Dermoid with a sinus, whose tract runs under the nasal bones to the upper septum.
- Dermoid with a tract through the foramen caecum or cribriform plate to the dura, or intradural extension, which risks meningitis.
!fig d25-3 | Types of nasal dermoid: (A) simple, (B) with an external pit, lying deep to the nasal bones, (C) with a connection to the dura, (D) intradural.
### Treatment
Complete excision of the cyst, the tract and any involved bone, cartilage and septum, often by an open rhinoplasty approach; combined neurosurgical repair when the tract reaches the dura.
## Nasal glioma
### Picture
A mass of ectopic glial tissue, not a true tumour, sometimes called a nasal glial heterotopia. About 60% are extranasal (firm, reddish swelling on the bridge or side of the nose with telangiectasias), 30% intranasal (a pale, firm polypoid mass, mistaken for a polyp) and 10% both. About 15 to 20% have a fibrous stalk to the dura. Non-pulsatile and non-compressible.
!fig d25-4a | Extranasal glioma on the right side of a child's nose.
### Treatment
Complete excision by an external approach; craniotomy if there is an intracranial connection.
## Encephalocele
### Picture
A herniation of meninges (meningocele), or meninges with brain (meningoencephalocele), through a skull base defect, communicating with the subarachnoid space. It is soft, bluish, *pulsatile* and *compressible*, enlarges on crying, straining or jugular compression (*Furstenberg sign*), and may transilluminate.
### Types by site
- **Sincipital (frontoethmoid), presenting outside the nose:** nasofrontal (glabella), nasoethmoidal (side of the nose) and naso-orbital (medial orbit).
- **Basal, presenting inside the nose or nasopharynx:** transethmoidal, sphenoethmoidal, transsphenoidal and sphenomaxillary, often mistaken for a polyp.
!imaging meningoencephalocele | Meningoencephalocele through the skull base.
### Treatment
Neurosurgical or endoscopic: dividing the stalk, excising the extracranial part and repairing the dural and bony defect.
!flag Image before you touch
A midline nasal mass in a child may connect with the brain. Biopsy or simple removal can cause CSF leak and meningitis. Obtain MRI (with CT for the bone) first.
!!
!table Telling the midline masses apart
 | Dermoid | Glioma | Encephalocele
Consistency | Firm | Firm | Soft
Pulsatile or compressible | No | No | Yes
Furstenberg sign | Negative | Negative | Positive
Pit or hair | Often | No | No
Intracranial connection | Sometimes | About 15 to 20%, fibrous stalk | Always, with CSF

# Other congenital anomalies | Less common.
## Nasolacrimal duct cyst
A cystic dilatation of the lower nasolacrimal duct when Hasner's valve fails to open, seen as a bluish swelling under the inferior turbinate; bilateral cysts can obstruct a newborn's breathing. Endoscopic marsupialisation.
!anat s101_33 | The lacrimal drainage system and Hasner's valve in the inferior meatus.
## Haemangioma
A strawberry swelling of the nasal tip that grows in the first year and regresses over several years; propranolol for problem lesions.
## Rare anomalies
Arhinia (absent nose), proboscis lateralis, bifid nose and congenital atresia of the nares.
