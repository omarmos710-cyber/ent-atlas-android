@stand Cidofovir is a nucleotide analogue antiviral injected into papillomas at endoscopic debulking as an adjuvant treatment for aggressive recurrent respiratory papillomatosis. Its use has fallen with growing experience of systemic bevacizumab and HPV vaccination, and with concerns about dysplasia and systemic toxicity.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Cidofovir
 | Summary
Class | Acyclic nucleotide analogue of cytosine
How it works | Inhibits viral DNA polymerase after cellular phosphorylation (no viral kinase needed); in HPV-infected tissue, induces apoptosis
ENT uses | Adjuvant intralesional treatment of recurrent respiratory papillomatosis needing frequent surgery (for example more than four procedures a year), distal spread, or rapid regrowth
Dose | Intralesional injection of 5 to 7.5 mg/ml at the base of papillomas after debulking, at intervals of weeks, with a maximum per session related to body weight (often around 3 mg/kg)
Avoid | Renal impairment; pregnancy
!!

# Using it in ENT | Indications.
## Adjuvant therapy in papillomatosis
The mainstay is surgical debulking with microdebrider or laser while preserving normal tissue. Adjuvants are considered for severe disease: intralesional cidofovir, systemic **bevacizumab**, and the **HPV vaccine** (Gardasil), which may lengthen intervals between surgeries. Evidence from trials of cidofovir is mixed.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Systemically **nephrotoxic** (proximal tubule) and neutropenic; with intralesional use, systemic toxicity is uncommon but renal function and blood counts are checked. Concern that it may promote **dysplasia** has led to cautious use, with biopsies before each treatment.
