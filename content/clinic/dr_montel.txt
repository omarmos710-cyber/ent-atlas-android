@stand Montelukast blocks the cysteinyl leukotriene receptor. It is a modest treatment for allergic rhinitis, best suited to patients who also have asthma, and it has a role in aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease and, as an alternative to surgery, in children with mild obstructive sleep-disordered breathing. It is a once-daily tablet with an important warning: neuropsychiatric reactions, including in children.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary

# How it works | Mechanism and handling.
## Mechanism
Cysteinyl leukotrienes (LTC4, LTD4, LTE4) are released by mast cells and eosinophils. In **aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease**, inhibition of cyclo-oxygenase shunts arachidonic acid into the lipoxygenase pathway and floods the airways with leukotrienes, which is why leukotriene modifiers help these patients.

# Using it in ENT | Indications.
## Allergic rhinitis
Less effective than nasal steroids and similar to oral antihistamines; most useful when asthma coexists.
## Aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease
Asthma, nasal polyps and respiratory reactions to aspirin and NSAIDs: montelukast helps nasal and asthma symptoms alongside nasal steroids, surgery and, in selected patients, aspirin desensitisation or biologics.
## Childhood sleep-disordered breathing
Montelukast with a nasal steroid for 12 weeks reduces the apnoea-hypopnoea index in children with mild sleep apnoea and adenotonsillar hypertrophy; it is an option when surgery is not wanted or not yet possible.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- **Neuropsychiatric reactions**: sleep disturbance, nightmares, agitation, depression and, rarely, suicidal thoughts. Regulators (MHRA, FDA) require warnings; tell families and stop the drug if they occur.
- Headache, abdominal pain; rarely eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis is unmasked as steroids are reduced.
