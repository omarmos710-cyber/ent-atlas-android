@stand Most otitis externa is a painful, swollen canal that needs cleaning and the right drops, not oral antibiotics. The exception changes everything: an older diabetic with severe night pain and granulation in the canal has skull base osteomyelitis until proven otherwise.

# Overview | Infection of the ear canal skin.
## Definition
Inflammation of the skin of the external auditory canal, bacterial, fungal or reactive. The canal is a blind, warm, dark tube lined by thin skin over bone and cartilage, and anything that breaks its defences (moisture, scratching, loss of wax) lets infection in.
## Forms
!tree Otitis externa
Bacterial | Acute diffuse otitis externa | furuncle (localised)
Fungal | Otomycosis | Aspergillus, Candida
Reactive | Eczematous, seborrhoeic | contact dermatitis from drops or moulds, neurodermatitis
Chronic | Chronic otitis externa | stenosis of the canal
Invasive | Necrotising (malignant) otitis externa | skull base osteomyelitis
!!
## Applied anatomy
The outer third of the canal is cartilaginous, with hair follicles and sebaceous and ceruminous glands; the inner two-thirds is bony with thin, gland-free skin. Infection spreads from the canal through the *fissures of Santorini* in the cartilage and the tympanomastoid suture to the parotid, the skull base and the temporomandibular joint.

!anat s33_35 | Fissures of Santorini and the foramen of Huschke, routes from the canal to the parotid and skull base.

# Acute diffuse otitis externa | Swimmer's ear.
## Causes
Moisture (swimming, humid climates), trauma from cotton buds and fingernails, hearing aids and earbuds, eczema and loss of protective wax. *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Staphylococcus aureus* are the usual organisms.
## Clinical picture
### Symptoms
Pain that develops over a day or two, itching, a feeling of blockage and a scanty discharge.
### Signs
Pain on moving the tragus or pinna; a red, swollen canal with debris; tender nodes in front of or behind the ear. When visible, the drum moves normally on pneumatic otoscopy, unlike otitis media.
## Management
### Aural toilet
Clean the canal under vision by suction or dry mopping. Debris collects in the anteroinferior recess, so look there.
!ops
### Wick
If the canal is swollen shut, insert an expanding wick soaked in antibiotic-steroid drops, or in an astringent such as aluminium acetate 8% or silver nitrate 3%.
!differ Changing the wick
Dhingra: Daily for two to three days.
Lee: Every three to five days.
!!
### Drops
Antibiotic-steroid drops for a week to ten days. Acidifying with dilute vinegar helps mild cases and prevention.
### Oral antibiotics
Not needed, unless there is spreading cellulitis or the patient is diabetic or immunocompromised.
### Keep it dry
No swimming until healed; cotton wool with petroleum jelly when bathing.
!pearl When drops fail
Think of contact allergy to neomycin, fungal superinfection, or an underlying necrotising infection.
!!

# Furuncle | A boil in the canal.
## Picture
A staphylococcal infection of a hair follicle, so it occurs only in the hair-bearing outer cartilaginous canal. Pain is out of proportion to its size and worse on chewing; it can swell the postauricular groove and mimic mastoiditis.
## Distinguishing from mastoiditis
The drum is normal, there is no preceding otitis media and no mucoid discharge, and pinna movement is painful.
## Treatment
Analgesia, oral antistaphylococcal antibiotics, heat, and an ichthammol-glycerine pack; incise when it points. Recurrent furuncles call for testing for diabetes and treating nasal staphylococcal carriage.

# Otomycosis | Itch and spores.
## Organisms
*Aspergillus niger* (black-headed spores), *Aspergillus fumigatus* (pale blue or green) and *Candida* (creamy white). Repeated antibiotic drops, humidity and swimming predispose.
## Picture
Intense itching, discomfort, blockage and a watery discharge with a musty smell. The canal contains a mass like *wet newspaper*, with black, grey or white spores.
!fig d8-11 | Endoscopic view of otomycosis with yellow (A) and black (B) fungal spores.
## Treatment
Meticulous cleaning, then a topical antifungal, continued for a week beyond apparent cure: clotrimazole for most, nystatin for *Candida*. Salicylic acid in alcohol and povidone iodine are alternatives.

# Chronic and reactive otitis externa | Itch, scaling and narrowing.
## Chronic otitis externa
Long-standing itch and irritation with thick, dry, scaling skin, which may narrow the canal. Treat underlying eczema or seborrhoeic dermatitis, use steroid drops or ointment, and stop the patient scratching.
## Reactive forms
Eczematous otitis externa from drops or hearing-aid moulds, seborrhoeic dermatitis with scalp involvement, and neurodermatitis from habitual scratching.
## Otitis externa haemorrhagica
Blood blisters on the deep canal and drum, often with influenza.
## Chronic stenosing otitis externa
Fibrous narrowing of the deep canal. Treated by excising the thick skin, widening the bony canal and grafting.

# Necrotising otitis externa | The dangerous one.
## What it is
An invasive *Pseudomonas* infection (about 90% of cases) that spreads from the canal to the bone of the skull base: a skull base osteomyelitis. It affects older diabetics and the immunocompromised. *Aspergillus* causes a minority.
## Clinical picture
### Symptoms
Severe, deep, unrelenting pain, worse at night and out of proportion to what is seen, with a persistent discharge despite treatment.
### Signs
Granulation tissue on the floor of the canal at the bony-cartilaginous junction. Cranial nerve palsies mark spread: the facial nerve first at the stylomastoid foramen, then the ninth, tenth and eleventh at the jugular foramen, and the twelfth. Multiple palsies carry a worse prognosis.
## Investigations
!table Which scan?
Test | Use | Limitation
Technetium-99 bone scan | Confirms early bone involvement | Stays positive for months
Gallium or indium scan | Follows response; repeated every few weeks | Less anatomical detail
CT | Bone erosion | Changes appear late
MRI | Soft tissue, marrow and dura | Not a guide to cure
!!
Add ESR or CRP (useful to follow), glucose, culture, and biopsy of the granulation to exclude carcinoma.
!differ Monitoring and duration
Dhingra: Gallium repeated every three weeks; treatment for six to eight weeks, including intravenous aminoglycoside combinations.
Lee: Gallium every four weeks; oral fluoroquinolone for about six weeks as first line.
!!
!imaging | Necrotising otitis externa, canal stenosis and canal carcinoma.
## Treatment
Admit; control the diabetes; regular cleaning; high-dose oral ciprofloxacin, or intravenous anti-pseudomonal antibiotics for advanced disease; voriconazole for *Aspergillus*. Continue until the examination, inflammatory markers and scans have normalised. Surgery is limited to biopsy and removal of sequestra or abscesses. Hyperbaric oxygen is unproven.
## Outcome
Mortality remains around 5 to 20%, from sepsis, sinus thrombosis and cranial neuropathies. Quinolone resistance is rising.
!flag Suspect it when
- A diabetic or immunocompromised patient has severe pain and granulation in the canal.
- Otitis externa fails to respond after a week of proper treatment.
- A cranial nerve palsy appears with ear pain.
!!
!update Necrotising otitis externa: current practice (Oxford Textbook)
Infection spreads from the canal floor through the **fissures of Santorini** into the infratemporal fossa and skull base. Between 65 and 100% of patients are diabetic, but the classic "elderly diabetic with a painful discharging ear" is not always present, especially in other forms of immunosuppression. *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* is the usual organism, about a quarter of isolates are resistant, and fungi are cultured in over 20% of cases, more often in HIV with low CD4 counts and with a worse prognosis. Surface swabs may grow nothing, so send both a swab and a **granulation biopsy** for bacterial and fungal culture and for histology, because squamous carcinoma of the temporal bone is the key differential; if there is no tissue in the canal, deep samples may be taken at cortical mastoidectomy.

**Imaging.** CT shows erosion only after about 30% demineralisation, so a normal early CT is falsely reassuring. Contrast MRI with fat suppression and diffusion shows soft tissue and marrow disease earlier and detects intracranial spread. Gallium and technetium scans have largely been replaced by MRI, and **FDG PET-CT** is increasingly used to diagnose and to monitor response. CT and MRI changes persist for up to a year after successful treatment, so they cannot be used to decide when to stop.

**Treatment.** Admit for multidisciplinary care (otology, diabetes, microbiology, radiology, elderly care), start empirical intravenous antipseudomonal therapy at once, and give **dual therapy**, typically piperacillin–tazobactam or ceftazidime with a fluoroquinolone, adjusted to cultures. There is no agreed guideline, but consensus favours at least **six weeks** of intravenous treatment, often completed at home, guided by clinical response, inflammatory markers and imaging. Surgery is now limited to biopsy, deep sampling and drainage of abscesses; debridement and nerve decompression have no proven benefit.
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## On call
!ward A painful, swollen ear canal
1. Clean under vision; insert a wick if the canal is closed.
2. Antibiotic-steroid drops; analgesia; keep dry.
3. Check glucose in diabetics and anyone with recurrent infection.
4. Diabetic or immunocompromised with severe pain and granulation: biopsy, ESR, imaging and admit.
!!
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Tragal tenderness** and a mobile drum versus otitis media.
- **Furuncle** in the cartilaginous canal only.
- **Otomycosis:** organisms and appearance.
- **Necrotising otitis externa:** who, organism, signs, scans and treatment.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Prescribing oral antibiotics instead of cleaning and drops.
- Missing a necrotising infection in a diabetic.
- Neomycin contact allergy mistaken for treatment failure.
- Drops into a canal full of debris.
!!
