@stand Postnasal drip, or catarrh, is the feeling of mucus running down the back of the nose and throat, with the urge to clear the throat and sometimes a cough. It is one of the most common and most frustrating complaints in rhinology, because the sensation often outlasts any visible mucus. The approach is to find and treat what can be treated, the nose, the sinuses and reflux, to recognise the patients whose problem is a hypersensitive throat rather than excess mucus, and to make sure that nothing serious in the nasopharynx, larynx or chest has been missed.

# What the patient means | Mucus, sensation or cough.
## Definition and mechanism
Normally, a litre or so of mucus a day is carried backwards by the cilia and swallowed without being noticed. A sensation of postnasal drip arises when the volume rises, when the mucus becomes thicker or more irritant, when mucociliary clearance slows, or when the sensory nerves of the pharynx become hypersensitive. Many patients have more than one of these. In chest medicine, a cough attributed to the nose is called *upper airway cough syndrome*, but many patients with postnasal drip have no cough, and many with chronic cough have a sensitised cough reflex rather than a nasal cause.
!flag Do not miss
- **Unilateral nasal symptoms, blood-stained mucus or a neck lump:** nasopharyngeal and sinonasal tumour.
- **Hoarseness, dysphagia, weight loss or pain on swallowing:** laryngeal or pharyngeal malignancy.
- **A smoker with a cough that has changed, haemoptysis or breathlessness:** chest X-ray and respiratory referral.
- **Persistent cough in a patient taking an ACE inhibitor:** the drug.
!!

# History | Four sources to explore.
## The questions
Ask what exactly is felt: mucus that can be spat out, or a sensation that nothing relieves; and whether the mucus is clear, thick or discoloured. Ask about the nose, blockage, discharge, sneezing, smell loss and facial pressure, which point to rhinitis or rhinosinusitis. Ask about heartburn, a sour taste, hoarseness worse in the morning, globus and throat clearing, which point to reflux. Ask about cough, its duration (chronic cough lasts eight weeks or more), smoking, asthma and medication, including ACE inhibitors and drugs that dry the mucosa (antidepressants, anxiolytics, anticholinergics). The *Reflux Symptom Index* and *SNOT-22* quantify the symptoms.

# Examination | Look at both ends.
## Nose, nasopharynx and larynx
Examine the nose with an endoscope, looking for mucopurulent discharge in the middle meatus or sphenoethmoidal recess, polyps, adenoidal tissue, a Thornwaldt cyst and any nasopharyngeal mass. Pass the scope down to the larynx and look for signs associated with reflux (posterior commissure thickening, oedema, erythema) and for any lesion that explains the throat symptoms. Examine the neck. A CT of the sinuses is arranged only if chronic rhinosinusitis is suspected and not settling on medical treatment.

# Management | Treat what is found.
## When a cause is found
**Rhinitis** is treated with saline irrigation and a steroid spray, with an antihistamine for allergy and ipratropium for watery discharge. **Chronic rhinosinusitis** follows its own pathway: saline, steroids, a long course of a macrolide in selected patients, and surgery if medical treatment fails. **Reflux** symptoms are managed with diet and lifestyle changes, alginates and, for typical acid symptoms, a proton pump inhibitor trial; evidence for proton pump inhibitors in throat symptoms alone is weak. Any drying or causative medication is reviewed with the prescriber.
## When the sensation persists
Many patients have a normal nose and larynx and still feel the drip. Explanation, reassurance that nothing serious has been found, adequate hydration and steam inhalation help. Throat clearing perpetuates the irritation, so **speech and language therapy** for laryngeal hygiene and cough suppression techniques is effective. In refractory chronic cough, a neuromodulator such as low-dose amitriptyline or **gabapentin** may be tried under specialist care.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **The mechanisms:** volume, viscosity, clearance, hypersensitivity.
- **Upper airway cough syndrome** and cough hypersensitivity.
- **The red flags** that need endoscopy and imaging.
- **Treating the nose, the sinuses and reflux;** speech therapy when nothing is found.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Repeating antibiotics for "catarrh" with no evidence of infection.
- Missing the ACE inhibitor in a patient with chronic cough.
- Operating on the sinuses for postnasal drip alone when the CT and endoscopy are normal.
!!
