@stand Myringitis is inflammation of the drum itself. In the bullous form, blood-filled blisters on the drum cause sudden, intense pain that settles in a few days. In the granular form, the drum's skin is replaced by wet granulation tissue that discharges without pain, and the middle ear behind it is healthy.

# Summary | Disease of the drum, not the middle ear.
Myringitis is inflammation confined to the tympanic membrane, sometimes with the adjacent deep canal skin, without primary disease of the middle ear or mastoid. It takes two forms, each in its own chapter:
- [[@Bullous myringitis|Acute bullous myringitis]]: blebs or bullae on the outer surface of the drum and the deep canal. The same picture is also described as *otitis externa haemorrhagica* ([[t:oe]]).
- [[@Granular myringitis|Chronic granular myringitis]]: the epithelium of the drum is replaced by granulation tissue, without a perforation.
!anat s37_43 | Parts of the tympanic membrane.

# Differential diagnosis | Other painful or wet drums.
- [[t:om|Acute otitis media]]: bulging drum with poor mobility.
- [[t:csom|Chronic otitis media]]: a perforation and middle ear disease.
- [[t:perich|Herpes zoster oticus]]: vesicles on the concha and canal, facial palsy.
- [[t:oe|Otitis externa]]: canal swelling and tragal tenderness.

# Bullous myringitis {dz} | Blisters and pain.
## Etiology
It follows an upper respiratory infection. The organisms are those of acute otitis media: *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae* and *Moraxella*. *Mycoplasma* is the classic association, but it has not been proven. It is now regarded as a variant of acute otitis media: the same organisms (*Streptococcus pneumoniae* first) are found in the middle ear fluid.
!differ Mycoplasma
Dhingra: Lists it as a possible cause.
Lee: The link is unproven.
In practice: Treat it as acute otitis media; *Mycoplasma* is rarely responsible.
!!
## Clinical features
Sudden, intense ear pain for three or four days that then settles, sometimes with a little bloody discharge when the blebs burst. Haemorrhagic bullae sit on the drum and deep canal. A third to two-fifths have a middle ear effusion as well.
## Diagnosis
Always check hearing: a sensorineural or mixed loss, often in the high frequencies, is common.
## Management
Analgesia; lancing the blebs relieves pain; keep the ear dry; topical drops to prevent secondary infection; treat acute otitis media if present, with an antibiotic when indicated.
!update Current evidence
**Bullous myringitis** is now regarded as a variant of acute otitis media, and *Mycoplasma* is rarely responsible. Treat it as acute otitis media, with analgesia and an antibiotic when indicated. It causes a sensorineural hearing loss in a minority, which usually recovers (Drendel et al., *Auris Nasus Larynx* 2012); evidence for steroids in that setting is limited to small series.
!!
### Drugs and doses
!table Bullous myringitis: drugs and doses
Step | Drug and dose | Course and notes
Every patient | Paracetamol or ibuprofen at full dose | Pain peaks in the first days
Antibiotic when acute otitis media criteria are met | Amoxicillin: 1 to 11 months 125 mg, 1 to 4 years 250 mg, 5 to 17 years and adults 500 mg, three times a day | 5 to 7 days (NICE); AAP high dose 80 to 90 mg/kg/day in two doses for children
Penicillin allergy | Clarithromycin (adults 250 to 500 mg twice a day; children by weight) | 5 to 7 days
Pain, drum intact | Phenazone–lidocaine drops, 4 drops two or three times a day | Up to 7 days; not with a perforation
Sensorineural loss | Audiogram; steroids as for sudden hearing loss on specialist advice | Usually recovers
!!
Doses from NICE NG91 (otitis media), the AAP guideline (Lieberthal et al., *Pediatrics* 2013) and the BNF.
## Complications
Sensorineural hearing loss, in a minority, which usually recovers.

# Granular myringitis {dz} | A wet, painless drum.
## Etiology
Usually *Staphylococcus*, *Pseudomonas* or *Proteus*.
!differ Predisposing factors
Dhingra: Links it to wax, foreign bodies and external ear infection.
Lee: No predisposing factor has been identified.
In practice: Look for wax, a foreign body or canal infection and treat any you find.
!!
## Clinical features
Itching, fullness and a foul, painless discharge. Granulation tissue covers part of the drum, often posterosuperiorly; the drum may perforate and heal repeatedly. Hearing is normal or slightly reduced.
## Diagnosis
Clinical, under the microscope. CT only if chronic otitis media or cholesteatoma has to be excluded.
### Investigations: what and when
!table Granular myringitis: investigations
Role | Test | When and what it shows
Diagnosis | Microscopy after cleaning | Granulation on the drum, no middle-ear disease
Every patient | Swab for culture | *Pseudomonas*, *Staphylococcus*, *Proteus*; fungi
Every patient | Audiogram | Normal or mild conductive loss
Chronic otitis media or cholesteatoma not excluded | High-resolution CT | Normal aerated middle ear and mastoid
Persistent granulation, especially in the elderly or diabetic | Biopsy; CRP or ESR | Carcinoma; necrotising otitis externa
!!
## Management
- **First:** regular cleaning, dilute vinegar irrigation and topical antibiotic–steroid drops.
- **Resistant cases:** cauterising the granulation with silver nitrate or trichloroacetic acid, or laser; removal of the granulation.
- **Severe or recurrent cases:** tympanoplasty with canalplasty. Recurrence is common after surgery.
!update Current evidence
**Granular myringitis** is treated with careful debridement and topical antibiotic–steroid drops, with cautery or laser for resistant cases; evidence is from case series only.
!!
### Drugs and doses
!table Granular myringitis: drugs and doses
Step | Drug and dose | Course and notes
First line | Microsuction; topical quinolone–steroid drops (ciprofloxacin–dexamethasone 4 drops twice a day) or neomycin–steroid drops if the drum is intact | 7 to 14 days, repeated with cleaning
Adjunct | Dilute acetic acid (vinegar) irrigation or 2% acetic acid spray | 
Resistant granulation | Silver nitrate or trichloroacetic acid cautery, or laser | Under the microscope
Recurrent | Tympanoplasty with canalplasty | Recurrence common
!!
Doses from product licences and the BNF; evidence for treatment is from case series.
## Complications
Late fibrosis can blunt the anterior angle between the drum and canal wall, causing a conductive loss.
