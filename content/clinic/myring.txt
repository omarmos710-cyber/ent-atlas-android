@stand Myringitis is inflammation of the drum itself. In the bullous form, blood-filled blisters on the drum cause sudden, intense pain that settles in a few days. In the granular form, the drum's skin is replaced by wet granulation tissue that discharges without pain, and the middle ear behind it is healthy.

# Overview | Disease of the drum, not the middle ear.
## Definition
Inflammation confined to the tympanic membrane, sometimes with the adjacent deep canal skin, without primary disease of the middle ear or mastoid.
!anat s37_43 | Parts of the tympanic membrane.
## Forms
### Acute bullous myringitis
Blebs or bullae on the outer surface of the drum and the deep canal. Dhingra describes the same picture as *otitis externa haemorrhagica*.
### Chronic granular myringitis
The epithelium of the drum is replaced by granulation tissue, without a perforation.

# Bullous myringitis | Blisters and pain.
## Cause
It follows an upper respiratory infection. The organisms are those of acute otitis media: *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae* and *Moraxella*. *Mycoplasma* is the classic association, but it has not been proven.

## Clinical picture
Sudden, intense ear pain for three or four days that then settles, sometimes with a little bloody discharge when the blebs burst. Haemorrhagic bullae sit on the drum and deep canal. A third to two-fifths have a middle ear effusion as well.
## Hearing
A sensorineural or mixed loss, often in the high frequencies, is common and usually recovers. Always check hearing.
## Treatment
Analgesia; lancing the blebs relieves pain; keep the ear dry; topical drops to prevent secondary infection; treat acute otitis media if present.

# Granular myringitis | A wet, painless drum.
## Cause
Usually *Staphylococcus*, *Pseudomonas* or *Proteus*.
!differ Predisposing factors
Dhingra: Links it to wax, foreign bodies and external ear infection.
Lee: No predisposing factor has been identified.
!!
## Clinical picture
Itching, fullness and a foul, painless discharge. Granulation tissue covers part of the drum, often posterosuperiorly; the drum may perforate and heal repeatedly. Hearing is normal or slightly reduced.
## Diagnosis
Clinical, under the microscope. CT only if chronic otitis media or cholesteatoma has to be excluded.
## Treatment
Regular cleaning, dilute vinegar irrigation and topical drops; cauterising the granulation with silver nitrate or trichloroacetic acid, or laser; removal of the granulation. Severe or recurrent cases need tympanoplasty with canalplasty. Recurrence is common after surgery.
## Complication
Late fibrosis can blunt the anterior angle between the drum and canal wall, causing a conductive loss.

# Differential diagnosis | Other painful or wet drums.
- **Acute otitis media:** bulging drum with poor mobility.
- **Chronic otitis media:** a perforation and middle ear disease.
- **Herpes zoster oticus:** vesicles on the concha and canal, facial palsy.
- **Otitis externa:** canal swelling and tragal tenderness.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Not checking hearing in bullous myringitis.
- Calling granular myringitis chronic otitis media without examining under the microscope.
