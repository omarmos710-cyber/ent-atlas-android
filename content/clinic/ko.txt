@stand A hard plug of keratin in the deep canal can be one of two conditions. In keratosis obturans the plug fills the canal all round, presses on it and widens it, painfully. In canal cholesteatoma the problem is a focal patch of eroded, dead bone on the canal floor. Both come from skin that has stopped migrating outwards.

# Overview | When the canal stops cleaning itself.
## Normal self-cleaning
The skin of the drum and deep canal migrates outwards, carrying keratin and wax to the entrance of the canal. When this migration fails, keratin builds up in the deep canal.
## Two conditions
!table Keratosis obturans or canal cholesteatoma?
 | Keratosis obturans | Canal cholesteatoma
Keratin | Plug filling the whole canal | Focal, over an area of eroded bone
Bone | Widened all round, smooth | Focal erosion and necrosis, usually posteroinferior
Pain | Acute, severe | Dull, chronic, or none
Hearing | Conductive loss | Usually normal
Sides | Often both | Usually one
Age | Younger | Older
Associations | Bronchiectasis, chronic sinusitis | Previous surgery, trauma, radiation
!!

# Keratosis obturans | The circumferential plug.
## Cause
Failure of outward migration, possibly from inflammation of the canal skin. In young people it is associated with bronchiectasis and chronic sinusitis, which should be looked for.
## Clinical picture
Acute, severe pain, conductive hearing loss and sometimes otorrhoea. A dense, white, laminated keratin plug fills the deep canal; once removed, the canal is widened and the drum may be thickened.
!differ Age and pain
Dhingra: Typically 5 to 20 years; pain is among the presenting symptoms.
Lee: Typically 30 to 60 years; severe pain is characteristic.
!!
## Treatment
Removal of the plug under the microscope, which may need a general anaesthetic in children, treatment of any secondary otitis externa, and regular cleaning. Salicylic acid 2% in alcohol reduces reaccumulation. Follow-up is lifelong.
## Complication
Progressive widening of the canal can erode into the mastoid (an *auto-mastoidectomy*) and expose the facial nerve.

# Canal cholesteatoma | Focal bone erosion.
## Cause
Spontaneous in older patients, or after surgery, trauma, radiation or canal stenosis.
## Clinical picture
A dull, chronic ache and a foul, scanty discharge, with normal hearing. A localised ulcer on the floor or posterior wall of the bony canal, with keratin debris and exposed, necrotic bone.
## Investigations
CT shows the extent of the erosion and its relation to the mastoid and facial nerve. Biopsy granulation tissue to exclude carcinoma.
!imaging | Canal cholesteatoma with canal atresia.
## Treatment
Regular debridement and topical drops; when the disease keeps returning, surgery removes the matrix and dead bone and covers the defect with fascia or a skin graft.
!ops

# Differential diagnosis | What else causes a canal plug or erosion.
- Impacted wax.
- Necrotising otitis externa.
- Squamous cell carcinoma of the canal.
- Middle ear cholesteatoma eroding the canal wall.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Failure of epithelial migration** as the cause.
- **The comparison table:** circumferential versus focal.
- **Association with bronchiectasis and sinusitis.**
- **Biopsy** to exclude carcinoma.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Syringing a keratin plug repeatedly instead of removing it under the microscope.
- Missing carcinoma in canal granulation.
!!
