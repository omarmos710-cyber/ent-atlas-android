@stand Tuberculous otitis media hides in plain sight as an ordinary chronic ear that simply will not get better. The clues are a painless, thin discharge, several holes in a thickened drum, pale granulation, and a hearing loss worse than the symptoms suggest. Send the tissue to the laboratory, because the treatment is medical, not surgical.

# Overview | The chronic ear that refuses to heal.
## Definition
Infection of the middle ear cleft by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. Syphilitic otitis media, much rarer, is included here because it shares the picture of bone destruction and a disproportionate hearing loss.
## Epidemiology
Uncommon in low-burden countries, but important where tuberculosis is endemic, and rising with drug-resistant strains and immunosuppression, particularly HIV.
## Routes of infection
- Through the Eustachian tube, from active pulmonary disease, which most patients have.
- Through the blood, from a distant focus.
- In infants, through the tube from infected milk.

# Clinical features | Painless, thin, and resistant.
## Symptoms
- **Discharge:** painless, thin and cloudy, persisting despite treatment.
- **Hearing loss:** out of proportion to the symptoms, often mixed, from early ossicular and cochlear involvement.
## Signs
- A thickened drum early on, then *multiple perforations* that merge into one large defect.
- Pale, thick mucosa and abundant pale granulation.
- Bone sequestra in the mastoid.
- Non-tender enlarged cervical and preauricular nodes.
- Facial palsy in about 10%, sometimes the presenting feature in a child.
## Complications
Facial palsy, sequestrum, postauricular fistula, Bezold abscess and labyrinthitis.
!pearl Suspect it when
A chronic ear fails ordinary treatment, a mastoidectomy fails, granulation is pale, or facial palsy appears early in a painless ear.
!!

# Investigations | Prove it.
## From the ear
- **PCR of the discharge or tissue:** gives an answer within days.
- **Histology:** caseating granulomas with Langhans giant cells.
- **Acid-fast smear and culture:** a negative smear does not exclude it.
## General
Chest imaging, tuberculin skin test or interferon-gamma release assay, and HIV testing.
## CT
Often looks like ordinary chronic otitis media, with more bone erosion and sequestra.
## Differential diagnosis
Ordinary chronic suppurative otitis media, carcinoma of the middle ear, granulomatosis with polyangiitis, and fungal infection.

# Management | Drugs first, repair later.
## Antituberculous chemotherapy
Standard multidrug therapy with isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide and ethambutol, guided by sensitivities, for at least six months.
## Surgery
Limited to biopsy, removal of sequestra and drainage of abscesses or complications. Reconstruction is delayed until treatment is complete, because healing is slow and fistulae are common during active disease; afterwards, repair succeeds in most.
## Facial palsy
Usually recovers well with medical treatment.

# Syphilitic otitis | Rare, destructive.
Congenital or acquired syphilis can cause osteitis of the temporal bone with necrosis, and labyrinthitis with profound, often fluctuating sensorineural loss and vertigo. Diagnose with treponemal tests (FTA-ABS) and treat with penicillin and steroids.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **The features:** painless discharge, multiple perforations, pale granulation, early facial palsy, disproportionate hearing loss.
- **Diagnosis** by PCR, histology and culture.
- **Treatment is medical;** surgery for complications only.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Operating on a chronic ear without sending the tissue for tuberculosis.
- Being reassured by a negative acid-fast smear.
- Reconstructing during active treatment.
!!
