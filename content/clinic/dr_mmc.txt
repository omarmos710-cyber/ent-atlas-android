@stand Mitomycin C is an antitumour antibiotic that, applied topically for a few minutes, inhibits fibroblast proliferation. ENT surgeons apply it after endoscopic treatment of laryngotracheal stenosis, choanal atresia repair, dacryocystorhinostomy and frontal sinus surgery to reduce restenosis. The evidence is mixed, and its effect depends on technique.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Mitomycin C
 | Summary
Class | Alkylating antitumour antibiotic
How it works | Cross-links DNA and inhibits fibroblast proliferation and collagen formation
ENT uses | Adjunct after endoscopic incision and dilatation of subglottic and tracheal stenosis; choanal atresia repair; endoscopic dacryocystorhinostomy; frontal sinus and other stenoses; glottic webs
Dose | Topical 0.4 to 0.5 mg/ml on a cottonoid or pledget for 2 to 5 minutes, then irrigation with saline
Handling | Cytotoxic: prepare and dispose of according to cytotoxic protocols; avoid contact with skin and eyes
!!

# Using it in ENT | Practice.
## Airway stenosis
After radial incisions and balloon dilatation of an idiopathic or post-intubation subglottic stenosis, topical mitomycin may lengthen the interval between procedures in some series; randomised evidence is limited. Inhaled steroids, co-trimoxazole in some idiopathic cases, and treatment of reflux are other adjuncts.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Excessive fibrinous debris or granulation that can obstruct a small airway (check endoscopically), delayed healing, and theoretical carcinogenicity; systemic absorption is negligible at topical doses.
