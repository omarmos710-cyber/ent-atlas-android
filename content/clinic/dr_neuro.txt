@stand Neuropathic and facial pain present to ENT surgeons as ear, throat, tongue, face and sinus pain without a structural cause: trigeminal and glossopharyngeal neuralgia, burning mouth syndrome, post-herpetic neuralgia, persistent idiopathic facial pain, and neuropathic pain after neck dissection or other surgery. The drugs are anticonvulsants and antidepressants, used at doses built up slowly.

# At a glance | The drugs on one page.
## Summary
!table Drugs for neuropathic and facial pain
 | Summary
Carbamazepine | First-line for trigeminal and glossopharyngeal neuralgia: 100 mg once or twice daily, increased gradually to the lowest effective dose (commonly 200 mg three or four times daily, maximum 1.6 g a day); oxcarbazepine is an alternative
Gabapentin | 300 mg at night, increasing by 300 mg every few days to 900 to 3,600 mg a day in three doses
Pregabalin | 75 mg twice daily, increasing to 150 to 300 mg twice daily
Amitriptyline | 10 mg at night, increasing to 25 to 75 mg (nortriptyline is better tolerated)
Duloxetine | 60 mg once daily
Burning mouth syndrome | Topical clonazepam (dissolved in the mouth and spat out), alpha-lipoic acid, cognitive behavioural therapy, amitriptyline, gabapentin
ENT uses | Trigeminal and glossopharyngeal neuralgia; burning mouth syndrome; post-herpetic neuralgia (after Ramsay Hunt or trigeminal zoster); persistent idiopathic facial pain; post-surgical neuropathic pain; chronic tension-type headache presenting as "sinus" pain

# Using them in ENT | Practice.
## Neuralgias
**Trigeminal neuralgia** causes electric-shock pains triggered by touch, eating or talking; **glossopharyngeal neuralgia** causes similar pains in the throat, tongue base and ear on swallowing, occasionally with syncope. Carbamazepine helps most; MRI looks for a vascular loop, multiple sclerosis or a tumour; microvascular decompression is considered when drugs fail. Eagle syndrome (elongated styloid) is a differential.
## "Sinus" headache
Most patients referred with facial pain and a normal nasal endoscopy and CT do not have sinusitis; migraine, tension-type headache and midfacial segment pain respond to amitriptyline or migraine treatment, not to sinus surgery.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- **Carbamazepine**: dizziness, ataxia, diplopia, hyponatraemia, leucopenia, raised liver enzymes; **Stevens-Johnson syndrome**, strongly linked to HLA-B*15:02 in patients of Han Chinese, Thai and some other Asian ancestry (test before starting); potent enzyme inducer (reduces contraceptive pill, warfarin and many other drugs); teratogenic.
- **Gabapentinoids**: drowsiness, dizziness, weight gain, misuse and dependence (controlled drugs in the UK), respiratory depression with opioids.
- **Tricyclics**: anticholinergic effects, sedation, postural hypotension, cardiac toxicity in overdose.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- Features of **glossopharyngeal neuralgia** and its treatment.
- **HLA-B*15:02** testing before carbamazepine.
- Facial pain that is not sinusitis.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Sinus surgery for facial pain with a normal CT.
- Starting carbamazepine at full dose.
!!
