# Summary | A first-arch malformation.
- **Microtia:** a small, malformed pinna; *anotia* is its complete absence.
- **Congenital aural atresia:** absence or narrowing of the external canal, usually with a malformed middle ear.
The two usually occur together, on the same side. The inner ear is usually normal, so the loss is conductive. Hearing comes first: a bone-conduction aid by six months in bilateral atresia. The pinna is reconstructed before any canal surgery, and most families now choose bone-conduction devices over canal surgery.

# Epidemiology | Who gets it.
Microtia occurs in roughly 1 in 6,000 to 1 in 12,000 births, more often in boys and on the right. About 10% are bilateral.

# Etiology | Embryology and syndromes.
## Embryology
The pinna develops from the hillocks around the first branchial cleft, the canal from the first branchial groove, and the malleus and incus from the first and second arches. The inner ear develops separately from the otic placode, so it is usually normal. A normal pinna with atresia implies the insult happened later in development, after the pinna had formed.

## Associated syndromes
Treacher Collins, Goldenhar and hemifacial microsomia, Crouzon, and branchio-oto-renal syndrome ([[t:syndr]]).

# Clinical features | Hearing and appearance.
## Hearing
- Atresia: a conductive loss of about 60 to 70 dB.
- Ossicular malformation alone: about 25 to 50 dB.
- Congenital stapes fixation: about 50 to 65 dB.
- Bilateral atresia threatens speech and language development.
## Associated findings
Preauricular tags, pits and sinuses, facial asymmetry, jaw underdevelopment, and syndromic features. Airway problems in some syndromes.

# Diagnosis | Hearing first, imaging before surgery.
## Audiology
OAE and ABR from birth, including bone-conduction ABR, to measure cochlear function on each side.
## Imaging
Thin-section CT of the temporal bones before any surgery, usually around five or six years: the size and aeration of the middle ear and mastoid, the ossicles, the oval and round windows, the inner ear, and the course of the facial nerve. MRI if there is a sensorineural loss.
!imaging | Canal atresia with cholesteatoma, Treacher Collins syndrome, and inner ear and ossicular malformations.
## Other tests
Renal ultrasound when preauricular pits accompany neck fistulae (branchio-oto-renal syndrome).
## Investigations: what and when
!table Canal atresia and microtia: investigations
Role | Test | When and what it shows
From birth | Otoacoustic emissions and ABR, including bone-conduction ABR | Cochlear function on each side; type and degree of loss
Childhood | Behavioural audiometry by age | Confirms thresholds for aid fitting

# Subtypes and variants | Pinna and middle ear.
## Microtia
- **Grade I:** a recognisable but smaller or deformed pinna.
- **Grade II:** a deformed framework with some parts missing.
- **Grade III:** a soft-tissue remnant, the *peanut ear*.
- **Grade IV:** anotia.
!fig d8-3 | Grade III microtia of the right ear (peanut ear).
## Middle ear
- **Class I:** normal pinna with an ossicular defect, the commonest.
- **Class II:** microtia and atresia with an aerated middle ear and mastoid.
- **Class III:** poorly aerated, underdeveloped middle ear and mastoid.
!pearl Pearl
A normal pinna with atresia implies the insult happened later in development, after the pinna had formed.
!!

# Management | Hearing, then the ear.
## Approach
!ward On call: a newborn with bilateral atresia
1. Early audiology with bone-conduction testing.
2. A bone-conduction aid as soon as possible.
3. Look for syndromic features and airway problems.
!!
## Hearing
- **Bilateral atresia:** a bone-conduction aid on a headband within the first months, by six months at the latest.
- **Unilateral atresia with a normal other ear:** speech usually develops normally, but these children have more educational difficulty than once thought. Preferential seating at school and monitoring; early bone-conduction amplification is increasingly offered.
- A bone-anchored implant from around five or six years.
## The pinna
Reconstruction with autologous rib cartilage in stages, once there is enough cartilage, or a porous polyethylene framework, or an osseointegrated prosthesis. The pinna is reconstructed before any canal surgery.
!differ Age for rib cartilage reconstruction
Some texts: About five to eight years.
Others: About eight to ten years.
In practice: Plan rib cartilage reconstruction for about eight to ten years, when there is enough cartilage; some centres start earlier.
!!
## The canal
### Selection
Canal surgery suits ears with a conductive loss over 30 dB, bone conduction better than 20 dB, and a well-aerated middle ear with a favourable facial nerve on CT. The *Jahrsdoerfer score* (10 points from CT and the appearance of the pinna) predicts hearing results: a score of 7 or more gives a good chance of a useful result. The pinna is reconstructed first so the canal can be placed correctly.
### Results
Even in good hands, only about half of patients reach an air–bone gap under 30 dB long term, and canal restenosis and facial nerve risk are real, so **bone-conduction devices** (headband in infancy, then an implant) are now chosen by most families ([[t:rehab]]).
!flag The facial nerve
In atresia the facial nerve often runs more anteriorly and laterally than usual, crossing the area where the canal would be drilled. First branchial cleft anomalies also run close to it.
!!
!ops
!update Current evidence
**Candidacy for atresia repair.** The Jahrsdoerfer score comes from Jahrsdoerfer et al. (*Am J Otol* 1992). Long-term series confirm the modest hearing results of canal surgery, which is why most families now choose bone-conduction devices. **Unilateral atresia.** Educational difficulty in these children is now recognised, and early amplification is increasingly offered.
!!
## Preauricular sinus
Leave it alone unless it becomes infected; then excise the whole tract ([[t:earminor]]).
## Treatment: what and when
!table Canal atresia and microtia: treatment
Age or situation | Treatment | Notes
Bilateral atresia, from the first months (by 6 months) | Bone-conduction aid on a softband | Language development
Unilateral atresia | Monitor; preferential seating; consider a bone-conduction aid | Educational support
From about 5 years | Bone-anchored or active transcutaneous bone-conduction implant | Reliable hearing
Selected ears (Jahrsdoerfer 7 or more of 10) | Canal atresiaplasty, after pinna reconstruction | About half keep a gap under 30 dB; restenosis and facial nerve risks
About 8 to 10 years | Pinna reconstruction with rib cartilage (or porous polyethylene, or prosthesis) | Staged
Cholesteatoma behind a canal stenosis | Surgery | Stenosis under 4 mm is the risk group
!!

# Complications | Of canal surgery.
Facial nerve injury, restenosis of the new canal, lateralisation of the graft and sensorineural loss.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Delaying amplification in bilateral atresia.
- Opening a canal before reconstructing the pinna.
- Drilling without a CT map of the facial nerve.
