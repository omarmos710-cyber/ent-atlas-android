@stand Azelastine is a topical H1 antihistamine with mast-cell stabilising and anti-inflammatory effects, acting within 15 to 30 minutes. It treats allergic rhinitis alone, but its main role is in combination with fluticasone in a single spray, which works better than either drug alone for moderate to severe allergic rhinitis. It also helps some patients with non-allergic rhinitis.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Azelastine and combination sprays
 | Summary
Preparations | Azelastine 0.1% spray (137 micrograms per spray); azelastine with fluticasone propionate (137 and 50 micrograms per spray); olopatadine with mometasone is a newer combination
How it works | H1 antagonism, mast-cell stabilisation and reduced inflammatory mediator release
ENT uses | Seasonal and perennial allergic rhinitis; moderate to severe allergic rhinitis not controlled by a nasal steroid; allergic conjunctivitis (eye drops); some non-allergic rhinitis
Adult dose | Azelastine 1 spray into each nostril twice daily; combination 1 spray into each nostril twice daily
Child dose | Azelastine from 6 years (1 spray each nostril twice daily); the combination from 12 years
!!

# Using it in ENT | Indications.
## Place in treatment
Use a nasal steroid first for persistent or moderate to severe allergic rhinitis. When it is not enough, the steroid-antihistamine combination spray (ARIA and BSACI guidance) is the next step, before adding oral treatments. Its rapid onset helps patients who take sprays only when symptomatic.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
A **bitter taste** (the commonest complaint; tilting the head forward helps), epistaxis, headache and, uncommonly, drowsiness. The combination adds the steroid's effects.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
