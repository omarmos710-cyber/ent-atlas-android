@stand Azithromycin is a macrolide with a very long tissue half-life, so short courses of 3 days give a week or more of tissue levels. It is an alternative to clarithromycin in penicillin allergy and an alternative long-term anti-inflammatory macrolide in chronic rhinosinusitis and bronchiectasis. It interacts less through CYP3A4 than clarithromycin but still prolongs the QT interval.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Azithromycin

Class | Macrolide (azalide)

Adult dose | 500 mg once daily for 3 days; long-term 250 mg once daily or 500 mg three times a week

# How it works | Mechanism and handling.
## Mechanism
The 15-membered azalide ring improves acid stability and Gram-negative penetration. Its antibacterial and anti-inflammatory actions are those of other macrolides.
## Handling
Takes up into tissues and phagocytes, from which it is slowly released; terminal half-life is about 68 hours. Mostly excreted unchanged in bile. Antacids reduce absorption.

# Using it in ENT | Indications and doses.
## Acute infection
A 3-day course is equivalent to longer courses of other agents for acute otitis media and sinusitis in penicillin allergy, but pneumococcal and streptococcal macrolide resistance is a real cause of failure.
## Long-term use
For chronic rhinosinusitis, especially with lower-airway disease, azithromycin three times a week is an option when clarithromycin interacts with other drugs. Long-term use selects macrolide resistance and has been linked to **hearing loss** (usually reversible), so check hearing if symptoms occur.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- Gastrointestinal upset; hepatitis; hearing loss and tinnitus with long-term use.
- QT prolongation and increased risk of cardiovascular death in patients with cardiac disease.
- Rarely, hypersensitivity including DRESS.
## Interactions
Weaker CYP3A4 effect than clarithromycin, but still caution with warfarin, ciclosporin, digoxin and other QT-prolonging drugs; antacids reduce absorption.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- Why a 3-day course works (tissue half-life).
- Differences from clarithromycin in spectrum and interactions.
- Long-term macrolide risks: QT, resistance, hearing.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Using azithromycin for streptococcal tonsillitis when penicillin can be given.
- Long-term therapy without an ECG baseline in patients at cardiac risk.
!!
