@stand Phenoxymethylpenicillin (penicillin V) and benzylpenicillin (penicillin G) are the original narrow-spectrum penicillins. Group A streptococcus has never developed resistance to them, which makes them the first-line drugs for streptococcal tonsillitis and pharyngitis, and, in NICE guidance, for acute sinusitis in adults. Penicillin V is the oral form; benzylpenicillin is given intravenously to patients who cannot swallow, with severe tonsillitis or quinsy.

# How they work | Mechanism and handling.
## Mechanism
Like all penicillins they bind penicillin-binding proteins and stop peptidoglycan cross-linking. Their narrow spectrum is an advantage: less disruption of the gut and oral flora, less C. difficile and less resistance selection.
## Absorption and handling
Penicillin V is acid-stable and absorbed by mouth but best taken **on an empty stomach**, an hour before food. Benzylpenicillin is destroyed by gastric acid and must be given parenterally. Both have short half-lives (about 30 to 60 minutes), hence dosing four times a day; probenecid prolongs their levels.

# Using them in ENT | Indications and doses.
## Sore throat
Use a clinical score to decide: **FeverPAIN** (fever, purulence, attend rapidly within 3 days, inflamed tonsils, no cough or coryza) or **Centor** (exudate, tender anterior nodes, fever, absent cough). With FeverPAIN 0 to 1 or Centor 0 to 2, no antibiotic; FeverPAIN 2 to 3, consider a back-up prescription; FeverPAIN 4 to 5 or Centor 3 to 4, consider an immediate antibiotic. Penicillin V is first-line; clarithromycin or erythromycin in allergy.
## Severe tonsillitis and quinsy
A patient who cannot swallow fluids or has a peritonsillar abscess is admitted: **intravenous benzylpenicillin** (often with **metronidazole** for anaerobes), fluids, analgesia, and drainage of any abscess; many units give a single dose of dexamethasone. Step down to oral penicillin V (with metronidazole) once swallowing.
## Other uses
- **Actinomycosis** of the jaw and neck: high-dose benzylpenicillin for weeks, then oral penicillin for months.
- **Acute sinusitis in adults** where an antibiotic is indicated.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- Hypersensitivity: from rash to anaphylaxis; the commonest cause of drug anaphylaxis.
- Diarrhoea and nausea, less than with broader agents.
- Very high doses of benzylpenicillin in renal failure can cause **seizures** (encephalopathy).
- The **Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction** in syphilis.
## Contraindications and cautions
- Penicillin allergy. About 10% of patients carry a label, but most are not truly allergic; formal de-labelling widens safe options.
- Renal impairment with high-dose intravenous benzylpenicillin.
## Interactions
Methotrexate (reduced excretion); probenecid (raised levels).

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **FeverPAIN** and **Centor** scores and how they guide antibiotics.
- Why penicillin V, not amoxicillin, is first-line for a sore throat.
- Treatment of **quinsy**: drainage plus intravenous benzylpenicillin and metronidazole.
- Group A streptococcus remains universally penicillin-sensitive.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Prescribing penicillin V to be taken with meals.
- Short courses that fail to eradicate streptococcus in a patient with recurrent tonsillitis.
- Relying on antibiotics alone for a peritonsillar abscess.
!!
