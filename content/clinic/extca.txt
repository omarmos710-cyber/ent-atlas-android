@stand The pinna faces the sun every day of a person's life, and most cancers of the ear start there. Any lesion of the pinna that does not heal deserves a biopsy. In the canal, a hard lump is often just bone, an osteoma or exostoses, while a painful, bleeding canal may be carcinoma.

# Summary | Tumours of the pinna and canal.
About 85% of ear cancers arise on the pinna, 10% in the canal and 5% in the middle ear ([[t:tumear]]). Each group has its own chapter.
!tree External ear tumours
Pinna, benign | [[@Benign lesions of the pinna|Cysts, keloid]] | haemangioma, papilloma, cutaneous horn, keratoacanthoma, neurofibroma
Pinna, malignant | [[@Malignant tumours of the pinna|Basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma]] | melanoma
Canal, benign | [[@Osteoma and exostoses|Osteoma, exostoses]] | ceruminous adenoma, papilloma
Canal, malignant | [[@Squamous cell carcinoma of the canal|Squamous cell carcinoma]] | basal cell carcinoma, adenoid cystic and [[@Ceruminous gland tumours|ceruminous carcinoma]], melanoma
!!

# Diagnosis | Biopsy and image.
- Biopsy any non-healing lesion of the pinna or canal.
- CT and MRI for deep or canal tumours.
- Examine the anterior and posterior auricular, parotid and upper cervical nodes.
!imaging | Canal carcinoma and exostoses.

# Differential diagnosis | Lesions that mimic tumours.
Chondrodermatitis nodularis helicis, [[t:perich|perichondritis]], [[t:oe|necrotising otitis externa]] and granulation from chronic otitis ([[t:csom]]). **Chondrodermatitis nodularis** responds in most cases to pressure relief (a pillow with a hole), with excision of cartilage for persistent lesions.

# Benign lesions of the pinna {dz} | Lumps that are not cancer.
## Keloid
Overgrowth of scar after piercing, more common in darker skin. Excision alone recurs; combine with steroid injection, pressure clips or, rarely, radiotherapy.
!fig d16-1 | Keloid after ear piercing.
!differ Radiotherapy for keloid
Dhingra: Postoperative radiation is an option.
Lee: Radiation is rarely used.
In practice: Combine excision with steroid injection or pressure clips; radiotherapy is used rarely.
!!
## Haemangioma and vascular malformations
A *strawberry haemangioma* grows in the first year and regresses by about five years; a *port-wine stain* does not. Venolymphatic malformations may need excision.
!fig d16-2 | A venolymphatic malformation of the pinna in a child: before, during operation, and the excised specimen.
## Keratoacanthoma
A rapidly growing crater with a keratin plug that may regress on its own; it looks malignant and is usually excised.
## Others
Sebaceous and dermoid cysts, papillomas, cutaneous horns and neurofibromas.

# Malignant tumours of the pinna {dz} | Sun-exposed skin.
## Etiology
Sun exposure, fair skin, male sex, older age and immunosuppression.
## Subtypes and variants
### Squamous cell carcinoma
An ulcer, usually on the helix, with raised, everted edges. It spreads to the preauricular, postauricular, parotid and upper cervical nodes.
!fig d16-3 | Squamous cell carcinoma of the pinna.
### Basal cell carcinoma
A nodule with a pearly, rolled, beaded edge and central crusting, usually behind the ear or on the helix; it rarely spreads to nodes but invades locally.
!differ The commonest ear malignancy
Dhingra: Emphasises squamous carcinoma on the helix.
Lee: Basal cell carcinoma is the commonest, at about 45%.
In practice: Basal cell carcinoma is the commonest; squamous carcinoma is the typical helical ulcer and the one that spreads to nodes.
!!
### Melanoma
A pigmented, irregular lesion; spreads early to the parotid and neck nodes.
## Management
- **Squamous carcinoma:** excision with about 1 cm margins for small lesions; larger lesions, those within 1 cm of the canal, or with nodes need partial or total amputation of the pinna with parotidectomy and neck dissection.
- **Basal cell carcinoma:** radiotherapy for superficial lesions not involving cartilage; excision (Mohs surgery where available) for deeper lesions.
- **Melanoma:** wedge excision if small on the helix; wider resection with parotid and neck surgery as staged.
!ops
!update Current evidence
**Skin cancer of the ear.** The ear is a high-risk site for both basal and squamous cell carcinoma; NCCN and British Association of Dermatologists guidelines recommend wider margins (or Mohs micrographic surgery) for high-risk tumours, with SCC of the ear carrying a higher risk of nodal spread to the parotid and neck than other sites. **Melanoma** of the ear is managed by wide local excision with sentinel node biopsy for tumours over 0.8 mm thick, following general melanoma guidelines.
!!
More on skin cancer of the head and neck is in [[t:skinca]].

# Osteoma and exostoses {dz} | Often just bone.
## Differential diagnosis
!table Osteoma or exostoses?
 | Osteoma | Exostoses
Number | Single | Multiple
Sides | One | Usually both
Shape | Smooth, pedunculated | Sessile, broad-based
Site | Outer bony canal, near the bony-cartilaginous junction, at a suture line | Deep bony canal, near the drum, from the anterior and posterior walls
Cause | True benign tumour | Reaction to cold water (swimmers, surfers)
!!
!fig d16-4 | An osteoma arising from the anterior wall of the right ear canal (arrow).
## Management
None if small. When they block the canal, trap wax or cause recurrent otitis externa: an osteoma can be removed by fracturing its pedicle or drilling; exostoses are drilled away through the canal or a postauricular approach. Their effect on the canal, and recurrence after cold water, are covered in [[t:canal]].
!pearl Pearl
Use a drill, not a gouge and hammer, for exostoses: the facial nerve lies close to the posterior canal wall.
!!

# Ceruminous gland tumours {dz} | Glands of the canal.
## Clinical features
A firm, skin-covered mass in the outer canal. Malignant ones (adenoid cystic carcinoma, ceruminous adenocarcinoma) outnumber benign ones about two to one.
## Management
Wide excision, with radiotherapy for malignant tumours.

# Squamous cell carcinoma of the canal {dz} | The painful, bleeding canal.
## Etiology
Often follows long-standing chronic discharge.
## Clinical features
Bloody discharge, pain and granulation that will not heal; facial palsy means advanced disease.
## Staging (Pittsburgh)
- **T1:** confined to the canal, without bone erosion.
- **T2:** limited bone erosion, or soft tissue extension under 0.5 cm.
- **T3:** full-thickness bone erosion with soft tissue under 0.5 cm, or middle ear or mastoid involvement.
- **T4:** cochlea, petrous apex, medial wall, carotid canal, jugular foramen, dura, soft tissue over 0.5 cm, or facial palsy.
## Management
Sleeve or lateral temporal bone resection, with parotidectomy and neck dissection as needed, and postoperative radiotherapy ([[t:tumear]]).

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Treating a non-healing helical ulcer with creams for months.
- Using a gouge on exostoses.
- Missing canal carcinoma in a patient with chronic discharge and new pain.
!!
