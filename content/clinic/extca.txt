@stand The pinna faces the sun every day of a person's life, and most cancers of the ear start there. Any lesion of the pinna that does not heal deserves a biopsy. In the canal, a hard lump is often just bone, an osteoma or exostoses, while a painful, bleeding canal may be carcinoma.

# Overview | Tumours of the pinna and canal.
## Classification
!tree External ear tumours
Pinna, benign | Cysts, keloid | haemangioma, papilloma, cutaneous horn, keratoacanthoma, neurofibroma
Pinna, malignant | Basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma | melanoma
Canal, benign | Osteoma, exostoses | ceruminous adenoma, papilloma
Canal, malignant | Squamous cell carcinoma | basal cell carcinoma, adenoid cystic and ceruminous carcinoma, melanoma
!!
## Where ear cancers arise
About 85% on the pinna, 10% in the canal and 5% in the middle ear.

# Benign lesions of the pinna | Lumps that are not cancer.
## Keloid
Overgrowth of scar after piercing, more common in darker skin. Excision alone recurs; combine with steroid injection, pressure clips or, rarely, radiotherapy.
!fig d16-1 | Keloid after ear piercing.
!differ Radiotherapy for keloid
Dhingra: Postoperative radiation is an option.
Lee: Radiation is rarely used.
!!
## Haemangioma and vascular malformations
A *strawberry haemangioma* grows in the first year and regresses by about five years; a *port-wine stain* does not. Venolymphatic malformations may need excision.
!fig d16-2 | A venolymphatic malformation of the pinna in a child: before, during operation, and the excised specimen.
## Keratoacanthoma
A rapidly growing crater with a keratin plug that may regress on its own; it looks malignant and is usually excised.
## Others
Sebaceous and dermoid cysts, papillomas, cutaneous horns and neurofibromas.

# Malignant tumours of the pinna | Sun-exposed skin.
## Risk factors
Sun exposure, fair skin, male sex, older age and immunosuppression.
## Squamous cell carcinoma
An ulcer, usually on the helix, with raised, everted edges. It spreads to the preauricular, postauricular, parotid and upper cervical nodes.
!fig d16-3 | Squamous cell carcinoma of the pinna.
## Basal cell carcinoma
A nodule with a pearly, rolled, beaded edge and central crusting, usually behind the ear or on the helix; it rarely spreads to nodes but invades locally.
!differ The commonest ear malignancy
Dhingra: Emphasises squamous carcinoma on the helix.
Lee: Basal cell carcinoma is the commonest, at about 45%.
!!
## Melanoma
A pigmented, irregular lesion; spreads early to the parotid and neck nodes.
## Treatment
- **Squamous carcinoma:** excision with about 1 cm margins for small lesions; larger lesions, those within 1 cm of the canal, or with nodes need partial or total amputation of the pinna with parotidectomy and neck dissection.
- **Basal cell carcinoma:** radiotherapy for superficial lesions not involving cartilage; excision (Mohs surgery where available) for deeper lesions.
- **Melanoma:** wedge excision if small on the helix; wider resection with parotid and neck surgery as staged.
!ops

# Bony growths of the canal | Often just bone.
!table Osteoma or exostoses?
 | Osteoma | Exostoses
Number | Single | Multiple
Sides | One | Usually both
Shape | Smooth, pedunculated | Sessile, broad-based
Site | Outer canal, at the bony-cartilaginous junction | Deep canal, near the drum
Cause | True benign tumour | Reaction to cold water (swimmers, surfers)
!!
!fig d16-4 | An osteoma arising from the anterior wall of the right ear canal (arrow).
!differ Site of exostoses
Dhingra: Deep in the canal, near the drum.
Lee: On the anterior and posterior canal walls.
!!
## Treatment
None if small. When they block the canal, trap wax or cause recurrent otitis externa: an osteoma can be removed by fracturing its pedicle or drilling; exostoses are drilled away through the canal or a postauricular approach.
!pearl Pearl
Use a drill, not a gouge and hammer, for exostoses: the facial nerve lies close to the posterior canal wall.
!!

# Tumours of the canal | Glands and skin.
## Ceruminous gland tumours
A firm, skin-covered mass in the outer canal. Malignant ones (adenoid cystic carcinoma, ceruminous adenocarcinoma) outnumber benign ones about two to one. Treated by wide excision, with radiotherapy for malignant tumours.
## Squamous cell carcinoma of the canal
Often follows long-standing chronic discharge. It presents with bloody discharge, pain and granulation that will not heal; facial palsy means advanced disease.
### Staging (Pittsburgh)
- **T1:** confined to the canal, without bone erosion.
- **T2:** limited bone erosion, or soft tissue extension under 0.5 cm.
- **T3:** full-thickness bone erosion with soft tissue under 0.5 cm, or middle ear or mastoid involvement.
- **T4:** cochlea, petrous apex, medial wall, carotid canal, jugular foramen, dura, soft tissue over 0.5 cm, or facial palsy.
### Treatment
Sleeve or lateral temporal bone resection, with parotidectomy and neck dissection as needed, and postoperative radiotherapy.

# Assessment | Biopsy and image.
- Biopsy any non-healing lesion of the pinna or canal.
- CT and MRI for deep or canal tumours.
!imaging | Canal carcinoma and exostoses.
- Examine the anterior and posterior auricular, parotid and upper cervical nodes.
## Differential diagnosis
Chondrodermatitis nodularis helicis, perichondritis, necrotising otitis externa and granulation from chronic otitis.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Squamous versus basal cell carcinoma** of the pinna, and their treatment.
- **Osteoma versus exostoses.**
- **Pittsburgh staging** of canal carcinoma.
- **Biopsy any non-healing lesion.**
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Treating a non-healing helical ulcer with creams for months.
- Using a gouge on exostoses.
- Missing canal carcinoma in a patient with chronic discharge and new pain.
!!
