@stand Bevacizumab is a monoclonal antibody that binds vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and suppresses new vessel formation. Given intravenously, it has transformed the management of severe recurrent respiratory papillomatosis, reducing the frequency of surgery, and it reduces bleeding and transfusion needs in hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia with severe epistaxis or gastrointestinal bleeding. It carries the class risks of anti-VEGF drugs: hypertension, proteinuria, bleeding, thrombosis and poor wound healing.

# At a glance | The drug on one page.
## Summary
!table Bevacizumab
 | Summary
Class | Anti-VEGF monoclonal antibody
How it works | Binds VEGF-A, preventing receptor activation; reduces angiogenesis and vascular permeability
ENT uses | Severe recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (systemic, as adjuvant to surgery); hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia with severe or transfusion-dependent bleeding; intralesional use in papillomatosis has been reported
Dose | Regimens vary: commonly 5 to 10 mg/kg intravenously every 2 to 3 weeks for an induction phase, then extended intervals for maintenance (specialist protocols)
Avoid | Recent major surgery (wait at least 4 weeks; stop before elective surgery), uncontrolled hypertension, active bleeding, pregnancy
!!

# Using it in ENT | Practice.
## Recurrent respiratory papillomatosis
For children and adults with frequent surgery or pulmonary spread, systemic bevacizumab with debulking surgery lengthens intervals between procedures and can induce remission in many case series; it is used under a multidisciplinary team.
## Hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia
For severe epistaxis despite local treatments (laser, coblation, septodermoplasty, Young's procedure), systemic bevacizumab reduces bleeding and transfusion needs. Topical intranasal bevacizumab did not help in trials. Tranexamic acid and iron replacement continue.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
**Hypertension**, **proteinuria**, epistaxis or bleeding, arterial and venous thrombosis, delayed wound healing and wound dehiscence, gastrointestinal perforation, osteonecrosis of the jaw, posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome.
