@stand Facial plastic surgery begins with analysis: proportions, angles, the skin and the structures beneath it. It then asks what the patient wants and whether that is achievable and wise. For the ENT surgeon the core is the nose, where form and airway must be fixed together. The other procedures are the ear, the ageing face and the injectables, each with its own anatomy and its own complications to avoid.

# Facial analysis | Measure before you cut.
## Landmarks
- **Trichion:** the midline hairline.
- **Glabella:** the most prominent point of the forehead.
- **Nasion:** the deepest point of the nasofrontal angle, at the nasofrontal suture.
- **Rhinion:** the junction of the bony and cartilaginous dorsum, where the skin is thinnest.
- **Tip-defining points:** the light reflexes of the domes.
- **Subnasale:** where the columella meets the lip.
- **Stomion:** where the lips meet.
- **Pogonion:** the most anterior point of the chin.
- **Menton:** the lowest point of the chin.
- **Cervical point:** the deepest point between the submental region and the neck.

## Proportions
- **Horizontal thirds (height):** trichion–glabella, glabella–subnasale and subnasale–menton are about equal. In the lower third, the upper lip is a third and the lower lip and chin two-thirds.
- **Vertical fifths (width):** each about one eye's width. The intercanthal distance equals an eye width and the width of the nasal base.
- **Width to height:** about 3:4.
!table Facial angles
Angle | Defined by | Ideal
Nasofrontal | Glabella–nasion and nasion–tip | 115 to 130°
Nasofacial | Facial plane (glabella–pogonion) and the dorsum | 30 to 40°
Nasolabial | Columella and upper lip at subnasale | 90 to 95° in men, 95 to 110° (up to 120°) in women
Nasomental | Dorsum and tip–pogonion | 120 to 132°
Mentocervical | Facial plane and the submental line | 80 to 95°

## The chin and occlusion
- **Chin position:** in profile the chin sits on, or just behind (women), a vertical line from the lower lip.
- **Microgenia versus retrognathia:**
  - *Microgenia* is a small chin with normal occlusion. It is treated with an implant or a *sliding genioplasty*.
  - *Retrognathia* is a retruded mandible with a class II bite. It needs orthognathic surgery (sagittal split osteotomy).
- **Angle classification of occlusion:**
  - Class I: the mesiobuccal cusp of the upper first molar sits in the buccal groove of the lower first molar. This is normal.
  - Class II: the upper molar sits forward of the lower (retrognathia).
  - Class III: the upper molar sits behind the lower (prognathism).
- **Chin implants:**
  - Silastic, porous polyethylene or ePTFE, placed in a subperiosteal pocket through a submental or intraoral incision.
  - Complications: displacement, infection, bone resorption, mental nerve injury.
- **Sliding genioplasty:** adds both projection and height. It risks injury to the mental nerve and tooth roots.
## Skin
- **Fitzpatrick types I to VI:**
  - Type I always burns and never tans. Type VI never burns.
  - Types III to VI are at more risk of pigment change after peels and lasers.
- **Glogau photoageing classes:** I no wrinkles, II wrinkles in motion, III wrinkles at rest, IV only wrinkles.
- **Nasal skin:** thick and sebaceous at the tip and supratip, thinnest at the rhinion. Thick skin hides tip work and swells. Thin skin shows every irregularity.

# Rhinoplasty | Form and function together.
## Surgical anatomy
- **Nasal thirds:**
  - The *upper (bony) third*: the nasal bones.
  - The *middle third*: the upper lateral cartilages fused to the dorsal septum, forming the *internal nasal valve* (normally 10 to 15°).
  - The *lower third*: the lower lateral (alar) cartilages, with medial, intermediate and lateral crura, the domes, and the external valve.
- **The scroll:** the junction of the upper and lower lateral cartilages.
- **The keystone area:** where the nasal bones meet the upper lateral cartilages and septum.
- **Tip support:**
  - *Major mechanisms*:
    - The size, shape and strength of the lower lateral cartilages.
    - The attachment of the medial crural footplates to the caudal septum.
    - The attachment of the upper to the lower lateral cartilages (the scroll).
  - *Minor mechanisms*:
    - The interdomal ligament.
    - The dorsal cartilaginous septum.
    - The sesamoid complex.
    - The skin and soft-tissue envelope.
    - The nasal spine.
    - The membranous septum.
- **The tripod concept:** the two lateral crura and the conjoined medial crura form a tripod. Shortening or lengthening the legs changes *projection* and *rotation*.

## Analysis of the nose
- **Frontal view:**
  - Deviation, by thirds.
  - Width of the bony base, compared with the alar base.
  - Smooth *brow–tip aesthetic lines* from the medial brow to the tip-defining point.
  - Tip definition.
  - A "gull in flight" alar–columellar outline.
- **Profile:**
  - Radix position, between the lash line and the upper lid crease.
  - Dorsal hump.
  - A supratip break, desirable in women.
  - *Tip projection*. The *Goode ratio*: tip projection is 0.55 to 0.6 of nasal length. The 3:4:5 triangle.
  - *Rotation* (the nasolabial angle).
  - *Columellar show* of 2 to 4 mm.
- **Base view:** an equilateral triangle. Columella to lobule 2:1. Nostril symmetry. Caudal septal deviation.
- **Inside the nose:**
  - The septum, turbinates and valves.
  - The *Cottle manoeuvre* (lateral cheek traction improves the airway in valve collapse) and the modified Cottle test with a cotton bud.
- **Records and the patient:** standard photographs. Watch for body dysmorphic disorder and unrealistic expectations.
## Approaches and incisions
- **Incisions:**
  - Marginal: along the caudal edge of the lateral crus.
  - Intercartilaginous: between the upper and lower laterals.
  - Transcartilaginous.
  - Transcolumellar: an inverted V or stair step.
  - Transfixion or hemitransfixion: between the caudal septum and the medial crura.
- **Endonasal (closed) rhinoplasty:**
  - *Non-delivery* approach: cartilage-splitting or retrograde.
  - *Delivery* approach: the alar cartilages are delivered as bipedicled flaps.
  - No external scar and less swelling, but limited exposure.
- **External (open) rhinoplasty:**
  - Transcolumellar and marginal incisions, lifting the whole skin envelope.
  - Best exposure, and precise sutures and grafts.
  - Used for revision, cleft and crooked noses, major tip work, and teaching.
  - Costs: a columellar scar, more oedema, and disruption of a minor support.
!table Common manoeuvres
Problem | Manoeuvre
Dorsal hump | Component reduction: rasp the bone, cut the cartilage; then osteotomies to close the open roof; spreader grafts to keep the middle vault and valve
Wide or crooked bony vault | Medial, lateral (low-to-high along the nasomaxillary groove, starting above the inferior turbinate attachment) and intermediate osteotomies
Crooked middle third | Septoplasty, asymmetric spreader grafts or spreader flaps, camouflage onlay grafts
Bulbous tip | Cephalic trim, keeping at least 6 to 10 mm of lateral crus; dome-binding (transdomal, interdomal) sutures; lateral crural strut grafts
Under-projected tip | Columellar strut, caudal septal extension graft, dome sutures, tip (shield, cap) grafts, lateral crural steal
Over-projected tip | Full transfixion, medial or lateral crural overlay, reducing the footplates
Under-rotated (drooping) tip | Trim the caudal septum, tongue-in-groove, lateral crural steal or overlay, cephalic trim
Over-rotated tip | Caudal extension graft, medial crural overlay
Internal valve collapse | Spreader grafts, flaring sutures, butterfly graft
External valve collapse | Alar batten grafts, lateral crural strut grafts, alar rim grafts
Saddle nose | Dorsal onlay or reconstruction with septal, ear or *rib cartilage*; rebuild the L-strut
!!
- **Grafts:**
  - Septal cartilage is first choice. Leave a 10 to 15 mm dorsal and caudal *L-strut*.
  - Ear cartilage (conchal bowl), for alar grafts.
  - *Costal cartilage*: plentiful, for revisions and saddle noses, but it can warp.
  - Fascia, and diced cartilage wrapped in fascia.
- **The guiding principle:** "Preserve and conserve, do not resect and regret". Reshape with sutures and grafts rather than removing support.

## Complications and revision
- **Revision rates:** about 5 to 15%. Wait about a year before revising.
- **Pollybeak deformity:** fullness above the tip. Causes: an under-resected cartilaginous dorsum, supratip dead space and scar, or loss of tip support. Treatment: steroid injection, trimming, or restoring tip support.
- **Saddle nose:** over-resection of the dorsum, septal collapse, abscess or haematoma, or granulomatous disease (granulomatosis with polyangiitis, cocaine). Treatment: grafts.
- **Inverted-V deformity:** the upper lateral cartilages collapse away from the nasal bones. Treatment: spreader grafts.
- **Open roof:** osteotomies omitted after hump removal.
- **Rocker deformity:** an osteotomy carried too high into the thick nasal bone.
- **Alar retraction and notching:** over-resection of the lateral crus. Treatment: alar rim and composite grafts.
- **Tip problems:** bossae, a pinched tip, tip ptosis.
- **Nasal obstruction:** valve collapse, synechiae, residual septal deviation.
- **Others:** septal haematoma or perforation, epistaxis, CSF leak from a high septal or osteotomy fracture (rare), and dissatisfaction.

# The ageing face | Upper, middle and lower thirds.
## What ages
- **Skin:** thinner dermis, less collagen and elastin, photodamage, wrinkles.
- **Soft tissue:** descent and loss of volume (fat pads).
- **Bone:** resorption, especially the orbital rims, piriform aperture and maxilla, and the pre-jowl mandible.
- **By third:**
  - Upper: forehead lines and brow ptosis.
  - Middle: eyelid laxity and fat bulges, tear trough, deeper nasolabial folds.
  - Lower: jowls, marionette lines, neck laxity and platysmal bands.
## Brow and forehead lift
- **The ideal brow:**
  - Begins over the medial canthus.
  - Ends on a line from the alar base through the lateral canthus.
  - In women, arches above the orbital rim with its peak between the lateral limbus and lateral canthus.
  - In men, lies flatter, at the rim.
- **Approaches:**
  - *Coronal*: well hidden, but raises the hairline and leaves scalp numbness.
  - *Pretrichial*: for a high hairline.
  - *Endoscopic*: small incisions and subperiosteal release, with fixation.
  - *Direct* and *midforehead*: for older men and facial palsy.
- **The frontal branch of the facial nerve:** lies in the temporoparietal fascia, along *Pitanguy line* (from 0.5 cm below the tragus to 1.5 cm above the lateral brow). Lateral dissection is kept *deep*, on the deep temporal fascia.

## Blepharoplasty
- **Upper lid:**
  - Removes excess skin (*dermatochalasis*) with a strip of orbicularis and conservative fat (medial and central compartments).
  - The crease incision is about 8 to 10 mm above the lashes.
  - Leave at least *20 mm* of skin between the lash line and the brow.
  - Brow ptosis may be the real problem.
- **Assessment before surgery:**
  - Vision and fields.
  - Dry eye (*Schirmer test*).
  - Thyroid eye disease.
  - Previous surgery.
  - True *blepharoptosis*, which needs levator surgery, not blepharoplasty.
- **Lower lid approaches:**
  - *Transconjunctival* for fat alone.
  - *Subciliary* skin or skin–muscle flap for excess skin.
- **Lower lid tests:**
  - Lid laxity: the *snap test* and the distraction test (over 6 to 10 mm is abnormal).
  - *Negative vector* (globe ahead of the cheek): avoid removing fat.
  - Lax lids need canthopexy or canthoplasty to avoid ectropion.
- **Complications:**
  - *Retrobulbar haematoma*, which can blind: pain, proptosis, reduced vision. Emergency *lateral canthotomy and cantholysis*, open the wound, and give mannitol, acetazolamide and steroids.
  - Lagophthalmos and dry eye.
  - Ectropion and scleral show.
  - Ptosis, diplopia (inferior oblique injury), and milia.
!flag Pain and proptosis after eyelid surgery
Severe pain, a tense proptosed eye and falling vision after blepharoplasty mean a retrobulbar haematoma. Release the sutures and perform a lateral canthotomy and cantholysis at the bedside within minutes, then call ophthalmology. Vision can be lost within about 90 minutes.
!!
## Rhytidectomy (facelift)
- **The SMAS:** the superficial musculoaponeurotic system, continuous with the platysma below and the temporoparietal fascia above. The facial nerve branches run *deep* to it.
- **Techniques, by depth:**
  - Skin-only.
  - SMAS plication.
  - SMAS imbrication or lateral SMASectomy.
  - *Deep-plane* lift: sub-SMAS, carrying the malar fat. The strongest midface effect, with more nerve risk.
  - Composite lift.
- **Neck procedures:** liposuction, platysmaplasty (midline plication), and submandibular gland or digastric contouring.
- **Dedo classification of the neck:**
  - Class I: minimal deformity.
  - Class II: skin laxity.
  - Class III: fat.
  - Class IV: platysmal banding.
  - Class V: micrognathia.
  - Class VI: a low hyoid.
- **Complications:**
  - *Haematoma*: the commonest, about 3 to 15%, more in men and hypertensive patients. Evacuate promptly.
  - Skin necrosis: postauricular, worse in smokers.
  - *Great auricular nerve* injury: the commonest nerve injury, giving ear lobe numbness.
  - Facial nerve injury: temporal and marginal mandibular branches. Usually temporary.
  - Alopecia, hairline shift, pixie-ear (pulled earlobe), unattractive scars.

# The ear | Otoplasty.
- **The prominent ear:** the commonest auricular deformity.
  - A deficient *antihelical fold*.
  - A deep *conchal bowl*.
  - Or both.
  - Lobule protrusion.
- **Normal measurements:**
  - Auriculocephalic angle about 20 to 30°.
  - Helix 15 to 20 mm from the scalp at the top, about 20 mm at mid-ear.
  - Ear length about 6 cm, width about 55% of length.
- **Timing:**
  - At five to six years, when the ear is about 85 to 90% of adult size and before school teasing.
  - In newborns, *ear moulding* splints in the first weeks can correct many deformities without surgery.
- **Techniques:**
  - *Mustardé* horizontal mattress sutures create the antihelical fold.
  - *Furnas* conchomastoid sutures set back the concha. Place them posteriorly so they do not narrow the canal.
  - Cartilage scoring or excision (Chongchet, Stenström, Converse) for stiff cartilage.
  - Conchal bowl excision.
  - Lobule correction.
- **Complications:**
  - Haematoma, which can lead to perichondritis and cauliflower ear.
  - Infection and chondritis.
  - Suture extrusion.
  - Under-correction or recurrence (the commonest).
  - Over-correction and the "*telephone ear*" (middle overcorrected, poles still prominent).
  - Asymmetry.

# Skin and injectables | Resurfacing, toxin, fillers.
## Resurfacing
All resurfacing methods remove the epidermis and injure the papillary dermis, so that new collagen and epidermis form. Injury into the reticular dermis scars.
- **Chemical peels:**
  - *Superficial*: glycolic acid, Jessner solution, 10 to 25% TCA.
  - *Medium*: Jessner solution followed by 35% TCA.
  - *Deep*: Baker–Gordon phenol.
  - Phenol is cardiotoxic (*arrhythmias*), as well as nephrotoxic and hepatotoxic. Monitor the ECG, hydrate, and treat one facial unit at a time.
- **Dermabrasion:** mechanical, to the papillary dermis (pinpoint bleeding). Used for acne scars, rhinophyma and scar revision.
- **Lasers:**
  - *Ablative* (CO2, Er:YAG): CO2 is deeper and tightens. Er:YAG is shallower and heals faster.
  - *Fractional*: microscopic columns of injury with rapid healing.
  - *Non-ablative*.
- **Contraindications and precautions:**
  - Isotretinoin within 6 to 12 months (some say 2 years).
  - Active herpes: give antiviral prophylaxis.
  - Keloid tendency, previous irradiation, scleroderma.
  - Darker skin types (pigment changes).
## Botulinum toxin
- **Mechanism:** blocks the presynaptic release of acetylcholine by cleaving SNARE proteins (SNAP-25 for type A). Onset in 2 to 3 days, peak at 1 to 2 weeks, lasting 3 to 4 months.
- **Cosmetic uses:**
  - Glabellar lines (corrugator, procerus).
  - Forehead lines (frontalis).
  - Crow's feet (orbicularis oculi).
  - Platysmal bands.
  - The gummy smile.
- **ENT uses:**
  - Spasmodic dysphonia.
  - Hemifacial spasm and blepharospasm.
  - Synkinesis after facial palsy, and the opposite side for symmetry.
  - Frey syndrome, sialorrhoea and sialoceles.
  - Cricopharyngeal dysfunction.
  - Palatal myoclonus.
  - Migraine.
- **Complications:** bruising and headache. *Eyelid ptosis* from spread to the levator, treated with apraclonidine drops. Brow ptosis, an asymmetric smile, dysphagia.
- **Contraindications:** myasthenia gravis, Lambert–Eaton syndrome and other neuromuscular disease, aminoglycosides, pregnancy and breastfeeding.
## Fillers and fat
- **Hyaluronic acid:** the commonest filler, lasting 6 to 18 months. Reversible with *hyaluronidase*.
- **Others:**
  - Calcium hydroxylapatite.
  - Poly-L-lactic acid (stimulates collagen over months).
  - Permanent fillers (PMMA, silicone), which carry the risk of late granulomas.
- **Autologous fat grafting:** harvest, centrifuge, then inject in small aliquots in multiple layers. Survival is unpredictable.
- **Danger zones:** the glabella, nasal dorsum, nasolabial fold and temple.
!flag Vascular occlusion from filler
- **Skin occlusion:** blanching, pain, then a livedo or dusky pattern after injection. Stop injecting, flood the area with high-dose hyaluronidase (for hyaluronic acid fillers), massage, and apply warm compresses.
- **Visual loss:** retrograde embolism into the ophthalmic artery, from glabellar, nasal or nasolabial injection. This is an ophthalmic emergency.
!!

## Scar revision
- **Placement:** scars are best hidden in *relaxed skin tension lines*, facial creases and the borders of aesthetic units.
- **Z-plasty:**
  - Lengthens a scar and changes its direction.
  - With 30°, 45° and 60° limbs it lengthens by about 25%, 50% and 75%, and turns the scar by up to 90°.
  - Used for scars 30 to 60° off the relaxed skin tension lines and for contracted scars.
- **W-plasty and geometric broken-line closure:** irregularise long scars that run against the lines.
- **Adjuncts:** dermabrasion or laser at 6 to 8 weeks, and steroid injection or silicone for hypertrophic scars and keloids.
## Hair restoration
- **Male pattern baldness:** androgenetic alopecia, graded by the *Norwood classification*.
- **Medical treatment:** minoxidil, finasteride, platelet-rich plasma.
- **Hair transplantation:** follicular unit grafts from the androgen-resistant occipital scalp, by strip harvest or *follicular unit extraction*. Single-hair units go at the hairline. Transplanted hairs shed at a few weeks and regrow from about three months.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Facial proportions** (thirds and fifths) and the main angles.
- **Major and minor tip support mechanisms;** the tripod concept.
- **The internal and external nasal valves:** the Cottle test, spreader and batten grafts.
- **Open versus closed rhinoplasty;** osteotomies; the L-strut.
- **Complications:** pollybeak, saddle nose, inverted V.
- **The frontal branch** and its safe plane; the SMAS.
- **Facelift haematoma;** great auricular nerve injury.
- **Blepharoplasty:** tests, retrobulbar haematoma.
- **Otoplasty:** Mustardé and Furnas sutures; the telephone ear.
- **Botulinum toxin:** mechanism, uses, complications.
- **Filler vascular occlusion and hyaluronidase.**
- **Z-plasty geometry.**
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Removing a dorsal hump without osteotomies or spreader grafts (open roof, inverted V).
- An aggressive cephalic trim leading to alar collapse and notching.
- Blepharoplasty without recognising true ptosis, dry eye or a lax lower lid.
- Injecting filler in danger zones without aspiration, slow low-pressure technique, or hyaluronidase available.
- Operating on a patient with body dysmorphic disorder.
!!
