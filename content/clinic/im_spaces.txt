@stand The neck looks like a crowded jumble on CT until you see it as a set of tubes and compartments wrapped by the three layers of the deep cervical fascia. Each compartment, or **space**, contains a limited set of normal structures, and nearly every lesion in it comes from one of them. Harnsberger's insight, the backbone of *Diagnostic Imaging: Head and Neck*, is that if you can name the space a mass arises in, the differential diagnosis shrinks to a few lines. This lesson teaches the map and the one trick that tells you where a deep mass began.

# The fascia and the spaces | A map of the neck.
## Three layers, many spaces
The **superficial layer** of the deep cervical fascia invests the neck: it surrounds the masticator and parotid spaces above the hyoid and the sternocleidomastoid, trapezius and strap muscles below. The **middle layer** lines the deep surface of the pharyngeal mucosa above and wraps the visceral space (larynx, trachea, thyroid, oesophagus) below. The **deep layer** surrounds the perivertebral space, and a slip of it, the **alar fascia**, separates the retropharyngeal space from the danger space behind it. All three contribute to the **carotid sheath**.
## The suprahyoid neck
Above the hyoid, a pair of fat-filled **parapharyngeal spaces** sits at the centre, surrounded by four key spaces: the **pharyngeal mucosal space** medially, the **masticator space** in front, the **parotid space** laterally and the **carotid space** behind. In the midline behind the pharynx are the **retropharyngeal space** and the **perivertebral space**.
!fig di33-1 | The spaces of the suprahyoid neck at the level of the nasopharynx, colour-coded: pharyngeal mucosal space (blue), masticator spaces (purple), parotid spaces (green), carotid spaces with the artery and vein (red), the fat of the parapharyngeal spaces (yellow-orange) in the middle of them, and the perivertebral space (magenta) behind. The fascial layers are drawn as coloured lines.
!fig ia301-1 | The same spaces on axial T2 MRI: the pharyngeal mucosal space, masticator space, parotid space (outlined) and carotid space arranged around the parapharyngeal fat, with the prevertebral muscles behind.
## The infrahyoid neck
Below the hyoid, the **visceral space** in the centre holds the larynx and hypopharynx, the trachea and oesophagus, the thyroid and parathyroid glands, and the recurrent laryngeal nerves. On either side are the **carotid spaces**, behind them the **posterior cervical spaces** (fat, the accessory nerve and level V nodes), and in the midline behind are the retropharyngeal and perivertebral spaces. The **anterior cervical space** is the fat between the carotid space and the visceral space.
!fig ia296-3 | Axial contrast CT at the glottis: the visceral space with the larynx and strap muscles, the carotid spaces behind it on either side, the posterior cervical spaces and the perivertebral space.

# What each space contains | And therefore what arises there.
!table The spaces, their contents and their common lesions
Space | Normal contents | Common lesions
Pharyngeal mucosal | Mucosa, lymphoid tissue (adenoids, tonsils), minor salivary glands, constrictor muscles | Squamous cell carcinoma, lymphoma, tonsillar abscess, minor salivary tumour
Parapharyngeal | Fat, occasional minor salivary rests | Rare primary lesions; mostly displaced by its neighbours. Pleomorphic adenoma of salivary rests, lipoma
Masticator | Mandibular ramus and body, muscles of mastication, V3, pterygoid venous plexus | Odontogenic abscess, sarcoma, perineural tumour on V3, benign masseter hypertrophy
Parotid | Parotid gland, facial nerve, intraparotid nodes, retromandibular vein | Pleomorphic adenoma, Warthin tumour, carcinoma, nodes
Carotid | Internal carotid, internal jugular vein, IX to XII (only X below the hyoid), sympathetic chain | Paraganglioma, schwannoma, thrombosis, nodes alongside
Retropharyngeal | Fat; lateral (Rouvière) and medial retropharyngeal nodes above the hyoid | Nodal metastasis, suppurative node, abscess, oedema
Perivertebral | Vertebrae, prevertebral and scalene muscles, brachial plexus, vertebral artery | Discitis and osteomyelitis, metastasis, chordoma, brachial plexus schwannoma
Visceral | Larynx, hypopharynx, trachea, oesophagus, thyroid, parathyroids | Carcinoma of each, thyroid and parathyroid lesions
Posterior cervical | Fat, accessory nerve, level V nodes | Nodes, lymphatic malformation, lipoma, schwannoma

# Where did it start? | The parapharyngeal fat tells you.
## The displacement rule
The parapharyngeal space is mostly fat and rarely the origin of disease, so it acts as a marker. A mass growing in one of the four surrounding spaces pushes the fat away from itself:
- a **pharyngeal mucosal** mass pushes the fat **laterally**;
- a **masticator** space mass pushes it **posteriorly** (and medially);
- a **deep lobe parotid** mass pushes it **medially** (and widens the stylomandibular gap);
- a **carotid space** mass pushes it **anteriorly**, and lifts the styloid process forwards;
- a lateral **retropharyngeal** mass pushes it anteriorly without displacing the styloid.

A mass completely surrounded by fat arises in the parapharyngeal space itself, usually from salivary rests.

## The carotid space and the vessels
In the carotid space, the position of the vessels refines the diagnosis. A **carotid body paraganglioma** sits in the bifurcation and **splays** the internal and external carotid arteries apart. A **vagal** paraganglioma or schwannoma, higher up, pushes the internal carotid **anteriorly** (and medially). A **sympathetic** schwannoma, medial to the vessels, separates neither. Paragangliomas enhance intensely and show flow voids ("salt and pepper"); schwannomas enhance less and may be cystic.
!fig di167-1 | Carotid body paraganglioma. Left: drawing of the tumour in the bifurcation, splaying the internal and external carotids. Right: contrast CT, an avidly enhancing mass sitting in the notch between the two arteries.

# Spaces as pathways | How disease spreads.
## Infection
The spaces also explain how infection travels. A **peritonsillar abscess** can rupture through the superior constrictor into the parapharyngeal fat and on to the masticator or carotid spaces. Dental infection enters the masticator, submandibular and sublingual spaces. The **retropharyngeal space** extends down to about T1–T6, and the **danger space** behind it to the diaphragm, so infection there threatens the mediastinum. A suppurative retropharyngeal node in a child (a rim-enhancing node, often lateral) is not the same as a true retropharyngeal abscess (fluid distending the space from side to side, often with mass effect on the airway).
!fig di55-2 | Tonsillar abscess complications. Left: a tonsillar abscess extending through the capsule into the buccal space and the medial pterygoid. Right: a large abscess that has ruptured into the carotid space and the submandibular space.
!fig di195-1 | Retropharyngeal abscess. Left: drawing of the collection behind the pharynx. Right: contrast CT in an infant, a low-density collection distending the retropharyngeal space and pushing the pharynx forwards.
## Abscess, phlegmon or cellulitis?
On contrast CT, **cellulitis** is stranding of the fat; **phlegmon** is ill-defined, enhancing soft tissue without a fluid centre; an **abscess** is a fluid collection with a well-formed enhancing rim. Only a mature abscess is drained. Look also for the airway, the internal jugular vein (thrombosis, Lemierre syndrome), the carotid, the mediastinum and the cause (tooth, stone, tonsil, foreign body).

!path Placing a deep neck mass
Above or below the hyoid | suprahyoid: find the parapharyngeal fat ; infrahyoid: visceral, carotid, posterior cervical
Which way is the fat pushed | laterally: mucosal ; posteriorly: masticator ; medially: deep parotid ; anteriorly: carotid or retropharyngeal ; surrounded: parapharyngeal itself
What normally lives there | match to the contents table
Look at the vessels | splayed: carotid body ; ICA pushed forwards: vagal ; stylomandibular gap widened: deep parotid
Character | cyst, fat, enhancement, flow voids, bone
Differential | two to four diagnoses by space and character

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **The three layers** of deep cervical fascia and what they wrap.
- **The suprahyoid spaces** around the parapharyngeal fat, and the **infrahyoid spaces**.
- **The displacement rule** for the parapharyngeal fat.
- **Contents of each space** and the lesions that arise there.
- **Carotid space masses:** carotid body versus vagal paraganglioma versus schwannoma.
- **Danger space** and mediastinal spread; abscess versus phlegmon.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Describing a deep neck mass without naming its space.
- Calling a deep lobe parotid tumour "parapharyngeal" when the fat is pushed medially.
- Draining a phlegmon.
- Calling a suppurative retropharyngeal node a retropharyngeal abscess.
!!
