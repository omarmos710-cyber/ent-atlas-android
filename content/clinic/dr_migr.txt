@stand Vestibular migraine is the commonest cause of recurrent spontaneous vertigo in adults. Treatment combines identifying triggers and lifestyle changes (sleep, meals, hydration, caffeine, analgesic overuse) with migraine prophylaxis when attacks are frequent or disabling. The drugs are those used for migraine headache, chosen by the patient's comorbidities; evidence specific to vestibular migraine is limited.

# At a glance | The drugs on one page.
## Summary
!table Vestibular migraine prophylaxis
 | Summary
Agents | Propranolol; amitriptyline (or nortriptyline); topiramate; pizotifen; candesartan; venlafaxine; flunarizine (not licensed in the UK); CGRP monoclonal antibodies (erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab) for refractory migraine under specialists
Indications | Frequent (for example several a month) or disabling attacks despite lifestyle measures
Adult doses | Propranolol 40 mg twice or three times daily, up to 80 to 160 mg a day; amitriptyline 10 mg at night, increasing to 25 to 50 mg; topiramate 25 mg at night, increasing to 50 mg twice daily; pizotifen 1.5 mg at night; candesartan 8 to 16 mg daily; venlafaxine 75 to 150 mg daily
Trial length | At least 2 to 3 months at an adequate dose before judging
!!

# Using them in ENT | Choosing a drug.
## Matching drug to patient
- **Propranolol**: good with anxiety or hypertension; avoid in asthma, heart block, and use with care in depression and diabetes.
- **Amitriptyline**: good with insomnia, tension-type headache or neck pain; causes drowsiness, dry mouth and weight gain; cautious in the elderly (anticholinergic, falls, cardiac).
- **Topiramate**: suits overweight patients; causes paraesthesia, cognitive slowing, kidney stones and weight loss; it is **teratogenic**, so women of childbearing potential need a pregnancy prevention programme and highly effective contraception (it also reduces the effect of oral contraceptives).
- **Pizotifen**: causes weight gain and drowsiness.
- **Venlafaxine**: when anxiety or depression coexists.
## Acute treatment
Triptans may help headache; vestibular sedatives for severe vertigo; antiemetics.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Key cautions
Beta blockers mask hypoglycaemia; tricyclics are dangerous in overdose; topiramate and valproate must not be used in pregnancy; sodium valproate is generally avoided in women who could become pregnant.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- Diagnostic criteria of vestibular migraine (Bárány Society and International Headache Society).
- The prophylactic drugs and how comorbidities guide choice.
- Topiramate and pregnancy.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Stopping prophylaxis after 2 weeks for lack of effect.
- Prescribing topiramate without a contraception discussion.
!!
