@stand Ceftazidime is the third-generation cephalosporin with reliable activity against Pseudomonas aeruginosa. In ENT it is used intravenously for necrotising otitis externa and skull base osteomyelitis, for Pseudomonas wound and cartilage infection that oral ciprofloxacin cannot treat, and in the immunocompromised patient. Its Gram-positive activity is weak.

# How it works | Mechanism and handling.
## Mechanism
Like other cephalosporins it blocks peptidoglycan cross-linking; its side chain gives it good penetration and affinity in Pseudomonas. Resistance arises from AmpC derepression, efflux and ESBL production; avibactam (ceftazidime-avibactam) restores activity against many resistant Gram-negatives and is reserved for specialist use.
## Handling
Intravenous or intramuscular; renally excreted, so the dose falls with the eGFR.

# Using it in ENT | Indications and doses.
## Necrotising otitis externa
Ceftazidime, or piperacillin-tazobactam, is the intravenous anti-pseudomonal choice, frequently with oral ciprofloxacin. After clinical response, falling CRP and ESR, and a resolving scan, therapy is continued orally with ciprofloxacin for a total of 6 weeks or more; recurrence is common in the first year. If cultures grow Aspergillus, add voriconazole.
## Cartilage and wound infection
Pseudomonas perichondritis of the pinna with abscess, or not responding to oral ciprofloxacin, needs drainage and intravenous ceftazidime.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- Diarrhoea and C. difficile; hypersensitivity.
- Neurotoxicity (encephalopathy, myoclonus, seizures) if the dose is not reduced in renal failure.
- Positive Coombs test, eosinophilia, raised liver enzymes.
## Interactions
Aminoglycosides (additive nephrotoxicity; do not mix in one bag); chloramphenicol (antagonism).

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- The intravenous and oral options for **Pseudomonas** in ENT.
- How you monitor treatment of necrotising otitis externa: pain, granulation, CRP and ESR, imaging (gallium or MRI).
- Why ceftazidime is not a good drug for streptococcal or staphylococcal infection.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Using ceftazidime as general broad cover in head and neck sepsis (it misses Gram-positives and anaerobes).
- Forgetting renal dose adjustment in elderly diabetics.
!!
