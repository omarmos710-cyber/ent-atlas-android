@stand Otology is full of names. This page gathers the signs, syndromes and anatomical landmarks that examiners love, each explained in a line or two and pointing to the topic where it belongs. Use it for revision the night before a viva.

# Signs | What you see or elicit.
## Abscesses around the mastoid
### Bezold abscess
Pus breaking through the medial side of a pneumatised mastoid tip and collecting under the sternocleidomastoid. See Mastoiditis.
### Citelli abscess
Pus tracking from the tip along the posterior belly of digastric, behind the mastoid or in the digastric triangle.
### Luc abscess
A meatal abscess: pus from the antrum tracking under the periosteum of the bony canal.
## Signs of intracranial spread
### Griesinger's sign
Oedema over the mastoid emissary vein in lateral sinus thrombosis.
### Tobey-Ayer test
Compressing the jugular vein on the healthy side raises CSF pressure, on the thrombosed side does not; historical.
### Crowe-Beck test
Compressing the healthy jugular vein engorges the retinal veins in lateral sinus thrombosis; historical.
### Gradenigo's triad
Ear discharge, deep pain behind the eye and a sixth nerve palsy in petrous apicitis.
## Labyrinth
### Hennebert's sign
A positive fistula test with an intact drum: congenital syphilis, Ménière's disease, superior canal dehiscence.
### Tullio phenomenon
Vertigo triggered by loud sound: superior canal dehiscence, perilymph fistula, hydrops.
## Otosclerosis
### Schwartze sign
A red blush on the promontory through the drum, from an active, vascular focus.
### Carhart's notch
A dip of bone conduction maximal at 2000 Hz, mechanical, disappearing after stapes surgery.
### Paracusis Willisii
Hearing better in noisy surroundings.
## Tumours
### Brown's sign
A glomus tumour pulsates, then blanches, as pressure in the canal is raised.
### Rising sun
A red glomus tumour seen rising from the floor of the middle ear behind the drum.
### Hitselberger's sign
Reduced sensation of the posterior canal wall, from pressure on the sensory fibres of the facial nerve by a vestibular schwannoma.
## Facial nerve
### Bell's phenomenon
The eye rolls up and out when the patient tries to close it.
## Mastoiditis
### Reservoir sign
The canal refills with pus straight after it is mopped.
### Light-house sign
Pulsatile discharge through a pars tensa perforation.

# Syndromes | Named collections of findings.
## Facial nerve
- **Ramsay Hunt:** herpes zoster oticus with facial palsy, often with hearing loss and vertigo.
- **Melkersson-Rosenthal:** recurrent facial palsy, swelling of the lips and a fissured tongue.
- **Heerfordt:** sarcoidosis with uveitis, parotid swelling and facial palsy.
- **Möbius:** congenital bilateral facial palsy with sixth nerve palsy.
- **Bogorad (crocodile tears):** tearing while eating after facial nerve regeneration.
- **Frey:** gustatory sweating after parotid surgery.
- **Bannwarth:** facial palsy in Lyme meningoradiculitis.
## Inner ear
- **Cogan:** interstitial keratitis with audiovestibular loss.
- **Vogt-Koyanagi-Harada:** uveitis, alopecia, white hair and deafness.
- **Lermoyez:** hearing improves with the vertigo attack.
- **Tumarkin's otolithic crisis:** sudden drop attacks in Ménière's disease.
- **Mal de débarquement:** a rocking sensation persisting after travel by sea or air.
- **van der Hoeve:** osteogenesis imperfecta, blue sclerae and deafness.
## Pain
- **Costen:** a faulty bite with earache, fullness, tinnitus and dizziness.
- **Eagle:** an elongated styloid process with throat and ear pain.
## Jugular foramen
- **Vernet:** palsy of the ninth, tenth and eleventh nerves.
- **Collet-Sicard:** ninth to twelfth nerves.
- **Villaret:** ninth to twelfth nerves with Horner syndrome.

# Spaces and landmarks | Named anatomy.
## Middle ear and mastoid
- **Prussak's space:** between the pars flaccida and the neck of the malleus; where attic cholesteatoma begins.
- **Trautmann's triangle:** bounded by the bony labyrinth, the sigmoid sinus and the superior petrosal sinus (dura); a route to the posterior fossa.
- **Citelli's angle:** the sinodural angle, between the sigmoid sinus and the middle fossa dura.
- **MacEwen's triangle:** the suprameatal triangle, marking the antrum.
- **Körner's septum:** a persistent petrosquamous plate in the mastoid, which can be mistaken for the floor of the antrum.

## Canal
- **Foramen of Huschke:** a defect in the anteroinferior bony canal in children, a route between the canal and the parotid.
- **Fissures of Santorini:** clefts in the cartilaginous canal through which infection passes to the parotid.
## Skull base
- **Dorello's canal:** where the sixth nerve passes under the petroclinoid ligament at the petrous tip.
- **Meckel's cave:** the trigeminal ganglion on the petrous apex.
- **Hyrtl's fissure:** an infant fissure from the hypotympanum to the subarachnoid space.
## Nerves
- **Arnold's nerve:** the auricular branch of the vagus; explains the ear cough reflex.
- **Jacobson's nerve:** the tympanic branch of the glossopharyngeal; origin of glomus tympanicum and route for referred pain.

# For the examination | Using this page.
!exam How to use the names
- Say what the sign is, then what it means, then which disease it points to.
- Know which tests are historical (Tobey-Ayer, Crowe-Beck) and what has replaced them (imaging).
- Link each landmark to the operation where it matters.
