@stand Oral H1 antihistamines relieve the histamine-driven symptoms of allergic rhinitis (sneezing, itch, watery rhinorrhoea and eye symptoms) within an hour, but do little for congestion. The second-generation drugs (cetirizine, loratadine, fexofenadine and their metabolites) are non-sedating or minimally sedating and are the ones to prescribe. The first-generation drugs such as chlorphenamine cross the blood-brain barrier, sedate and have anticholinergic effects.

# How they work | Mechanism and handling.
## Generations
Second-generation drugs are substrates of P-glycoprotein and are excluded from the brain, so they cause little sedation (cetirizine sedates a minority; fexofenadine is least sedating). First-generation drugs cross freely, sedate, impair driving even when the patient feels alert, and block muscarinic, alpha-adrenergic and serotonin receptors.

# Using them in ENT | Indications and doses.
## Allergic rhinitis
ARIA guidance: an oral antihistamine is reasonable for mild intermittent symptoms; for persistent or moderate to severe disease, a nasal steroid is more effective, and an intranasal antihistamine-steroid combination comes before adding an oral antihistamine. Oral antihistamines are useful for prominent eye symptoms and for patients who cannot use sprays.
## Angioedema and anaphylaxis
In anaphylaxis, **adrenaline** is the treatment; antihistamines are third-line and only treat skin symptoms. In histaminergic angioedema without airway involvement, a non-sedating antihistamine at up to four times the licensed dose (specialist advice) is used. Antihistamines do not work in **bradykinin-mediated** angioedema (ACE inhibitor or hereditary).

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- Second generation: headache, dry mouth, occasional drowsiness (cetirizine).
- First generation: **sedation**, psychomotor impairment, anticholinergic effects (urinary retention, confusion and falls in the elderly), paradoxical excitation in children.
- Older drugs such as terfenadine and astemizole caused QT prolongation and were withdrawn.
## Interactions
Alcohol and other sedatives (first generation); fruit juices reduce fexofenadine absorption; antacids with aluminium and magnesium reduce fexofenadine absorption.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- First- versus second-generation antihistamines.
- Which symptoms antihistamines do and do not treat.
- Their limited place in **anaphylaxis** and none in bradykinin angioedema.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Prescribing chlorphenamine for daily allergic rhinitis.
- Relying on antihistamines in anaphylaxis.
- Treating ACE inhibitor angioedema with antihistamines.
!!
