# Summary | Silent until they escape.
Sinonasal tumours hide behind the symptoms of ordinary sinusitis until they break out of their bony box into the cheek, palate, orbit or skull base. Every one-sided nasal mass is sent for histology. Each tumour has its own chapter below.
!tree Sinonasal tumours
Benign epithelial | Papillomas | [[@Squamous papilloma|squamous]], [[@Inverted papilloma|inverted]], oncocytic, exophytic
Benign other | [[@Vascular and other benign tumours|Haemangioma, schwannoma, meningioma]] | pleomorphic adenoma, chondroma, angiofibroma
Benign bony | [[@Benign bony tumours|Osteoma, fibrous dysplasia]] | ossifying fibroma, ameloblastoma
Malignant epithelial | [[@Carcinoma of the maxillary sinus|Squamous cell carcinoma]] | adenocarcinoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, undifferentiated carcinoma
Malignant other | [[@Mucosal melanoma|Melanoma]], [[@Olfactory neuroblastoma|olfactory neuroblastoma]] | lymphoma, plasmacytoma, sarcomas, haemangiopericytoma

# Epidemiology | Rare.
Sinonasal cancer is rare (well under 1% of all cancers). The maxillary sinus is the commonest site, then the ethmoids; frontal and sphenoid primaries are rare.

# Etiology | Risk factors.
- **Hardwood dust** (furniture workers): adenocarcinoma of the ethmoids and upper nose.
- **Nickel refining:** squamous and anaplastic carcinoma.
- **Leather work, chromium, mustard gas, formaldehyde.**
- **Snuff** rich in nickel and chromium (Bantu populations).
- **Smoking** and HPV for some squamous carcinomas.

# Pathophysiology | Histology.
More than 80% of malignant tumours are squamous cell carcinoma; the rest are adenocarcinoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, melanoma, olfactory neuroblastoma, lymphoma and sarcomas. The WHO classification (fourth edition 2017, fifth edition 2022) reorganised sinonasal tumours and added new entities, such as SMARCB1-deficient and HPV-related multiphenotypic carcinoma, so histology now carries more weight than ever.

# Squamous papilloma {dz} | Of the vestibule.
A warty lesion of the vestibule or anterior septum; excised.
!fig d39-1 | Squamous papilloma of the left nasal vestibule.

# Inverted papilloma {dz} | The one that isn't quite benign.
## Etiology and pathology
A Schneiderian papilloma in which the epithelium grows inwards into the stroma. Typically in men of 40 to 70, almost always one-sided, arising from the lateral wall near the middle meatus and extending into the maxillary and ethmoid sinuses. HPV is found in some.
## Behaviour: three warnings
- It destroys bone by pressure.
- It recurs if incompletely removed.
- It harbours dysplasia or squamous cell carcinoma in about 5 to 10%, so the whole specimen must be examined; carcinoma arising in it behaves like de novo squamous carcinoma.
## Clinical features
One-sided obstruction, discharge and bleeding, with a firm, greyish, irregular mass that can masquerade as a simple polyp.
!fig d39-2 d39-3 | First, inverted papilloma in a 79-year-old man, with its CT. Second, an inverted papilloma in the right nose looking like a simple polyp.
!fig d39-4 | Histology of a Schneiderian papilloma: ribbons of thickened epithelium growing into the stroma.
## Diagnosis
CT shows a one-sided mass and focal hyperostosis at the site of attachment; MRI shows a *convoluted cerebriform* pattern.
### Investigations: what and when
!table Inverted papilloma: investigations
Role | Test | When and what it shows
Diagnosis | Endoscopic biopsy of any unilateral polyp | Inverted papilloma; carcinoma in about 5 to 10%
Extent | CT of the sinuses | Focal hyperostosis marks the attachment site
Extent and malignancy | Contrast MRI | Convoluted cerebriform pattern; loss of it suggests carcinoma
Follow-up | Endoscopy for years; MRI when the cavity cannot be seen | Recurrence
!!
## Management
Complete removal including the mucoperiosteum and drilling of the bone at the site of attachment, now usually endoscopic (endoscopic medial maxillectomy) and formerly by lateral rhinotomy or midfacial degloving. Long-term endoscopic follow-up.
!update Current evidence
**Inverted papilloma.** Endoscopic resection with drilling of the attachment site gives lower recurrence than older open surgery.
!!

# Vascular and other benign tumours {dz} | Rarer benign masses.
- **Haemangioma** (lobular capillary haemangioma, *bleeding polyp of the septum*): a red, friable mass on the anterior septum, often in pregnancy; excised with its base.
- **Schwannoma, meningioma, chondroma, pleomorphic adenoma** of the septum.
- **Angiofibroma** ([[t:juv]]).
- **Encephalocele, glioma and dermoid** ([[t:congnose]]).
!fig d39-5a | A bleeding polyp arising from the right side of the septum.

# Benign bony tumours {dz} | Usually found by chance.
## Osteoma
The commonest benign sinus tumour, usually in the frontal sinus, then ethmoid; often an incidental finding on CT. Symptoms arise when it blocks drainage (mucocele, sinusitis), bulges into the orbit or erodes the skull base. Multiple osteomas suggest *Gardner syndrome* (with colonic polyposis). Removed endoscopically or through an external approach when symptomatic.
!fig d40-1 d40-2 | First, a frontoethmoidal osteoma invading the orbit. Second, an osteoma of the right frontal sinus.
## Fibrous dysplasia
Bone replaced by fibrous tissue and woven bone; *monostotic*, *polyostotic*, or with skin pigmentation and endocrine disorders (*McCune-Albright syndrome*). A painless swelling of the maxilla in a child or adolescent, with a *ground-glass* appearance on CT. Growth usually slows after puberty; conservative recontouring for deformity or compression.
!fig d40-3 | Fibrous dysplasia of the maxilla in a 13-year-old girl.
## Ossifying fibroma
A well-defined, expansile lesion with a bony rim, more aggressive in its juvenile form; excised completely.
## Ameloblastoma
An odontogenic tumour of the jaw, commonest in the mandible, that may invade the maxillary sinus; locally aggressive and recurrent unless widely excised.
!imaging osteoma fibrous-dysplasia-monostotic myxoma inverted-papilloma | Inverted papilloma, osteoma, fibrous dysplasia and myxoma.

# Carcinoma of the maxillary sinus {dz} | The classic sinonasal cancer.
## Clinical features
Usually men of 40 to 60. Early symptoms are easily mistaken for sinusitis: nasal stuffiness, bloodstained discharge, facial pain or numbness, and epiphora. Late features depend on the direction of spread.
!table Spread of maxillary carcinoma
Direction | Structures | Features
Medial | Nasal cavity, ethmoids | Obstruction, discharge, epistaxis
Anterior | Cheek | Swelling, then skin invasion
Inferior | Alveolus, palate | Toothache, loose teeth, ill-fitting dentures, palatal swelling
Superior | Orbit | Proptosis, diplopia, pain, epiphora
Posterior | Pterygoid plates and muscles, infratemporal fossa | Trismus, spread to nasopharynx and skull base
Intracranial | Through the ethmoids, cribriform plate or foramen lacerum | Neurological signs
!!
!fig d40-6 | Carcinoma of the left maxilla in a 75-year-old man: (A) swelling and redness of the cheek, (B) expansion of the upper alveolus with an intraoral growth, (C) CT showing destruction of the maxilla and orbital floor.
## Pathophysiology: lymph node and distant spread
Nodes are uncommon and late (submandibular and upper jugular); the first-echelon retropharyngeal nodes cannot be felt. Distant metastases go to the lungs and bone.
## Diagnosis
- **CT** in three planes: bone destruction and staging.
- **MRI:** separates tumour from retained secretions, and shows orbital, perineural and intracranial spread.
- **Biopsy:** endoscopic, or from the mouth or nose when the tumour presents there; the Caldwell-Luc route is no longer preferred.
- **Staging:** chest CT or PET-CT.
!imaging sinonasal-tumours malignancy-of-the-pterygopalatine-and-infratempo | Sinonasal malignancy and spread into the pterygopalatine and infratemporal fossae.
### Investigations: what and when
!table Sinonasal carcinoma: investigations
Role | Test | When and what it shows
First (clinic) | Nasal endoscopy; cranial nerve and eye examination; neck | Mass, orbital signs, V2 numbness, nodes
Bone extent | CT of the sinuses in three planes | Bone erosion; T stage
Soft tissue extent | Contrast MRI | Tumour versus retained secretions; orbit, perineural spread, dura and brain
Diagnosis (gold standard) | Endoscopic biopsy (after imaging, so a vascular or CSF-containing lesion is not biopsied blind) | Histology and subtype; immunohistochemistry
Staging | CT chest or PET-CT; neck imaging | Nodes and distant spread
Before treatment | Dental and ophthalmic assessment | Obturator planning; orbital function
!!
## Subtypes and variants: classifications
### Ohngren's line
An imaginary line from the medial canthus to the angle of the mandible. Tumours above and behind it (suprastructure) have a worse prognosis than those below and in front of it.
### Lederman's classification
Two horizontal lines of Sebileau, through the floors of the orbits and the floors of the antra, divide the region into *suprastructure* (ethmoid, sphenoid, frontal sinuses and olfactory area), *mesostructure* (maxillary sinus and respiratory nose) and *infrastructure* (alveolus).
!fig d40-7 d40-8 | First, Ohngren's line. Second, Lederman's classification.
### TNM for the sinuses (UICC TNM 9; T unchanged from the eighth edition)
!table T staging of maxillary sinus carcinoma
T | Extent
T1 | Limited to the mucosa, no bone erosion
T2 | Bone erosion including the hard palate or middle meatus, but not the posterior wall or pterygoid plates
T3 | Posterior wall, subcutaneous tissue, floor or medial wall of the orbit, pterygoid fossa, ethmoids
T4a | Anterior orbit, cheek skin, pterygoid plates, infratemporal fossa, cribriform plate, sphenoid or frontal sinus
T4b | Orbital apex, dura, brain, middle cranial fossa, cranial nerves other than V2, nasopharynx, clivus
!!
!table T staging of nasal cavity and ethmoid carcinoma (UICC TNM 9)
T | Extent
T1 | One subsite of the nasal cavity or ethmoid, with or without bone invasion
T2 | Two subsites of one site, or an adjacent site within the nasoethmoidal complex, with or without bone invasion
T3 | Medial wall or floor of the orbit, maxillary sinus, palate or cribriform plate
T4a | Anterior orbital contents, skin of nose or cheek, minimal extension into the anterior cranial fossa, pterygoid plates, sphenoid or frontal sinus
T4b | Orbital apex, dura, brain, middle cranial fossa, cranial nerves other than V2, nasopharynx or clivus
!!
!table Sinonasal carcinoma: stage groups (UICC TNM 9)
Stage | TNM
I / II | T1 N0 / T2 N0
III | T3 N0, or T1–T3 N1
IVA | T1–T3 N2, or T4a N0–N2
IVB | T4b any N, or any T N3
IVC | M1
!!
N categories are those of the other HPV-independent head and neck sites (cN1 to cN3b), with unequivocal ENE on imaging counting for cN3b in TNM 9.
## Management
### Surgery
Resection with clear margins, followed by radiotherapy for most stages:
- **Medial maxillectomy** (endoscopic or open) for medial tumours.
- **Total maxillectomy** through a *Weber-Fergusson* incision (lateral rhinotomy with lip split, extended below the eye), with **orbital exenteration** when the orbit is invaded beyond the periosteum.
- **Craniofacial resection** when the cribriform plate or anterior skull base is involved.
- **Endoscopic resection** now achieves oncological outcomes equivalent to open surgery for most tumours, with less morbidity, provided clear margins can be obtained.
!fig d40-9 d40-10 | First, the Weber-Fergusson incision. Second, maxillectomy with orbital exenteration, before and after rehabilitation.
### Reconstruction and rehabilitation
A dental obturator closes the palatal defect, or a free flap reconstructs it; orbital prostheses after exenteration.
### Radiotherapy and chemotherapy
Postoperative radiotherapy for most; primary chemoradiotherapy for unresectable tumours; neoadjuvant chemotherapy in selected cases: advanced tumours involving the orbit or skull base may first have induction chemotherapy, with the response guiding radiotherapy or surgery.
### Neck
Neck dissection or irradiation for nodal disease, and elective treatment in some advanced tumours. Nodal metastases at presentation suggest a high-grade tumour such as squamous carcinoma or SNUC.
!ops
!update Current evidence
**Endoscopic surgery.** The European position paper on endoscopic management of sinonasal and skull base tumours concluded that, in selected patients and experienced hands, endoscopic resection gives survival comparable to open surgery with less morbidity (Lund et al., *Rhinology Suppl* 2010); later large series and database studies support this for appropriately selected T1 to T3 tumours.
!!
### Treatment steps
!table Sinonasal carcinoma: treatment
Situation | Treatment | Notes

High-risk features (positive margins, extranodal spread) | Postoperative chemoradiotherapy with cisplatin (commonly 100 mg/m² every 3 weeks) | 
Unresectable, or orbit preservation strategies | Chemoradiotherapy; induction chemotherapy in selected tumours |

!!
Based on the UK national multidisciplinary guidelines for sinonasal tumours (Lund et al., *J Laryngol Otol* 2016) and the European position paper on endoscopic management of tumours of the nose, sinuses and skull base.
## Prognosis
Five-year survival is around a third to a half overall, worse with orbital apex, skull base or intracranial spread.

# Tumours of the ethmoid, frontal and sphenoid {dz} | Rarer sites.
## Ethmoid
Often adenocarcinoma in woodworkers. **Intestinal-type adenocarcinoma**, linked to hardwood dust, looks like bowel cancer under the microscope but is not a metastasis; it is often multifocal, so wide removal of the sinus mucosa is advised. Low-grade non-intestinal adenocarcinoma may need surgery alone; high-grade disease needs radiotherapy too. Nasal obstruction, bloodstained discharge, a mass in the medial canthus, proptosis with lateral displacement of the eye, and early skull base spread. Craniofacial or endoscopic resection with radiotherapy.
!fig d40-11 | Carcinoma of the ethmoid.
## Frontal
Very rare: forehead swelling, frontal pain, displacement of the eye downwards, and early intracranial spread.
## Sphenoid
Usually secondary or from the nasopharynx; presents with cranial nerve palsies (second to sixth) and headache.

# Olfactory neuroblastoma {dz} | Esthesioneuroblastoma.
*Esthesioneuroblastoma*, from the olfactory epithelium at the roof of the nose, at any age with peaks in the second and sixth decades. One-sided obstruction, epistaxis and loss of smell, with a mass high in the nose that may extend through the cribriform plate (a *dumb-bell* shape). Staged by the Kadish system (A nasal cavity, B sinuses, C beyond), which discriminates poorly, so the Dulguerov TNM-based system is often preferred. Somatostatin-receptor PET (DOTATATE) helps stage and follow it, as it does neuroendocrine tumours. Treated by craniofacial or endoscopic resection with radiotherapy. Up to 20% spread to the neck, sometimes late, so long-term neck surveillance is needed.
!table Olfactory neuroblastoma: staging and grading
System | Categories
Modified Kadish (Morita 1993) | A nasal cavity; B nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses; C beyond them (orbit, skull base, intracranial); D cervical nodes or distant metastasis
Dulguerov TNM | T1 nasal cavity and/or sinuses (not the sphenoid), sparing the uppermost ethmoid cells; T2 including the sphenoid, or extending to or eroding the cribriform plate; T3 into the orbit or the anterior cranial fossa without dural invasion; T4 into the brain; N1 any cervical node; M1 distant
Hyams grade | I to IV from the histology; I–II low grade, III–IV high grade, with worse survival and recurrence
UICC/AJCC | No separate classification; the nasal cavity and ethmoid TNM can be applied
!!
Neither Kadish nor Dulguerov includes grade; adding the Hyams grade to either improved prediction of recurrence in a 2023 multicentre series (Choby et al., *JAMA Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg* 2023), but this is not yet an official system.
!imaging meningioma-with-skull-base-destruction | A meningioma destroying the skull base, a differential of olfactory neuroblastoma.

# Mucosal melanoma {dz} | Pigmented or amelanotic.
Older adults, on the septum or lateral wall; pigmented or amelanotic, bleeding; spreads by blood and lymph and has a poor prognosis; wide excision and radiotherapy.

T3 | Limited to the epithelium and/or submucosa (mucosal disease); T1 and T2 do not exist
T4a | Deep soft tissue, cartilage, bone or overlying skin
T4b | Brain, dura, skull base, lower cranial nerves (IX to XII), masticator space, carotid artery, prevertebral space or mediastinum
N1 | Any regional node
Stage III | T3 N0
Stage IVA | T4a N0, or T3–T4a N1
Stage IVB | T4b any N
Stage IVC | M1

# Other malignant tumours {dz} | Each with its clue.
## Lymphoma, plasmacytoma and sarcomas
NK/T-cell lymphoma ([[t:gpa]]), extramedullary plasmacytoma, and rhabdomyosarcoma in children.
!fig d39-7 | Rhabdomyosarcoma of the nose in a two-and-a-half-year-old boy.
## Sinonasal undifferentiated carcinoma (SNUC)
Aggressive, often presenting with intracranial and nodal spread; it responds well to **induction chemotherapy** followed by chemoradiotherapy, with surgery when the response is poor.
!update Current evidence
**SNUC.** In a multicentre study, patients whose SNUC responded to induction chemotherapy did best with definitive chemoradiotherapy, whereas non-responders did best with surgery followed by chemoradiotherapy (Amit et al., *J Clin Oncol* 2019).
!!
## NUT carcinoma
Defined by a *NUTM1* rearrangement; affects young adults and has a very poor prognosis.
## Neuroendocrine carcinoma
Small and large cell types, treated with combined chemotherapy and radiotherapy.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Treating one-sided sinus symptoms with antibiotics for months.
- Removing a one-sided polyp without sending it for histology.
- Calling numbness of the cheek sinusitis.
