@stand Two cancers share this chapter and differ in almost everything except their histology. Oral cancer is visible, tied to tobacco, areca nut and alcohol, common in South Asia, and treated first by surgery. Oropharyngeal cancer hides behind the tongue and in the tonsil, presents with a lump in the neck, is increasingly caused by human papillomavirus in younger non-smokers, and is often treated by radiation. Both demand an examination that includes palpation, and both punish delay.

# Overview | Two regions, one disease.
## Subsites
- **Oral cavity:** mucosal lip, buccal mucosa, oral tongue (anterior two-thirds), floor of mouth, upper and lower alveolus (gingiva), retromolar trigone and hard palate.
- **Oropharynx:** base of tongue, tonsil and tonsillar fossa with the pillars, soft palate and uvula (the faucial arch), and the posterior and lateral pharyngeal walls.
!anat s166_10 s211_28 | The subsites of the oral cavity, and the oropharynx through the open mouth.
## Epidemiology
India carries one of the highest burdens of oral cancer in the world: lip, tongue and mouth together make up about a fifth of all male cancers there, with buccal mucosa and tongue the leading sites, driven by tobacco chewing with lime, pan and areca nut, bidi smoking and alcohol. In the West the lip is the commonest oral subsite and tongue cancer is rising in young adults. Oropharyngeal cancer is rising rapidly in Europe and North America because of HPV.
## Causes
- **Tobacco:** smoking increases oral cancer about sixfold; chewing tobacco places carcinogen at the site where the quid is held; *reverse smoking* (the burning end inside the mouth) causes palatal cancer.
- **Alcohol:** multiplies, rather than adds to, the effect of tobacco.
- **Areca nut and pan:** directly and through submucous fibrosis.
- **Human papillomavirus (HPV 16):** most cancers of the tonsil and base of tongue in North America; associated with oral sexual behaviour rather than tobacco.
- **Others:** chronic trauma from jagged teeth and dentures, poor oral hygiene, Plummer–Vinson syndrome, immunosuppression, sunlight (lower lip), and rare syndromes (Fanconi anaemia).
## Field cancerisation
Tobacco and alcohol alter the whole mucosa of the upper aerodigestive tract, so dysplasia and cancer arise in more than one place. About 15% of patients have, or will develop, a second primary in the head and neck, oesophagus or lung.
## Pathology
Squamous cell carcinoma in about 90%; minor salivary gland carcinomas (adenoid cystic, mucoepidermoid, adenocarcinoma), lymphoma (especially tonsil and base of tongue), melanoma (palate and gum) and Kaposi sarcoma make up the rest. Verrucous carcinoma is a slow, warty, well-differentiated variant of the buccal mucosa that rarely metastasises.

# Clinical picture | Look, feel, and feel the neck.
## Gross forms
Exophytic, ulcerative (a non-healing ulcer with raised, rolled, everted edges, a shaggy base and induration), infiltrative, and superficially spreading (often arising in leukoplakia or erythroplakia).
## Symptoms
- An ulcer or lump that does not heal; bleeding.
- Pain, often *referred to the ear* through the lingual and auriculotemporal nerves (V3) or the glossopharyngeal nerve.
- Difficulty in chewing, swallowing and speaking; a *hot-potato voice* with tongue base tumours; loosening of teeth or ill-fitting dentures; trismus when the pterygoids or masseter are invaded; numbness of the lip from inferior alveolar nerve invasion.
- A lump in the neck, sometimes the only complaint.
- Foul breath, weight loss and, late, a fungating mass or orocutaneous fistula.
!flag Referral now
A mouth ulcer or white or red patch that persists beyond three weeks, an unexplained lump in the mouth or neck, unexplained tooth mobility, persistent one-sided sore throat or earache with a normal ear: refer urgently for examination and biopsy.
!!
## Examination
Inspect every subsite with good light, palpate the lesion for induration and depth, palpate bimanually the floor of mouth and tongue, examine the tongue base and tonsils by palpation and endoscopy, and examine both sides of the neck level by level. Cranial nerves V, VII and XII; mouth opening; teeth.

# Oral cavity cancer | Site by site.
## Lip
The commonest oral subsite in the West; more than 95% squamous, 95% on the *lower lip*, between the midline and the commissure, in older men with outdoor occupations; basal cell carcinoma is relatively commoner on the upper lip. Early blistering, crusting or ulceration on a background of actinic cheilitis. Nodes (submental and submandibular, bilateral near the midline) are late and occur in only about 10%.
!fig d44-10 | Carcinoma of the upper lip and oral commissure, with leukoplakia of the buccal mucosa.
### Treatment
Excision with a safe margin. Defects up to a third of the lip close primarily after a V or W excision; a third to a half with lip-switch flaps (Abbe, or Estlander at the commissure); a half to two-thirds with a Karapandzic flap; larger defects with Gillies fan, Bernard–Burow or free flaps, always realigning the vermilion border. Radiotherapy is an alternative for small lesions; neck dissection for nodes. Cure exceeds 90% in early disease; upper lip and commissure tumours do worse.
!ops 27 | Surgery for cancer of the lip.
## Buccal mucosa
Very common in India, equal in both sexes, at the angle of the mouth, along the occlusal line or in the gingivobuccal sulcus where the quid is kept; often multicentric because the whole cheek is "condemned". Exophytic or ulceroinfiltrative; it spreads deeply through buccinator and fat to the skin, back to the retromolar trigone and pterygoids (trismus), and up and down into the maxilla and mandible. About half have nodes (submandibular, then upper jugular). Verrucous carcinoma favours this site.
!fig d44-11 | Carcinoma of the buccal mucosa.
### Treatment
T1 lesions by excision. Larger lesions, and any with muscle or bone involvement, by wide excision with marginal or segmental mandibulectomy or partial maxillectomy, reconstruction with local, pectoralis major or free flaps, neck dissection, and postoperative radiotherapy for stage III and IV.
## Oral tongue
Usually men of fifty to seventy, though increasingly young adults; may arise in leukoplakia, at a chronic dental ulcer, or in smokers and chewers. The commonest site is the *middle of the lateral border* and the ventral surface; the tip and dorsum less often. It appears as an exophytic papillary growth, an indurated ulcer with rolled edges, or a submucosal indurated nodule. It infiltrates the tongue musculature (fixation, slurred speech), the floor of mouth and mandible. Nodes: the lateral tongue drains to the submandibular and upper jugular nodes (and can skip to level IV), the tip to the submental and jugulo-omohyoid nodes; midline and ventral lesions spread to both sides.
!fig d44-12 d44-13 | First, carcinoma of the lateral border of the tongue (arrow) with leukoplakia of the floor of mouth (double arrows). Second, an ulcerative squamous carcinoma of the tongue in a 40-year-old woman.
!fig d44-14 d44-15 | First, an exophytic growth on the right lateral border of the tongue. Second, cancer of the undersurface of the tongue.
!pearl Depth of invasion
The thickness of a tongue cancer predicts nodal spread better than its width: occult metastases rise sharply beyond about 4 mm. Current staging (AJCC 8th edition) therefore includes depth of invasion in the T category, and elective neck dissection is advised for tumours deeper than about 3 to 4 mm.
!!
### Treatment
T1 and T2: partial glossectomy (transoral or with a pull-through), healing by secondary intention, primary closure, graft or free flap; radiotherapy (external beam with brachytherapy) is an alternative. Elective selective neck dissection of levels I to III (supraomohyoid) for an N0 neck when depth exceeds the threshold, bilateral for midline lesions; sentinel node biopsy is an alternative in early cancers. Advanced lesions: hemiglossectomy or total glossectomy with the floor of mouth, mandibulectomy and neck dissection (the *commando operation*), free flap reconstruction and postoperative radiotherapy or chemoradiation; total glossectomy may require laryngectomy to prevent aspiration.
## Floor of mouth
Squamous carcinoma, men four times more than women, usually starting anteriorly near the submandibular duct openings, which may obstruct and enlarge the gland. Ulcerative or infiltrative, spreading to the ventral tongue, lingual gingiva, mandible and submental space; a submandibular swelling may be gland or node. Small lesions by excision or radiotherapy; larger ones by wide excision with marginal or segmental mandibulectomy; elective neck treatment even for N0 stage II disease, since occult nodes occur in about 40%; nodes often bilateral.
!fig d44-16 | Ulcerative squamous carcinoma of the floor of mouth (arrows) in a 55-year-old man.
## Alveolus (gingiva)
Mostly men, on the lower jaw behind the first molar; early invasion of the bone and along the inferior alveolar nerve; nodes to the submandibular and upper jugular groups. Surgery is preferred because radiotherapy risks osteoradionecrosis: marginal mandibulectomy for superficial lesions, segmental or hemimandibulectomy when the medulla is invaded; partial maxillectomy for upper alveolar lesions.
## Retromolar trigone
Primary, or by extension from the gingiva, floor of mouth, buccal mucosa or faucial arch; it reaches the pterygomandibular raphe, pterygoids (trismus) and mandible early. Wide excision with mandibular resection and neck dissection.
!fig d44-17 | Cancer of the right retromolar trigone with trismus in a 36-year-old man.
## Hard palate
Squamous or, more often, minor salivary gland carcinoma (adenoid cystic, mucoepidermoid, adenocarcinoma); squamous cancer is linked to reverse smoking. A painless irregularity felt with the tongue, a superficial ulcer, or a submucosal mass; it may invade the nasal floor and antrum and must be told apart from an antral carcinoma breaking through. Resection with the underlying bone or a partial maxillectomy, closed with an obturator prosthesis or flap.
!fig d44-18 | A mixed salivary tumour of the palate.
!ops 28 29 30 32 181 182 | Glossectomy, floor of mouth excision, transoral inferior maxillectomy and mandibulectomy.
## The mandible
Tumour reaches the mandible through the alveolar crest in the dentate jaw and through the occlusal surface in the edentulous jaw. Periosteal involvement needs at least a *marginal (rim) mandibulectomy*, preserving the lower border; invasion of the medulla, or an edentulous atrophic mandible, needs a *segmental* resection, reconstructed with a fibula free flap. Radiotherapy to a mandible already invaded leads to osteoradionecrosis.

# Oropharyngeal cancer | Behind the tongue.
## HPV and the changing disease
!table HPV-positive and HPV-negative oropharyngeal cancer
Feature | HPV-positive | HPV-negative
Typical patient | Younger, healthier, male (about 9:1), non-smoker | Older, smoker and drinker
Cause | HPV 16; oral sexual exposure | Tobacco and alcohol
Marker | p16 overexpression on immunohistochemistry | p16 negative
Primary | Often small, in the tonsil or tongue base crypts | Often larger, infiltrative
Nodes | Early, often large and *cystic* | Solid
Prognosis | Much better; staged separately (AJCC 8th) | Worse
!!
The viral E6 and E7 oncoproteins inactivate p53 and Rb, and p16 is overexpressed as a result. Vaccination against HPV is expected to prevent most of these cancers.
!flag The cystic neck lump in an adult
A cystic lump in the upper neck of an adult is a metastasis from an HPV-related tonsil or tongue base cancer until proven otherwise, not a branchial cyst. Examine and image the oropharynx, aspirate for p16 or HPV, and if no primary is seen, perform tonsillectomy and tongue base biopsy or mucosectomy.
!!
## Base of tongue
Silent for a long time; early symptoms (soreness, a lump feeling, mild dysphagia) are attributed to the lingual tonsil. Many present with nodes, often bilateral: about 70% have neck metastases at presentation, so the tongue base is a classic site of the occult primary. Late: referred otalgia, dysphagia, bleeding, a hot-potato voice and tongue fixation. Deeply infiltrative into the tongue, vallecula, pre-epiglottic space, epiglottis and tonsil. It is larger on palpation than it looks in the mirror: always palpate, preferably under anaesthesia.
!fig d53-1 d53-2 | First, an exophytic growth at the base of the tongue. Second, carcinoma of the right base of tongue: (A) endoscopic view, (B) secondaries in the jugulodigastric nodes.
## Tonsil and tonsillar fossa
Squamous carcinoma presents as an ulcer with a necrotic base, or as a smooth, enlarged tonsil when it lies within the crypts; lymphoma as a smooth, one-sided enlargement, sometimes ulcerated. Persistent sore throat, dysphagia, otalgia and a neck lump, later bleeding, fetor and trismus. It spreads to the palate, pillars, tongue base, pharyngeal wall, pterygoids and mandible, and the parapharyngeal space; about half have nodes at presentation, the jugulodigastric node first.
!fig d53-3 d53-4 | First, squamous cell carcinoma involving the tonsil, pillars and soft palate. Second, lymphoma of the right tonsil presenting as one-sided tonsillar enlargement.

## Faucial arch (soft palate, uvula, anterior pillar)
Usually well-differentiated, superficially spreading squamous carcinoma on the oral surface, noticed early and slow to metastasise, behaving more like oral cancer; often accompanied by other primaries. Radiotherapy or transoral excision.
!fig d53-5 | An ulcerative lesion of the palatine arch: a well-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.
## Posterior and lateral pharyngeal walls
Silent, spreading submucosally up to the nasopharynx and down to the hypopharynx, and back to the prevertebral fascia; nodes in about 60%, often bilateral and retropharyngeal. Radiotherapy or excision through a lateral pharyngotomy, often with mandibulotomy, and neck dissection.
## Benign tumours of the oropharynx
Papilloma (pedunculated, on the tonsil, palate or pillars; excised), haemangioma (treated only if growing or bleeding), pleomorphic adenoma of the palate (excised completely), vallecular mucous cysts (excised or marsupialised), and rarely lipoma, fibroma and neuroma. Parapharyngeal tumours (deep-lobe parotid, schwannoma, paraganglioma, lipoma, carotid aneurysm) push the tonsil and palate medially and mimic oropharyngeal tumours: never biopsy through the mouth without imaging.

# Evaluation and staging | Before treatment.
## Investigations
- **Biopsy** of the primary; fine-needle aspiration of nodes; p16 immunohistochemistry (and HPV testing) for every oropharyngeal cancer.
- **CT and MRI** of the primary and neck: depth, bone invasion (CT and orthopantomogram for the mandible), extension into the pterygoids, parapharyngeal space, pre-epiglottic space and skull base; nodes, including retropharyngeal.
- **Chest CT or PET-CT** for distant metastases and second primaries; PET-CT also for the unknown primary.
- **Panendoscopy** under anaesthesia (laryngoscopy, pharyngoscopy, oesophagoscopy) to map the tumour and look for synchronous cancers.
- Dental assessment before radiotherapy; nutrition and swallowing assessment.
## TNM essentials
!table T staging (AJCC 8th edition)
Category | Oral cavity | Oropharynx
T1 | 2 cm or less, depth of invasion 5 mm or less | 2 cm or less
T2 | 2 cm or less with depth over 5 mm, or 2 to 4 cm with depth 10 mm or less | Over 2 cm, up to 4 cm
T3 | Over 4 cm, or depth over 10 mm | Over 4 cm, or extension to the lingual surface of the epiglottis
T4a | Cortical bone, maxillary sinus, skin of face (and, for lip, inferior alveolar nerve or floor of mouth) | Larynx, extrinsic tongue muscle, medial pterygoid, hard palate or mandible
T4b | Masticator space, pterygoid plates, skull base, or encasing the internal carotid | Lateral pterygoid, pterygoid plates, lateral nasopharynx, skull base, or encasing the carotid
!!
For oral cavity and HPV-negative oropharyngeal cancer, N staging follows node size, number and side, and *extranodal extension* (which upstages); for HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancer the N categories are simpler and the stages lower, reflecting the better prognosis. Dhingra prints the older (6th edition) tables, based on size alone.

# Treatment | By site, stage and the person.
## Principles
Treatment is planned by a multidisciplinary team (surgeons, radiation and medical oncologists, radiologists, pathologists, reconstructive surgeons, dentists, speech and swallowing therapists, dietitians), balancing cure against speech, swallowing and appearance.
- **Oral cavity:** surgery first. Early (stage I and II): excision with a margin, with elective neck treatment according to depth. Advanced (III and IV): resection, reconstruction and neck dissection, followed by radiotherapy, or chemoradiation when margins are positive or there is extranodal extension.
- **Oropharynx:** early: radiotherapy (IMRT) or transoral surgery (robotic or laser) with neck dissection, both treating the neck. Advanced: concurrent chemoradiation with cisplatin (organ preservation), or surgery with adjuvant (chemo)radiation; surgery through a mandibulotomy is mostly reserved for salvage. Radiosensitive lymphoepithelioma and lymphoma are treated by radiotherapy or chemotherapy.
- **Systemic agents:** cisplatin is the standard radiosensitiser; cetuximab (an EGFR antibody) is an alternative for those unfit for cisplatin, though inferior in HPV-positive disease; immunotherapy for recurrent and metastatic disease.
## The neck
- **N0:** elective selective neck dissection (levels I to III for oral cavity, II to IV for oropharynx) or elective radiotherapy when the risk of occult metastasis exceeds about 20%; sentinel node biopsy in early oral cancer.
- **N+:** comprehensive (modified radical) neck dissection, followed by radiotherapy for multiple nodes or extranodal extension.
!anat s286_80 | The lymph node levels of the neck.
## Reconstruction
Primary closure, skin grafts, local flaps (nasolabial, tongue, facial artery musculomucosal), regional flaps (pectoralis major, deltopectoral) and free flaps (radial forearm for the tongue and floor, anterolateral thigh for bulk, fibula for the mandible). Tracheostomy protects the airway after major resections.
!ops | Glossectomy, floor of mouth and tonsil surgery, base of tongue resection, neck dissection and free flaps.
!imaging | Tonsillar carcinoma, base of tongue tumour, oral and oropharyngeal squamous carcinoma, and synchronous malignancies.
## Palliation
Radiotherapy or chemotherapy for pain and bleeding, tracheostomy and gastrostomy for airway and nutrition, and good analgesia and palliative care.
## Prognosis
Early oral cancer is cured in most patients; advanced disease in roughly a third to a half. Extranodal extension, nodes low in the neck, positive margins and perineural invasion worsen survival. HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancer has markedly better survival than HPV-negative. Lifelong follow-up looks for recurrence (most in the first two years) and second primaries, and stopping smoking improves outcome.
## Chemoprevention
Retinoids, beta-carotene and vitamin E can induce regression of leukoplakia and reduce second primaries while taken, but no agent has replaced stopping tobacco, areca nut and alcohol.

# Non-squamous cancers | The others.
- **Minor salivary gland carcinoma:** 80 to 90% of minor salivary tumours are malignant in some series; the palate is the commonest site; adenoid cystic (about 40%, with perineural spread), adenocarcinoma and mucoepidermoid. Wide excision with neck dissection for nodes, and radiotherapy.
- **Mucosal melanoma:** palate and gum, sixth decade, men twice as often; pigmented, later ulcerated, sometimes amelanotic; wide excision including bone; five-year survival around 15%.
- **Lymphoma:** mostly non-Hodgkin, in the tonsil and tongue base, as a smooth bulky submucosal mass, with neck nodes in many; chemotherapy and radiotherapy.
- **Kaposi sarcoma:** reddish-purple palatal plaques, mainly in AIDS; responds to antiretroviral treatment, radiotherapy or chemotherapy.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Treating a persistent ulcer with mouthwashes rather than a biopsy.
- Judging a tongue base cancer by looking without feeling.
- Excising a cystic neck lump as a branchial cyst in an adult without looking for a primary.
- Irradiating a mandible already invaded by tumour.
!!
