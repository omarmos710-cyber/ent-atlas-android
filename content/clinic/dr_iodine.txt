@stand Operating on a thyrotoxic patient risks thyroid storm, a life-threatening surge of thyroid hormone with fever, tachycardia, arrhythmia, heart failure and agitation. Patients are prepared with antithyroid drugs and beta blockers, and potassium iodide (as Lugol's iodine or tablets) is given for about 10 days before thyroidectomy for Graves' disease to reduce thyroid hormone release and gland vascularity. Thyroid storm itself is treated with a sequence of drugs given in a particular order.

# At a glance | The drugs on one page.
## Summary
!table Preoperative preparation for thyroid surgery
 | Summary
Potassium iodide | Lugol's iodine (aqueous iodine oral solution, about 130 mg/ml total iodine) 0.1 to 0.3 ml three times daily in milk or water, or potassium iodide tablets, for 7 to 10 days before surgery in Graves' disease
How iodine works | High iodide concentrations acutely inhibit hormone synthesis (Wolff-Chaikoff effect) and release, and reduce gland vascularity; the effect wanes after 10 to 14 days (escape)
Beta blockers | Propranolol 40 mg three or four times daily (up to 80 mg four times daily) to control heart rate and tremor; also reduces peripheral T4 to T3 conversion at high doses; use cardioselective agents or diltiazem in asthma
Steroids | Hydrocortisone or dexamethasone reduce T4 to T3 conversion and treat relative adrenal insufficiency in storm
Cholestyramine | Binds thyroid hormone in the gut and reduces enterohepatic recirculation; an adjunct for rapid preparation
Plasma exchange | For refractory cases before urgent surgery
!!

# Using them in ENT | Practice.
## Elective surgery
The patient should be clinically and biochemically euthyroid on carbimazole (or PTU), with beta blockade for symptoms. Iodine is then added for the last 7 to 10 days in Graves' disease. Toxic nodular goitre does not usually need iodine.
## Thyroid storm
Recognise it by fever, tachycardia or atrial fibrillation, heart failure, agitation or confusion and gastrointestinal upset (the Burch-Wartofsky score helps). Treat in critical care:
!path Thyroid storm
Block synthesis | PTU (loading 500 to 1,000 mg, then 250 mg every 4 hours) or carbimazole or methimazole
Block release | iodine, given at least an hour after the thionamide, so it is not used to make more hormone
Block peripheral effects | propranolol (or esmolol) ; hydrocortisone 300 mg then 100 mg every 8 hours
Support | cooling, fluids, treatment of heart failure and arrhythmias, paracetamol (not aspirin, which displaces T4 from binding proteins) ; find and treat the trigger
!!

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
Iodine: metallic taste, sialadenitis and salivary swelling ("iodide mumps"), rash, gastrointestinal upset, hypersensitivity. Beta blockers: bronchospasm, bradycardia, heart failure.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Giving iodine before the thionamide in thyroid storm.
- Proceeding with elective surgery in a thyrotoxic patient.
!!
