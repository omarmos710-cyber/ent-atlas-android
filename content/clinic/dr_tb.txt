@stand Tuberculosis reaches the ENT clinic as a neck lump far more often than as anything else, but also as laryngeal tuberculosis mimicking cancer, painless otorrhoea with multiple perforations, and sinonasal or oral ulceration. The treatment is a fixed combination given for at least 6 months under directly observed or closely supervised therapy by a tuberculosis team. The ENT surgeon's part is the diagnosis, the samples that make drug-susceptibility testing possible, and knowing the drugs' toxicities, including the ototoxicity of streptomycin and amikacin.

# At a glance | The drugs on one page.
## Summary
!table Antituberculous drugs
 | Summary
First-line drugs | Rifampicin (R), isoniazid (H), pyrazinamide (Z), ethambutol (E)
How they work | Rifampicin blocks bacterial RNA polymerase; isoniazid blocks mycolic acid synthesis; pyrazinamide acts in acidic intracellular sites; ethambutol blocks arabinogalactan synthesis
Standard regimen | 2 months of RHZE (intensive phase) then 4 months of RH (continuation phase): 6 months in total for lymph node, laryngeal and most extrapulmonary tuberculosis; 12 months for central nervous system disease
Adjuncts | Pyridoxine 10 mg daily with isoniazid to prevent neuropathy; steroids for tuberculous meningitis and pericarditis
Drug-resistant disease | Bedaquiline, linezolid, fluoroquinolones, delamanid, pretomanid and injectables (amikacin) under specialist care
Doses | By weight: rifampicin about 10 mg/kg, isoniazid 5 mg/kg, pyrazinamide 25 mg/kg, ethambutol 15 mg/kg once daily, given as fixed-dose combination tablets
Pregnancy | First-line drugs are used; streptomycin is contraindicated (fetal ototoxicity)
!!

# How they work | Mechanism and handling.
## Why combinations and long courses
Mycobacteria divide slowly and exist in different populations: actively dividing extracellular bacilli (killed by isoniazid), slowly metabolising intracellular bacilli in acidic phagosomes (pyrazinamide), and intermittently active bacilli (rifampicin). Combinations prevent the emergence of resistance from spontaneous mutations; six months are needed to sterilise the lesions and prevent relapse.

# Using them in ENT | What ENT surgeons do.
## Diagnosis first
For a suspected **tuberculous lymphadenitis** (scrofula: a matted, painless, often cold neck mass with possible sinus formation), obtain tissue: ultrasound-guided core or FNA with samples for **mycobacterial culture and PCR** (GeneXpert, which also detects rifampicin resistance) as well as histology, or an excision biopsy. Do not incise and drain a tuberculous node: a chronic discharging sinus results. Send a chest radiograph, HIV test and contact history. Notify the case.
## Paradoxical reactions
Nodes may enlarge or new ones appear during the first months of treatment (paradoxical upgrading reaction) in up to a fifth: this is not treatment failure. Aspirate fluctuant nodes; consider steroids for severe reactions.
## Non-tuberculous mycobacteria in children
Atypical mycobacterial lymphadenitis (violaceous skin over a node in a well child aged 1 to 5) is not tuberculosis: excision is curative and antituberculous drugs are ineffective; clarithromycin with rifampicin is used when excision is unsafe.

# Safety | What goes wrong.
## Adverse effects
- **Hepatotoxicity** with rifampicin, isoniazid and pyrazinamide: baseline liver function and review if symptoms or abnormal results.
- **Rifampicin**: orange-red urine, tears and secretions (stains contact lenses); flu-like syndrome; potent enzyme induction.
- **Isoniazid**: peripheral neuropathy (prevent with pyridoxine), hepatitis, drug-induced lupus.
- **Pyrazinamide**: hyperuricaemia, gout, arthralgia, hepatotoxicity.
- **Ethambutol**: **optic neuritis** with loss of colour vision and acuity; baseline visual acuity and colour testing, and stop at once if vision changes.
- **Streptomycin and amikacin**: vestibular and cochlear ototoxicity, nephrotoxicity; audiometry during treatment.
## Interactions
Rifampicin induces CYP enzymes and lowers the levels of **oral contraceptives**, warfarin, DOACs, steroids (double the steroid dose), antiretrovirals, ciclosporin, azoles, methadone and many others.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- The standard **RHZE regimen** and its duration.
- How to sample a suspected tuberculous node (culture, PCR, not incision and drainage).
- The key toxicities: liver, **ethambutol and vision**, isoniazid and neuropathy, **streptomycin and the ear**.
- Rifampicin interactions, especially contraception.
- Atypical mycobacteria in children: excision, not antituberculous drugs.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Sending a node only for histology in formalin, with nothing for culture.
- Incising and draining a tuberculous node.
- Mistaking a paradoxical reaction for treatment failure.
- Forgetting pyridoxine or baseline vision testing.
!!
