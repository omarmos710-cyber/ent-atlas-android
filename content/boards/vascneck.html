PARAGANGLIOMAS, NEURAL TUMOURS & VASCULAR ANOMALIES

never needle a lump at the bifurcation · haemangioma grows then involutes, a malformation grows with the child ✎

1Paraganglioma biology

  • Neural-crest paraganglia · chief (I) + sustentacular (II) cells = Zellballen, very vascular
  • Carotid body ~half to 60% · jugulare · tympanicum · vagale ~5–10%
  • Up to 40% hereditary (SDHB, C, D): younger, multiple, bilateral (~a third), phaeos · SDHB most malignant · test every patient
  • Malignant 5–10% carotid (more vagal) · defined only by metastasis
  • Only 1–3% secrete, but screen all: plasma free metanephrines or 24-hour urine (find a phaeo before anaesthesia)
  • High-altitude hypoxia → carotid body hyperplasia

2Carotid body tumour

  • Painless mass at bifurcation, anterior SCM, hyoid level · ~1 mm a year (doubles in four to five years)
  • Fontaine: side to side, not vertical · pulsation, bruit, oropharyngeal bulge · palsies uncommon · pain/rapid growth → malignancy
  • CT/MRI: splays ICA + ECA · MRI salt-and-pepper · angiography lyre sign · Ga-68 DOTATATE PET · balloon occlusion if sacrifice possible
No FNA, never open/core biopsy · they bleed, imaging is characteristic

3Which way the carotid moves

Sympathetic schwannoma: carotids forward, vein not separated · prestyloid pleomorphic adenoma pushes carotids back

5Vagal, jugular, tympanic

  • Vagal: nodose ganglion, high parapharyngeal · ~a third already hoarse · more malignant · surgery sacrifices X → observe or irradiate small working ones; operate if growing or nerve dead
  • Jugulare/tympanicum: pulsatile tinnitus, conductive loss, rising sun, Brown's sign · jugular foramen syndrome · Fisch, Glasscock–Jackson

6Schwannoma and neurofibroma

  • Schwannoma: encapsulated, eccentric · Antoni A (Verocay) + B · second commonest parapharyngeal tumour · vagus, sympathetic, IX, XI, XII, plexuses
  • Painless, side-to-side mobile · pressure → pain along nerve · nerve usually works · less enhancement, T2 target
  • Intracapsular enucleation, keep fascicles · still hoarseness (vagal), Horner + first bite (sympathetic) · observe small in elderly
  • Neurofibroma: unencapsulated, intertwined → nerve sacrificed · plexiform = NF1 · rapid growth, pain → MPNST

8Infantile haemangioma

  • Commonest infant tumour, ~4–5% · girls, premature · proliferates to ~9–12 months, involutes by five to ten years · superficial, deep, mixed, segmental · RICH, PICH, NICH
  • Beard distribution → subglottic haemangioma in ~two-thirds (stridor ~two to three months) · segmental face → PHACES (MRI, echo) · ≥ five skin lesions → liver
  • Kasabach–Merritt = kaposiform haemangioendothelioma, tufted angioma, not common haemangioma
  • Treat: airway, periocular (amblyopia), ulceration, lip/nose/ear, large facial, heart failure
  • Propranolol ~2 mg/kg/day (glucose, bradycardia, bronchospasm) · 60% vs 4% (2015) · timolol small · steroids second · pulsed dye · surgery for residue

9Malformations

  • Capillary (port-wine): flat, darkens + cobblestones · V1 → Sturge–Weber (seizures, glaucoma) · pulsed dye
  • Venous: soft blue compressible, swells dependent/Valsalva · phleboliths, painful thrombosis, high D-dimer · sclerotherapy (ethanol, STS, bleomycin), Nd:YAG, excision
  • Lymphatic: macro/microcystic · sclero (doxycycline, bleomycin, OK-432) · sirolimus for complex (2016)
  • AVM: high flow, nidus, warm, thrill, bruit · quiet in childhood, expands at puberty/pregnancy/trauma · Schobinger I–IV · embolise then excise within days · AV fistula: close it
Never ligate AVM feeders proximally: no cure, recruits collaterals, blocks embolisation

10Exam traps ✗

  • Needling a pulsatile mass at the bifurcation
  • Operating on a secreting tumour or hidden phaeo without alpha blockade
  • Both carotid body tumours at one sitting
  • Venous malformation called "haemangioma" and left to involute
