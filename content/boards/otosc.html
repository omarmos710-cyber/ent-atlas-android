OTOSCLEROSIS

a young adult, a normal drum, a creeping hearing loss ✎

1What and who

Primary disease of the otic capsule (otospongiosis): dense enchondral bone → vascular spongy bone → hardens again · oval window → fixed footplate = conductive · cochlea → + sensorineural
FactNumber
histological foci3–4% of temporal bones (some series 8–12%)
clinical disease0.5–2% of adults; 3% over 60
women : men (surgical series)~2 : 1
familial (AD, incomplete penetrance)25–50%
onset20–30 years; <10 or >40 unusual
Less common in Black and Asian people · aid or surgery restores hearing in nine out of ten, but the operation opens the inner ear

2Pathology

Capsule layers: periosteal · enchondral (middle, normally inert = the diseased one) · endosteal
Favourite site: fissula ante fenestram, in front of the oval window

Active

otospongiotic · red vascular focus · osteoclasts + osteoblasts · blue mantles of Manasse

Mature

sclerotic · less vascular, laminated bone, stains red · loss depends on site, not activity
  • Stapedial (commonest): anterior · posterior · circumferential · biscuit (thick footplate, annular ligament free) · obliterative
  • Cochlear: round window / capsule → SNHL (enzymes, toxins); often mixed
  • Histological: silent, found post-mortem

3Aetiology

  • family history in 25–50% · AD, incomplete penetrance
  • Worse in pregnancy, menopause, after accident / major operation
  • van der Hoeve: osteogenesis imperfecta + blue sclerae + conductive deafness (type I collagen)
  • Measles RNA in active foci

4The history tells you; the drum doesn't

  • Painless, insidious, progressive loss from the twenties, usually both ears, one first
  • Paracusis Willisii: hears better in noise (others raise voices, noise barely reaches the ear)
  • Tinnitus common (cochlear, active) · vertigo uncommon
  • Soft, monotonous, well-modulated voice (own voice loud by BC)
  • Drum normal + mobile, tube normal · Schwartze sign (pink promontory blush, active focus) in ~one in ten

5Forks, audiogram, impedance

Rinne − at 256→then 512→then 1024 Hz = large gap
gap ≈ 15 / 30 / 45 dB (Dhingra) or 15 / 25 / 35 (Lee) · Weber → worse conductive ear · ABC normal unless cochlea
  • Carhart's notch: BC dip at 2000 Hz = mechanical artefact, disappears after surgery
  • Pure conductive rarely >~60 dB; worse = cochlea · good discrimination
  • Tympanogram normal → shallow As · reflex absent (early: biphasic on-off)
  • HRCT not routine: rapid progression, cochlear (double ring halo), doubtful gap, revision, vestibular symptoms
Normal drum + conductive loss + reflex PRESENT ≠ otosclerosis → superior canal dehiscence

Select for surgery

feels handicap (AC ~30 dB or worse) · real gap, Rinne − at 256 + 512 · good discrimination · poorer ear first
Dhingra: gap ≥15, discrimination ≥60%, age 20–30 · Lee: gap ≥20, ≥70%, age 30–50
Contraindications: only / better-hearing ear · active Ménière's · active middle ear disease or perforation · children · divers, pilots, frequent flyers, roofers, athletes, noisy work · relative: OE, exostoses, pregnancy

8Stapes surgery

Stapedotomy (fenestra ~0.6–0.8 mm) has largely replaced stapedectomy: less inner-ear trauma, better high tones · usually LA, transcanal
TM flap→curette overhang, save chorda→stapes fixed, M + I mobile→measure→cut tendon, remove arch→fenestra→piston on incus, crimp→flap back
Gap within 10 dB in ~90% · notch goes · tinnitus better in ~¾, worse ~5% · total SNHL <1%
Far advanced (>~85 dB, BC unmeasurable): stapedotomy + powerful aid → cochlear implant (facial nerve stimulation risk) · Lempert fenestration obsolete (cavity, ~25 dB loss)

9Complications

  • During: flap tear · chorda (metallic taste 5–10%, briefly up to 30%) · incus dislocation · floating footplate · gusher ~1 in 300 (wide aqueduct, X-linked) · facial nerve
  • Early: vertigo (trauma, serous labyrinthitis, long piston) · reparative granuloma at 1–2 weeks: falling SN hearing + vertigo → urgent re-exploration
  • Late: conductive loss returns (incus erosion ~5%, commonest at revision) · perilymph fistula · SNHL ~2%, dead ear 0.5–1%
After surgery: operated ear up · vertigo + nystagmus, sudden loss or facial weakness → same-day senior (delayed palsy usually recovers in ~six weeks on steroids) · no straining, nose blowing, lifting, flying ~two to four weeks · audiogram at ~six weeks
Traps ✗ operating the only / better ear · missing SCD (reflex present) · reading Carhart's notch as cochlear loss · not asking job, flying, diving
