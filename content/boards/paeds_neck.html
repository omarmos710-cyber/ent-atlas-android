CONGENITAL NECK MASSES

embryology explains them · each is cured by removing the whole tract, and recurs if it's left ✎

1Arches, clefts, pouches

  • Weeks four to eight · arches (cartilage, artery, nerve, muscle) · clefts outside (ectoderm), pouches inside (endoderm) · second arch overgrows → cervical sinus of His
  • Remnant = cyst (no opening), sinus (one), fistula (both) · skin opening anterior to SCM, tract deep to platysma

2Thyroglossal cyst

  • Commonest congenital mass · midline 2–4 cm, at/below hyoid (subhyoid commonest) · rises on swallowing + tongue protrusion · infects after URTI · incision → sinus
  • May be the only thyroid · ~1% carcinoma (papillary) · US: confirm a normal gland, TFTs · scan if no gland · FNA solid areas in adults
  • Sistrunk: cyst + tract + central hyoid + tongue-base core to foramen caecum · simple cystectomy recurs in up to half · Sistrunk < 5–10% · settle infection first

3Other midline masses

  • Dermoid: skin + adnexa · submental, sublingual, suprasternal · doesn't move with tongue · vs ranula · mouth or neck by mylohyoid
  • Teratoma: neonatal airway → EXIT delivery · ectopic thyroid, Delphian node, bronchogenic cyst, thymic cyst

4Second cleft tract

  • ~90% of branchial anomalies · cyst: young adult, upper/middle third anterior SCM, swells with URTI, cholesterol crystals
  • Sinus/fistula from infancy · deep to 2nd-arch (ECA, stylohyoid, posterior digastric), superficial to 3rd (ICA, IX) → between the carotids, over XII
  • US/MRI · excise when quiet · ellipse + step-ladder incision
Adult > ~40 with a new cystic lateral mass = cystic metastasis (HPV oropharynx, papillary thyroid) · examine, US-FNA, p16, thyroglobulin, image

6Lymphatic malformation

  • Sequestered jugular lymph sacs · half at birth, 90% by two years · posterior triangle → floor of mouth, tongue, parotid, axilla, mediastinum
  • Macrocystic (cystic hygroma): soft, multilocular, brilliantly transilluminant · microcystic: infiltrates, mucosal vesicles
  • Sudden swelling with infection/bleed · airway, feeding, labour · MRI · EXIT if large antenatally
  • Macro: sclerotherapy (bleomycin, doxycycline, OK-432, ethanol) · excision sparing nerves (half recur if partial) · micro: sirolimus, laser · trach if needed
  • Haemangioma: after birth, involutes · beard → subglottic · PHACES · propranolol

7Other masses

  • Sternomastoid tumour: baby two to four weeks, difficult delivery · torticollis (tilt towards, chin away) · US · stretching · tenotomy if persists
  • Thymic cyst: along thymic descent, usually left, may enter chest
  • Commonest of all: reactive nodes, suppurative, atypical mycobacteria, cat-scratch · Kawasaki (> five days fever, eyes, mucosa, rash, node → coronary aneurysms)
  • Malignancy: lymphoma (Hodgkin, teens), rhabdomyosarcoma, neuroblastoma, thyroid, NPC
Hard, fixed, matted > 2–3 cm · supraclavicular · growing over two to six weeks despite antibiotics · B symptoms → biopsy
Atypical mycobacteria (2007): excision 96% vs 66% drugs, better scar · observation acceptable

8Exam traps ✗

  • Thyroglossal cyst removed without hyoid + tract
  • Removing the patient's only thyroid
  • Incising a branchial cyst or mycobacterial node
  • First cleft anomaly missed as "parotid abscess" or discharging ear
