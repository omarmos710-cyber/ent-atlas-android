JUVENILE NASOPHARYNGEAL ANGIOFIBROMA

teenage boy + blocked nose + recurrent bleeds · diagnosed by imaging, never by biopsy in clinic ✎

1The bleeding tumour of boys

  • Benign, locally aggressive, very vascular · commonest benign nasopharyngeal tumour, yet < 1% of head + neck tumours
  • Boys, 10–20 years · does not regress: waiting is not an option
  • Origin: back of the nose, upper margin of the sphenopalatine foramen (palatine sphenoidal process meets pterygoid root)
  • Testosterone-dependent nidus woken at puberty · about three-quarters carry androgen receptors · VEGF, IGF-II, β-catenin · ~24 times commoner in FAP
  • Red-purple, lobulated, no capsule · vessels without muscle or elastic → can't contract · collagen stroma sets firmness

2Through the doorways

  • Spreads by least resistance, expansion (bows the back wall of the antrum) and bone erosion (pterygoid root, greater wing)
  • Intracranial: mainly middle fossa, usually extradural · lateral or medial to the ICA

3Bleeding, blocked teenage boy

  • Profuse, recurrent, spontaneous epistaxis (commonest) + anaemia
  • Progressive block, hyponasal voice, discharge · glue ear, conductive loss
  • Orbit / skull base: proptosis, diplopia, II, III, IV, VI · broad bridge, cheek swelling, frog face
  • Endoscopy: pink-purple mass, surface vessels, back of nose + nasopharynx, may push the soft palate down
Never biopsy or finger it in clinic. If tissue is truly needed: theatre, GA, blood cross-matched

4Imaging makes the diagnosis

  • Contrast CT = first choice · anterior bowing of the posterior antral wall: Holman-Miller (antral) sign, pathognomonic · wide PPF, eroded vidian canal
  • MRI: soft tissue, cavernous sinus, orbit · flow voids = salt and pepper
  • Angiography: feeders from the ECA (internal maxillary, ascending pharyngeal, ascending palatine) ± ICA when large
  • Embolise ECA feeders, operate within 24–48 hours (up to 72) before collaterals · risks stroke, blindness, nerve palsy → some skip it for small tumours
  • Cross-match two to three units
DDx: antrochoanal polyp, adenoids, olfactory neuroblastoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, lobular capillary haemangioma, haemangiopericytoma

5Staging

Extent (UPMC)
Inasal cavity, medial PPF
IIsinuses, lateral PPF · no residual vascularity
IIIskull base erosion, orbit, ITF · no residual vascularity
IVas III + residual vascularity
Vintracranial + residual vascularity
Older systems (Sessions, Fisch, Radkowski, Andrews) = extent only · UPMC adds ICA supply after embolisation → predicts blood loss + recurrence better

6Surgery is the treatment

ExtentApproach
nose, nasopharynxendoscopic, or transpalatal (Sardana extension)
+ sinuses, PPFendoscopic · lateral rhinotomy + medial maxillectomy · midfacial degloving · Le Fort I
+ infratemporal fossaextended endoscopic · ITF approach · maxillary swing
+ cheekmaxillary swing (Wei's facial translocation)
+ intracranialcombined skull base with neurosurgery · radiotherapy if inaccessible
  • Endoscopic now for most: less bleeding, no scars · hormones no longer used
  • Early tumours often without embolisation · advanced with ICA supply: embolise ECA first

7Radiotherapy & recurrence

  • Radiotherapy: unresectable intracranial, residual near critical structures, recurrence · radiosurgery for small residuals · risks in the young: growth, cataract, second cancer
  • Recurrence up to about a third, usually within two years · similar after open and endoscopic
  • Small residual: watch · accessible: endoscopic revision · cavernous sinus: watch or radiosurgery
  • Contrast MRI follow-up for at least three years
Tumour left in the vidian canal / basisphenoid = commonest source of recurrence → drill the canal

8Evidence

  • 1047 cases (Laryngoscope 2013): endoscopic lost ~540 ml vs 1580 ml open, fewer recurrences, but not after adjusting for stage
  • UPMC (2010): ICA vascularity predicts blood loss, staged surgery, recurrence
  • Embolisation 24–72 h before cuts loss in larger tumours · debated for small endoscopic ones
  • Flutamide shrinks some · not standard

9Exam traps ✗

  • Biopsy in clinic
  • Recurrent epistaxis in a teenage boy without endoscopy
  • Leaving tumour in the vidian canal
  • Embolising and then waiting a week: collaterals return
