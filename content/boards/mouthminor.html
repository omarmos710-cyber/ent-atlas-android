QUINSY, TONGUE-TIE, LIP BIOPSY & DROOLING

four small procedures · each has one structure or one decision that makes it safe ✎

1Recognise the quinsy

  • Pus between tonsil capsule + superior constrictor
  • One-sided pain, fever, trismus, hot-potato voice, drooling, earache · palate bulges, uvula pushed across
  • Bilateral swelling, no trismus = tonsillitis · CT: young child, unclear, deep extension
  • IV fluids, penicillin, steroid dose · sit up 45°, headlight, sucker · lidocaine

2Where to put the needle

patient's right quinsy, schematic · incision level with uvula base, a third from medial edge

3Aspirate first

  • 5 ml syringe (2 ml if tight trismus), 18G needle or cannula · tape so 1 cm protrudes
  • Three points, from above + lateral to upper pole downwards · culture
  • No pus, recurs, or not settled in 24 hours → incise (guarded blade), open with sinus forceps
Internal carotid deep to the tonsil bed: guard to 1 cm, point slightly medial, never posterolateral

4Quinsy decisions

QuestionAnswer
Trismus, uvula across?yes: aspirate · no: tonsillitis, review
Pus on aspiration?yes: antibiotics, review at 24 h · no: incise or image
Not settling at 24 h?incise · CT for deep extension
Second quinsy, or recurrent tonsillitis?interval tonsillectomy
  • Oral antibiotics once swallowing · hot tonsillectomy only for airway or failed drainage
  • Cochrane (11 trials, 674, 2016): more recurrence after aspiration than incision (RR 3.74), very low quality
  • Three RCTs (2023): adding metronidazole: no benefit, more side effects

5Tongue-tie

  • Short/thick frenulum near tip → heart-shaped tip · 4–11% of newborns, most no trouble
  • Divide for: breastfeeding difficulty + nipple pain (after feeding specialist) · older: protrusion, elevation, hygiene, tip sounds
  • Protrudes past lower incisors → speech unlikely affected
  • Frenotomy (infant, no anaesthetic): swaddle, lift tongue, blunt scissors close to the tongue · gauze · feed at once
  • Frenuloplasty (GA): transverse release, vertical closure · Z-plasty for long band
Find the submandibular duct papillae first · cutting them or sublingual veins = bleeding floor of mouth
  • Bleeding, ulcer, re-attachment, scar, duct injury (ranula)
  • Cochrane (302 infants, 2017): less nipple pain short term · feeding scores inconsistent · small, biased trials

6Lip biopsy

  • Sjögren (focal lymphocytic sialadenitis) · sarcoid, amyloid, IgG4
ACR/EULAR 2016Points
Focus score ≥ 13
Anti-Ro (SSA)3
Schirmer, ocular staining, unstimulated flow1 each
4 or more = primary Sjögren
  • LA · evert lower lip, chalazion clamp · 1.5–2 cm mucosal cut parallel to vermilion, between midline + commissure, mucosa only
  • Tease out four to six lobules (≥ 4 mm squared) · spare vertical sensory nerves · absorbable sutures
  • Numb lip (usually temporary), haematoma, mucocele, non-diagnostic

7Drooling: assess first

  • Normal up to ~4 years · beyond: poor oral motor control (cerebral palsy, Parkinson's, MND), rarely excess saliva
  • Anterior (out, social) vs posterior (into pharynx → aspiration)
  • Grade severity + frequency · check teeth, posture, nasal obstruction, drugs that increase saliva

8The ladder

conservative→drugs→botulinum→surgery
  • Positioning, oral motor therapy, dental + nasal care
  • Glycopyrronium or hyoscine patch · anticholinergic effects
  • Botulinum A, ultrasound-guided: submandibular (biggest effect) ~20 units, parotid ~10 units · 3–6 months · risk: dysphagia
  • Meta-analysis (181 pts, 2013): toxin cuts drooling, A ≈ B

9Duct operations

  • Ligation: two-duct (submandibular) to four-duct · day case, temporary swelling
  • Submandibular relocation: mucosal island with both openings tunnelled to tonsillar fossa + sublingual excision (ranula) · not if aspirating
  • Submandibular excision: posterior drooling + aspiration ± parotid ligation · 708 pts (2021): ~80–85% very good–excellent, parotid steps add nothing
  • Ranula, swelling, caries, thick saliva, bleeding · excision: marginal mandibular, lingual, XII
Posterior drooling: redirecting saliva back worsens aspiration → reduce it (excision, ligation, toxin)

10Where to incise a quinsy

  • Oxford handbooks: level with uvula base, a third from the tonsil's medial edge, or where it points
  • Classic landmark: just lateral to where a line through the uvula base meets a line along the anterior pillar
  • In practice: level with uvula base, above + lateral to the tonsil, where it points most · blade guarded to 1 cm

11Exam traps ✗

  • Unguarded needle in the tonsil bed
  • Bilateral tonsillar swelling called a quinsy
  • Cutting a tongue-tie through the duct papillae
  • Relocating ducts in a child who aspirates
  • Hot tonsillectomy as routine: keep it for airway or failed drainage
