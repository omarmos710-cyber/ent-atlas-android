ACQUIRED SENSORINEURAL HEARING LOSS

ototoxicity, noise, presbycusis and immune inner ear disease ✎

1Four causes

Slowly progressive bilateral SNHL = mostly age, noise or drugs → ask about each, stop what still harms · don't miss autoimmune: it can respond to steroids · much is preventable, some reversible

Also deferoxamine, aminoglycoside / polymyxin drops through a perforation · tobramycin: Dhingra vestibular, Lee cochlear · gentamicin used deliberately in Ménière's
  • Aminoglycosides: basal turn first → high tones · during or after treatment · risk: renal impairment, >65, high levels, long course, prior exposure, loop diuretics
  • Mitochondrial MT-RNR1 m.1555A>G (12S rRNA): one dose can deafen (~1 in 500 carry it) → ask about relatives deafened by these drugs · neonatal point-of-care test ~26 minutes (NICE 2023)
  • Cisplatin: bilateral permanent high-frequency loss · worse under five, elderly, with noise / RT · sodium thiosulfate six hours after each dose roughly halves loss in children (SIOPEL 6: 63% → 33%, survival kept)
On the ward: new tinnitus, loss or imbalance on an aminoglycoside / cisplatin → tell the team, levels + renal function, hearing test · baseline + serial audiograms incl. high frequencies

3Noise

schematic · symmetrical in both ears
  • Outer hair cells, basal turn (~4 kHz) fail first · TTS recovers (Dhingra up to two weeks, Lee within 24 hours) → repeated → PTS
  • High-pitched tinnitus, trouble hearing in noise · notch deepens + widens into speech frequencies
  • Acoustic trauma (~140 dB or more): drum, ossicles + cochlea at once, no preceding TTS → treat like sudden SNHL, early steroids
  • Hidden loss: synaptopathy (Kujawa & Liberman, mice, 2009); human effect debated · recreational noise rising in the young

6Immune inner ear disease

  • Primary: rapidly progressive bilateral, often asymmetric SNHL over weeks–months · age 40–50 · vestibular in ~half · ~15% another autoimmune disease
  • Secondary: Cogan (interstitial keratitis + audiovestibular loss), GPA, SLE, antiphospholipid
  • Dx: bilateral ≥30 dB, progressing on serial audiograms within three months · ESR, RF, ANA, complement · anti-Hsp70 (activity, not specific) · contrast MRI · steroid response supports

Treatment

Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day (≤60 mg) for four weeks · response = 15 dB at one / 10 dB at consecutive frequencies, or better discrimination → continue to plateau, taper to lowest dose · steroid-sparing (methotrexate, cyclophosphamide, biologics, IT steroid) on specialist advice: methotrexate = placebo for maintenance (Harris, JAMA 2003) · implant if lost

7Also exclude

vestibular schwannoma · Ménière's · syphilis · multiple sclerosis · familial progressive SNHL

8Exam traps ✗

  • Not asking about drugs and noise
  • Missing a mitochondrial aminoglycoside susceptibility in the family history
  • Calling rapidly progressive bilateral loss presbycusis
