CSF RHINORRHOEA

a clear drip that gushes on bending forward · prove it, find the hole, close it ✎

1A skull base that leaks

CSF from the subarachnoid space into the nose through a defect in dura + bone · open door to meningitis (often pneumococcal, recurrent) · pneumocephalus
CSFNumbers
Madechoroid plexus, ~20 mL an hour (350–500 mL a day)
Volume90–150 mL, replaced three to five times a day
Pressure60–250 mm water · ↑ cough, sneeze, blowing, straining

  • Sites: cribriform plate (commonest), ethmoid roof, frontal sinus · middle fossa via sphenoid (lateral recess)
  • Temporal bone fracture → middle ear → Eustachian tube: CSF otorhinorrhoea

3A drip you can't sniff back

  • Clear, watery, one side · worse bending, straining, lifting · salty or sweet
  • Reservoir sign: a gush on first bending in the morning (sphenoid)
  • Headache (low or high pressure) · anosmia after trauma · past meningitis · halo / double ring on filter paper: hint, not proof
  • Endoscopy: fluid from olfactory cleft, middle meatus, sphenoethmoidal recess, ET · look in the ear

4Confirm, then localise

  • β2-transferrin: only in CSF, perilymph, aqueous · a few drops · β-trace protein also specific · glucose unreliable
  • HRCT fine coronal + axial = first: bony defect
  • Heavy T2 MRI: fluid through the defect, encephalocele, empty sella, no injection
  • CT cisternography if active but unlocalised · intrathecal fluorescein: yellow, green under blue light, at surgery

5Most traumatic leaks stop

  • Conservative: bed rest, head up, stool softener, no blowing, sneeze mouth open · acetazolamide · lumbar drain
  • Most close in one to two weeks · prophylactic antibiotics: no proof (Cochrane 2015) → pneumococcal vaccine

Repair when

persists beyond one to two weeks · recurs · seen at surgery · spontaneous · meningitis · big defect or encephalocele

6Endoscopic multilayer repair

  • Expose, strip mucosa from the edges, reduce or cauterise an encephalocele, close in layers
  • Inlay fascia, fat or dural substitute between dura + bone · onlay mucosa or fascia · nasoseptal (Hadad-Bassagasteguy) flap on the posterior septal artery for large defects
  • > 90% first time

7Afterwards

  • Head up, no straining, stool softeners, no nose blowing for weeks
  • Open repair (craniotomy) for huge or multiple defects, brain injury · external for high frontal leaks
  • Spontaneous leak = think idiopathic intracranial hypertension · weight loss, acetazolamide, sometimes a shunt · or it recurs

8Evidence

  • 55 studies (2012): ~90% sealed first time, ~97% after revision, any graft
  • Nasoseptal flap (2006): leaks after extended skull base surgery down to ~5%
  • Cochrane 2015: prophylactic antibiotics don't prevent meningitis after basal fracture
  • Little support for routine lumbar drains or long antibiotics after repair

9Exam traps ✗

  • One-sided clear drip called rhinitis
  • Relying on glucose testing
  • Repairing a spontaneous leak and ignoring raised pressure
  • The weak spot in ethmoid surgery: lateral lamella
  • Forgetting the ear: CSF otorhinorrhoea down the tube
