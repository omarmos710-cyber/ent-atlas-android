DEEP NECK SPACE INFECTION

airway first · image the neck · right antibiotics · drain the pus ✎

1Fascia and the spaces behind

  • Superficial (investing): SCM, trapezius, glands · middle (visceral): airway, gullet, thyroid · deep: prevertebral + alar · all three → carotid sheath
front to back: retropharyngeal · (alar) · danger · (prevertebral) · prevertebral
Parapharyngeal = crossroads (retropharyngeal, submandibular, parotid, carotid, visceral) · carotid sheath = "Lincoln highway" to the root of the neck

3Assess

  • Pain, fever, drooling, voice change, trismus, breathing · recent dental work, sore throat, intubation · diabetes, immunity
  • Teeth, floor of mouth, tonsils, posterior wall · crepitus (gas) · cranial nerves
  • Every patient: FBC, CRP, glucose (+ HbA1c), renal function, blood cultures
  • Flexible endoscopy if voice change, dyspnoea, stridor · sats can be normal with a critical airway
  • Contrast CT = key: which spaces, abscess vs phlegmon, gas, mediastinum, jugular thrombosis (+ upper chest if spread possible) · lateral film (child, stiff neck): prevertebral > ~7 mm at C2 (adult), > half a vertebra (child) · ultrasound: children, guides aspiration · pus culture (TB, fungal if chronic) · OPG for a dental source · MRI / MRA for intracranial or vascular spread
Adult "abscess" may be a necrotic HPV node or a branchial cyst hiding a primary → examine the upper aerodigestive tract

4Treat

airway: sit up, O₂, steroid, adrenaline nebs→narrowed > half: awake fibreoptic or tracheostomy under LA→fluids→IV antibiotics + anaerobic cover→drain→remove the tooth / FB · control glucose
  • Drain: abscess in a space, gas, threatened airway, no better in 48–72 h · small phlegmon (children) often settles
  • Single unilocular abscess, stable: image-guided aspiration · multi-space, multiloculated, gas, threatened airway → open drainage
  • Antibiotic choice: co-amoxiclav / ampicillin-sulbactam, or 3rd-gen cephalosporin + metronidazole · clindamycin (allergy, small children) · antipseudomonal if immunosuppressed · adjust to cultures
Never paralyse a patient whose airway you can't see · never send them to CT alone
60 studies: CT in 78%, open drainage usual · diabetes doubles multi-space spread (RR 1.96) + complications (2.42)

7Ludwig's angina

  • Sublingual (above mylohyoid) · submandibular (below) · submental · premolars + 1st molar above the line, 2nd + 3rd below
  • Bilateral cellulitis, usually no discrete abscess · teeth ~80% · drooling, trismus, floor of mouth pushes tongue up + back, woody-hard under both sides of the jaw, sits forward
  • Awake fibreoptic / tracheostomy · antibiotics + steroid · drain intraorally or below the jaw (often serous) · pull the tooth
Never lie down or sedate: can obstruct completely → theatre with the airway team

8Retropharyngeal abscess

  • Gillette nodes (regress ~4–5 years) · child under 3–5 · adult: fish bone, instruments
  • Feeding refused, drooling, stridor, croupy cough · torticollis (best sign), neck extended · bulge off the midline
  • Lateral film: wide prevertebral shadow ± gas · CT
  • Antibiotics · transoral drainage, supine head-low, aspirate then vertical incision (carotid lateral) · neck if low
Chronic (TB)Drain
spinal caries: midline · TB nodes: to one side · mild dysphagia, wall swelling · X-ray: spinethrough the neck · ATT, spinal team

10Parotid & masticator

  • Parotid: dehydrated, post-op (day 5–7) · S. aureus up Stensen's duct · red, hard, tender, pus from the duct · fluctuation hard to feel · US / CT: loculi · rehydrate, sialogogues, antibiotics, drain via parotidectomy incision along facial nerve lines · can break into parapharyngeal space
  • Masticator: second + third molars · marked trismus · drain lateral to the retromolar trigone, above the zygomatic arch for temporal
