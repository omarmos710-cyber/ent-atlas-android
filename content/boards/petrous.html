PETROUS APICITIS

infection at the tip of the temporal bone ✎

1What and why

  • Inflammation ± abscess of the petrous apex air cells, spreading from acute / chronic OM and mastoiditis
  • Only ~30% of apices are pneumatised; the rest diploic or sclerotic → infection can only track into an aerated apex
  • Neighbours: VI, trigeminal ganglion, ICA, meninges → meningitis, carotid injury, skull base osteomyelitis
  • Rare now; can appear just as the ear seems to improve

2Tracts into the apex

  • Posterosuperior: mastoid + antrum, above / behind the labyrinth, via Trautmann's triangle + subarcuate region
  • Anteroinferior: hypotympanum + around the Eustachian tube, below the cochlea

Dorello's canal

VI under the petroclinoid (Gruber's) ligament → lateral rectus palsy, diplopia

Meckel's cave

trigeminal ganglion on the apex → deep retro-orbital pain

3Gradenigo's triad

  • Often only one or two features
  • Fever, headache, neck stiffness, vomiting → meningeal spread · VII, VIII occasionally
Suspect it: discharge or deep pain persisting despite an adequate mastoidectomy, or new pain after the ear has settled · don't wait for the full triad

4CT and MRI together

  • HRCT: pneumatisation, broken septa, bone erosion
  • MRI + contrast (+ DWI): marrow vs fluid / pus, dural enhancement, intracranial spread, carotid + cavernous sinus · MRV for sinus thrombosis

6Cholesterol granuloma

Commonest primary apex lesion, ~ten times commoner than apical cholesteatoma · bleeding into an obstructed cell · asymptomatic → serial imaging · symptomatic → drain, keeping hearing (infracochlear / infralabyrinthine, or endoscopic transsphenoidal) · lower cranial palsies → think malignancy

7Antibiotics first

  • Admit · prolonged IV antibiotics covering OM organisms and penetrating bone
  • Treat the source: myringotomy / grommet or mastoidectomy
  • Most acute cases now settle on antibiotics

Surgery

for abscess, bone necrosis, failed medical treatment · route by the cell tracts + hearing
Hearing kept: Trautmann's triangle / subarcuate, infralabyrinthine, infracochlear, middle fossa · endoscopic transsphenoidal for medial lesions
Dead ear: translabyrinthine / transcochlear (widest access)

8Complications

meningitis, intracranial infection · ICA injury / thrombosis · skull base osteomyelitis · cavernous sinus thrombosis

9Exam traps ✗

  • Asymmetric apical marrow read as infection
  • Missed because the ear seemed to be improving
  • Waiting for the full triad
