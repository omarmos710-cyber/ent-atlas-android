OESOPHAGUS & DYSPHAGIA

where is it, solids or liquids, how fast · progressive dysphagia + weight loss in an older smoker = cancer until scoped ✎

1A 25 cm muscular tube

At the narrowings: foreign bodies lodge, caustic burns worst, instruments perforate

2Wall, supply, sphincters

  • Squamous mucosa · submucosa (Meissner) · circular + longitudinal muscle (Auerbach) · adventitia, no serosa → leaks spread, anastomoses fragile
  • Muscle: striated upper third, mixed, smooth lower → myositis hits top, scleroderma bottom
  • Inferior thyroid · aorta · left gastric + inferior phrenic · lower end = portosystemic (varices) · lymph: deep cervical, mediastinal, coeliac, long submucosal channels → skip lesions · vagus + sympathetic
  • UOS = cricopharyngeus, 3–5 cm, closed at rest · Killian's dehiscence above, Laimer's triangle below
  • LOS: physiological · crural pinch-cock, angle of His, mucosal folds, abdominal pressure

3Swallowing

  • Oral voluntary: chew, bolus, tongue pushes back → touches faucial pillars, triggers reflex
  • Pharyngeal < a second: palate to Passavant's ridge · larynx closes below upwards (true, false folds, arytenoids) · breathing stops · larynx rises under tongue base, tips epiglottis, → pulls UOS open (the key) · constrictors contract as cricopharyngeus relaxes
  • Oesophageal: 3–8 seconds · primary (swallow), secondary (distension), tertiary (non-propulsive: elderly, spasm)

4Congenital

  • Atresia + distal fistula ~85% · drools, chokes, cyanosed at first feed · NG coils at 9–13 cm · VACTERL · H-type ~4%: later cough on feeds, pneumonia
  • Aberrant right subclavian: lusoria + non-recurrent right laryngeal nerve

7Reflux and friends

  • GORD: weak/relaxing LOS, hiatus hernia, obesity, smoking, CCBs, nitrates
  • LPR: hoarseness, clearing, globus, cough · posterior laryngitis, granuloma, subglottic stenosis
  • Barrett's: salmon-pink intestinal metaplasia → adenocarcinoma · surveil by length + dysplasia (BSG 2014)
  • Lifestyle (no food three hours before bed) → PPI, H2 blocker, alginate → Nissen
  • Infective: candida (plaques, fluconazole) · HSV (punched-out, aciclovir) · CMV (large shallow, ganciclovir)
  • EoE: young atopic men, bolus impaction · rings, furrows · ≥ 15 eosinophils/HPF at two levels · PPI, swallowed budesonide, diet · dupilumab (phase 3, 2022)
  • Pill (doxycycline, KCl, iron, NSAIDs) lodges at the aortic narrowing

8Caustic injury

AlkaliAcid
liquefactive, deep · oesophaguscoagulative, limited · stomach (pyloric stenosis)
necrosis→slough→stricture→SCC
Slough at two to three weeks: wall weakest, perforation · stricture from the third week · cancer decades later
  • Airway first (stridor, drooling) · lips clean ≠ oesophagus safe · films, CT for perforation
  • Gentle scope at 12–48 hours · Zargar: 0–2a heal · 2b+ stricture
  • No vomiting, no neutralising, no blind NG · analgesia, acid suppression, NBM, NG under vision or gastrostomy · steroids not routine · resect for full-thickness necrosis
  • Stricture: dilate · retrograde over a string · colon or stomach replacement
  • Dhingra: neutralise within six hours, steroids 48–96 h · current: neither (heat, re-exposure, no proven benefit)

9Perforation

  • Instruments commonest: at Killian's dehiscence or distal · FB, trauma, tumour · Boerhaave: vomiting, left lower · ≠ Mallory–Weiss (mucosal, haematemesis)
  • Cervical: neck pain, fever, surgical emphysema · thoracic: interscapular pain, shock, Hamman's crunch, effusion · Mackler's triad: vomiting, chest pain, emphysema
  • Water-soluble contrast + CT · NBM, IV antibiotics + antifungals, nutrition · cervical contained: conservative ± drain · thoracic: repair within ~24 hours, or stent/clips
Pain, fever, tachycardia, crepitus after scope or dilatation = perforation until proved otherwise · every hour raises mortality

10Motility

  • Achalasia: lost inhibitory myenteric neurons → aperistalsis, LOS won't relax · solids + liquids from the start, night regurgitation · bird's beak, no gastric bubble · manometry, Chicago types I–III · exclude pseudoachalasia (cardia cancer) · Chagas · pneumatic dilatation, Heller + partial fundoplication, POEM · botox if frail · SCC risk
  • Distal spasm: angina-like pain · corkscrew · nitrates, CCB · jackhammer: strong but peristaltic
  • Scleroderma: lower two-thirds aperistaltic, weak LOS → severe reflux, stricture, Barrett's (CREST) · myositis: upper · also myasthenia, MND, diabetes, hypothyroid, presbyoesophagus
  • CP dysfunction: UOS won't open (fibrosis, stroke, Parkinson, MND, dystrophy, reflux) · posterior bar, also in up to a third of healthy elderly · dilate, botox, myotomy (not with severe reflux)

13Oesophagoscopy

  • Rigid (general anaesthetic): sharp or impacted FB (sheaths the point), postcricoid biopsy, dilatation, pouch, panendoscopy · not with bad neck, trismus, receding jaw, aneurysm
  • Sniffing position, protect teeth · right of tongue → behind cricoid · gentle sustained lift, never force · aortic pulsation at 25 cm · red gastric mucosa = cardia
  • Flexible: topical ± sedation, reaches stomach + duodenum, safe in neck disease, can miss postcricoid · transnasal: unsedated office, Barrett's, second primary, TEP
  • Food bolus: scope within 24 hours (sooner if can't swallow saliva) · no fizzy drinks or glucagon instead

14Exam traps ✗

  • Progressive dysphagia in an older patient called "globus", no scope
  • Forcing a rigid scope through cricopharyngeus · missing perforation after
  • Achalasia treated without excluding cardia cancer
  • Vomiting or neutralising after caustic ingestion
