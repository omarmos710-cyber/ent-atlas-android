THE DIZZY CHILD

rarely a sick child: sort the timing, check the ears, find the red flags ✎

1Children are different

  • Can't describe vertigo: cling, fall, refuse to walk, go pale, vomit, stop playing · "clumsy", "falls a lot"
  • Exam built from play + observation · compensate fast: few signs a week later
  • Over half also have headache · most migraine variants have a first-degree family history
  • Most causes benign · true BPPV + Ménière's rare

3The common three

  • BPVC (benign paroxysmal vertigo of childhood): commonest at 2–6 years, up to a third · migraine precursor · sudden seconds–minutes spells, pallor, fear, nystagmus, no hearing symptoms, normal between · ~half get migraine later · resolves
  • Same family: paroxysmal torticollis (infants under 2), cyclical vomiting, motion sickness (4–10 years)
  • Vestibular migraine: commonest in older children · rotatory vertigo minutes–hours + headache, vomiting, light + sound sensitivity · vs basilar-type (vertigo as aura with paraesthesia, ataxia, LOC): no ergots
  • OME: probably commonest cause of balance complaints overall · clumsy, conductive loss · improves when effusion clears / grommets

4Other causes

  • Neuritis / labyrinthitis: as adults, faster recovery; central cause considered first
  • Cholesteatoma fistula (pressure vertigo) · suppurative labyrinthitis → dead ear
  • Malformations: vestibular abnormality in up to 70% of permanent SNHL · late milestones · EVA, incomplete partition: episodic vertigo + stepwise loss after minor head injury (not Ménière's) · Pendred, BOR, Waardenburg, Usher (deaf + no vestibular function → eyes + ERG) · meningitis, CMV
  • Head injury: fracture, fistula, post-concussion · BPPV in children ≈ post-traumatic / after ear surgery
  • SCD (rare) · aminoglycosides (incl. gentamicin drops through a perforation): ataxia, oscillopsia · vision ~10% of over-5s · anxiety, school avoidance

5Central: red flags

posterior fossa tumour, Chiari, hereditary ataxia, encephalitis, MS (teens), epilepsy (movement + hallucinations, post-ictal) · arrhythmia = presyncope
MRI + paediatric neurology if: ataxia between attacks · morning headache / vomiting, changing headache · focal signs, vertical or direction-changing nystagmus · LOC, aura, confusion after · progressive / asymmetric SNHL · lost motor milestones

6History, then play

  • Timing · triggers · hearing, discharge, grommets · headache + family history · milestones (sit 6–9 months, walk 12–15 months, bike) · prematurity, meningitis, CMV, ototoxics, trauma · vision, school, mood
  • Ears (effusion, cholesteatoma, fistula sign) · nystagmus (Frenzel / video) · head impulse (~15° turn, child fixes on a cartoon) · head shake (head down 30°, 20 shakes)
  • Romberg, Unterberger, gait, cerebellar, cranial nerves · Dix-Hallpike only for positional history · lying + standing BP

7Tests

  • Audiometry + tympanometry for every child: separates glue ear, SNHL and normal hearing
  • Vestibular where available: VOG + calorics, vHIT, rotary chair (on a parent's lap), cVEMP, posturography from ~3 years
  • MRI brain + IAMs for red flags, unexplained / asymmetric SNHL, persistence · CT for chronic ear, trauma, malformation
  • Bloods rarely · optometry, ECG, EEG by history

8Explanation does most

  • BPVC: explain, review; resolves (migraine may follow)
  • Sedatives briefly in severe attacks · phenothiazines with care (extrapyramidal) · never long (slow compensation)
  • Migraine: sleep, meals, fluids, screen limits, stress, vision → prophylaxis via paediatrics
  • OME: imbalance strengthens the case for grommets
  • Vestibular rehab after neuritis, head injury, fixed loss · bilateral loss: physio, swimming + dark safety, school OT
  • Bárány 2021: vestibular migraine of childhood (≥ five episodes, 5 min–72 h, migraine features in ≥ half) · recurrent vertigo of childhood (no migraine features)

9Exam traps ✗

  • BPPV without a head injury history
  • Glue ear missed in a "clumsy" child
  • EVA called Ménière's
  • Forgetting eyes, BP, mood · missing ataxia between attacks
