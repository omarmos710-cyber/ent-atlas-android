WAX, FOREIGN BODIES & CANAL STENOSIS

common problems, easy to make worse ✎

1The canal

schematic, not to scale
  • ~24 mm long · bony skin thin, sensitive
  • Isthmus ~6 mm from the drum: objects pushed past it impact
  • Anterior recess (in front of + below the drum) collects debris
  • Straighten: adult pinna up + back · infant down + back

2Wax

  • Ceruminous + sebaceous secretion + keratin · acidic, water-repellent, antibacterial · cleared by migration
  • Impacts: narrow / tortuous canal, stiff hairs, exostoses, overactive glands, cotton buds, hearing aid moulds
  • Blocked ear, hearing loss, ± tinnitus, giddiness · cough via Arnold's nerve (X) · sudden block after bathing (dry wax swells)
  • Treat only if symptoms or the drum must be seen · no cotton buds, no ear candling

Irrigation (syringing)

usual in primary care · pinna up + back · water at body temperature along the posterosuperior wall → flows behind the wax · dry, inspect drum
Complications: perforation, laceration, bleeding, OE, vertigo (wrong temperature)
Don't irrigate: perforation, grommet, discharge or ear surgery history · thin old perforation · previous irrigation complication · only hearing ear · vegetable matter (swells) or button battery
ENT clinic: microscope — suction, wax hook, Jobson-Horne, crocodile forceps

3Wax: treatment steps

StepTreatment

5Getting it out safely

  • Direct vision, good light, microscope, right instrument · GA for children, impacted objects, failed first attempt
  • A bad first attempt makes the second harder: most can wait
  • Afterwards inspect the drum, the other ear (and a child's nostrils)

6Acquired stenosis

Fibrous narrowing after chronic OE, trauma, burns, surgery, radiation → meatoplasty: excise fibrous tissue, flaps / grafts, stent · congenital: see atresia

8Exam traps ✗

  • Irrigating an ear with a perforation
  • Grasping a bead with forceps
  • Irrigating vegetable matter
  • Delaying a button battery
