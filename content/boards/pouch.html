3Assess

  • Level, solids / liquids, progression, regurgitation, cough, weight, voice, heartburn, neuro · mouth, flexible laryngoscopy (pooling, fold movement), neck, nerves
  • Videofluoroscopy: oral + pharyngeal phases, aspiration, postures · FEES · barium swallow: pouch, web, ring · endoscopy (incl. transnasal) + biopsy · manometry: motility · CT

5Cricopharyngeal dysfunction

Sphincter opens too little · bar at C5–C6 seen in about a third without symptoms → match it to the complaint · high-resolution manometry: normal pharyngeal push vs high residual pressure predicts benefit · myotomy most durable

7Pharyngeal pouch (Zenker)

posterior view, schematic · two parts of the inferior constrictor
  • Starts midline behind the gullet, grows down + left · its mouth lines up with the pharynx → food enters the sac

8The pouch patient

  • Pulsion diverticulum · cricopharyngeus fails to relax / opens poorly · elderly men, usually over sixty
  • Dysphagia after a few swallows · gurgling · left neck swelling that empties · regurgitates old food, night cough, aspiration · halitosis, weight loss · rarely cancer in the sac
  • Barium swallow, lateral view · endoscopy risks perforating the sac · NG tubes with care

9Treating the pouch

  • Small, silent: watch · endoscopic stapling (Weerda scope): divide the party wall (cricopharyngeus) → sac joins the gullet · commonest, quick · not for small pouches (< ~2–3 cm) or a stiff neck
  • Dohlman / CO₂ laser: frail patients
  • External (left neck): excise, invert or suspend the sac + cricopharyngeal myotomy
  • Flexible septotomy, Z-POEM: frail patients
Always divide cricopharyngeus: it treats the cause · excision without myotomy → recurrence, fistula
903 pts (2021): open beats laser for persistent symptoms, laser = stapling
Risks: mediastinitis, fistula, RLN palsy, recurrence

10Exam traps ✗

  • Rigid oesophagoscope passed blindly with a pouch
  • Pouch excised without myotomy
  • Progressive dysphagia in an older smoker put down to age
