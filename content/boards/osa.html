SNORING & OBSTRUCTIVE SLEEP APNOEA

every apnoeic snores, not every snorer is apnoeic · CPAP first, surgery by the level of collapse ✎

1Words of the sleep study

  • Apnoea: no airflow ≥ 10 seconds · hypopnoea: flow ↓ 30–50% + sats ↓ 3–4% or arousal
  • AHI = events per hour of sleep (= RDI) · arousal = brief EEG waking from effort · sleep efficiency = asleep ÷ in bed · MSLT: timed naps (sleepiness, narcolepsy)
  • Under 5 normal · mild 5–14 · moderate 15–29 · severe 30 or more
  • Obstructive: effort continues · central: no effort · mixed: central then obstructive
  • Spectrum: primary snoring (AHI < 5, no sleepiness) → upper airway resistance (arousals, sleepy) → OSA

2Why the throat closes

  • No bony support palate → larynx · tone falls most in REM + stage 2 · each apnoea ends in an arousal → fragmented sleep
  • Snoring: palate, uvula, pillars, tongue vibrate (up to 90 dB) · ~a quarter of men, 15% of women
  • Sleep: 3–5 cycles of 90–120 min · NREM 75–80% · REM 20–25% (muscle near-paralysis)
  • Often more than one level, and it can change from night to night

3More than noise

  • Witnessed choking, unrefreshing sleep, daytime sleepiness, morning headache, memory, mood, libido, nocturia
  • Hypertension (independent risk; treatment lowers even resistant BP) · untreated severe OSA ↑ mortality · arrhythmia, ischaemia, heart failure, pulmonary hypertension · stroke · insulin resistance, polycythaemia
  • Difficult airway + opioid sensitivity · road accidents: ask about driving, advise on licensing

Children

Usually adenotonsillar hypertrophy (peak two to six years) · snoring, pauses, neck hyperextended, sweating, bedwetting · by day hyperactive, poor school, failure to thrive (not sleepy) · AHI > 1 abnormal · 10–15% snore, 1–4% OSA

4History & examination

  • From the bed partner: snoring, pauses, gasping, sweating · alcohol, sedatives, caffeine, shift work, position, weight, menopause, nose, driving
  • Epworth: eight situations scored 0–3, total out of 24 · > 10 = excessive sleepiness · STOP-BANG, Berlin screen
  • BMI ≥ 30 · neck > 42 cm (men), 37.5 cm (women) · BP, heart failure, hypothyroidism
  • Nose + endoscopy · tonsils, palate, uvula, tongue scalloping, bite, retrognathia · tongue base, lingual tonsil, epiglottis

5Find the level, prove the apnoea

  • Müller: forced inhale, nose + mouth shut · poorly reproducible
  • DISE (propofol / midazolam) · VOTE: velum, oropharynx, tongue base, epiglottis · AP, lateral or concentric · before / after failed surgery
  • Polysomnography = gold standard (EEG, EOG, EMG, ECG, flow, effort, sats, position) · cephalometry for the skeleton · home study (level 3) for simple adults · oximetry alone not enough · split-night under-counts

6First line

  • Weight loss, bariatric surgery · tirzepatide halved AHI (SURMOUNT-OSA, 2024) · no evening alcohol, sedatives · stop smoking · side sleeping · treat the nose
  • CPAP 5–20 cm H₂O = pneumatic splint · bilevel, auto-titrating · only ~half use it ≥ 4 h × 5 nights · coaching helps
  • Mandibular advancement device: snoring, mild–moderate OSA, retrognathia · jaw pain, tooth movement
  • NICE NG202 (2021): CPAP for moderate + severe, mild if symptoms affect life · SAVE (2016, 2,717): CPAP at 3.3 h a night didn't prevent CV events, but helped sleepiness, mood, QoL

7Children

  • History + exam, parental video · oximetry triage (normal ≠ excluded) · PSG for < 2, obese, Down, craniofacial, neuromuscular, cardiac
  • Adenotonsillectomy first · mild: wait, steroid spray, montelukast · persistent: DISE / MRI
  • CHAT 2013 + PATS 2023: surgery didn't improve attention or executive function, but symptoms, behaviour, QoL did · 46% of waiters normalised

  • UPPP risks: pain, bleed, VPI, nasopharyngeal stenosis, voice · palatal surgery: snoring ~80%, apnoea ~half
  • After surgery: overnight sats if severe, cardiac, tongue base, small child · restart CPAP

9Evidence

  • SAMS (JAMA 2020): multilevel surgery AHI 47.9 → 20.8 vs 45.3 → 34.5
  • Hypoglossal stimulation (2014): AHI 29.3 → 9.0 (−68%) at a year

10Exam traps ✗

  • Palate surgery when the tongue base collapses
  • Oximetry alone to diagnose or exclude
  • Free opioids after surgery in severe OSA
  • Promising nasal surgery will cure it
