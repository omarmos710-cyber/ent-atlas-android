APPROACH TO A RUNNY NOSE

bilateral = mucosal disease · unilateral clear = CSF until proven otherwise · unilateral foul or bloody = foreign body or tumour until scoped ✎

2Red flags

  • Clear one-sided after head injury or sinus surgery, worse bending, salty → beta-2 transferrin / beta-trace
  • Past meningitis + runny nose → CSF leak until proven otherwise
  • Adult one-sided blood-stained → endoscopy + imaging
  • Child one-sided foul → foreign body

3Pathway

Bilateral: itchy, atopic → allergic · watery, triggers → ipratropium · discoloured > 12 weeks → CRS · decongestant overuse → stop Unilateral: clear → beta-2 transferrin, CT, MRI · foul → scope, FB, dental, CT · bloody → scope, image, biopsy CSF confirmed: traumatic → conservative · spontaneous → repair + treat pressure

4ARIA: allergic rhinitis

  • Severity = sleep, daily activity, work, troublesome symptoms · skin-prick or specific IgE · local allergic rhinitis: negative skin tests, IgE in mucosa
  • Avoidance + saline throughout · combined spray: azelastine–fluticasone (2020) · montelukast if asthma · no depot or turbinate steroid injections

5Non-allergic rhinitis

  • Vasomotor: temperature, smells, alcohol · gustatory: hot, spicy food · elderly: profuse clear drip
  • Hormonal: pregnancy, hypothyroid · drugs: decongestants, antihypertensives, OCP, aspirin/NSAIDs · occupational
  • NARES: eosinophils, no atopy → may precede polyps, asthma, aspirin sensitivity · steroid helps
  • Watery: ipratropium before meals or triggers
  • Intractable: vidian neurectomy (dry eye) → now posterior nasal nerve neurectomy, cryo, radiofrequency
  • Common cold settles in about ten days · > twelve weeks discoloured = CRS

6CSF rhinorrhoea

  • 80–90% traumatic (injury, surgery) · rest non-traumatic: spontaneous in obese women with IIH (cribriform, lateral sphenoid recess) · highest recurrence
  • Clear, one-sided, teapot sign, straining, runs back lying · no sneeze or itch
  • Beta-2 transferrin / beta-trace · halo and glucose unreliable
  • HRCT: bony defect · MRI: encephalocele · cisternography, intrathecal fluorescein
  • Traumatic: bed rest, head up, softeners, no blowing ± lumbar drain · many close within a week
  • Persistent/spontaneous: endoscopic repair (graft, nasoseptal flap) + treat raised pressure · pneumococcal vaccine

7Other one-sided

  • FB: child, or after dental/sinus surgery
  • Odontogenic sinusitis, fungal ball: one maxillary sinus, foul
  • Unilateral choanal atresia · IP, sinonasal cancer: block, bleed, pain
  • Watery + tearing in severe orbital pain attacks → cluster headache

8Exam traps ✗

  • One-sided watery discharge treated as rhinitis for months
  • Glucose test to diagnose CSF
  • Steroid spray for the elderly drip (ipratropium works better)
  • Spontaneous leak repaired, pressure ignored
