APPROACH TO A MOUTH ULCER

most are traumatic or aphthous and heal within two weeks · a single ulcer not healed in three weeks is biopsied · the pattern finds the cause ✎

1Benign or malignant?

2Red flags

  • Single > three weeks, indurated, raised edge, lateral tongue, floor of mouth, neck node
  • Leukoplakia, erythroplakia next to it · erythroplakia transforms in about a third, leukoplakia about one in ten (2020)
  • Erosions + skin blisters → pemphigus · + eye → pemphigoid, Behçet's · on carbimazole → neutropenia

3History and exam

  • Duration, frequency, onset age, number, size, triggers (trauma, stress, menses, foods, stopping smoking), scarring
  • Genital, eye, rash, joints, bowel, weight, fevers · drugs · smoking, alcohol, betel, HIV risk
  • Site, size, base, edge · feel it: induration, fixity · sharp tooth, denture · patches, blisters, striae · neck, skin, eyes

5Aphthous stomatitis

  • Commonest recurrent · starts in childhood/adolescence
  • Minor: under 1 cm, non-keratinised, 10–14 days, no scar · major: larger, deeper, weeks, scars · herpetiform: crops of pinheads
  • Triggers: trauma, stress, hormones, SLS toothpaste, iron/B12/folate deficiency
  • Bloods: FBC, ferritin, B12, folate, coeliac serology

6Other key causes

  • SCC: painless at first, indurated, everted · smoking, alcohol, betel, chewing tobacco · tongue + floor of mouth highest risk
  • Lichen planus: white lacy striae, buccal, both sides · erosive: small malignant risk, follow up
  • Pemphigus: fragile blisters → erosions, mouth first · pemphigoid: tougher, desquamative gingivitis, scars eyes, larynx · perilesional biopsy for DIF
  • Behçet's · Crohn's, coeliac, SLE, reactive arthritis, cyclic neutropenia · PFAPA in children

7Investigate and treat

  • Recurrent: FBC, ferritin, B12, folate, coeliac · CRP, HIV as indicated · HSV PCR for vesicles
  • Persistent single → biopsy · blistering → perilesional tissue fresh for immunofluorescence
  • Treat cause: smooth tooth, adjust denture, replace deficiency, stop drug
  • Aphthae: chlorhexidine, benzydamine · topical steroid (betamethasone mouthwash, beclometasone spray) · avoid SLS · colchicine (specialist)
  • NG12: urgent referral for ulcer > three weeks, lump, red or red-white patch

8Pathway

Single > three weeks → urgent biopsy · trauma → remove, review two weeks Recurrent → bloods · aphthae: topical · genital/eye → Behçet's Blisters → biopsy + DIF, specialist Acute + fever → viral, supportive · carbimazole → FBC

9Exam traps ✗

  • Repeated steroids for a persistent single ulcer
  • Biopsy of the necrotic centre only
  • Iron, B12, folate, coeliac missed
