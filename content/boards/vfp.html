VOCAL FOLD PARALYSIS

a sign along a very long nerve · find the cause, then choose voice, airway or swallow ✎

1Two nerves

  • RLN: every fold mover except cricothyroid (external laryngeal) · interarytenoid from both sides · sensory below folds
  • Internal laryngeal: sensory above folds
  • Right hooks subclavian · left leaves vagus in the mediastinum, hooks aortic arch at ligamentum → longer, palsied far more often
  • SLN: inferior ganglion, behind ICA, divides at greater horn of hyoid · external branch beside superior thyroid artery

2Where the fold lies

  • Semon: progressive lesions hit (newer) abductor fibres first → fold near midline
  • Wagner–Grossman: SLN intact → cricothyroid pulls to paramedian · both lost → cadaveric
  • Misdirected regeneration: tone + synkinesis, no useful movement · often medialises over months

3Causes by level

  • Nuclear (nucleus ambiguus): lateral medullary stroke, MND, MS, syrinx, Chiari · other cranial nerves
  • High vagal (combined palsy): posterior fossa, skull base fracture, NPC, glomus, parapharyngeal tumour, nodes
  • Systemic: diabetes, viral, diphtheria, syphilis, lead · idiopathic ~20–30% (exclusion)

5Superior + combined palsy

  • SLN: no cricothyroid + numb supraglottis · can't raise pitch (singers notice) · glottis askew, commissure to healthy side, short lax wavy fold · EMG · voice therapy
  • Bilateral SLN: aspiration, choking, husky voice · cuffed trach, tube feeding, epiglottopexy · CT approximation for pitch
  • Combined unilateral: cadaveric fold · hoarse, breathy, aspirates liquids, weak cough
  • Combined bilateral: insensitive larynx, aphonia, pneumonia → trach + gastrostomy → epiglottopexy, plication → separation / diversion → laryngectomy

6Paralysis or fixation?

  • Flexible + strobe: movement, gap, level, arytenoid tilt · phonate, sniff, cough
  • Scar, cricoarytenoid arthritis/dislocation, tumour fix a fold → palpate the arytenoid at DL
  • LEMG: paralysis vs fixation, synkinesis, prognosis (absent/synkinetic = poor)
  • CT skull base → arch or MRI brainstem · chest · thyroid US · FEES/videofluoroscopy · glucose, serology
  • Minimum set: strobe, GRBAS, maximum phonation time, VHI (2018)

7Closing a unilateral gap

  • Voice therapy early · wait six to twelve months for recovery
  • Injection: CMC 2–3 months, hyaluronic acid 4–9 months (early, even weeks, if aspirating) · calcium hydroxylapatite ~18 months, fat · no Teflon (granuloma)
  • Early injection (within six months) → fewer later framework operations (2010)
  • Type I thyroplasty: window + silastic/Gore-Tex, under LA to tune voice · permanent palsy
  • Arytenoid adduction: wide posterior gap, level difference
  • Reinnervation (ansa → RLN): bulk + tone · children · RCT 2011: better under 52, thyroplasty better over 52

8Bilateral: the airway

  • Widening the glottis costs voice
  • Tracheostomy: safest, gold standard · speaking valve
  • Posterior cordotomy (Kashima), partial medial arytenoidectomy, suture lateralisation
  • Older: Woodman arytenoidopexy, type II thyroplasty · botox to adductors · pacing (experimental)
After thyroidectomy: temporary trach + wait · many recover · nothing irreversible early

10Thyroid surgery + infants

  • US guideline (2013): voice before thyroidectomy · scope pre-op if voice off or invasive cancer · scope after if voice changed
  • Nerve monitoring + staging when first-side signal lost avoids most bilateral palsies
  • Infant unilateral: weak cry, feeding (birth, cardiac) · bilateral: stridor (Chiari, hydrocephalus) → trach · many recover

11Exam traps ✗

  • Left palsy called idiopathic without imaging to the arch
  • Fixed arytenoid treated as paralysis, no palpation or EMG
  • Irreversible widening soon after thyroidectomy
