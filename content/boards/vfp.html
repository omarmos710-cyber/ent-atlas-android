VOCAL FOLD PARALYSIS

a sign along a very long nerve · find the cause, then choose voice, airway or swallow ✎

1Two nerves

  • RLN: every fold mover except cricothyroid (external laryngeal, a tensor) · interarytenoid from both sides · sensory below folds
  • Internal laryngeal: sensory above folds (through thyrohyoid membrane)
  • Right leaves vagus at root of neck, hooks subclavian · left leaves vagus in the mediastinum, hooks aortic arch at ligamentum → longer, palsied far more often · both climb in tracheo-oesophageal groove
  • SLN: inferior ganglion, behind ICA, divides at greater horn of hyoid · external branch beside superior thyroid artery

2Causes by level

  • Overall: malignancy (lung, thyroid, oesophagus) + surgery (thyroid, anterior cervical spine, cardiothoracic)
  • Supranuclear: rare · nuclear (nucleus ambiguus): lateral medullary stroke, MND, polio, MS, syrinx, Chiari · other cranial nerves
  • High vagal (combined palsy): posterior fossa, basal meningitis, skull base fracture, NPC, glomus, parapharyngeal tumour, nodes, lymphoma
  • Systemic: diabetes, viral, diphtheria, syphilis, lead · idiopathic ~20–30% (exclusion)

3Where the fold lies

  • Semon: progressive lesions hit (newer) abductor fibres first → fold near midline
  • Wagner–Grossman: SLN intact → cricothyroid pulls to paramedian · both lost → cadaveric
  • Misdirected regeneration: tone + synkinesis, no useful movement · often lateral first, medialises over months

4Paralysis or fixation?

  • Flexible + strobe: movement, gap, level, arytenoid tilt · phonate, sniff, cough
  • Scar, cricoarytenoid arthritis/dislocation, tumour fix a fold

  • No Teflon (granuloma) · early injection (within six months) → fewer later framework operations (2010)
  • Type I thyroplasty: window + silastic/Gore-Tex, under LA to tune voice · gap of a few mm, symmetrical arytenoids
  • Arytenoid adduction: suture through muscular process · wide posterior gap, lateral vocal process, level difference
  • Reinnervation (ansa → RLN): bulk + tone, not movement · nerve cut, children · RCT 2011: better under 52, thyroplasty better over 52

8Bilateral abductor: the airway

  • After thyroidectomy, or neuritis · widening the glottis costs voice · tracheostomy: safest, gold standard, finger occlusion or speaking valve
  • Posterior transverse cordotomy (Kashima, laser), partial medial arytenoidectomy, suture lateralisation · repeat if needed
  • Older: arytenoidectomy, Woodman arytenoidopexy, type II thyroplasty · botox to adductors · reinnervation, pacing (experimental)
After thyroidectomy: temporary trach + wait · many recover · nothing irreversible early

9Superior + combined palsy

  • SLN: no cricothyroid + numb supraglottis · can't raise pitch (singers notice) · glottis askew, commissure to healthy side, short lax wavy flapping fold · cricothyroid EMG · voice therapy
  • Bilateral SLN: aspiration, choking, husky voice · cuffed trach, tube feeding, epiglottopexy · cricothyroid approximation for pitch
  • Combined unilateral: cadaveric fold · hoarse, breathy, aspirates liquids, weak cough
  • Combined bilateral: insensitive larynx, aphonia, pneumonia → trach + gastrostomy → epiglottopexy, plication (reversible) → separation / diversion → laryngectomy

10Infants

  • Unilateral: weak cry, feeding (birth trauma, cardiac, great vessels) · bilateral: stridor (Chiari, hydrocephalus, haemorrhage, meningocele) → trach · many recover, delay surgery

11Exam traps ✗

  • Left palsy called idiopathic without imaging to the arch
  • Fixed arytenoid treated as paralysis, no palpation or EMG
  • Irreversible widening soon after thyroidectomy
