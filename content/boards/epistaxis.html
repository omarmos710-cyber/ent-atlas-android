EPISTAXIS

a sign, not a diagnosis · resuscitate, find the point, ask why ✎

1Who bleeds, and why

~1 in 6 bleed each year, few seek help · bimodal: children + young adults (front of septum) vs past middle age (admissions, posterior) · ED patient: older, hypertensive, anticoagulated → a resuscitation problem
GroupExamples
Localtrauma (picking: commonest) · dry air, rhinitis, sinusitis · spur, perforation · tumour · surgery, tubes, sprays, cocaine
Systemicanticoagulants, antiplatelets, NSAIDs, supplements · haemophilia, von Willebrand (commonest inherited), low platelets, leukaemia · HHT · hypertension · liver, renal failure
Idiopathicearned only after the search
Teenage boy, unilateral bleed + block = angiofibroma, no biopsy in clinic · adult unilateral + crusts / pain → endoscopy for tumour · under two → bleeding disorder or non-accidental injury · massive bleed after trauma / skull base surgery → carotid pseudoaneurysm

2Two carotids meet in the nose

schematic · blue = internal carotid · green = external carotid
  • Kiesselbach's plexus in Little's area: anteroinferior septum · dry air, thin bound mucosa, fingernail reach → about nine in ten bleeds
  • Sphenopalatine (maxillary): main supply, dominant in ~85% · enters just behind the crista ethmoidalis → classic posterior bleed
  • Ethmoidals (ophthalmic): anterior via the roof, posterior smaller · greater palatine via the incisive canal · superior labial (facial): front of septum, vestibule
  • Woodruff's plexus: other classic posterior site · Dhingra: veins below the back of the inferior turbinate · Lee: naso-nasopharyngeal plexus over the posterior centimetre of floor, inferior + middle meatus
  • Bleeding above the middle turbinate = ethmoidal = internal carotid
  • Free anastomoses → bleeds persist; ligating one vessel may not be enough · young: retrocolumellar vein (venous)

3Resuscitate first, then look

  • How much lost? Airway safe? · swallowed blood hides loss · dark vomit ≠ haematemesis
  • History: side of onset, volume, front or throat, previous bleeds · block, crusts, pain · drugs + supplements, family history
  • Exam: pulse, BP, pallor, telangiectasias (lips, tongue, fingers) · Little's area first after clot removal + vasoconstriction
  • Endoscopy: recurrent, not localised, or suspicious unilateral bleed

5Special patients

  • Child: Little's area, picking · recurrent: antiseptic cream (chlorhexidine + neomycin) twice daily × four weeks (no arachis oil in peanut allergy) · bruising or family history → clotting + von Willebrand
  • Anticoagulated: local control first, resorbable pack, INR · reverse only if life-threatening, with the prescriber · ask about garlic, ginkgo, vitamin E
  • HHT: repeated packing + cautery make it worse → laser, bevacizumab, septodermoplasty, Young's
  • Late massive bleed after head injury → ICA pseudoaneurysm: pack, resuscitate, urgent angiography

6The ladder, one rung at a time

sit up, lean forward→pinch soft nose ≥5 min (10–15), no peeking→suction, vasoconstrictor, look
cautery of the point→anterior pack→posterior pack→endoscopic cautery→ligation / embolisation
  • Trotter's method: over a basin, mouth breathing · ice in the mouth helps a little; ice on the bridge: no proof
  • Anaesthetise first · treat only the bleeding point
  • Silver nitrate: ring around the vessel, edge first, then inwards · wipe the vestibule after
  • Bipolar (endoscopic) preferred: fails less, fewer packs, admissions, less pain
  • Ribbon gauze 2.5 cm (child 12 mm), paraffin or BIPP, in layers · out at 24 h
  • Sponge / balloon = ED standard · resorbable if anticoagulated or bleeding disorder
Never cauterise both sides of the septum at one sitting → perforation

7Posterior pack, packed patient

  • Postnasal gauze: three ties · two over a dental roll at the columella · third taped to the cheek to pull it out
  • Foley 12–14 F, balloon in the nasopharynx with saline, not air · protect ala + columella · or a dual balloon
  • Admit: posterior packs fail in about a third · oxygen: nasopulmonary reflex → hypoxia
  • Hypertension: treat pain + anxiety first, no sharp drops

8Drugs and doses

  • Before cautery / pack: co-phenylcaine spray (lidocaine 5% + phenylephrine 0.5%) or 1:1000 adrenaline pledget · wait 3–4 min
  • Silver nitrate 3–10 s to a visible point · one side at a time
  • After cautery (adult): Naseptin four times a day × 10 days (twice a day up to 2 weeks) · child: see 5 · old stock has peanut oil
  • Peanut, soya, neomycin allergy: mupirocin 2% two or three times a day × 5–7 days
  • Packs: antibiotic ointment · no routine systemic antibiotics for anterior packs
  • TXA: oral 1 g three times a day, up to ~4 days · topical: NoPAC (496 patients) 43.7% vs 41.3% packed = no benefit · oral helps in HHT
  • Warfarin + major bleed: vitamin K 5–10 mg IV + prothrombin complex concentrate
  • DOAC, life-threatening: idarucizumab (dabigatran) · andexanet alfa or prothrombin complex (factor Xa inhibitors)

  • TESPAL: flap back to the crista, clip every branch · control 95–98% · fails → accessory branch or ethmoidal source
  • Ethmoidal via Lynch incision · 24-12-6 mm: lacrimal crest → anterior → posterior foramen → optic canal
  • Embolisation (internal maxillary) ~88% · unfit, tumours, pseudoaneurysm · risks stroke, blindness
  • Historical: maxillary ligation (fails 10–15%), external carotid ligation

10Complications, outlook, traps ✗

  • Bleed: shock, anaemia, aspiration, myocardial ischaemia · cautery: perforation · packs: alar necrosis, sinusitis, otitis media, hypoxia, toxic shock
  • Anterior cauterised → home with cream · significant posterior → observe 6–12 h · any pack → admit
  • UK audit (1,826): 1 in 7 back within 30 days, 3.4% dead at 30 days
  • Traps: missing loss in a patient swallowing blood · blind packing · stopping anticoagulants a pack would cover · embolising an ethmoidal bleed · repacking HHT
