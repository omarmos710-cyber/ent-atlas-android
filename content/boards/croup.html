CROUP, EPIGLOTTITIS & ACUTE LARYNGITIS

the barking child can be soothed · the drooling child must not be upset ✎

1Acute laryngitis

  • Viral after a cold (parainfluenza, influenza, rhino) → bacterial · measles, chickenpox, pertussis · candida (inhaled steroid)
  • Non-infective: shouting, allergy, irritants, burns, reflux, intubation
  • Abrupt hoarseness or aphonia, pain after talking, dry cough at night · early: red supraglottis, white true folds · later red folds, sticky mucus, subglottic swelling · abuse → fold haemorrhage
  • Breathlessness is not a feature → think again
WhenTreatment
Every patientvoice rest (most important; not whispering), hydration, humidification/steam, stop smoking, paracetamol · settles in 1–2 weeks
Not routineantibiotics or steroids in uncomplicated viral (Cochrane: no meaningful benefit) · antibiotics only if bacterial (fever, purulent sputum) · PPI if reflux · steroid kept for burns
Performer, urgentshort oral steroid, specialist advice, after laryngoscopy
> 3 weeks, or smokerlaryngoscopy · urgent referral if over 45 with persistent hoarseness (NICE)
Membranous: pyogenic membrane → swab + culture to exclude diphtheria

2Epiglottitis

  • Classically two – seven years, H. influenzae b · since Hib vaccine: mostly adults (strep, staph)
  • Supraglottis swells → ball valve
  • Hours · high fever, septic · child: stridor, tripod, drooling, muffled, no cough · adult: pain out of proportion to a normal oropharynx, hot-potato voice, tender hyoid
✗ tongue depressor ✗ cannula ✗ lying down ✗ radiology alone · stay with parent, oxygen without a struggle → theatre: senior anaesthetist, gas induction, ENT ready to trach

5Croup

  • Commonest acute stridor · six months – three years, boys, autumn–winter · parainfluenza 1 + 2, RSV, influenza, adeno
  • Subglottis swells inside the rigid cricoid + crusts
  • Cold → hoarse, barking cough, stridor worse at night · not toxic
  • Recession, tachypnoea → restless, rising pulse, pallor, cyanosis, drowsy = late
  • Clinical, severity by Westley score · no tests in typical croup · atypical: senior airway, AP film only if stable → steeple
SeverityDrug and dose
Every childoral dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg once (up to 0.6 mg/kg if severe; lower as good) or prednisolone 1–2 mg/kg · repeat after 12–24 hours if needed · mild → home
Vomiting, no oralnebulised budesonide 2 mg, single dose
Moderate–severehumidified O₂ + nebulised adrenaline 1:1000, 400 micrograms/kg (0.4 ml/kg), max 5 mg (5 ml) · works in minutes, lasts ~2 hours → watch ≥ 2–3 hours for rebound · admit
Failingintubate in theatre, smaller tube · trach rarely
Not helpfulantibiotics (unless secondary), steam, cough medicines
Recurrent, under six months, or won't settle → MLB when well: stenosis, cyst, haemangioma, cleft
  • Spasmodic croup: sudden night attacks, no fever, well by morning · allergy, reflux
  • Cochrane (43 trials, 4,565 children, 2018): steroids help within two hours, last ≥ 24 hours · return visits RR 0.52 · ~15 hours shorter stay · adrenaline: better at 30 minutes, not at two to six hours
  • Complication: bacterial tracheitis

6Croup that isn't croup

  • Bacterial tracheitis (S. aureus, strep, pneumococcus): after viral croup · high fever, toxic, thick secretions, fails croup treatment · bronchoscopy: strip membranes (often repeatedly), intubate · ceftriaxone 50–80 mg/kg once daily + flucloxacillin 50 mg/kg every 6 hours (or clindamycin) · MRSA: + vancomycin · 10–14 days · pneumonia
  • Laryngeal diphtheria: unimmunised under ten, from faucial · low fever, marked toxaemia · grey membrane bleeds when lifted, bull neck
treat on suspicion→antitoxin→penicillin→airway
Antitoxin 20,000–100,000 units IV after test dose · benzylpenicillin or erythromycin 7–10 days · bed rest two to four weeks, cardiac monitor · isolate, immunise contacts · exotoxin → myocarditis, palatal, pharyngeal, ocular, diaphragmatic paralysis

7Laryngeal oedema

  • Loose mucosa: supraglottis + subglottis, rarely folds
  • Infection (incl. quinsy, Ludwig's) · trauma, intubation, endoscopy, burns, caustics · cancer · allergy, ACE-i, hereditary · RT · nephritis, heart failure, myxoedema
  • Stridor + distress → airway · allergy: IM adrenaline 0.5 mL 1:1000 (repeat after five minutes), antihistamine, steroid · infective, traumatic, post-RT: steroid · hereditary: C1 inhibitor or icatibant · stop ACE-i

9Exam traps ✗

  • Tongue depressor on a drooling, stridulous child
  • Epiglottitis sent to radiology alone
  • Discharge soon after nebulised adrenaline
  • Missing adult epiglottitis behind a normal oropharynx
