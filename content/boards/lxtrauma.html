LARYNGOTRACHEAL TRAUMA & STENOSIS

nearly half die at the scene · the rest may look well for hours · airway first, framework early ✎

1How the larynx breaks

  • Blunt: dashboard/steering wheel with head extended, blows, clothesline wire, strangulation · penetrating: knife, glass, gunshot · internal: intubation, endoscopy, burns, caustics, FB
  • Shielded by jaw, sternum, spine + mobile · fractures need a direct blow, head extended · after forty cartilage calcifies, brittle · children bend, but swell dangerously
  • Haematoma, oedema · mucosal tears + emphysema · arytenoid dislocation · cricothyroid dislocation (RLN behind) · hyoid
  • Thyroid fracture: upper → avulsed epiglottis · lower → folds, commissure · cricoid fracture · upper rings
  • Laryngotracheal separation: trachea retracts into the chest, often both RLNs
Talks + breathes on arrival, obstructs hours later → admit every significant anterior neck injury, scope the airway

2Voice, breath, swallow, blood

  • Stridor, hoarse or aphonic, painful swallow + aspiration, pain on speaking, haemoptysis
  • Bruising, tenderness, surgical emphysema (grows with cough), lost thyroid prominence, step, crepitus
  • Assume cervical spine injury · vessels, oesophagus, pneumothorax, head

3Assess as the airway allows

  • Flexible nasendoscopy first (stable): oedema, tears, exposed cartilage, arytenoids, movement, lumen
  • CT + 3D: fractures, dislocations · spine, vessels · only with a safe airway
  • Rigid laryngo-, oesophago-, bronchoscopy after airway secured · chest film · contrast swallow · angiography

4Airway, then framework

  • Distress: awake tracheostomy under LA → laryngoscopy · intubation can enter a false passage or complete a separation · if tried: most senior, fibreoptic, neck prepped
  • Minor: admit, voice rest, humidity, head up, early full-dose steroid, antibiotics (perichondritis), anti-reflux
  • Repair for displaced/unstable fracture, exposed cartilage, big tear, commissure, arytenoid, immobility, separation · 24–48 hours, before ten days
  • Thyrotomy · suture mucosa, cover cartilage · fix with wire or miniplates · reattach epiglottis · reduce arytenoid · keel at commissure · soft stent two to six weeks (granulation)
  • Stenosis, perichondritis, chondronecrosis, palsy, web, poor voice

5Stenosis: the late result

  • Intubation commonest (tube + cuff necrosis posterior glottis, subglottis) · trauma, high trach, cricothyroidotomy, burns, RT, GPA, polychondritis, sarcoid, reflux, idiopathic (women)
  • Progressive dyspnoea, biphasic stridor · called asthma
  • Office scope, CT · DL + bronchoscopy: length, grade, site, palpate arytenoids

7Penetrating neck: zones

  • Penetrating = breaches platysma · mortality up to ~6%, half from bleeding
  • I: great vessels, trachea, oesophagus, lung apices, thoracic duct · III: distal carotid, vertebral, IX–XII

8Selective, not zonal

  • ATLS: airway (early intubation or surgical), pressure on bleeding
  • Hard signs → theatre: expanding haematoma, active bleeding, air through wound, refractory shock, absent pulse, bruit, stroke
  • Soft signs (haemoptysis, dysphonia, dysphagia, emphysema, stable haematoma) or none → CT angiography + endoscopy or swallow
  • "No zone" approach replaced mandatory zone II exploration · carotid: repair, not ligate

9Exam traps ✗

  • Sending home a talking patient who later obstructs
  • Blind intubation through a disrupted larynx
  • Repair delayed beyond the first week or two
  • Post-intubation subglottic stenosis treated as asthma
