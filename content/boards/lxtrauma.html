LARYNGOTRACHEAL TRAUMA & STENOSIS

nearly half die at the scene · the rest may look well for hours · airway first, framework early ✎

1How the larynx breaks

  • Blunt: dashboard/steering wheel with head extended, blows, kicks, clothesline wire, strangulation · penetrating: knife, glass, gunshot · internal: intubation, endoscopy, burns, caustics, FB
  • Shielded by jaw, sternum, spine + mobile · fractures need a direct blow, head extended · after forty cartilage calcifies, brittle · children bend, but swell dangerously
  • Haematoma, oedema · mucosal tears + emphysema · arytenoid dislocation · cricothyroid dislocation (RLN behind) · hyoid
  • Thyroid fracture: upper → avulsed epiglottis, false folds · lower → folds, commissure · cricoid fracture · upper rings
  • Laryngotracheal separation: trachea retracts into the mediastinum, usually both RLNs

2Voice, breath, swallow, blood

  • Stridor, hoarse or aphonic, painful swallow + aspiration, pain on speaking, haemoptysis
  • Bruising, tenderness, surgical emphysema (grows with cough), lost thyroid prominence, step, crepitus, cricoid separation
  • Assume cervical spine injury · vessels, oesophagus, pharynx, pneumothorax, head, abdomen
Talks + breathes on arrival, obstructs hours later → admit every significant anterior neck injury, scope the airway

4Airway, then conservative

  • Distress: awake tracheostomy under LA → laryngoscopy under GA · intubation can enter a false passage or complete a separation · if tried: most experienced, fibreoptic, neck prepped
  • Minor (near-normal scope, undisplaced): admit, observe, early full-dose steroid, antibiotics (perichondritis), anti-reflux

5Open repair

  • For displaced/unstable fracture, exposed cartilage, big tear, commissure, arytenoid dislocation, immobility, separation · 24–48 hours, before ten days
  • Thyrotomy · suture mucosa, cover cartilage · wire or miniplates · epiglottis reattached/excised · arytenoid reduced/removed · keel at commissure · soft stent two to six weeks (granulation) · separation: anastomosis
  • Complications: stenosis, perichondritis, abscess, chondronecrosis, palsy or fixation, web, poor voice

6Stenosis: the late result

  • Intubation commonest (tube + cuff necrosis posterior glottis, subglottis) · trauma, high trach, cricothyroidotomy, burns, RT, GPA, polychondritis, sarcoid, reflux, idiopathic (women)
  • Progressive dyspnoea, biphasic stridor, hoarse · called asthma · office scope, CT · DL + bronchoscopy: length, grade, site, palpate arytenoids (fixation vs paralysis)

8Penetrating neck: zones

  • Penetrating = breaches platysma · mortality up to ~6%, half from bleeding
  • I: great vessels, trachea, oesophagus, lung apices, thoracic duct · II: commonest, easiest to expose, best prognosis · III: distal carotid, vertebral, IX–XII

9Selective, not zonal

  • Trauma life support: airway (early intubation or surgical), pressure on bleeding
  • Hard signs → theatre: expanding haematoma, active bleeding, air through wound, refractory shock, absent pulse, bruit, stroke
  • Soft signs (haemoptysis, dysphonia, dysphagia, emphysema, stable haematoma) or none → CT angiography + endoscopy or swallow
  • "No zone" approach (trauma societies) replaced mandatory zone II exploration: fewer negative explorations, no important injuries missed
  • Carotid injury at exploration: repair rather than ligate

10Exam traps ✗

  • Sending home a talking patient who later obstructs
  • Blind intubation through a disrupted larynx
  • Repair delayed beyond the first week or two
  • Post-intubation subglottic stenosis treated as asthma
