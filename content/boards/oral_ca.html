ORAL PREMALIGNANCY & ORAL CANCER

a cancer you can see, still found late · early 80–90% cured, advanced 30–40% · depth, the neck, the pathology report ✎

1Oral SCC

  • > 90% SCC · rising in young non-smokers (often women) with tongue cancer
  • Tobacco (smoked + chewed), alcohol multiplies it, betel quid + areca (South Asia) · HPV small role (unlike oropharynx) · Fanconi anaemia
  • West: lateral tongue + floor · South Asia: buccal + GB sulcus (quid)
  • Ulcer, lump, red/white patch, referred otalgia, bleeding, loose teeth, ill-fitting denture, trismus, numb lip (mental nerve), neck lump
Single ulcer not healed in three weeks: biopsy

4Depth ≠ thickness

DOI: from the level of the adjacent normal basement membrane to the deepest invasion · predicts nodes better than surface size · thickness also counts exophytic growth

7The pathway

biopsy→stage→primary→neck→pathology
Surgery first + risk-based adjuvant: best control at every stage for operable disease

8The primary

  • 5 mm margin in three dimensions · specimen: ≥ 5 mm clear, 1–5 mm close, < 1 mm involved (worse survival)
  • Marginal mandibulectomy: abutting, superficial erosion (keeps continuity) · segmental: medullary invasion, thin edentulous jaw
  • RT/brachytherapy for some lip + hard palate where surgery costs too much function
  • Rebuild like with like · small: secondary intention, graft, local flap · tongue/floor: radial forearm, ALT · segmental jaw: fibula (implants later)

9The neck

  • D'Cruz 2015 (500, early N0): elective dissection I–III beat watchful waiting · three-year survival 80.0% vs 67.5%
  • Elective when DOI > ~3–4 mm, lower for high-risk sites
  • Sentinel node: three trials, 608 pts (2022): similar survival + neck control, better shoulder
  • N+: levels I–V (or I–IV) · opposite side for midline or extensive disease

11Complications · follow-up

  • Neck surgery: bleeding, infection (reconstruction, drinkers, tracheostomy), seroma, chyle leak, marginal mandibular + XI weakness
  • Carotid blowout: rare after primary surgery · irradiated/recurrent neck, salivary fistula, flap necrosis
  • RT: dry mouth, caries, trismus, osteoradionecrosis → lifelong dental care, jaw exercises
  • Review often first two years (most recurrences), at least five · second primaries (mouth, pharynx, larynx, oesophagus, lung) · stopping tobacco + alcohol matters most

12Potentially malignant disorders

  • WHO 2005: "precancer" → OPMD: risk of cancer, not always before it
  • Leukoplakia: white patch not any other lesion · commonest · erythroplakia: rare, highest risk, most already severe dysplasia or cancer
  • PVL: multifocal, spreading, older non-smoking women · submucous fibrosis (areca) · lichen planus, esp. erosive

Higher risk if

  • red or red-and-white · PVL · lateral/ventral tongue, floor
  • multiple, large, long-standing · non-smoker, woman
  • dysplasia: ~5–18% transform

13A white or red patch

  • Inspect + palpate mouth, neck · remove irritants, treat candida · review in 2–3 weeks
  • Persists → incisional biopsy of the reddest, most indurated, irregular part · mild, moderate, severe dysplasia · large/multiple: mapping biopsies
  • Toluidine blue, Lugol, autofluorescence, NBI, brush cytology: choose sites · never replace biopsy
  • Stop tobacco, alcohol, areca · excise moderate/severe dysplasia, erythroplakia · follow for life (field change)

14Exam traps ✗

  • Repeated topical steroids for a persistent ulcer, no biopsy
  • Watching the N0 neck in a deep tongue cancer
  • Calling tumour thickness "depth of invasion"
  • No dental assessment before RT
