TONSILLITIS & PERITONSILLAR ABSCESS

most sore throats need no antibiotic · the one who can't swallow his saliva has a quinsy ✎

1The palatine tonsil

  • In the fossa between anterior (palatoglossal) + posterior (palatopharyngeal) pillars · bigger than it looks
  • Medial: 12–15 crypts · crypta magna = second pouch remnant · lateral: capsule + loose tissue = dissection plane + quinsy space · lower pole: tonsillolingual sulcus (hidden carcinoma)
  • Bed: superior constrictor + styloglossus · IX and an elongated styloid low in the fossa · ICA ~2.5 cm behind + lateral
  • Five arteries: tonsillar branch of facial (main, lower pole), ascending palatine, ascending pharyngeal, dorsal lingual, descending palatine
  • Paratonsillar vein across the capsule = usual bleeder · nodes: jugulodigastric · IX → earache
  • Most active 4–10 years · removal hasn't been shown to harm immunity

3Tonsillectomy: who

Paradise / SIGN

7 in one year · 5 a year × two · 3 a year × three · disabling · below: watch
  • OSA = commonest indication in children · ≥ two weeks off school / work a year · quinsy (child: 4–6 weeks after; adult: after a second) · febrile seizures · one-sided big tonsil: lymphoma (child) / SCC (adult) · unknown primary
  • Relative: carriers, halitosis, valvular heart disease · part of UPPP, IX neurectomy, styloidectomy
  • Not: Hb < 10 g/dL, acute infection, under three (relative), (submucous) cleft, bleeding disorders, uncontrolled disease
  • NATTINA (Lancet 2023, 453 adults): sore-throat days 23 vs 30, rate ratio 0.53 → offer adults who meet criteria

4The operation

  • Rose's position, Boyle-Davis gag on Draffin's bipods · incise at the anterior pillar, upper pole first, outside the capsule · snare the lower pole
  • Hot (diathermy, coblation, laser): less bleeding on the day, more pain + secondary haemorrhage · lowest power · intracapsular (tonsillotomy): less pain + bleeding, may regrow
  • Dexamethasone once · no routine antibiotics · paracetamol + ibuprofen (no aspirin) · no codeine under 12 (or under 18 for OSA) · pain peaks days 5–7 · back in ~2 weeks

6Acute tonsillitis

catarrhalfollicular (spots)parenchymatousmembranous
  • School children + young adults · mostly viral · bacterial: group A strep (also staph, pneumococcus, H. influenzae)
  • Sore throat, odynophagia, fever 38–40°C, earache, abdominal pain (mesenteric adenitis) · tender jugulodigastric nodes
  • Tests: FeverPAIN / Centor decides antibiotics · rapid antigen / swab: selected · FBC + film, Monospot: membrane, spleen · diphtheria swab: unvaccinated / travel · toxic: FBC, CRP, renal, cultures
  • → chronic, quinsy, parapharyngeal / neck abscess, otitis media, rheumatic fever, glomerulonephritis, endocarditis

8Faucial diphtheria

C. diphtheriae, incubation two to six days · grey membrane, bull neck · toxin → myocarditis, weeks later palatal, ocular, diaphragm palsies · airway membrane · antitoxin IV on suspicion, dose from UKHSA (textbook: 20,000–40,000 units < 48 h, tonsil only · 80,000–120,000 later / extensive) · mild adult: clarithromycin 500 mg bd × 14 days (azithromycin / erythromycin; IV benzylpenicillin if can't swallow) · isolate until three negative swabs · booster · contacts: swab, azithromycin / clarithromycin, vaccinate

9Chronic tonsillitis · PFAPA

  • Follicular (cheesy crypts) · parenchymatous (huge: OSA, cor pulmonale) · fibroid (small, infected)
  • Halitosis, bad taste, cough · flushed anterior pillars + persistent jugulodigastric nodes · tonsillectomy if criteria met
  • Size grade 0 in the fossa · 1 < 25% · 2 25–50% · 3 50–75% · 4 > 75% (kissing)
  • → intratonsillar abscess, tonsillolith, cyst · lingual tonsil hypertrophy / abscess
  • PFAPA: fever, aphthae, pharyngitis, adenitis every 3–8 weeks · exclude cyclic neutropenia · prednisolone 1–2 mg/kg (max 60 mg) once aborts · tonsillectomy cures most

10Quinsy

Pus between capsule + superior constrictor · blocked crypt (crypta magna) or Weber glands · mixed aerobes + anaerobes · young adults, one side
mouth open, patient's left quinsy · ✕ = incision: base-of-uvula line meets anterior pillar line, just lateral
  • Fever 40°C, one-sided pain, drooling, hot-potato voice, earache, trismus (best single sign of pus), torticollis
  • Palate + pillar bulge above the tonsil · uvula pushed across · tonsil down + in
  • Clinical diagnosis · unsure → aspirate · contrast CT if uncertain, trismus prevents a look or parapharyngeal spread

11Treat the quinsy

admit, IV fluids→IV antibiotic ± one steroid dose→drain: needle or guarded-blade stab + sinus forceps
  • Benzylpenicillin 1.2 g IV 6-hourly (child 30 mg/kg, max 1.2 g) ± metronidazole 400 mg oral / 500 mg IV 8-hourly · or co-amoxiclav · allergy: clindamycin 600 mg 8-hourly · step down to penicillin V 500 mg 6-hourly · 7–10 days total
  • Dexamethasone 8–10 mg IV (child 0.15–0.6 mg/kg, max 10 mg) → less pain + trismus
  • Interval tonsillectomy at four to six weeks after a second adult quinsy (recurs ~17%), more readily in children · hot tonsillectomy: more bleeding
  • → parapharyngeal abscess, laryngeal oedema, aspiration, sepsis, Lemierre, carotid erosion
