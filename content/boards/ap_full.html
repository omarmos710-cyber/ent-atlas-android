APPROACH TO A BLOCKED OR FULL EAR

mostly wax, effusion or a sluggish tube · don't miss sudden SNHL, the adult one-sided effusion, early Ménière's ✎

1Four places

SiteCauses
Canalwax, debris, FB, OE, exostoses
Middle eareffusion, retraction, perforation, haemotympanum, mass
Tubeobstructive or patulous
Inner earMénière's, sudden SNHL, SCD, raised ICP
Also TMJ disorder, anxiety

2Don't miss

  • Sudden loss + fullness: test hearing that day · ≥ 30 dB over three frequencies within 72 hours → steroids now, MRI later
  • Adult one-sided effusion → scope ± biopsy the nasopharynx
  • Fullness + low-tone loss + episodic vertigo → Ménière's
  • Pulsatile tinnitus, headache, blurred vision → IIH

3History

  • Sudden, on waking, muffled + tinnitus = sudden SNHL until tested
  • After cold, flight, dive → tube dysfunction, barotrauma
  • Pops and relieves → obstructive · autophony, own breathing, better lying down or with a cold, after weight loss, pregnancy → patulous
  • Hears own eye movements, footsteps; vertigo with sound/straining → SCD
  • Vertigo, roaring tinnitus · rhinitis, reflux · jaw clicking · epistaxis, neck lump, ethnicity (NPC)

4Look and measure

  • Otoscopy: wax, OE, perforation, air–fluid level, bubbles, blue drum · drum moving with breathing = patulous
  • Forks: Weber to blocked ear + Rinne negative = conductive · Weber away + Rinne positive = sensorineural
  • Endoscope nose and nasopharynx · TMJ, neck
  • PTA: conductive → canal/middle ear · low-tone SNHL → hydrops · flat/high SNHL → sudden loss

5Tympanograms

x-axis: ear canal pressure · respiratory wobble on the trace → patulous tube

6Other tests

  • ETDQ-7: pressure, pain, clogged, with colds, popping, tinnitus, muffled → selects for treatment
  • Tubomanometry (specialist) · MRI: asymmetric SNHL, nasopharynx · CT: dehiscence

7Canal and tube

  • Wax commonest: microsuction, drops, irrigation (not with perforation, surgery, infection)
  • Obstructive ETD: mucosal inflammation (rhinitis, CRS, reflux, smoke) · child: adenoids, cleft · about 1% of adults · around 40% of children before age ten
  • Autoinflation (Valsalva, nasal balloon) + saline, steroid spray · tubes for effusion, retraction
  • Balloon tuboplasty: cartilaginous tube, ~10 bar for two minutes · select by ETDQ-7 · RCT benefit (2018)

8Patulous tube

  • Autophony, own breathing · weight loss, pregnancy, hormones, RT, neuromuscular
  • Sniffers → retraction, cholesteatoma
  • Explain, hydrate, saline drops, lie down · augment/reconstruct if disabling
  • Decongestants make it worse

9Adult effusion, inner ear

  • Persistent one-sided adult effusion → scope nasopharynx, MRI/biopsy if abnormal (endemic areas) · grommet only once clear
  • Sudden SNHL: audiogram, oral or IT steroids at once, MRI · test before blaming wax (US 2019)
  • Ménière's: fluctuating fullness, roaring tinnitus, low-tone loss
  • SCD: apparent conductive loss, normal tympanometry, reflexes preserved · IIH: pulsatile, headache

10Pathway

Otoscopy: wax → remove, re-test · effusion/retraction → step 3 · normal → step 2 Forks + audiogram today: sudden SNHL → steroids, MRI · low-tone + vertigo → Ménière's · normal → step 4 Effusion: adult one-sided → nasopharynx · after cold/flight → autoinflate · persistent + loss → tube Normal hearing + drum: autophony lying better → patulous · popping with colds → ETDQ-7, balloon · clicking → TMJ · sound vertigo → CT

11Exam traps ✗

  • Sudden SNHL called "just wax" or "tube"
  • Grommet in adult one-sided effusion without nasopharynx
  • Decongestants for a patulous tube
