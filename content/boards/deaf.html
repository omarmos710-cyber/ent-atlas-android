THE DEAF CHILD

and congenital hearing loss: a child who does not speak has usually not heard ✎

1The critical years

  • Speech + language are built from birth to five on hearing · no input → auditory cortex taken over
  • Helped before six months → far better · early aids / implant → near-normal language in many
  • Prelingual (before speech) hurts language most · postlingual after
  • JCIH 2019 keeps 1-3-6; 1-2-3 in good programmes

2Causes

~half genetic (70% non-syndromic, mostly AR · 30% syndromic) · ~a quarter environmental · ~a quarter unexplained
  • GJB2 (connexin 26, DFNB1): up to half of recessive non-syndromic · panels find a cause in ~40%
  • Mitochondrial 12S rRNA 1555A>G: deaf after one aminoglycoside dose

4Inner ear malformations

  • Michel: no inner ear → no aid or implant helps
  • Mondini: one and a half turns, often EVA
  • Scheibe: cochleosaccular, commonest
  • Alexander: basal turn only → high tones lost
  • Bing-Siebenmann: membranous labyrinth absent
  • EVA (Dhingra >2 mm, Lee ≥1.5 mm): fluctuating, progressive loss after minor head injury → avoid contact sport; closing it causes loss

6Hearing first, then language

  • Counsel parents, early educators
  • Hearing aids early, both ears if usable hearing
  • Cochlear implant: severe–profound, little aid benefit · ideally around the first birthday (before 12–18 months better) · both ears at one sitting (NICE TA566: 80 dB HL at ≥2 frequencies) · urgent after meningitis
  • Auditory-oral, sign or total communication · radio aids · SLT · vocational guidance

7Exam traps ✗

  • Reassuring after a failed screen (repeat → diagnostic ABR)
  • Relying on OAE in a NICU baby
  • Missing late CMV loss after a normal screen
  • Forgetting the ECG
