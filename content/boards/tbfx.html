TEMPORAL BONE FRACTURE

and CSF otorrhoea: is the face moving, can they hear, is CSF leaking? ✎

1Who and why it matters

  • Holds cochlea, labyrinth, facial nerve, ossicles, carotid, dura → document face + hearing early
  • Temporal bone involved in 30–70% of head injuries; a true fracture in only 14–22% of these
  • Road traffic accidents > assaults, falls · young men (second to fourth decades), ~three times women
  • Longitudinal / transverse or, better, otic capsule sparing / violating · HRCT shows it, beta-2 transferrin confirms CSF
  • Trauma care first, the ear second

2Look, listen, test the face

  • Base of skull: Battle's sign (mastoid bruising after a day or two), periorbital bruising, ↓ consciousness
  • Ear: bleeding + torn drum / canal step (longitudinal) · blue-black haemotympanum behind intact drum (transverse)
  • Conductive: haemotympanum (commonest early) · perforation · ossicles
  • Sensorineural: through cochlea or labyrinthine concussion; often permanent · mixed common
  • Vertigo: severe + spontaneous nystagmus (transverse) · milder positional (concussion, BPPV)
  • CSF leak ~17%: clear fluid from the ear through a torn drum, or from the nose via the tube (intact drum)
IS joint separation→incus dislocation→stapes crura fracture
ossicular injury by frequency · IS joint ~80% · conductive loss ≥30 dB
Facial palsy in ~a quarter (half of transverse, 10–20% of longitudinal) · record immediate or delayed, complete or not · immediate complete → transection / bone spicule · ~three-quarters delayed (one to sixteen days) → oedema, good recovery

3Image the bone, confirm the fluid

  • HRCT temporal bones (with the head CT): fracture line, capsule, ossicles, facial canal, tegmen · CTA if it crosses the carotid canal (dissection)
  • Beta-2 transferrin (or beta-trace protein) confirms CSF
  • Hearing: tuning forks early · audiogram at 2–3 days (once awake) and at 6–8 weeks
  • Complete palsy: ENoG day 3 to ~14 + EMG · >90% degeneration supports exploration
  • Persistent / intermittent leak: CT or MRI cisternography for the site

Modern: the otic capsule

Sparing = by far the most · violating ~6% → far more facial palsy, SNHL, CSF leak, meningitis (Brodie & Thompson, 820 fractures) · predicts outcome better · Dhingraconductive = longitudinal, SNHL = transverse Leeseries don't fit the old scheme · know both for exams

trauma protocol, neuro→face before sedation→haemotympanum, step, CSF, nystagmus→HRCT (± CTA)
Don't syringe, probe or pack the ear deeply

7Early and late complications

  • Meningitis, early or years later (unhealed fracture)
  • Permanent SNHL · facial palsy
  • Late cholesteatoma (skin trapped in the line)
  • Chronic vertigo, perilymph fistula
  • Carotid injury, dissection
The capsule heals only by fibrous union → leaks recur with coughing / straining, meningitis years later, meningo- / encephalocele (conductive loss, effusion, meningitis)

8Viva & traps ✗

Viva: longitudinal vs transverse table · capsule sparing vs violating, and why it predicts outcome · immediate vs delayed palsy + Rx of each · beta-2 transferrin · ossicular injuries by frequency
  • No facial record before sedation

  • Calling a conductive loss permanent before the blood clears
