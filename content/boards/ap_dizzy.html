APPROACH TO DIZZINESS AND VERTIGO

the diagnosis comes from timing and triggers, not the word "dizzy" · history and the eyes · don't miss the cerebellar stroke ✎

1What kind?

  • Vertigo: illusion of movement → vestibular
  • Disequilibrium: unsteady feet → bilateral loss, neuropathy, cerebellum
  • Presyncope: about to faint → orthostatic, cardiac
  • Visual/motion sensitivity (supermarkets, screens) → migraine, PPPD, uncompensated loss

2Timing is the diagnosis

  • Seconds with straining/loud sound → SCD, fistula · many brief spells a day → paroxysmia · minutes with brainstem signs → TIA
  • Triggers: rolling in bed → BPPV · standing → orthostatic · straining → third window · sleep loss, stress, light → migraine

3Central red flags

  • Cannot stand or walk unaided
  • New occipital headache, neck pain (dissection)
  • Diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, numbness, weakness, ataxia
  • Direction-changing, vertical, torsional, fixation-resistant nystagmus
  • Normal head impulse in acute continuous vertigo
  • Vascular risk + vertigo + sudden hearing loss (AICA)

  • About one in ten are posterior circulation strokes · HINTS beats early MRI in first 48 hours (2009; beat ABCD2, 2013)
  • Peripheral only if all three agree · any central sign → MRI-DWI + stroke team
  • Neuritis: sedatives a few days only, fluids, early mobilising, rehab ± early steroids

6Positional tests

  • Dix–Hallpike: head 45° to test ear, lie back, ~20° below horizontal, watch 30 seconds
  • Positive: upbeat torsional, upper pole to lower ear · latency · under a minute · reverses · fatigues
  • Supine roll: horizontal canal → geotropic or apogeotropic
  • No latency, no fatigue, downbeat, no vertigo → central

7Rest of the exam

  • Otoscopy, forks, fistula test · cranial nerves, cerebellar, Romberg, tandem, walk
  • Orthostatic: fall ≥ 20 systolic or 10 diastolic within three minutes (some allow ten) · HR up 30 or more = PoTS

8The big three

  • BPPV: commonest · posterior canal 85–90% · Epley/Semont · horizontal: Lempert, Gufoni · self-treatment · no drugs · atypical → MRI · US 2017: no routine imaging or suppressants
  • Ménière's (Bárány): two or more spells 20 min–12 h + documented low–mid SNHL + fluctuating aural symptoms · audiogram, MRI if asymmetric · salt, caffeine, alcohol (weak evidence) · betahistine (trial: no benefit) · diuretics → IT steroid → IT gentamicin → surgery
  • Vestibular migraine: commonest spontaneous recurrent · five or more episodes, 5 min–72 h · migraine history · at least half with headache, photo + phonophobia or aura · triggers · prochlorperazine, triptan · prophylaxis: propranolol, amitriptyline, candesartan

Ménière's or migraine?

Progressive low-tone loss → Ménière's · headache, aura, motion sensitivity, spells > 12 h or only minutes → migraine · trial prophylaxis

9Less common, chronic

  • SCD: sound/strain, autophony, low BC · VEMP + CT · fistula · paroxysmia → carbamazepine · EA2 → acetazolamide
  • Chronic: uncompensated loss · bilateral (gentamicin): oscillopsia, dark · PPPD: most days > three months, upright, busy visuals → rehab, CBT, SSRI · elderly multisensory · central (rare)
  • Rehab for most · stop sedatives · medical: orthostatic, arrhythmia, aortic stenosis, glucose, anaemia, drugs, panic

10Tests and children

  • PTA for all with ear symptoms · MRI: asymmetry, central signs, atypical positional, stroke · VNG, calorics, vHIT, VEMP · CT for dehiscence · ECG, BP, glucose for presyncope
  • Children: vestibular migraine, BPV of childhood · OME, neuritis, trauma, EVA · exclude posterior fossa tumour

11Exam traps ✗

  • "Labyrinthitis" without examining the eyes
  • Long-term sedatives delay compensation
  • Vestibular migraine missed: no headache
  • Betahistine for BPPV instead of Epley
