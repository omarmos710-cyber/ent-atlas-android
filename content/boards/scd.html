SUPERIOR CANAL DEHISCENCE

a third window: check the reflexes before any stapes operation ✎

1What it is

  • Bone defect over the superior canal where it rises to the middle fossa floor → membranous canal covered only by dura
  • Probably developmental thin bone, last layer lost later (minor trauma, straining, brain pulsation)
  • Often bilateral on CT, usually symptomatic on one side
  • In short: gap with present reflexes · low VEMP thresholds · CT in the canal plane confirms · surgery only if disabling
  • Described by Minor, 1998

2The third window

schematic
  • Pressure (middle ear or cranial) now moves endolymph in the superior canal → vertigo
  • Sound diverted from the cochlea → apparent conductive loss
  • Bone-conducted sound heard louder

3Sound, pressure, own body

Vestibular

Tullio: vertigo / oscillopsia from loud sound
Hennebert: vertigo with canal pressure, Valsalva, coughing, straining
chronic imbalance · eye movements in the superior canal plane

Auditory

Autophony
hears footsteps, chewing, heartbeat, own eye movements
pulsatile + gaze-evoked tinnitus · fullness

4The clues

  • Bedside: tuning fork heard at the ankle · Valsalva + loud sound while watching the eyes (Tullio, Hennebert; nystagmus in the superior canal plane)
  • Audiogram (first line): low-frequency air-bone gap, bone may be better than 0 dB (supranormal)
  • Stapedial reflexes present: a middle ear conductive loss would abolish them
  • VEMPs: cervical threshold abnormally low, ocular amplitude high (raised at 4 kHz most specific) · a true conductive loss abolishes VEMPs
  • ECochG may show raised SP/AP
  • HRCT = gold standard, reformatted in the canal plane (Pöschl + Stenver views) · thin bone can look dehiscent → read with symptoms + VEMPs

5Mimics

ConditionTells it apart
Otosclerosisreflexes absent, VEMPs absent, no sound-induced vertigo
Patulous tubeautophony with breathing, drum moves with respiration, no gap
Perilymph fistulaafter surgery, trauma, straining; fluctuating hearing
Ménière'svertigo for hours, fluctuating low-frequency SNHL
Otosyphilispositive serology, Hennebert
Before stapes surgery: conductive loss + normal drum + present reflexes ≠ otosclerosis · stapes surgery here doesn't help, may harm

6Treat only if disabling

  • Reassure, explain, avoid triggers (noise, straining): many need nothing more
  • Surgery for disabling vertigo or autophony: plug the canal or resurface the defect · middle fossa or transmastoid
  • Plugging more reliable for vestibular symptoms, sacrifices the canal · round window reinforcement in some centres
  • Helps vestibular symptoms more than hearing · systematic review (2017) favoured transmastoid plugging for disabled patients (fewer complications, revisions, shorter stay)
Complications of repair: SNHL, vestibular loss, middle fossa craniotomy risks

7Exam traps ✗

  • Stapes surgery for a gap with present reflexes
  • Diagnosis from CT alone
