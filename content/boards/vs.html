VESTIBULAR SCHWANNOMA

and cerebellopontine angle tumours: the ear is the first to know ✎

1What and who

  • Benign Schwann cell tumour, usually of the vestibular division of VIII in the IAC · "acoustic neuroma" is wrong twice: rarely acoustic, never a neuroma
  • 8–10% of intracranial tumours · ~80% of CPA tumours · age 40–60 · mostly sporadic, one-sided
  • Commonest serious cause of asymmetric SNHL: every asymmetric loss is screened
  • Small → mostly watched; growing → irradiated or removed · large untreated → brainstem compression, hydrocephalus · bilateral = NF2

3A stepwise spread

VIII→V ~2.5 cm→VII sensory→IX, X→cerebellum, brainstem
  • VIII: progressive one-sided SNHL, discrimination far worse than thresholds · ~one in ten present as sudden loss · one-sided tinnitus · unsteadiness, not vertigo (slow growth = compensation)
  • V: reduced corneal reflex = often earliest sign · numbness only 7–9% at diagnosis
  • VII: sensory first: Hitselberger's sign (posterior meatal wall numb), taste, lacrimation · facial weakness late, ~5%
  • IX, X: dysphagia, hoarseness, ↓ gag
  • Ataxia, nystagmus → raised ICP: headache, vomiting, papilloedema
Large tumour + headache, vomiting, ataxia, papilloedema = hydrocephalus / brainstem compression → urgent neurosurgery

4MRI first

  • Audiometry (retrocochlear): asymmetric SNHL, poor discrimination, roll-over, absent / decaying reflex, abnormal tone decay
  • MRI + gadolinium = gold standard: enhancing mass on the IAC, ice-cream cone, finds tumours of a few mm
  • ABR if no MRI: ↑ interaural wave V / I–V (Dhingra >0.2 ms, Lee 0.4–0.5) · misses small tumours
  • Caloric reduced in most (normal if very small) · CT (with contrast) if no MRI / for bony anatomy
  • Screen asymmetry (e.g. ≥15 dB at two adjacent frequencies) · high-resolution T2 for screening · serial MRI for growth · before treatment: speech audiometry, caloric + head impulse · bilateral / young → NF2 genetic test
No lumbar puncture: CSF protein high, but herniation risk with a large tumour

Observation

MRI at six months, then yearly while stable, then less · treat if it grows · growth over four years: 17% intracanalicular, 29% extrameatal (Stangerup 2006), usually 1–2 mm a year · cost = hearing may fall anyway

Stereotactic radiosurgery

single session, 11–14 Gy margin (often 12–13) · up to ~2.5–3 cm · control 90–95% · first-year swelling ≠ failure · hearing not reliably kept · VII risk ~1% · malignancy ~1 in 1000 · salvage surgery → worse facial outcome · conventional RT: no role
Dhingra favours surgery; Lee: all three equal → in practice most small tumours are watched first

8Surgery details + evidence

  • Translab: no brain retraction, best VII view · HB I–II in most <~3.5 cm
  • Retrosigmoid: hearing possible if <~2 cm and not filling lateral third of canal · useful hearing kept 57% intracanalicular, 37% <2 cm, 12% >2 cm
  • No approach clearly best for facial function
  • Subtotal on the facial nerve keeps the face; remnant watched or irradiated
  • V-REX (JAMA 2023, 100 pts): upfront SRS shrank volume vs wait-and-scan (42% of waiters needed SRS) but 25 of 26 secondary outcomes (hearing, balance, QoL) no different
  • EANO 2020: observe small asymptomatic · SRS small–medium growing / symptomatic · surgery large with mass effect · CNS guidelines (Olson 2018): no approach better for the face

10Exam traps ✗

  • No MRI for an asymmetric loss
  • Sudden loss blamed on a virus without imaging
  • LP with a large tumour
  • Promising hearing after radiosurgery
