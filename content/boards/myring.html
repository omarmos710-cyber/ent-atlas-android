MYRINGITIS

disease of the drum, not the middle ear ✎

1Two forms

Inflammation confined to the tympanic membrane (± deep canal skin), without primary middle ear or mastoid disease
schematic · granulation often posterosuperior · no perforation

3Bullous myringitis

  • After a URTI · organisms of AOM: S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, Moraxella · Mycoplasma classic but unproven (Dhingra: possible; Lee: unproven)
  • Also called otitis externa haemorrhagica · treat as AOM
  • Sudden, intense pain for three or four days, then settles · a little bloody discharge when blebs burst
  • Haemorrhagic bullae on drum + deep canal · effusion too in a third to two-fifths
Always check hearing: sensorineural or mixed loss, often high-frequency, common; usually recovers
Rx: analgesia · lance blebs for pain · keep dry · topical drops vs secondary infection · treat AOM if present

Now seen as

a variant of AOM (same organisms in the fluid; Mycoplasma rare) → analgesia + antibiotic when indicated · SNHL in a minority (Drendel 2012) · steroids: small series only

4Granular myringitis

  • Staph, Pseudomonas, Proteus · predisposition: Dhingra wax, FB, OE; Lee none identified
  • Itch, fullness, foul painless discharge
  • Granulation over part of the drum, often posterosuperior; may perforate and heal repeatedly · hearing normal or slightly ↓
  • Diagnosis clinical, under the microscope · CT only to exclude COM / cholesteatoma
clean + vinegar + drops→cautery (silver nitrate, TCA) / laser→tympanoplasty + canalplasty
recurrence common after surgery · evidence from case series only
Late fibrosis blunts the anterior drum–canal angle → conductive loss
