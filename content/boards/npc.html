NASOPHARYNGEAL CARCINOMA

hides where no one looks · shows through its neighbours · treated with radiation, not the knife ✎

1Geography and virus

  • Highest: southern China, Hong Kong, Taiwan · SE Asia, N Africa, Mediterranean, Inuit, Aleut · rare in India except the North-East · Chinese born in North America: risk below China, above other Americans
  • Men ~three to one · peak fifth + sixth decades · ~one in five under thirty (undifferentiated) · HLA genes (risk persists after migration) · EBV in virtually every non-keratinising tumour cell (clonal in premalignant lesions: early) · salted fish (nitrosamines), low vitamin C, incense + wood smoke, opium, fumes · tobacco + alcohol → keratinising

3Spread from Rosenmüller

  • Nodes early, often both sides: Rouvière (retropharyngeal: stiff neck, lower nerves), high level II under the mastoid, level V
  • Highest distant spread of any head + neck cancer: bone, lung, liver · ~4% at diagnosis · later: ~a quarter after IMRT (older, pre-IMRT series: a quarter to a third)

5See it, sample it, map it

  • Nasendoscopy first → biopsy + EBER = gold standard · nothing seen → blind / MRI-guided biopsies of Rosenmüller + posterior wall under GA (sometimes transpalatal)
  • Node first: US-guided FNA or core + EBER · avoid open biopsy of a high level II node
  • MRI: soft tissue, spaces, perineural, skull base marrow, intracranial, nodes · CT: skull base erosion · PET-CT: distant, residual (chest + abdomen imaging if no PET)
  • Plasma EBV DNA: screening, prognosis, response, relapse (20,174 men: sensitivity 97.1%, specificity 98.6%) · EBV IgA (early antigen specific, VCA sensitive)
  • Before treatment: audiogram, dental, renal + thyroid function

8Radiation first

  • Trials used 8th-edition groups (v9 in brackets) · T1 N0 (8th I; v9 IA): IMRT alone ~70 Gy in 33–35 fractions, nasopharynx + both necks
  • 8th II–IVA (v9 IA T2 N0, IB, II, III): IMRT 70 Gy + concurrent cisplatin 100 mg/m² 3-weekly (or 40 mg/m² weekly) · cumulative ≥ 200 mg/m² matters (160 after induction)
  • Induction gemcitabine 1 g/m² days 1 + 8 + cisplatin 80 mg/m² day 1, 3-weekly × 3, 8th III–IVA (v9 II–III) (NEJM 2019, 480 patients: RFS 85.3% vs 76.5%) · adjuvant metronomic capecitabine × a year, high risk (2021)
  • Recurrent / metastatic (v9 IVA–IVB): gemcitabine + cisplatin + PD-1 inhibitor (toripalimab, camrelizumab) · no cisplatin: carboplatin / nedaplatin
  • IMRT spares brainstem, cord, optic, temporal lobes, cochleae, parotids · protons: unproven benefit

9Residual, recurrent

  • Neck: comprehensive neck dissection (older texts: radical)
  • Nasopharynx: restage → endoscopic nasopharyngectomy: better survival, fewer late effects than IMRT re-irradiation (2021) · open: maxillary swing, transpalatal, Le Fort I, midfacial degloving, infratemporal · not if around ICA, intracranial
  • Re-RT: IMRT, protons, brachytherapy (implants, gold grains) · skull base + temporal lobe necrosis, nerve palsy, carotid rupture

10Late effects · outlook

  • Xerostomia, caries, glue ear (grommets discharge), SNHL, trismus, hypothyroid, pituitary failure, ORN, temporal lobe necrosis, neuropathy, carotid stenosis, second cancers
  • Control ~70–80%, five-year survival ~70–80% · failure now mostly distant
  • Follow-up 3–4 monthly × two years, 6-monthly to five, imaging yearly × five
