MASTOIDITIS

the whole topic on one board ✎

1When OM escapes the middle ear

  • Every AOM inflames the cells (antrum continuous with the middle ear) → an opaque mastoid on CT ≠ mastoiditis
  • Acute mastoiditis = clinically evident infection of the mastoid process
  • Coalescent = bony septa destroyed → one cavity full of pus
  • Half are ≤4 years; only ~30% had recurrent OM: often the first severe ear infection
  • Gateway to neck/facial abscess, facial palsy, labyrinthitis, intracranial infection: up to one in ten intracranial, even on IV antibiotics

2Pathology

S. pneumoniae leads (Lee) · S. pyogenes, S. aureus, H. influenzae · serotype 19A: resistant + virulent · cultures often negative after antibiotics · Dhingra: β-haemolytic strep

3The child, the ear, the bone

Symptoms

post-auricular pain after an improving OM · fever persisting or returning · discharge profuse, creamy or >3 weeks (mucoid = middle ear: canal has no mucous glands)
→ pain, fever or discharge that continues or comes back = mastoiditis
  • Mastoid: tender over tip + posterior border (MacEwen's triangle is tender in plain OM too) · compare sides · later red, boggy, fluctuant
  • Pinna: pushed forwards + downwards, sulcus lost
  • Sagging posterosuperior canal wall (periostitis over antrum)
  • Reservoir sign: canal refills with pus straight after mopping
  • Pars tensa perforation, pulsatile discharge (light-house reflex) · conductive loss
Masked mastoiditis: repeated / inadequate antibiotics sterilise the middle ear while bone destruction goes on · little pain, no discharge (Dhingra) or persistent otalgia (Lee) · dull thick drum, lost landmarks, ↓ hearing · may hide extradural / perisinus abscess → ear never settles = CT

5The abscesses

  • Subperiosteal: through the cribriform area, displaces the pinna
  • Luc (meatal): antrum → between posterior canal wall and periosteum
  • Bezold: pneumatised tip (adult / older child) bursts medially → tender upper neck mass
  • Citelli: tip → posterior belly of digastric → behind mastoid / digastric triangle
  • Deep neck: parapharyngeal, retropharyngeal via tip or petrous cells

Bezold

DDx: jugular nodes, parotid mass, infected branchial cyst, parapharyngeal abscess, IJV thrombosis
Rx: cortical mastoidectomy + find tip fistula + drain neck by separate incision

6Diagnosis

  • Mainly clinical · FBC (↑WCC), markers, blood cultures, pus culture
  • Contrast CT temporal bone: coalescence, cortical erosion, abscess, intracranial spread
  • MRI + MRV: intracranial complication or sinus thrombosis

7Admit, drain, decide

admit + cultures→IV 3rd-gen cephalosporin→VII, balance, neck, neuro→contrast CT (MRI/MRV)→wide myringotomy + grommet
+ anti-pseudomonal cover in chronic ears · senior if abscess, coalescence or no response at 24–48 h
IV → by culture → oral step-down: 3–6 weeks total · uncomplicated, non-coalescent often settles with antibiotics + myringotomy

Cortical mastoidectomy if…

1subperiosteal abscess 2coalescence on CT 3sagging wall / reservoir sign 4no better after 48 h 5any complication or cholesteatoma
Schwartze: clears cells, opens antrum to middle ear; canal wall + ossicles intact
Small child: small subperiosteal abscess → aspirate / incise + myringotomy + antibiotics; mastoidectomy if fails or recurs · infant mastoid small, vascular, poor landmarks, facial nerve superficial

8Evidence

Loh 2018 (21 studies, 564 children): cure 96% medical, 96% conservative surgery, 89% mastoidectomy → conservative first if uncomplicated · Bartov 2019: aspiration avoided mastoidectomy in most · withholding antibiotics in uncomplicated OM raises incidence only slightly

9Exam traps ✗

  • Opaque mastoid on CT without signs called mastoiditis
  • Relying on suprameatal tenderness alone
  • Missing masked mastoiditis when otalgia never settled
  • Forgetting the superficial facial nerve behind an infant's ear
