HEREDITARY HAEMORRHAGIC TELANGIECTASIA

nosebleeds that never stop coming back · screen the lungs and the brain ✎

1A disease of vessels

  • Osler-Weber-Rendu · autosomal dominant disorder of angiogenesis · telangiectasias in skin + mucosa, AVMs in organs
  • About 1 in 5,000 to 10,000 · mainly white populations · men = women · penetrance rises with age
  • TGF-β signalling fails in endothelium → no supporting matrix around new vessels · three genes explain ~90%
  • The nose is the visible part of a systemic disorder → every patient screened for silent lung + brain AVMs

2The lesion: no capillary bed

3Nose, skin and organs

  • Epistaxis: commonest + earliest · spontaneous, recurrent, both sides, from childhood, worse with age · 80–90% by age 21
  • Telangiectasias: red spots that blanch · lips, tongue, palate, fingertips, face, nasal mucosa · 80–90% by their thirties or forties
  • Iron-deficiency anaemia from nose + gut

7Spare the mucosa

  • Goal: fewer, smaller bleeds + a better Hb, not cure · treat nose + Hb together
  • Step up by bleed frequency, Hb, transfusions · track with the Epistaxis Severity Score
  • Acute bleed: almost always the anterior third → posterior packs rarely needed · gentle pressure, resorbable pack (Nasopore, Surgicel) or haemostatic matrix · no conventional packs or expanding sponges · antibiotic cover · fails → embolise facial + maxillary feeders, no coils
  • Visceral: significant lung AVMs → embolisation
Repeated packing + wide cautery destroy healthy mucosa, breed new lesions, perforate the septum

9Procedures: step up by severity

EUA + KTP / argon laser→septodermoplasty or systemic drugs→nasal closure
  • Laser: green light absorbed by Hb · circle each lesion, then ablate · TXA + antibiotic at induction, no pack · repeat every few months · bipolar better in a bloody field
  • Laser controls it < about three months → step up
  • Septodermoplasty: skin graft replaces anterior septal mucosa · halves later laser sessions
  • Young's procedure (Lund: three vestibular flaps): nostrils closed → bleeding stops, often for good · costs nasal breathing + smell · for the transfusion-dependent · rebleeds after reopening
  • Embolisation: temporary (collaterals) → emergencies only
how long each step controls bleeding

10Evidence

  • NOSE (JAMA 2016): topical bevacizumab, oestriol, TXA sprays = placebo
  • PATH-HHT (NEJM 2024, 144 patients): oral pomalidomide cut epistaxis scores over 24 weeks
  • Guidelines 2020: moisture → oral TXA → ablation → graft / closure · IV bevacizumab if severe · screen lungs + brain

11Complications, traps ✗

  • Disease: anaemia, transfusion dependence · lung AVM → paradoxical embolism, stroke, brain abscess · brain AVM → haemorrhage
  • Bad treatment: septal perforation, new telangiectasias
  • Traps: simple epistaxis assumed, no look at lips + fingers · ribbon gauze, repeated packs, wide cautery · forgetting to screen the lungs · topical bevacizumab spray
