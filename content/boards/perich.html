2Why the lobule is spared

Cartilage has no blood supply of its own: it lives on the perichondrium · pus or blood separates them → cartilage starves, necroses
Lobule has no cartilage → a cartilage disease spares it

3On call: a red, swollen ear

lobule spared? = cartilage→fluctuant: drain under anaesthesia + bolster→anti-pseudomonal
Vesicles + facial weakness → steroids, antiviral, eye protection · eye symptoms → check cornea

4Perichondritis

  • Causes: infected haematoma, laceration, burn, high (cartilage) piercing, ear surgery, spread from OE
  • Organism: Pseudomonas ± staph · its link with high piercing is why oral ciprofloxacin is first line
  • Early: red, hot, tender, thickened pinna, sparing the lobule · late: fluctuant pus between perichondrium and cartilage → necrosis
  • Tests: clinical · swab / aspirate culture · ultrasound if fluctuance unsure · recurrent / bilateral / nose, eye → inflammatory markers + rheumatology · recurrent → glucose
abscess: incise along a fold→remove dead cartilage, irrigate, send pus→bolster for days
StepDrug and dose
first line, adultsciprofloxacin 750 mg by mouth 12-hourly (500 mg if milder) · 7–10 days; 2–4 weeks after abscess drainage · MHRA quinolone warnings
childrenciprofloxacin 10–15 mg/kg (max 500 mg) 12-hourly
severe / failingadmit · IV ciprofloxacin 400 mg 8–12-hourly or piperacillin–tazobactam 4.5 g 8-hourly
every patientanalgesia, remove the piercing · cool aluminium acetate compresses
Complication: lost cartilage → shrivelled cauliflower ear · flucloxacillin alone won't cover Pseudomonas

5Skin infections

  • Cellulitis: staph / strep after scratch, piercing, OE · may involve the lobule
  • Erysipelas: strep, sharp raised red edge, can spread to the face → penicillin
  • Flucloxacillin 500 mg–1 g four times a day × 5–7 days · children four times a day: 1 month–1 year 62.5–125 mg, 2–9 years 125–250 mg, 10–17 years 250–500 mg
  • Allergy: clarithromycin 500 mg twice a day (erythromycin in pregnancy) · severe: flucloxacillin 1–2 g IV 6-hourly, review at 48 h · lobule spared + cartilage → treat as perichondritis

6Ramsay Hunt syndrome

Herpes zoster oticus: VZV reactivates in the geniculate ganglion
severe burning pain→vesicles: concha, canal, ± palate, tongue→facial palsy
VIII often: tinnitus, SNHL, vertigo
  • Antiviral × 7 days: aciclovir 800 mg five times a day / valaciclovir 1 g / famciclovir 500 mg three times a day · immunocompromised: until 48 h after crusting
  • Prednisolone as for Bell's (50–60 mg daily, 10 days in total) · text: high-dose, ~1 mg/kg × 14 days
  • Analgesia · eye protection (drops, night ointment + tape) · neuralgia: amitriptyline 10 mg at night or gabapentin
  • No decompression: damage is diffuse
  • Worse than Bell's: only ~60% regain normal function
  • Cochrane: no randomised evidence antivirals help; steroids insufficient → combined treatment rests on observational data

7Not infections

  • Relapsing polychondritis: autoimmune, recurrent painful red pinna sparing lobule, often both ears · saddle nose, larynx, trachea → airway threat (flexible laryngoscopy, CT trachea, lung function; may need tracheostomy / stents) · flare: prednisolone 0.5–1 mg/kg a day, tapered · methotrexate steroid-sparing · rheumatology
  • Chondrodermatitis nodularis helicis: painful nodule on the helix, older men sleeping on that side · relieve pressure; excise + histology (skin cancer)
  • DDx: gout (tophi on helix), lymphoma, skin tumours
