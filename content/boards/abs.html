ACUTE RHINOSINUSITIS

a cold that reached the sinuses · a swollen eye in a child with a cold is an emergency ✎

1A cold that reaches the sinuses

  • Nose + sinuses, < 12 weeks, full resolution · "rhinosinusitis": the nose is always involved
  • Mostly viral, settles without antibiotics · only 0.5–2% turn bacterial · coloured mucus alone ≠ bacteria · diagnosis is clinical, CT kept for complications
  • Viruses: rhinovirus, influenza, parainfluenza · droplets · 1–4 day incubation
  • Bacteria: S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis, S. aureus · rising penicillin + macrolide resistance · anaerobes with teeth
  • S. anginosus (milleri): complicated disease in older children + adolescents → intracranial abscess
  • Predisposed: swimming, diving, teeth, trauma, septum, concha bullosa, polyps, allergy, adenoids, immune deficiency, CF

2Why the eye and the brain

coronal schematic · red = the paper-thin wall infection crosses
  • Frontal, anterior ethmoid, maxillary → middle meatus (OMC) · posterior ethmoid → superior meatus · sphenoid → sphenoethmoidal recess · cilia sweep to the natural ostium
  • At birth: maxillary + ethmoid · frontal ~ sixth year · sphenoid later → ethmoiditis = infants, frontal = adults
  • Ethmoid ↔ orbit: only the paper-thin lamina papyracea · frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid border the anterior cranial fossa → complications go to the eye + brain

3Virus first, then bacteria

virus: congestion, sneezing, low fever settle in 7–10 days→cilia damaged, ostia swell shut→secretions stagnate→bacteria
Symptoms: block, discharge (front or back), facial pain / pressure, ↓ smell · cough, fever, fatigue, dental pain, halitosis, headache, ear fullness

Bacterial = 3 or more of

discoloured discharge (one side) · severe local pain (one side) · fever > 38 °C · ↑ ESR / CRP · double sickening · or severe from the start

7Most need no antibiotic

  • Viral: saline, analgesia (non-aspirin), short decongestant, fluids · post-viral > 10 days → nasal steroid
  • Bacterial: watchful waiting + safety net if mild · saline, steroid spray · topical decongestant a few days · antihistamine only with allergy
  • Early antibiotics don't prevent complications · given to > 90% in some UK series

First-line antibiotic

Textbooks + US: amoxicillin ± clavulanate × five to ten days · NICE NG79: phenoxymethylpenicillin × five days · back-up prescription beyond ten days · immediate only if very unwell / high risk
Cochrane, 15 trials: one extra cure in 19 at 7–14 days · side effects in one in eight · steroid spray: modest benefit, more with higher dose

9Surgery for acute disease

  • Frontal trephine if worse despite 48 hours: incision below the medial eyebrow, culture, irrigate until methylene blue reaches the nose · or endoscopic
  • Maxillary: washout rarely · endoscopic middle meatal antrostomy
  • Ethmoid: drain orbital abscess + ethmoids, endoscopic or external

10Local complications

  • Mucocele: blocked ostium, expands, erodes bone · infected = pyocele · frontal > ethmoid > maxillary > sphenoid
  • Frontal: swelling above medial canthus (90%), eye forward, down, out, egg-shell crackle · ethmoid: medial orbital wall · sphenoid: SOF or apex syndrome
  • Endoscopic marsupialisation has replaced external surgery
  • Retention cyst: incidental, no bone erosion, leave it
  • Osteomyelitis: maxilla in infants (tooth buds) · frontal in adults → Pott's puffy tumour + extradural abscess beneath
