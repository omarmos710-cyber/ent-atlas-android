1The order never changes

A (+ C-spine), B, C: midface can drop back→stop bleeding: packs, reduce a mobile maxilla→eyes now: acuity, pupils, movement, proptosis→CSF rhinorrhoea / otorrhoea→septum: haematoma?→CT + 3D (beyond a simple nasal fracture)
Retrobulbar haemorrhage → lateral canthotomy within minutes
  • Thirds: upper (frontal) · middle (nose, NOE, zygoma, orbit floor, maxilla) · lower (mandible)
  • Close lacerations early, save tissue · cheek wounds: check parotid duct + facial nerve, repair primarily

2Nasal fracture

  • Commonest facial fracture · frontal blow: depressed lower bones → open-book, flat wide dorsum · lateral blow: one bone in, or both + septum → bent bridge
  • Swelling, bruising, crepitus, deformity, epistaxis, block · clinical diagnosis, no X-ray

Local = general anaesthesia in trials · a third or more need later septorhinoplasty if the septum is displaced
A missed septal haematoma today = abscess + saddle nose tomorrow · drain it

3Naso-orbito-ethmoid

  • Blow at the nasion drives nose, ethmoids, medial walls back · cribriform, frontal outflow, lacrimal system, canthal tendon, eye at risk
  • Telecanthus · flat bridge, upturned pug tip · orbital haematoma, CSF leak, epiphora
  • CT · ORIF with miniplates · medial canthal tendon + lacrimal repair come before the nasal contour

4Zygoma

Tripod (second commonest): apart at frontal, temporal, maxillary attachments, rotated in + back
flat cheekinfraorbital stepnumb cheek, nose, liptrismus (coronoid)slanted fissurediplopialid emphysema
  • CT (old: Waters' view) · treat only if displaced: Gillies (temporal) or intraoral elevation · ORIF at FZ suture, infraorbital rim, ZM buttress
  • Arch: breaks in three, dimple, trismus · Gillies elevator deep to temporalis fascia, usually stable

5Orbital floor blow-out

  • Object bigger than the orbit (fist, ball) → floor gives, rim intact · fat + inferior rectus into the antrum
  • Diplopia on upgaze · enophthalmos once swelling settles · numb cheek
  • Coronal CT · forced duction test: entrapment vs nerve / muscle · eye review
Child, "white-eyed", vomiting, bradycardia = trapdoor with trapped muscle → release within 24–48 hours
Adults repair (within about two weeks): persistent diplopia from entrapment, enophthalmos > 2 mm, floor defect over half · lower lid / transconjunctival or endoscopic · mesh, bone, plate

6Mandible & frontal sinus

  • Mandible: condyle > angle > body > symphysis, often two places · malocclusion, trismus, step, numb lip, sublingual haematoma · OPG + CT · plates or MMF
  • Miniplates on Champy lines allow early function · antibiotics beyond 24 hours after surgery: no gain

9Exam traps ✗

  • Missing a septal haematoma
  • Reducing a nasal fracture after two weeks
  • Missing a trapdoor fracture in a vomiting child with a quiet eye
  • Forgetting the C-spine and airway in a midface fracture
