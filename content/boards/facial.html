FACIAL NERVE PALSY

forehead? ear? parotid? protect the eye today ✎

1Upper or lower?

  • Forehead nucleus gets fibres from both hemispheres; lower face only from the opposite side
  • UMN (supranuclear) lesion spares the forehead · emotional movement travels separately → a smile may survive a stroke
  • Eye at risk within hours · palsy may be the first sign of tumour, cholesteatoma, fracture

4Bell's and Ramsay Hunt

Bell's palsy

acute idiopathic LMN palsy = 60–75% · likely HSV reactivation; swelling in the labyrinthine segment · ↑ in diabetes, pregnancy · peaks within 72 h, often after pain behind the ear · full recovery 85–90% (95% if never complete) · a diagnosis of exclusion

Ramsay Hunt

zoster: vesicles (concha, canal, palate), severe pain, palsy ± SNHL, vertigo · worse outlook than Bell's
OM inflames a dehiscent nerve · cholesteatoma erodes (tympanic segment) → urgent otology

  • ENoG: ≥90% degeneration in the first three weeks = poor · meaningful ~day 3 to 14–21 of a complete palsy
  • EMG: fibrillation at 14–21 days = denervation · polyphasic 6–12 weeks before visible recovery · silence = end plates lost
  • MRI whole course + CT temporal bone: atypical, slow, recurrent, bilateral, or no recovery by three to four months

8Eye first, then steroids

Eye: drops by day, ointment + taping at night, sunglasses / moisture chamber · poor closure → gold / platinum lid weight, lateral tarsal strip, tarsorrhaphy

Bell's: steroids within 72 h

prednisolone 50 mg daily × ten days, or 60 mg × five days + five-day taper · full recovery at three months 83% vs 64% · age 16 and over · antiviral alone useless; with steroids = option (severe / complete) · caution: diabetes, hypertension, ulcer, TB, glaucoma, pregnancy
Don't routinely test, image, or do electrodiagnosis in incomplete palsy · image if atypical

9Other causes, surgery, repair

  • Ramsay Hunt: high-dose steroid + aciclovir / valaciclovir + analgesia
  • AOM: antibiotics + myringotomy · chronic: urgent exploration; leave granulation that has invaded the nerve
  • Trauma: immediate complete → explore / decompress / repair · delayed → conservative, steroids, serial ENoG
  • After ear surgery: remove packing first · complete + nerve not seen → explore
  • Decompression for Bell's: controversial; must include labyrinthine segment within two weeks

Repair & reanimation

tension-free anastomosis or cable graft (great auricular, sural, medial antebrachial) · transfer within 18 months: masseteric (most used), hypoglossal, cross-face · gracilis: masseteric (one stage, ~92%, not spontaneous) or cross-face (two stages, ~80%, spontaneous) · temporalis · static: brow lift, sling, lid

10Incomplete recovery

exposure keratitis · synkinesis (physio, botox) · contracture · crocodile tears (Bogorad) · distress · hemifacial spasm: vascular loop → botox or microvascular decompression

11Exam traps ✗

  • Bell's without looking in the ear or feeling the parotid
  • Forgetting eye care
  • Missing a slow or recurrent palsy that needs imaging
  • Not recording immediate vs delayed after trauma
