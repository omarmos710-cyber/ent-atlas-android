@stand A ranula is not a true cyst but a pool of saliva leaking from the sublingual gland into the tissues: a bluish "frog's belly" swelling in the floor of the mouth, or a soft neck swelling when it "plunges" through or behind the mylohyoid. Because the sublingual gland keeps secreting, draining or excising the pseudocyst alone lets it refill. Removing the sublingual gland through the mouth cures almost all, whether the ranula is oral or plunging; the skill is protecting the submandibular duct and lingual nerve that run right beside it.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Surgery for ranula
 | Summary
Indications | Any symptomatic ranula; urgently if it compromises the airway; recurrence; infection; speech or swallowing interference; obstructed Wharton's duct; cosmetic concern
Types | Oral (simple) ranula in the floor of the mouth; plunging ranula in the submandibular or parapharyngeal space, with or without an oral component
Imaging | Ultrasound, CT or MRI for plunging ranulas (the "tail sign" pointing to the sublingual space)
Options | Intraoral sublingual gland excision with drainage of the pseudocyst (most curative); marsupialisation or micromarsupialisation (children, small ranulas; higher recurrence); sclerotherapy (OK-432); transcervical excision rarely needed
Anaesthesia | General, nasotracheal tube helps
Antibiotics | Cover oral flora (for example clindamycin)
Tray | Head and neck set, intraoral retractors, mouth prop, bipolar, lacrimal probes or salivary dilators and stent for Wharton's duct, sialendoscopes if needed
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Recurrence,** mainly after marsupialisation or if gland is left behind.
- **Numbness or altered taste** of the tongue tip (lingual nerve), usually temporary.
- **Submandibular duct injury** causing swelling of the submandibular gland when eating; rarely gland removal later.
- **Bleeding, infection,** floor of mouth swelling.
!!
## Before you start
Check the airway with a large or plunging ranula. Image a neck swelling to confirm continuity with the sublingual space and to exclude other cystic neck masses (lymphatic malformation, dermoid, branchial cyst).

# The operation, stage by stage | Out with the sublingual gland.
## Protect the duct
Open the mouth with a prop and retract the tongue. Cannulate Wharton's duct at its papilla with a probe or stent so it can be felt and seen throughout.
!see What you should see
- The duct marked by its stent, running backwards under the floor of mouth mucosa.
!!
## Incise the floor of the mouth
Incise the mucosa over the sublingual gland lateral to the duct, from near the papilla backwards along the plica sublingualis, staying lateral to the duct.
!mf 3 | The incision outlined on the floor of the mouth.
!see What you should see
- The lobulated sublingual gland under thin mucosa, with the pseudocyst wall beneath or beside it.
!!
## Dissect the gland
Free the gland from the inner mandible laterally, then medially off the duct and lingual nerve, working from front to back. The lingual nerve runs under the gland and crosses below the duct from lateral to medial; identify it and keep it. Drain the ranula fluid and remove the pseudocyst wall where it comes easily; it has no epithelium and does not need complete excision.
!see What you should see
- The whole sublingual gland removed, the duct and lingual nerve intact on the floor of the wound.
!!
!danger The lingual nerve and Wharton's duct
Both lie directly deep and medial to the sublingual gland in the back of the floor of the mouth. Keep the stent in the duct and find the nerve before dividing anything that crosses the back of the gland.
!!
## Plunging ranula
The same intraoral excision of the sublingual gland usually cures a plunging ranula: drain the neck collection from inside the mouth and the pseudocyst collapses. A cervical approach is reserved for large or recurrent collections not managed intraorally.
!see What you should see
- The neck swelling collapsing once the gland is out and the fluid drained.
!!
## Close
If the duct was cut, marsupialise or reimplant it into the floor of mouth mucosa (sialodochoplasty) over a stent; otherwise close the mucosa loosely.
!mf 4 | Marsupialising an interrupted submandibular duct.
!see What you should see
- A loosely closed floor of mouth, the duct draining.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
Treat the source, not the pool. Excising the pseudocyst alone, or deroofing it without removing the gland, gives the highest recurrence rates.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Floor of mouth swelling and airway compromise after surgery
Act | sit up, assess airway ; evacuate haematoma ; secure the airway if needed
!!
!path Submandibular swelling with meals after surgery
Act | duct stenosis or injury: dilate, stent, sialendoscopy ; gland excision if it persists
!!

# Aftercare | Soft food, mouthwash.
## After surgery
Soft diet, chlorhexidine mouthwash, analgesia, antibiotics for oral flora; remove a duct stent at about 2 weeks. Review for recurrence.
## Complications
Recurrence, lingual nerve injury, duct stenosis or injury with obstructive sialadenitis, bleeding, haematoma, infection.
!update Current evidence
A proportion meta-analysis of 39 series found intraoral sublingual gland excision the most curative treatment for oral ranula; micromarsupialisation gave comparable but less consistent cure rates; for plunging ranula intraoral and transcervical approaches had similar cure rates, both higher than OK-432 injection, with more morbidity after the transcervical route (Chung et al., *Br J Oral Maxillofac Surg* 2019). A systematic review of paediatric ranulas found micromarsupialisation and marsupialisation useful first options in children, with gland excision for recurrence (Packiri et al., *J Clin Diagn Res* 2017).
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Myers: excision of the sublingual gland through the mouth with drainage of the pseudocyst, protecting the duct (stented) and lingual nerve.
Dhingra and K. J. Lee: the ranula as an extravasation pseudocyst of the sublingual gland, the plunging type, and the older marsupialisation.
The literature: intraoral gland excision gives the highest cure rates for both oral and plunging ranulas.
!!
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Pathology**: extravasation pseudocyst, not a true cyst.
- **Plunging ranula** and the differential of a cystic neck mass.
- **Treatment options** and why gland excision works.
- **Anatomy** of the sublingual space: duct and lingual nerve.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Excising the pseudocyst and leaving the gland.
- A neck approach for a plunging ranula that the mouth could cure.
- Cutting Wharton's duct without recognising it.
- Missing the lingual nerve behind the gland.
!!
