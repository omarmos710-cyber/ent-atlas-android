@stand A pharyngeal (Zenker) pouch herniates backwards through Killian's dehiscence, the weak triangle between the thyropharyngeus and the cricopharyngeus, because the cricopharyngeus does not relax properly. The treatment is therefore always the same idea: divide the cricopharyngeus. Endoscopically, a stapler or laser divides the "party wall" between pouch and oesophagus, cutting the muscle within it; through the neck, an open myotomy is combined with excision, inversion or suspension of the sac. A cricopharyngeal myotomy alone treats the tight sphincter without a pouch.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Pharyngeal pouch and cricopharyngeal dysfunction
 | Summary
Indications | Symptomatic pouch (dysphagia, regurgitation of undigested food, aspiration, weight loss); cricopharyngeal dysfunction with moderate to severe dysphagia and poor sphincter opening on barium or videofluoroscopy, with good pharyngeal propulsion and laryngeal elevation
Contraindications to the endoscopic route | Poor exposure (limited neck extension or mouth opening, retrognathia); a very small pouch (under about 2 to 3 cm, too short a wall to staple); suspicion of carcinoma in the sac
Options | Endoscopic stapled or laser diverticulotomy; flexible endoscopic septotomy or peroral endoscopic myotomy (gastroenterology); open myotomy with diverticulectomy, inversion or pexy
Work-up | Barium swallow (size, position, residue); endoscopy of the pouch lining if there is bleeding or a change in symptoms (carcinoma is rare but reported)
Anaesthesia | General
Tray | Weerda diverticuloscope, endoscopes, endoscopic stapler or CO2 laser; open neck set, nasogastric tube, bougie
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Recurrence** of symptoms, more often after endoscopic stapling, especially with small pouches.
- **Leak or perforation:** mediastinitis (rare but serious), neck infection, a salivary fistula after open surgery.
- **Recurrent laryngeal nerve** injury and hoarseness (open surgery).
- **Dental** injury from the scope.
- Conversion to an open operation if the pouch cannot be exposed.
!!
## Before you start
Review the barium swallow for pouch size and depth. Check neck extension, teeth and mouth opening. Keep the patient on clear fluids beforehand to empty the pouch.

# The operation, stage by stage | Endoscopic stapling, then the open route.
## Expose the party wall
Insert the bivalved diverticuloscope with one blade in the oesophagus and the other in the pouch, so the bar (the common wall containing the cricopharyngeus) lies between them. Suck out food debris and inspect the pouch lining.
!mf 8 | The bar dividing the oesophagus and the pouch.
!see What you should see
- A clear horizontal bar, oesophageal lumen in front, pouch behind, both visible.
!!
## Divide the bar
Place the stapler with its anvil in the pouch and cartridge in the oesophagus, centred on the bar, and fire to divide and seal it down to the floor of the pouch; a second firing may be needed. Laser division is an alternative.
!mf 9 10 | The stapler on the bar, and the divided party wall.
!see What you should see
- The bar divided to near the pouch base, edges sealed by staples, with a wide common lumen.
!!
!danger The tip of the pouch
The last few millimetres beyond the stapler cartridge cannot be divided safely; pushing further risks a perforation into the mediastinum. Accept a small residual lip.
!!
## Open approach: expose the retropharyngeal space
Pass a nasogastric tube. Through a transverse incision at the cricoid level (usually left), raise flaps, separate sternomastoid and the carotid sheath from the larynx and thyroid, divide the middle thyroid vein and omohyoid if needed, and rotate the larynx medially with a hook on the back of the thyroid lamina (not near the cricothyroid joint) to enter the retropharyngeal space.
!mf 4 | The carotid sheath retracted laterally to expose the prevertebral fascia.
!see What you should see
- The pouch and the cricopharyngeus behind the larynx, with the recurrent nerve safe in the tracheo-oesophageal groove.
!!
## Myotomy
Grasp the pharynx above and below the cricopharyngeus over the nasogastric tube, and divide all the muscle fibres between mucosa and muscle from the inferior constrictor (oblique fibres) through the cricopharyngeus (horizontal fibres) and 1 to 2 cm onto the oesophagus.
!mf 5 | Muscle fibres separated from the mucosa for the myotomy.
!see What you should see
- Mucosa bulging through a complete longitudinal myotomy, with no intact horizontal fibres.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
The myotomy is the operation; the sac is secondary. A pouch removed without dividing the cricopharyngeus recurs or leaks.
!!
## Deal with the sac
Small pouches need only the myotomy. For larger ones, excise the sac at its neck with a stapler or by excising and suturing the mucosa (without traction, to avoid narrowing), or invert or suspend it (diverticulopexy) to avoid opening the pharynx. Do not close muscle over the mucosa. Drain and close.
!mf 6 7 | The diverticulum dissected free and excised.
!see What you should see
- A closed mucosal suture line without narrowing, and a complete myotomy.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Fever, chest pain or surgical emphysema after endoscopic stapling
Act | nil by mouth, intravenous antibiotics, contrast swallow and CT
Leak or mediastinitis | surgical drainage ; nutrition by nasogastric tube or gastrostomy
!!
!path Salivary leak after open surgery
Act | nil by mouth, nasogastric feeding ; open the wound to drain ; most close in days
!!

# Aftercare | Slow return to food.
## After surgery
Endoscopic: fluids the same day or the next, then soft diet. Open: nasogastric tube or nil by mouth for a few days, contrast swallow before feeding if the pharynx was opened. Watch temperature, pain and neck swelling.
## Complications
Perforation and mediastinitis, salivary fistula, bleeding, recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy, dental injury, recurrence.
!update Current evidence
A meta-analysis of 11 studies (596 patients) found endoscopic treatment shorter, with shorter stay and earlier feeding and fewer complications, but a higher recurrence of symptoms than open surgery (Albers et al., *Endosc Int Open* 2016). Flexible endoscopic septum division achieved success in 91% with adverse events in 11% and recurrence in 11% in a meta-analysis of 813 patients (Ishaq et al., *Gastrointest Endosc* 2016), and peroral endoscopic myotomy (Z-POEM) is a promising newer option (Zhang et al., *Surg Endosc* 2022).
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Myers: open cricopharyngeal myotomy with excision, inversion or pexy of the sac, and endoscopic stapler division of the party wall.
Dhingra: Killian's dehiscence and the classical open diverticulectomy with myotomy.
The literature: endoscopic division is quicker and safer, open surgery recurs less.
!!
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Anatomy**: Killian's dehiscence, the cricopharyngeus, the recurrent nerve.
- **Symptoms** and barium findings.
- **Endoscopic versus open**: indications and outcomes.
- **Why the myotomy matters.**
- **Complications**, especially mediastinitis.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Stapling a pouch too small to hold the stapler.
- Pushing beyond the end of the bar.
- Excising the sac without a myotomy.
- Pulling the sac and narrowing the pharynx.
!!
