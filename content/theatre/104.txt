@stand The Caldwell-Luc operation opens the maxillary sinus through its front wall from under the upper lip. Its original purpose, stripping the sinus mucosa and draining it through the inferior meatus, is now known to be wrong: the mucociliary system drains to the natural ostium regardless, and stripped sinuses scar and hurt. What survives is the approach, a quick, direct view into the antrum for disease the endoscope cannot reach from the nose, for access to the orbital floor and pterygopalatine fossa, and for closing oroantral fistulae.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Anterior antrostomy (Caldwell-Luc)
 | Summary
Indications | Disease in the anterior, inferior or lateral antrum beyond endoscopic reach (fungal ball, antrochoanal polyp base, dental cysts and foreign bodies, calcified disease); biopsy or staging of maxillary tumours; access to the pterygopalatine fossa or orbital floor; first step in some maxillectomies; oroantral fistula closure
Contraindications | Unerupted teeth in children (risk to tooth buds); disease that endoscopic surgery can treat
Anaesthesia | General (or local with alveolar and greater palatine blocks)
Position | Supine, tube to the opposite corner of the mouth
Tray | Headlight, Army-Navy retractors, periosteal elevators, osteotome and Kerrison rongeurs, endoscopes, suction, absorbable sutures
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Numbness** of the cheek, upper lip, side of the nose or upper teeth (infraorbital or superior alveolar nerve), usually temporary.
- **Facial swelling** and discomfort in the gum.
- **Tooth injury,** devitalised teeth; **ill-fitting dentures.**
- **Oroantral fistula.**
- **Eye injury** and, very rarely, loss of vision if the orbital floor is breached.
- Injury to the **tear duct** (watering eye).
!!
## Before you start
Review the CT: the disease, the height of the tooth roots, the orbital floor and the infraorbital canal. Combine with endoscopic middle meatal antrostomy so the sinus drains through its natural ostium.
!mf 1 | Mucociliary flow is towards the natural ostium, which is why mucosal stripping and inferior meatal drainage fail.
!mf 2 3 | CT of an antrochoanal polyp and of calcified chronic maxillary disease.

# The operation, stage by stage | Under the lip, through the canine fossa.
## Prepare and incise
Decongest the nose. Retract the lip and inject the sublabial region. Incise horizontally for 2 to 3 cm above the tooth roots, leaving about 5 mm of mucosa on the gum side of the sulcus for closure (over attached gingiva in edentulous patients so dentures will fit).
!mf 4 | The incision in the buccogingival sulcus.
!see What you should see
- Mucosa and periosteum divided down to the maxillary bone above the canine root.
!!
## Expose the canine fossa
Elevate the periosteum up over the anterior wall of the sinus with a broad elevator, exposing about 2 by 2 cm of bone in the canine fossa above the tooth roots. Find and protect the infraorbital nerve as it leaves its foramen.
!mf 5 6 | Exposure of the canine fossa and the infraorbital nerve at its foramen.
!see What you should see
- The thin bone of the canine fossa with the infraorbital nerve above it.
!!
!danger Nerves and roots
The infraorbital nerve is above and the canine and premolar roots below. Open the sinus in between, and do not retract hard on the nerve.
!!
## Open the antrum
Break into the sinus with an osteotome or drill, then widen the opening with Kerrison rongeurs to the size you need (larger windows heal with a defect under the cheek).
!mf 7 | Opening the sinus with an osteotome and widening it with rongeurs.
!see What you should see
- A view into the antrum through a window in the anterior wall.
!!
## Deal with the disease
Remove the fungal ball, polyp base, cyst or foreign body, take biopsies, and preserve healthy mucosa. Open the natural ostium endoscopically (middle meatal antrostomy) for drainage. An inferior meatal antrostomy is now rarely needed.
!mf 8 | The historical inferior meatal antrostomy.
!see What you should see
- A cleared sinus with healthy mucosa left and a wide natural ostium.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
Strip nothing that is not diseased. Healthy maxillary mucosa recovers once the ostium is open; stripped mucosa is replaced by scar, and the patient is left with pain and a non-functioning sinus.
!!
## Close
Close the sublabial incision in one layer with absorbable sutures. Apply a firm cheek dressing if bleeding.
!see What you should see
- A watertight closure with no tension on the lip.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Orbital floor breached
Act | stop ; leave periorbita undisturbed ; check the eye and vision after surgery
Swelling with vision change | treat as orbital haematoma: canthotomy, ophthalmology
!!
!path Wound breaks down with an oral leak
Act | soft diet, mouthwash, antibiotics
Persisting | treat as an oroantral fistula
!!

# Aftercare | Swelling for a week.
## After surgery
Ice and head elevation for swelling, soft diet, mouthwashes, avoid nose blowing, saline douching. Review for numbness and the fistula.
## Complications
Cheek swelling and pain, infraorbital and superior alveolar numbness, devitalised teeth, oroantral fistula, dacryocystitis, orbital injury, chronic facial pain, scarred non-functioning sinus.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Myers: keeps the anterior antrostomy for inflammatory disease beyond endoscopic reach, tumour biopsy, access to the pterygopalatine fossa and orbital floor, and oroantral fistula repair.
Dhingra: describes the classical radical antrostomy with mucosal stripping and an inferior meatal window, which is now obsolete for chronic sinusitis because drainage follows the natural ostium.
The literature: endoscopic techniques (middle meatal antrostomy, canine fossa puncture, endoscopic medial maxillectomy) now cover most of its old indications.
!!
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Mucociliary clearance** and why the classical operation fails.
- **Indications** today.
- The **steps** and the structures at risk.
- **Complications**, especially numbness and oroantral fistula.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Stripping healthy mucosa.
- Operating on children with unerupted teeth.
- An incision in the sulcus itself, leaving nothing to close.
- Forgetting to open the natural ostium.
!!
