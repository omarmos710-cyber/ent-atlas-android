@stand The osteoplastic flap lifts the front wall of the frontal sinus on its pericranial hinge like a lid, gives a complete view of the sinus, and then either clears disease or obliterates the sinus with fat after removing every trace of mucosa. Endoscopic surgery and the Draf III have taken most of its work, but it remains the answer for lateral mucoceles and tumours beyond the endoscope's reach, severe frontal fractures and failed endoscopic surgery. Its two rules: cut the bone within the sinus outline, and leave no mucosa behind.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Osteoplastic flap with frontal sinus obliteration
 | Summary
Indications | Recurrent chronic frontal sinusitis after failed endoscopic surgery (including failed Draf III); lateral frontal mucoceles out of endoscopic reach (lateral to the mid-pupil and high on the posterior table); severe frontal fractures involving the outflow tract or with CSF leak; tumours or osteomas that cannot be removed endoscopically
Contraindications | A tiny frontal sinus; when an endoscopic route will do
Incision | Bicoronal (standard); mid-forehead crease (balding men); upper brow "gull wing" (quick, but numbs the forehead)
Outline | Navigation, or a template cut from a 6-foot Caldwell radiograph
Obliteration | Abdominal fat (most common); pericranium or bone; or leave unobliterated if the outflow is restored
Anaesthesia | General
Tray | Bicoronal set, navigation, oscillating saw with a fine blade, osteotomes, drills with cutting and diamond burrs, plates, fat harvest set
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Forehead numbness** (especially with a brow incision) and a **scar**; hair loss along a bicoronal scar.
- **CSF leak, dural injury, meningitis** if the osteotomy enters the skull.
- **Contour irregularity,** bossing or a dent of the forehead.
- **Mucocele** forming years later inside an obliterated sinus (lifelong follow-up, MRI).
- **Abdominal scar** from the fat graft.
- Facial nerve (temporal branch) weakness, rare.
!!
## Before you start
Map the sinus on CT (navigation) or make the Caldwell template. MRI helps separate a mucocele from fat or tumour, and is the baseline for future surveillance of an obliterated sinus.
!mf 1 2 | A large lateral frontal mucocele on CT and MRI.

# The operation, stage by stage | Lid open, mucosa out, sinus filled.
## Raise the scalp
Make a bicoronal incision behind the hairline from helix to helix and raise the flap in the subgaleal plane down to the supraorbital rims, keeping the supraorbital and supratrochlear bundles in the flap. Stay deep to the temporoparietal fascia laterally to protect the frontal branch.
!mf 3 | Bicoronal or brow incisions to approach the frontal sinus.
!see What you should see
- Intact pericranium over the frontal bone and the supraorbital rims exposed.
!!
## Incise the pericranium
Cut the pericranium above the planned upper osteotomy so it will cover the bone cut when replaced, and leave it attached to the bone flap below as its blood supply.
!see What you should see
- A pericranial hinge left on the lower part of the frontal bone.
!!
## Mark and cut the bone flap
Mark the sinus outline with navigation or the template, then cut *inside* it with a fine saw angled (bevelled) towards the sinus. Cut the medial and lateral limbs, then the upper limb.
!mf 4 5 | The sinus outlined from the template and the bevelled osteotomies cut with the saw.
!see What you should see
- Saw cuts entering the sinus cavity everywhere, never the cranium.
!!
!danger Stay inside the outline
An osteotomy outside the sinus margin goes straight into the anterior cranial fossa: dura, CSF and the superior sagittal sinus in the midline. Bevel the blade inwards and check with navigation.
!!
## Fracture the flap down
Lever the flap forwards with osteotomes through the upper cut and crack it along the sinus floor, hinging it down on its pericranium. Avoid fracturing the anterior table itself; plate it if it breaks.
!see What you should see
- The whole sinus open, with the anterior wall hinged down on the brows.
!!
## Clear the disease and every scrap of mucosa
Treat the pathology (drain the mucocele, remove the osteoma or tumour). If obliterating, strip all mucosa from the sinus walls and the underside of the flap and drill the inner cortex with a diamond burr so no epithelium survives in the bony pits. Invert the frontal recess mucosa and plug the outflow tracts with fascia or muscle.
!see What you should see
- Bare, freshly drilled bone throughout, with the ducts sealed.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
A mucocele after obliteration grows from mucosa left behind, often in the recesses or on the back of the flap. Drill every surface; it is the only part of the operation that determines its long-term success.
!!
## Obliterate and close
Harvest abdominal fat and pack the sinus with it, close the flap, fix it with plates, replace the pericranium, drain the scalp and close.
!see What you should see
- A flap seated flush with the forehead and the sinus filled with fat.
!!

# Aftercare | Watch it for life.
## After surgery
Scalp drain for a day or two, head dressing, antibiotics. MRI in the first year as a new baseline, then surveillance for mucocele by symptoms and imaging.
## Complications
CSF leak, dural injury, forehead numbness and contour deformity, alopecia along the scar, frontal branch palsy, infection, late mucocele, fat graft necrosis.
!update Current evidence
In 40 patients followed for a mean of almost 7 years after fat obliteration, MRI suggested possible mucoceles in 6 and revision was needed in 7.5% (Hansen et al., *Rhinology* 2018), so lifelong surveillance is needed. Endoscopic frontal surgery, including the Draf III with about 90% patency, has replaced obliteration for most chronic sinusitis (Shih et al., *Laryngoscope* 2018).
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Myers: bicoronal osteoplastic flap with the pericranial hinge, bevelled osteotomies within the sinus outline, and obliteration with fat after removing all mucosa.
Dhingra and K. J. Lee: external frontal approaches (Lynch-Howarth frontoethmoidectomy, osteoplastic flap) as alternatives to endoscopic surgery for complicated frontal disease.
The literature: obliteration carries a late mucocele risk; endoscopic surgery is now first-line.
!!

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Cutting outside the sinus outline.
- Leaving mucosa in the recesses or on the flap.
- Losing the pericranial hinge.
- No long-term imaging follow-up.
!!
