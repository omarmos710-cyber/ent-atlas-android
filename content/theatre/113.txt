@stand The Draf III, or endoscopic modified Lothrop procedure, turns both frontal sinuses into one wide common cavity draining into the nose through a single opening, by drilling away the frontal sinus floors, the intersinus septum and the top of the nasal septum. It is the endoscopic answer to frontal disease that keeps failing standard surgery, and it replaced most osteoplastic obliterations. The boundaries are fixed: the lamina papyracea on each side, the skull base and first olfactory fibres behind, the skin of the forehead in front.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Draf III (endoscopic modified Lothrop)
 | Summary
Indications | Recalcitrant frontal sinusitis after previous endoscopic frontal surgery; failed obliteration; mucociliary disease (cystic fibrosis, primary ciliary dyskinesia); frontal tumours (inverted papilloma, osteoma, fibrous dysplasia); selected trauma and posterior table CSF leaks; anterior access for skull base surgery
Contraindications | A narrow anteroposterior frontal dimension (under about 1 cm at the beak); unfitness; disease too lateral to reach (a supraorbital cell far out may need an external approach)
Approaches | Inside-out (from an identified frontal recess) or outside-in (from below, for scarred or lost recesses)
Anaesthesia | General, hypotensive
Tray | 0, 30, 45 and 70 degree endoscopes, navigation, angled drills with coarse diamond burrs, frontal instruments, optional frontal trephine for fluorescein irrigation
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **CSF leak** (repaired at the time if recognised), **pneumocephalus,** meningitis.
- **Orbital injury,** retrobulbar haematoma, double vision.
- **Bleeding.**
- **Reduced smell** if the olfactory area is disturbed.
- **Restenosis** of the opening and revision surgery (about one in eight over time).
- Cosmetic change if too much bone is removed in front (rare).
!!
## Before you start
On CT measure the anteroposterior diameter of the frontal beak, map the frontal cells, the anterior ethmoidal arteries, the skull base height and the olfactory fossa depth, and look for dehiscence of the posterior table or orbit. Load navigation.
!mf 1 | A completed Draf IIb on the right: the starting point for an inside-out Draf III.

# The operation, stage by stage | Inside-out or outside-in.
## Inside-out: open one frontal sinus fully
Identify the frontal recess on the easier side (navigation, or fluorescein irrigated through a trephine). Remove the head of the middle turbinate up to the back of the ostium, then remove the frontal sinus floor from the ostium to the septum: a Draf IIb.
!see What you should see
- One frontal sinus widely open to the nasal septum.
!!
## Septal window
Make a superior septectomy: start opposite the attachment of the middle turbinate, angle the cut forwards to end 5 to 10 mm in front of the turbinate head, run the lower cut back to the turbinate, and the posterior cut up along the front edge of the turbinate to preserve olfactory mucosa.
!mf 2 | The septal window.
!mf 5 6 7 | The planned superior septectomy and the view of both middle turbinates after it.
!see What you should see
- Both middle turbinate heads and both frontal recess areas visible through one nostril.
!!
## Find the first olfactory fibre
Elevate mucosa back along the remaining upper septum towards the olfactory cleft until the first olfactory fibre appears. It marks the posterior limit of drilling on each side.
!mf 9 | Identifying the first olfactory fibre.
!see What you should see
- A nerve fibre entering the cribriform plate at the front of the olfactory cleft.
!!
!danger The skull base is directly behind the first olfactory fibre
Drill only in front of the first olfactory fibres and the posterior table of the frontal sinus. The skull base is thinnest here and a CSF leak is the commonest serious complication.
!!
## Drill the floors and beak
With a 4 mm coarse diamond, drill in the midline from the septal window upwards and outwards, removing the nasofrontal beak, the frontal sinus floors and the frontal processes of the maxilla in a horseshoe shape, from one lamina papyracea to the other, as far forward as the skin of the glabella allows. Remove the intersinus septum.
!mf 10 11 | Drilling begins in the midline and extends laterally over the frontal process of the maxilla.
!see What you should see
- A single wide horseshoe-shaped opening joining both frontal sinuses, with intact mucosa on the posterior table.
!!
## Outside-in when no recess can be found
When scarring, new bone or tumour hides both recesses, do the ethmoidectomies to define the orbit and skull base, make the septal window first, and drill from below the frontal floor upwards in the midline (in front of the first olfactory fibres) until the sinuses are entered, then widen.
!mf 3 4 | The frontal floor exposed and the completed inside-out opening.
!see What you should see
- Entry into the frontal sinuses from below without having needed to find a recess.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
Make it as big as the bony limits allow: lamina to lamina and as far forward as safe. The opening narrows by a third or more as it heals, so a small Draf III becomes a stenosed Draf III. Preserve the mucosa on the posterior wall and lay free mucosal grafts on bare bone if you can.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path CSF leak from the skull base
Act | identify the defect ; repair at once with a multilayer graft or septal flap
After | head up, no straining ; watch for pneumocephalus
!!
!path Orbital haematoma
Act | stop, remove packing, lateral canthotomy and cantholysis ; ophthalmology
!!

# Aftercare | Keep the common cavity open.
## After surgery
High-volume saline irrigation and topical steroid irrigations; debridement in clinic over the first months to prevent scarring and crusting; treat infection. Long-term endoscopic review.
## Complications
CSF leak, pneumocephalus, orbital injury, haemorrhage, anosmia, restenosis, cosmetic depression, saddling if too much septum is removed.
!update Current evidence
In 229 patients with recalcitrant chronic rhinosinusitis (on average 3.8 previous operations), the Draf III succeeded without further surgery in 95% over about 4 years; allergic fungal sinusitis and recurrent *Staphylococcus aureus* infection predicted failure (Naidoo et al., *Laryngoscope* 2014). A meta-analysis of 1,205 patients found significant or complete symptom improvement in 87%, patency in 91% and revision in about 13%, with revisions rising after 2 years of follow-up (Shih et al., *Laryngoscope* 2018).
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Myers: inside-out and outside-in techniques, the first olfactory fibre as the posterior landmark and a horseshoe opening from lamina to lamina.
Dhingra and K. J. Lee: the Draf classification (I, IIa, IIb, III) and the place of external procedures such as the osteoplastic flap.
The literature: patency about 90% and success in most recalcitrant cases, with revisions accumulating over time.
!!
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- The **Draf classification.**
- **Indications** and the minimum anteroposterior diameter.
- The **boundaries**: laminae, skull base and first olfactory fibre, glabellar skin.
- **Inside-out versus outside-in.**
- **Stenosis** and how to prevent it.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Drilling behind the first olfactory fibre.
- Making the opening too small.
- Stripping mucosa off the posterior table.
- Over-resecting the septum and causing a saddle.
!!
