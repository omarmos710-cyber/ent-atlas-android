@stand Most septal perforations come from previous septal surgery, picking, cocaine, or a disease such as granulomatosis with polyangiitis, and most cause nothing at all. The symptomatic ones crust, bleed, whistle and block. Before any repair you must know the cause, because active disease or ongoing cocaine use will reopen any closure. The repair itself follows one principle: cover the hole on both sides with healthy, well-vascularised mucosa, closed without tension, with a graft between the layers.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Repair of a nasal septal perforation
 | Summary
Indications | Symptomatic perforation (crusting, bleeding, whistling, obstruction, pain, smell) not controlled by conservative care; cosmetic deformity from loss of support
Contraindications | Active vasculitis or granulomatous disease; ongoing cocaine or other intranasal drug use; malignancy not excluded; very large perforation with poor mucosa (consider a button)
Conservative first | Saline douching, emollient ointment, humidification; a silicone septal button
Work-up | Endoscopy, biopsy of the edge if the cause is unclear, ESR/CRP, ANCA, ACE, syphilis serology, urine drug screen if suspected
Size | Measure height and length: under 2 cm closes reliably; over 2 cm is harder
Anaesthesia | General
Tray | Endoscopes, septoplasty set, through-cutting forceps, backbiter, interposition graft (acellular dermis, fascia, septal or auricular cartilage), absorbable sutures, Silastic splints
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Closure is not guaranteed:** about 85% to 90% overall, less for large holes; the hole may enlarge or a new one form.
- **Bleeding, infection, septal haematoma.**
- **Adhesions,** reduced smell, rarely a watering eye.
- **Splints** for about 2 to 3 weeks.
- No cocaine or nose picking afterwards, or the repair will fail.
!!
## Before you start
Find the cause and treat it. Biopsy the posterior or superior edge, away from the support, if the cause is uncertain. Make sure active disease has been quiet for months and drug use has stopped. Measure the perforation and assess the remaining septal support and the mucosa around it.

# The operation, stage by stage | Bilateral flaps with an interposition graft.
## Vasoconstrict and hydrodissect
Inject both sides of the septum in the subperichondrial and subperiosteal planes and the nasal floors with lidocaine and adrenaline, from back to front, using a "pull-flash" technique; wait 10 minutes. Place oxymetazoline pledgets.
!see What you should see
- Blanched mucosa lifted from the cartilage around the perforation.
!!
## Clean and freshen the edge
Under the endoscope, remove crusts with warm saline irrigation. De-epithelialise the perforation margin all round (through-cutting forceps behind, a blade above and below, a backbiter in front), taking as little tissue as possible.
!see What you should see
- Raw, bleeding mucosal edges on both sides of the hole, no larger than before.
!!
!danger Do not enlarge the hole
Every millimetre removed from the edge adds to the defect you must close. Freshen; do not trim.
!!
## Raise the flaps
Through a hemitransfixion incision (or an open rhinoplasty approach for large or anterior holes), elevate subperichondrial flaps on both sides above and below the perforation, and separate the two mucosal layers at its edge. Design the closure: superior and inferior bipedicled advancement flaps (the inferior one extending along the nasal floor) on one side, and an anterior ethmoidal artery flap or advancement flaps on the other.
!see What you should see
- Mobile, intact flaps on each side that reach across the hole without pulling.
!!
## Interpose the graft
Lay a graft larger than the hole (acellular dermis, temporalis fascia or septal or auricular cartilage) between the two sides as a scaffold.
!see What you should see
- The graft covering the perforation with a generous overlap.
!!
## Close each side without tension
Advance and suture the flaps to close the mucosa on both sides with fine absorbable sutures. Where both sides cannot be closed, close one completely; the other heals over the graft. Use quilting sutures to close dead space, then insert Silastic splints sutured through the septum.
!see What you should see
- Both sides closed (or one closed and the other covered by graft), with splints in place.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
Tension is the enemy. Release the flaps until they lie across the hole on their own; a closure that needs pulling will break down at the suture line.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Septal haematoma after repair
Act | evacuate ; quilting sutures to close the space ; antibiotics
!!
!path Repair fails
Wait | at least 6 months for healing
Then | a septal button, or a second repair with an open approach or a different flap
!!

# Aftercare | Moist and protected.
## After surgery
Oral antibiotics while the splints are in; saline douching and ointment; no nose blowing for 2 weeks; remove splints at 2 to 3 weeks. Examine at 3 months for closure.
## Complications
Bleeding, septal haematoma or abscess, adhesions, reperforation or enlargement, reduced smell, epiphora, infection.
!update Current evidence
The size of the perforation is the strongest predictor of closure: failure is commoner above 2 cm, and bilateral vascularised flap cover improves success (Kim and Rhee, *Curr Opin Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg* 2012). A systematic review of 329 purely endoscopic repairs (mean size 17 mm) reported closure in 90%, with similar success for one-, two- and three-layer repairs (Gravina et al., *Ann Otol Rhinol Laryngol* 2023). For large perforations (2 cm or more), a meta-analysis of 408 patients found overall closure of 84%: about 88% with mucosal advancement and 87% with interposition grafts (Cheung et al., *Eur Arch Otorhinolaryngol* 2026).
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Myers: bilateral intranasal mucosal flaps (advancement on one side, an anterior ethmoidal artery flap on the other), tension-free closure and an acellular dermis interposition graft.
Dhingra and K. J. Lee: the causes (surgery, trauma, cocaine, granulomatous disease, syphilis) and conservative care, including the septal button.
The literature: closure rates of 85% to 90%, falling with size above 2 cm.
!!
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Causes** and the investigations to find them.
- **Who to repair** and who to button.
- The **principles**: vascularised flaps, tension-free closure, interposition graft.
- **Prognostic factors:** size, cause, mucosa.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Repairing a perforation from active disease or continuing cocaine use.
- Trimming rather than freshening the edge.
- Closing under tension.
- Biopsying the anterior edge and enlarging the hole.
!!
