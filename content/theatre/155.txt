@src Myers, Operative Otolaryngology
@stand Rhinoplasty changes how the nose looks and how it breathes, and every manoeuvre affects both. The nose is the central aesthetic unit of the face, in nine subunits; the airway begins at the nostril and can fail at the external or internal nasal valve, collapsing at rest or only on inspiration. The open approach lifts the skin off the skeleton so you can work on it in thirds: the bony upper third (hump, osteotomies), the cartilaginous middle third (septum, upper laterals, spreader grafts) and the lower third, the tip, a tripod of medial and lateral crura whose relative lengths predictably set rotation and projection. Mastery takes years. The commonest regrets come from taking away support without putting it back: an inverted V, a collapsed valve, a polybeak, an open roof. And the commonest unhappy patient is one whose motives and expectations were never explored. This chapter covers selection and analysis, the open approach, the three thirds, tip support, closure and the classic deformities.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Rhinoplasty and nasal valve surgery
 | Summary
Indications | Improving the appearance of the nose; nasal obstruction from **static or dynamic collapse of the internal or external valve**; reconstruction after trauma or cancer resection
Functional add-ons | Patients with obstruction often do best with a **septoplasty and inferior turbinate reduction** at the same time
Contraindications | Comorbidity raising anaesthetic or bleeding risk; **unrealistic expectations** or psychological disorders predicting dissatisfaction; active **rhinitis medicamentosa** (bleeding, and vasoconstrictors less effective)
Anaesthesia | General preferred (comfort, airway control if bleeding is heavy); local or sedation possible
Position | Supine, arms tucked, head of bed turned 90 degrees; tube secured so it does not distort the lip or upper face; whole face visible, prepared with ophthalmic povidone-iodine
Antibiotics | One preincision dose covering skin flora (weight-based cefazolin)
Tray | Septorhinoplasty set; straight, curved and guarded osteotomes (including a Rubin); rasps; Freer elevator; Converse scissors
Skills | Septoplasty
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Why:** to change the shape of your nose, to improve breathing, or both.
- **The result:** you will see improvement, but small asymmetries or irregularities you can feel but not see are usual. Swelling takes months to settle.
- **You may be unhappy** with the appearance; revision surgery is sometimes needed.
- **Breathing:** the nose can collapse if its support is weakened; scarring can block the airway.
- **Bleeding** after surgery, a **hole in the septum**, loss of smell, and rarely a leak of brain fluid.
- **Splints** for a week, saline sprays, no nose blowing for 2 weeks, limited activity.
- **Stop** blood thinners, fish oil and similar supplements 2 weeks before, if medically safe.
!!
## Before you start
Explore the **motivation** for surgery: a realistic patient with sensible expectations is the foundation of a good outcome, and unrealistic or unachievable goals must be discussed beforehand. Ask about previous nasal surgery or trauma; obstruction, at rest or on breathing in (**static or dynamic collapse**); anosmia; easy bleeding or bruising; and screen for psychiatric disorders at your discretion. Stop antiplatelets and anticoagulants 2 weeks beforehand where medically appropriate, and over-the-counter and herbal products that increase bleeding, such as high-dose fish oil.
## Analysis
Examine the whole face and the external nose:
- The height of the **nasion and rhinion**, and the length of the nasal bones relative to the cartilaginous midvault.
- The nasal bones, palpated for irregularity and old fractures.
- **Skin thickness.**
- The shape and support of the **medial and lateral crura**, the columellar relationship, tip projection.
- A **bulbous tip:** thick skin, or the shape of the cartilages?
- The base: nostril shape and symmetry.
- **Endoscopy** for obstruction: septal deviation and turbinate hypertrophy.
- Watch the valves during **quiet and slightly deeper inspiration** for static and dynamic collapse of the internal and external valves.
Imaging is not usually needed; review any existing scans for the bones and old trauma. Take the full set of photographs: frontal, oblique, basal, lateral and lateral smiling. Counsel about aftercare, activity, rinses and splints.

# Anatomy you must see | Landmarks and tip support.
## Landmarks
Glabella; nasion and radix; rhinion; supratip; tip; infratip lobule; the **anterior and posterior septal angles**; the head of the inferior turbinate; the cartilaginous and bony septum; the nasal bones; the **upper and lower lateral cartilages**; the maxillary spine; the nasolabial (nasal-columellar) angle.
## The tip tripod
The tip behaves like a tripod: the paired medial crura form the lower leg, each lateral crus an upper leg. Shortening the **lateral** crura **increases rotation**; shortening the **medial** crura **decreases rotation**. The lower laterals are often asymmetric, irregular at the domes and variably oriented, even in a symmetrical nose, so tip manoeuvres must be gentle and considered; the commonest functional penalty is **external valve collapse** from lost support.
!table Tip support
Major mechanisms | Minor mechanisms
Strength of the lower lateral cartilages | Dorsal septum
Attachment of the medial crura to the septum | Interdomal ligaments
Attachment of the upper to the lower lateral cartilages | Membranous septum; nasal spine; skin and soft tissue; alar sidewalls
!!

# The operation, stage by stage | Open rhinoplasty in thirds.
## Inject
Inject a 1:1 mixture of 1% lidocaine and 0.5% bupivacaine with 1:100,000 adrenaline into the columellar and marginal incision lines, the subcutaneous plane of the whole skin envelope and the osteotomy sites, and the septal mucosa if a septoplasty is planned.
!see What you should see
- The whole envelope blanched, with the tip and dorsum not distorted by the volume.
!!
## Marginal incisions
Incise along the **caudal margin of each lateral crus**, then dissect with Converse scissors over the crura toward the tip to make pockets.
!mf 1 | The marginal incision just caudal to the lateral crus, with dissection over its superficial surface.
!see What you should see
- The caudal edge of each lateral crus, with the cut on its margin, not through the cartilage.
!!
## The transcolumellar incision
Make a **gull-wing** (five-pointed) incision through **skin only**, sparing the medial crura, extending horizontally past their flare and then vertically toward the dome along the caudal margin of the lower laterals. Tunnel with Converse scissors subcutaneously from one vertical limb to the other, then complete the deep cut, cauterising the columellar artery.
!mf 2 | (A) The gull-wing transcolumellar design; (B) its lateral limb.
!mf 3 | Completing the transcolumellar incision with scissors, staying superficial to the medial crura.
!see What you should see
- Intact medial crura beneath the columellar skin cut, and the columellar arteries sealed.
!!
## Lift the envelope
Follow the caudal margins of the medial crura to the tip and join the lateral pockets. Continue up toward the nasion **superficial to the upper laterals**, then **subperiosteally** over the nasal bones to the nasion. Bleeding is minor unless you stray laterally into the angular artery anastomosis.
!mf 4 | (A) Sharp elevation of the skin envelope off the tip cartilages; (B) retracting the tip cartilages down to dissect toward the nasion.
!see What you should see
- The white lower lateral cartilages, the upper laterals and the bare nasal bones to the nasion, with the envelope lifted in one clean plane.
!!
## The septum
Separate the medial crura to reach the caudal septum and raise subperichondrial flaps on both sides beyond the bony-cartilaginous junction: ideal for severe caudal deviation and vestibular stenosis. For lesser deviations a hemitransfixion incision may be enough. Divide the upper laterals from the septum submucosally, protecting the lining. The septal cartilage is now available for grafts.
!see What you should see
- The caudal and dorsal septum from above, both flaps intact, and an L-strut of at least 1 to 1.5 cm marked.
!!
## Upper third
- **Low radix:** augment the nasion with autologous cartilage, with or without fascia or perichondrium, to balance a nose that looks short.
- **Dorsal hump:** resect the **cartilaginous hump sharply** (scalpel or angled Fomon scissors) and the **bony hump with an osteotome** (a Rubin), keeping the plane so that the nasal bones are left of equal height.
!mf 5 | Resecting the dorsum with angled Fomon scissors.
!mf 6 | Curved left and right osteotomes and the straight Rubin osteotome.
!mf 7 | The dorsal osteotomy with a Rubin osteotome.
!see What you should see
- A smooth, straight profile with nasal bones of equal height and an open roof.
!!
## Lateral osteotomies: high-low-high
Removing the hump leaves an **open roof**, which must be closed by medialising the bones with **lateral osteotomies**, usually endonasal (percutaneous for severe deformity or old fractures). Start **high** on the pyriform aperture **above the attachment of the inferior turbinate**, keeping a triangle of maxilla that preserves the lateral suspensory ligament and stops the valve's lateral wall from medialising. Go **low** along the ascending process of the maxilla at the nasofacial junction, where thick skin hides it and the bone is strong. Curve **high** again toward the apex for a predictable back fracture, stopping at the level of the **medial canthus** to avoid a rocker deformity. Marking the line on the skin helps the inexperienced keep both sides symmetrical. Rasp the medialised bones smooth, because the skin here is thin.
!mf 8 | The high-low-high lateral osteotomy: position 1, the first segment; position 2, angled up along the nasofacial junction.
!mf 9 | Before (left) and after (right) a dorsal reduction and deprojection: (A) lateral; (B) base.
!see What you should see
- The nasal bones moving in to close the roof, with no step on the side wall and no rocking of the bone.
!!
## Middle third
The midvault is the upper laterals and their septal attachment. Contour the dorsal septum carefully, as over-resection causes functional and cosmetic problems, and correct any twist. **Spreader grafts** (long, flat strips of septal or auricular cartilage beside the dorsal septum) support the midvault, open the **internal valve**, and prevent an **inverted V**; placed asymmetrically, they straighten a deviated dorsum. Fix them with three interrupted horizontal mattress sutures, then sew the upper laterals back to them. **Turn-in flaps** of the upper laterals that would otherwise be resected, scored and folded under, give similar support without harvesting cartilage.
!see What you should see
- Spreader grafts level with the dorsal septum, a straight dorsum, and a wider angle at the internal valve.
!!
## Lower third: the tip
- **Cartilage-sparing first:** change the relative crural lengths. In **lateral crural steal**, horizontal mattress sutures recruit lateral crus into the tip, lengthening the medial crura and **increasing projection**; recruiting medial into lateral does the opposite.
- **Cephalic trim:** remove the cephalic margins of the lateral crura to reduce bulbosity and increase rotation, measuring what remains with calipers to avoid over-resection.
- **Domal sutures:** transdomal horizontal mattress sutures narrow the tip.
- **Vertical dome division** with interdomal sutures and reattachment narrows a boxy tip.
- **Lateral crural overlay:** divide, overlap and suture the lateral crura to shorten them without removing cartilage; with medial crural overlay too, rotation and projection are controlled together. Do not destabilise the tip excessively.
- **Lateral crural strut grafts** under the lateral crura correct a boxy tip, malpositioned or cephalic crura, rim retraction and concave crura, and support a collapsing **external valve**.
- **Tip grafts** under the skin sculpt the tip and infratip and hide irregularities.
!mf 10 | A conservative cephalic trim (red).
!mf 11 | Transdomal horizontal mattress sutures to narrow the tip.
!mf 12 | Vertical dome division with cephalic trim, interdomal sutures and reattachment of the lateral crura, narrowing a boxy tip.
!mf 13 | Lateral crural overlay (red: the overlap).
!see What you should see
- Symmetrical domes from the base view, with at least 6 to 8 mm of lateral crus left after any trim.
!!
## Septal extension and caudal strut grafts
The open approach divides tip support, and scarring de-projects the tip over time. A **septal extension graft** overlaps the caudal septum and extends between the medial crura; fixing the lower laterals to it controls projection and rotation almost at will, and placed on one side it can also pull a deviated caudal septum to the midline. A **caudal strut graft** joins end to end with the caudal septum: thinner, but less stable, useful for irregular medial crura.
!see What you should see
- A tip that springs back when pressed, in the midline, at the planned projection and rotation.
!!
!danger Restore what you remove
Failing to restore tip support after interrupting it, or over-resecting dorsal or tip cartilage, leads to collapse of the valves and secondary deformity.
!!
## Close
Re-drape the skin envelope. Close the columella first at its midline, then at the apices of the gull-wing, with 6-0 permanent monofilament, then interrupted sutures between; close the marginal incisions with 6-0 fast-absorbing gut. Place silicone splints if synechiae are a concern. Suck out the nasopharynx and pass an orogastric tube before waking.
!see What you should see
- The columellar incision edges level with no notch, and a symmetrical tip once the skin is back.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Bleeding after surgery
First | topical vasoconstrictor such as oxymetazoline
If it persists | packing or absorbable haemostatic agents (infrequently needed)
!!
## Common errors in technique
!pit
- Poor exposure; poor haemostasis.
- Elevating in the wrong plane.
- Over-resecting the dorsum or tip cartilage.
- Uneven or incomplete osteotomies.
- Not restoring tip support.
!!

# Aftercare | Splints for a week.
## After the operation
External splint for **1 week**; internal silicone splints for 1 week with saline spray every 2 to 4 hours from the day of surgery to prevent crusting. Paracetamol and opioids for the first few days. **No nose blowing for 2 weeks**; saline spray instead. At the 1-week visit remove splints and tape, examine and clear crusts.
## Deformities and complications
!table Classic deformities
Deformity | Cause and treatment
Inverted V | Midvault and internal valve collapse after dorsal resection without support; spreader grafts
Polybeak | Supratip fullness from over-resected nasal bones, under-resected cartilaginous septum or supratip scarring; steroid for scar, revision for anatomy
Open roof | Wide, often asymmetric bony dorsum: greenstick lateral osteotomies that spring back, bones not medialised, aggressive packing, uncorrected deviated perpendicular plate
Bossae | Tip cartilage irregularities; revision directed at the cause
Valve collapse | Loss of support at the internal or external valve
!!
!mf 14 | The inverted V deformity.
Other risks: dissatisfaction, obstructive scarring, epistaxis, septal perforation, anosmia and CSF leak.
## Alternatives
**Hyaluronic acid fillers** are used by some as a "non-surgical rhinoplasty" to correct asymmetry and augment the dorsum; this is controversial because of reported vascular complications from embolised filler, but increasingly common.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Do measurements predict beauty?
Many ideals of nasal proportion exist. In a study of 300 lateral facial views, the Goode, Simons, Baum, Powell and Crumley measures of tip projection correlated neither with one another nor with attractiveness judged by 78 viewers: no single measurement should outweigh assessment of the whole face. The commonest is **Goode's ratio** of tip projection to nasal length, normally **0.55 to 0.60**, but experienced surgeons mostly judge projection by eye.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Operating on unrealistic expectations.
- A dorsal hump removal without lateral osteotomies.
- A cephalic trim without measuring the remaining cartilage.
- Forgetting the septum and turbinates in a patient who cannot breathe.
