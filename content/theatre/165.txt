@src Myers, Operative Otolaryngology
@stand The forehead flap is the oldest flap in surgery and still the gold standard for rebuilding the skin of the nose. Ancient Indian potters and tilemakers, not physicians, raised it to repair noses cut off as punishment; it reached the West in Carpue's hands in 1814. The paramedian flap is a long, narrow pedicle of forehead skin based on one supratrochlear artery at the medial brow, swung down onto the nose, left attached for at least three weeks while the nose grows new vessels into it, and then divided. Its success depends on measuring, measuring again, and remembering that rotation costs length; on a base narrow enough to rotate but wide enough to carry the artery; and on patience before dividing the pedicle. This chapter covers selection, the forehead's blood supply, the design and template, the stages, the donor site, and the delay before division.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Paramedian forehead flap
 | Summary
Indications | Cutaneous nasal defects; periorbital and cheek defects when other local flaps are not available
Contraindications | Unfit even for local anaesthesia with sedation; psychiatrically or emotionally unstable (especially for several stages); scarring, radiotherapy or previous surgery to the forehead (such as a bicoronal incision) that may have cut the blood supply
Caution | **Smoking** is not an absolute contraindication but raises flap ischaemia and donor site problems; a **delay** (incising the outline without raising it) may be advised
Pedicle | One **supratrochlear artery**, about **1.25 to 1.5 cm from the midline** at the orbital rim
Base width | **1.0 to 1.5 cm**: wide enough to carry the artery, narrow enough to rotate without losing length
Anaesthesia | General preferred; local (lidocaine with or without bupivacaine) with intravenous benzodiazepine sedation if needed
Position | Supine, bed turned 90 degrees so both sides of the face, forehead, scalp, nose, ears and neck are available (symmetry, cartilage grafts)
Antibiotics | First-generation cephalosporin; clindamycin in allergy
Tray | Plastic soft tissue tray (No. 15C blades, small double hooks, iris scissors, 0.5 toothed forceps); needle-tip and bipolar diathermy; sterile Doppler; rhinoplasty set
Skills | Soft tissue handling, advancement-rotation flaps, pedicled flaps, functional rhinoplasty
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Why:** to rebuild the nose with skin moved down from the forehead on its own blood supply.
- **Stages:** the flap stays attached to the forehead for about three weeks, with a bridge of skin you can see, before a second operation divides it; sometimes there is a middle stage to thin it.
- **A scar** down the forehead, closed directly or left to heal, which usually looks very acceptable.
- **The flap may partly die**, especially if you smoke.
- **Bleeding, infection, numbness** of the scalp, and a change in the position of the brow or hairline.
- **Further small procedures** (dermabrasion, thinning) at 6 to 12 months to refine the result.
- **Hair** may be carried onto the nose if the forehead is short.
!!
## Before you start
Forehead flaps mostly rebuild the nose after cancer excision, trauma, infection or congenital loss. Ask about previous forehead injuries or excisions that may have disrupted the blood supply, age and comorbidity (anaesthetic risk), psychiatric stability and readiness for a staged operation, anticoagulants (weigh the need against stopping or bridging) and **smoking**.

Characterise the defect:
- **Size:** most suit a paramedian flap; a median flap gives more surface for a near-total nose.
- **Shape:** a large three-dimensional defect needs a template of the nasal subunits drawn on the forehead.
- **Location:** to reach low defects such as the columella, the flap may need to run into the hairline; to avoid that, lengthen it by extending the base into the brow or angling it slightly.
- **Missing layers:** the flap replaces skin; cartilage and lining must be reconstructed by other means.
Examine the forehead for scars and actinic damage, note the **hairline**, measure the distance from the base to the defect carefully, and consider **tissue expansion** for very wide defects. Imaging is not needed.

## Preparation
Decide with the patient among the alternatives (secondary healing, primary closure, grafts, other local flaps). Timing: after Mohs surgery, the defect is known and the plan definite; at the time of excision, counsel for several options, and delayed reconstruction pending final margins is often better. Obtain medical clearance, stop anticoagulants and smoking if possible, and photograph the defect.

# Anatomy you must see | The forehead's blood supply.
## From the internal carotid (ophthalmic artery)
- **Supraorbital artery:** leaves the supraorbital foramen or notch above the mid-orbit to supply the forehead above.
- **Supratrochlear artery:** leaves the superomedial orbit above the medial canthus and runs up the forehead. It runs **superficial to corrugator** and **deep to orbicularis oculi and frontalis**, then pierces frontalis to become subcutaneous **1 to 2.5 cm above the orbital rim**.
- **Dorsal nasal artery:** runs down along the medial canthus to the upper nasal sidewall; where it meets the angular artery, a **central artery** often runs up to supply the glabella and lower central forehead.
## From the external carotid
- **Facial artery:** gives the labial arteries and ends as the **angular artery**, which gives the lateral nasal branch, passes the medial canthus and joins the dorsal nasal and sometimes the supratrochlear artery, contributing to the central artery.
- **Superficial temporal artery:** its frontal branch supplies the lateral forehead and joins the supraorbital.

# The operation, stage by stage | Design, raise, inset, divide.
## Choose the flap
- **Paramedian:** on one supratrochlear artery, centred over it. The usual choice.
- **Midline:** on the supratrochlear artery with the flap in the centre of the forehead and the artery at its edge; it can be based on the **central artery**, lower and more medially along the rim, gaining length before the hairline.
- **Median:** on both supratrochlear arteries, broader based, with a smaller arc of rotation.
- **Laterally based**, on one or both superficial temporal arteries, for orbital or cheek defects when other local flaps and the midline supply are unavailable; its donor site can be conspicuous.
!see What you should see
- A forehead with enough height below the hairline for the flap you have chosen, and no scars across its base.
!!
## Find the artery
A Doppler can mark it, but landmarks are reliable: about **1.25 to 1.5 cm lateral to the midline** at the orbital rim.
!see What you should see
- A Doppler signal or the landmark 1.25 to 1.5 cm from the midline at the rim, usually at the medial end of the brow.
!!
## Design with a template
Base the flap above the medial canthus, centred 1.25 to 1.5 cm from the midline, **1.0 to 1.5 cm wide**. Widen the distal paddle if the defect needs more skin. Before cutting, make a **template** (a piece of towel, for example), hold its base in place and rotate it to the defect to check length and shape. Keep the flap below the hairline where possible.
!see What you should see
- The template reaching the far edge of the defect without tension when pivoted at the base.
!!
!danger Too short
Measure and re-measure. Rotation shortens the flap, and the inset must be **tension-free**. A flap that falls short cannot be rescued.
!!
## Raise and inset
In a multistage nasal reconstruction, include **full-thickness frontalis** in the distal flap to limit contraction; it can be thinned later. Inset with 5-0 nylon and a few deep dermal sutures. Skin-graft the raw underside of the bridge over intact nasal skin, or cover it with an acellular dermal matrix, to reduce bleeding.
!see What you should see
- A pink flap reaching the defect without tension, its pedicle untwisted, the bridge spanning the intact nose.
!!
## The stages
1. **First operation:** sew the flap in with its middle bridging the intact skin, where you can watch it. If the pedicle is twisted and the flap is poorly perfused, detach it and return it to the forehead; re-rotate it 5 to 7 days later, when the delay phenomenon has improved its blood supply.
2. **Second operation (optional):** raise the flap from the defect, thin it to match, and place or thin cartilage grafts.
3. **Third operation:** divide the pedicle and inset the proximal edge to complete the closure.
A **single-stage island flap**, its pedicle de-epithelialised and tunnelled under the skin, avoids staging but risks compressing the artery and vein in the tunnel.
!see What you should see
- A pink paddle with brisk capillary refill at each stage, and bleeding from its edge when it is thinned.
!!
## The donor site
Close it **primarily** if the flap is narrow or was expanded. Otherwise let it heal by **secondary intention**, kept moist with antibiotic ointment or petroleum jelly, which usually leaves a very acceptable scar. Skin grafts are not routinely used.
!see What you should see
- A forehead closed without lifting the brow, or a clean, moist wound left to granulate.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Poor perfusion or heavy congestion
Look | many flaps look slightly blue after rotation; this settles in 2 to 3 days
Excessive congestion or doubtful perfusion | back to theatre: return the flap to its native position
Later | re-rotate after 5 to 7 days of delay
!!
## Common errors in technique
!pit
- A flap too short: measure, re-measure, allow for rotation.
- An inset under tension.
- Carrying frontal hair onto the nose.
!!

# Aftercare | Wait for the new blood supply.
## After the operation
Usually a day case; an overnight stay for oozing under the bridge, pain or comorbidity. **Divide the pedicle only after neovascularisation**, usually safely after **3 weeks**. Refinements such as dermabrasion or debulking come after final healing at **6 to 12 months**.
## Complications
!table Complications
Complication | Notes
Partial flap loss | Arterial insufficiency or venous congestion
Haematoma, infection | Early
Poor cosmesis | Scarring, notching, colour mismatch, brow malposition, hair on the nose
Donor site | Slow healing, scalp numbness, hairline distortion
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## When to divide the pedicle
The accepted standard is **at least 3 weeks**, longer in smokers and in hypertension or diabetes, while the flap gains new vessels from the recipient bed. Indocyanine green angiography studies suggest neovascularisation by 1 to 2 weeks and support earlier division to reduce deformity. In clinic, gently compress the pedicle: if the distal flap stays pink, proceed; if capillary refill is doubtful, wait. The editor adds that tissue expansion widens and lengthens the flap but expanded skin may contract, and expansion can also reduce wide donor site scars later.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Not checking for old forehead scars or radiotherapy.
- Reconstructing only skin when lining and cartilage are missing.
- Dividing the pedicle early in a smoker.
