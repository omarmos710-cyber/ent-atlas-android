@stand Bone in the ear canal is common and usually harmless. Exostoses are the cold-water swimmer's bilateral, broad-based bony swellings deep in the canal; an osteoma is a single, pedicled true tumour further out. Neither needs surgery until it traps wax and skin, causes repeated otitis externa or blocks the hearing. When you do operate, the whole skill lies in saving the canal skin and not touching the drum, the malleus, the jaw joint or the facial nerve, which here can lie lateral to the annulus.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Canalplasty for exostoses and osteoma
 | Summary
Indications | Complete or near-complete occlusion with trapped debris that cannot be cleaned in clinic; conductive loss from occlusion or impaction; recurrent otitis externa despite medical treatment; frequent impactions needing repeated visits
Contraindications | Active otitis externa (treat first); unfitness for general anaesthesia (a narrow-pedicled osteoma can be removed under local)
Anaesthesia | General for exostoses and the postauricular route; local, with or without sedation, for a lateral, narrow-pedicled osteoma
Position | Supine, head on a ring turned away; protect the other ear
Antibiotics | None unless the canal is infected (then an antipseudomonal agent)
Monitoring | Facial nerve monitoring strongly advised
Tray | Microscope, ear set, high-speed drill with 3, 2 and 1 mm coarse and fine diamond burrs, 1 mm osteotome, suction irrigation, Silastic sheet or foil from a suture pack, Gelfoam, rosebud packing
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Hearing:** a small risk of conductive loss (drum or ossicle injury) and of sensorineural loss from drill noise or contact with the malleus.
- **Perforation** of the eardrum, usually small and repaired at the time.
- **Narrowing of the canal** as it heals, which may need further treatment.
- **Facial weakness,** rare.
- **Jaw pain** if the joint capsule is breached.
- **Recurrence** of exostoses with continued cold-water exposure: wear earplugs.
!!
## Before you start
Ask about wax impaction, otitis externa and hearing, how often the patient needs clinic cleaning, and whether they surf, swim or kayak in cold water. Examine both ears under the microscope and estimate how much of the canal's cross-section is blocked; an endoscope can show the medial canal. Record an audiogram with tympanometry and reflexes: a pre-existing high-frequency sensorineural loss must be documented before drilling. Obtain a high-resolution temporal bone CT for an osteoma that hides the medial canal, or for near-complete exostoses, to look for cholesteatoma trapped behind the obstruction, to define the pedicle and to see how close bone lies to the drum. Treat active otitis externa with drops before the operation, and stop anticoagulants if safe.

# Know the disease | Exostosis or osteoma?
## Telling them apart
!table Exostoses and osteoma compared
 | Exostoses | Osteoma
Number and side | Multiple, usually bilateral | Single, unilateral
Site | Medial bony canal, near the drum | Lateral bony canal, at the tympanosquamous or tympanomastoid suture
Base | Broad, sessile, anterior and posterior | Narrow pedicle
Nature | Reactive lamellar bone after cold-water exposure | True benign neoplasm of bone
Typical patient | Surfer, swimmer, kayaker, male | Any adult
Usual surgery | Postauricular drill or osteotome canalplasty | Transcanal removal with a curette or osteotome
!!
## When to operate
Most lesions are found incidentally. Under about 80% occlusion, regular cleaning and earplugs in cold water are usually enough. Operate for symptoms, for impractical repeated cleaning, or for complete occlusion, which risks cholesteatoma behind the bone.

# The operation, stage by stage | Postauricular canalplasty.
## Prepare and choose the approach
Place the facial nerve electrodes, prepare and drape as for any ear operation, and inject all four quadrants of the canal with lidocaine and adrenaline. Choose the route: transcanal or endaural for a single lateral osteoma; postauricular for extensive medial exostoses, where a transcanal view is too restricted.
!see What you should see
- Blanched canal skin after the four-quadrant injection, and a working facial nerve monitor.
!!
## Raise the canal flap and open behind the ear
Make a vertical incision in the posterior lateral bony canal with releasing cuts at 6 and 12 o'clock to create a laterally based (Koerner's) flap. Make the postauricular incision, join the two, retract the pinna forward with tape and place self-retaining retractors.
!mf 5 | The laterally based Koerner's flap is cut through the meatus.
!mf 6 | The postauricular incision joined to the canal incision, with the pinna retracted forward.
!see What you should see
- The full length of the bony canal from behind, with the exostoses in view.
!!
## Preserve the skin
For each exostosis make a circumferential incision lateral to it with vertical releasing cuts, and elevate a *medially based* skin flap over the bone towards the drum. Fold the flaps over the drum and protect them, and the drum, with Silastic sheeting or foil from a suture pack.
!mf 7 | The posterior canal skin is elevated medially and protected under foil before drilling.
!see What you should see
- Intact medially based flaps tucked safely over the drum, and bare bone of each exostosis exposed.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
Skin is the operation. Every millimetre of canal skin you keep is a millimetre of canal that will not scar down. Elevate patiently and protect the flaps before the drill comes near.
!!
## Remove the bone
Drill from lateral to medial with coarse diamond burrs of decreasing size (3, 2, 1 mm) and copious irrigation, then polish with a fine diamond. Near the annulus, thin the bone and lift off the last lip with a 1 mm osteotome, or undermine it and fracture it gently away from the drum. Aim for a smooth, straight canal in which the whole annulus can be seen from the meatus.
!see What you should see
- A widened, smooth canal with the anterior annulus visible and no remaining medial lip of bone.
!!
!danger Five structures in the way
- **The drum and annulus:** the drill can tear the drum and the annulus can be avulsed.
- **The malleus:** touching the short process with the drill conducts noise to the cochlea and can disrupt the chain.
- **The attic:** drilling too far up the scutum opens the epitympanum.
- **The jaw joint:** anteriorly, halfway along the bony canal; stop if you see glenoid periosteum.
- **The facial nerve:** in about 70% of people its vertical segment lies lateral to the annulus in the posteroinferior canal, 2 to 5 mm away, with no surface landmark.
!!
## Replace the skin and pack
Lay the flaps back in position. Cover bare areas with a thin free skin graft from behind the ear if needed. Pack with Gelfoam soaked in antibiotic drops if no grafts were used; if grafts were used, place medial and lateral rosebud packs to hold them against the bone.
!mf 8 9 | Bare areas are grafted, and double rosebud packing holds the skin in place.
!see What you should see
- Skin lining the whole canal, held flat against the bone, with the drum intact.
!!
## Osteoma by the transcanal route
A narrow-pedicled osteoma can often be removed through the meatus with a curette or osteotome, sometimes after a small incision in the incisura to widen the meatus. For a broad-based osteoma, raise and preserve a skin flap as for exostoses and drill the base flush with the surrounding canal. A small bare area heals by secondary intention.

!mf 11 | A broad-based osteoma: skin flap, flush drilling of the base, and replacement of the skin.
!see What you should see
- The osteoma removed whole with its pedicle, and smooth bone where it was attached.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Drum torn during drilling
Small tear | support medially with Gelfoam ; it usually heals
Large tear | repair with fascia or perichondrium at the same operation
!!
!path Glenoid periosteum or jaw joint exposed
Stop drilling there | leave a thin layer of bone ; do not chase exostosis further forward
After | warn of jaw pain ; soft diet
!!
!path Facial monitor fires in the posteroinferior canal
Stop | irrigate ; reassess the depth
Proceed | change to a smaller diamond burr ; thin rather than remove the last bone there
!!

# Aftercare | Keep it dry and watch the skin.
## On the ward and in clinic
Start antibiotic drops twice daily. Keep the postauricular wound dry for a week, then protect the canal with Vaseline-coated cotton wool when washing. Remove packs at 7 to 10 days, or at 14 days if free grafts were used. Treat granulations with silver nitrate and hypertrophic scar with steroid injection, and keep a stent in the canal with weekly review until it is fully epithelialised. Repeat the audiogram after healing. Return to water sports only once healed, and always with earplugs.
## Complications
Incomplete removal and early recurrence; soft tissue stenosis; drum perforation; conductive loss from ossicular injury; sensorineural loss from drill noise or malleus contact; a mastoid fistula from drilling too deep posteriorly; jaw pain; facial palsy.
!update Current evidence
**Drill or osteotome.** A systematic review and meta-analysis of 15 studies (1,788 ears) found drum perforation in 5.3% with the osteotome and 3.8% with the drill, but sensorineural loss in 0.7% against 4.3% and canal stenosis in 1.1% against 4.1%: the osteotome may risk more perforations, the drill more inner ear injury and stenosis (Swisher et al., *Ann Otol Rhinol Laryngol* 2023). Endoscopic transcanal osteotome removal is an increasingly used, less invasive option (Kozin et al., *Am J Otolaryngol* 2015).

**Osteoma pedicle.** In a meta-analysis of 63 osteomas, drilling the stalk site after removal did not reduce recurrence (about 9% with drilling against 5% without; overall about 7%); avoid it unless a definite pedicle still projects into the canal (Argaman et al., *Eur Arch Otorhinolaryngol* 2024).

**Who gets them.** Exostoses relate to cumulative cold-water exposure: in 207 English surfers, 53% had exostoses, with severity rising with years surfed (Alexander et al., *Eur Arch Otorhinolaryngol* 2015).
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Myers: postauricular drill canalplasty with medially based skin flaps for exostoses; transcanal curette or osteotome removal for pedicled osteomas; facial nerve monitoring strongly advised.
Dhingra and K. J. Lee: describe the clinical difference between exostoses and osteoma and advise surgery only for obstruction, infection or hearing loss.
The literature: the osteotome trades a slightly higher perforation rate for less sensorineural loss and stenosis than the drill.
!!
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Exostosis versus osteoma:** number, site, base, cause.
- **Indications** for surgery and why complete occlusion is dangerous (trapped cholesteatoma).
- The five **structures at risk**, especially the facial nerve lateral to the annulus.
- **Skin preservation** with medially based flaps.
- **Drill or osteotome:** the trade-off in complications.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Attempting extensive medial exostoses through the canal.
- Drilling without protecting the drum and skin flaps.
- Touching the short process of the malleus with the burr.
- Drilling anteriorly into the jaw joint.
- Letting a patient return to cold water without earplugs.
!!
