@stand Cancer of the floor of the mouth makes up about a third of oral cavity cancers, over 95% of them squamous, in older men who smoke and drink. It is one of the most aggressive oral cancers: occult nodal disease is found in up to 30% even of early tumours, and the mandible and tongue lie so close that a small cancer quickly reaches them. Its surgery is a ladder: wide soft tissue excision when the cancer is clear of bone; marginal mandibulectomy when it reaches the periosteum; segmental mandibulectomy when it enters the marrow; a pull-through or mandibulotomy for extensive soft tissue disease. Each rung changes the reconstruction, and the airway, which caused half the postoperative deaths in one large series, must be planned at every stage. This chapter covers assessment of the mandible by palpation and imaging, the four operations, the duct, the skin graft and flaps, and the management of the clinically negative neck.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Excision of floor of mouth cancer
 | Summary
Soft tissue excision only | Cancer not approaching or involving the mandible
Marginal mandibulectomy | Cancer on the periosteum but not the cortex; close to the periosteum without bone invasion; abutting healthy teeth without periodontal ligament involvement
Not marginal | Bone invasion; gross marrow invasion; recurrence after radiotherapy; a hypoplastic mandible; less than **1 cm** of bone would remain
Segmental mandibulectomy | Marrow invasion; cancer reaching the occlusal surface of an edentulous mandible; an edentulous hypoplastic mandible that would otherwise suit a marginal; cancer abutting diseased teeth with periodontal ligament involvement; to improve exposure
Mandibulotomy | Better access to anterior cancers; not in an **atrophic** mandible
Airway | Nasal intubation; tracheostomy for extensive resections and flaps
Position | Table turned 180 degrees, shoulder roll, foam head ring; prepare neck, lower face and donor sites
Antibiotics | Ampicillin-sulbactam (clindamycin in allergy) at the start, every 6 hours, and for 24 hours

## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Why:** to remove the cancer under the tongue with a margin of healthy tissue, usually with a neck operation.
- **Bone:** part of the jawbone may need removing, either a strip along the top or a full segment, rebuilt with bone from the leg or back.
- **Reconstruction:** a skin graft, or a flap from the neck, chest or forearm.
- **A temporary tracheostomy** and feeding tube for larger operations.
- **Changes to speech and swallowing**; dentures may need implants.
- **Numbness or weakness of the tongue** (lingual and hypoglossal nerves), numbness of the chin.
- **A leak of saliva** to the neck, bleeding, infection, a fracture or failure of the jaw to heal.
- **Radiotherapy** may follow.
!!
## Before you start
Age itself is not a contraindication, but comorbidity predicts survival, so involve physicians, oncologists, speech and swallowing therapists, dietitians and anaesthetists. Ask about the lesion (size, onset, growth, pain), an **ill-fitting lower denture**, speech, swallowing and chewing, signs of perineural spread (a stiff tongue, taste change, numbness), odynophagia, dysphagia, otalgia, bleeding, weight loss and a recent biopsy. Record leukoplakia, dysplasia or lichen planus, sleep apnoea, heart and lung disease, radiotherapy, previous head and neck cancer and treatment to the site or neck, immunosuppression, previous oral, neck or dental surgery, a fixed mandibular fracture, a previous tracheostomy, anaesthetic or bleeding problems in the family, anticoagulants, herbal products, immunosuppressants and allergies; alcohol, tobacco and **betel quid**; occupation (especially cooks, and those who speak for a living), support, and whether the patient is a Jehovah's Witness.
!mf 1 | Squamous cell carcinoma of the floor of the mouth is strongly linked to tobacco and alcohol.
## Examination
Malnutrition and cachexia affect half of head and neck cancer patients and independently predict survival. Describe the cancer (exophytic, deeply infiltrating, ulcerated, papillary), its extent toward the tongue muscles (midline or lateral, anterior or lateral), tongue mobility, diameter for staging, its relation to the **duct papilla**, and the teeth.
!senior Feel the mandible
**Bimanual palpation** of the cancer against the mandible predicts cortical invasion better than imaging. Feel how close it is to the lingual periosteum, whether it has breached the cortex, reached the alveolus or buccal surface, or the skin; look for a pathological fracture, and judge mandibular height and thickness and any old plates.
!!
!mf 2 | (A) An advanced floor of mouth cancer; (B) invading the mandible and the skin of the chin.
Look for perineural spread (mental numbness, tongue numbness, taste change, reduced tongue movement) and test cranial nerves II to XII. Palpate both necks, note radiation fibrosis and old incisions, and look for a synchronous primary (a unilateral middle ear effusion, stridor, dysphonia). Assess donor sites: fibula, radial forearm, supraclavicular, pectoralis.
!mf 3 | A patient referred for laryngectomy in whom routine examination found an early floor of mouth cancer, excised and grafted at the same operation.
## Imaging and preparation
Contrast CT is first-line, with fine-cut multiplanar images for the mandibular cortex; MRI is rarely needed; an orthopantomogram for teeth and bone; chest CT for metastases and a lung primary; PET-CT is more accurate for nodes and distant disease but too costly as a routine screen. Arrange ultrasound-guided FNA of suspicious nodes, biopsy of the primary (and review of outside slides), panendoscopy (can be at the operation), extraction of carious teeth (also at the operation), nutritional bloods (albumin, half-life about 20 days; prealbumin, about 8 days) and haemoglobin where relevant, and a general surgeon if a feeding tube is planned.
!danger The airway
In Shaha and colleagues' review of 320 floor of mouth cancers, **half the postoperative deaths were from airway compromise**. Agree the airway plan with the anaesthetist for induction and for the days after surgery.
!!

# Anatomy you must see | The floor and its neighbours.
## Landmarks
- **Mylohyoid:** the diaphragm of the floor of mouth.
- **Extrinsic tongue muscles:** hyoglossus and geniohyoid.
- **The submandibular and sublingual glands**, and the duct opening (papilla).
- **The lingual, hypoglossal and mental nerves.**
- **The lingual artery.**
- **The mandible.**

# The operation, stage by stage | Four operations.
## Neck first
Do the neck dissection first.
!see What you should see
- The neck cleared, with the lingual and hypoglossal nerves and the submandibular duct identified from below.
!!
## Soft tissue resection: plan by palpation
Place a bite block and a plastic cheek retractor, and a 2-0 silk through the tongue tip for traction. **Palpate bimanually** to confirm the stage and plan. Mark at least a **1 cm** margin of normal mucosa, then inject 1% lidocaine with 1:100,000 adrenaline along the line (after palpating, not before). If the submandibular gland is staying, find the opening of Wharton's duct; if the papilla is within the resection, cannulate the duct with a lacrimal probe to help reroute it.
!see What you should see
- The limits of induration between your fingers, whether the tumour moves on the mandible, and a marked 1 cm margin.
!!
## Excise
Incise with a needle-tip diathermy, mark the anterior edge of the specimen with a 2-0 silk, and dissect from front to back. The deep margin lies at the level of the **sublingual glands**. Remove the specimen and send it for frozen section. Control bleeding by ligation or **bipolar** diathermy, not monopolar.
!mf 4 | (A) A 2 × 2 cm ulcerated exophytic cancer; (B) the specimen removed and haemostasis secured; (C) a split skin graft with long silk tie-overs for a Xeroform bolster; (D) the healed graft; (E) full dentures fitted over it.
!see What you should see
- The sublingual gland in the deep margin, the lingual nerve, and a specimen marked for orientation.
!!
## The duct
If the gland goes with the neck dissection, the duct is not reconstructed. If the gland stays and its duct is within the resection, **reroute** it: follow the probe, sew the cut end to the remaining floor of mouth mucosa, open the duct along 8 to 10 mm, bring it up to the mucosa and marsupialise it with 4-0 chromic.
!see What you should see
- A probe in the duct and saliva flowing from its new opening.
!!
## Graft and finish
Resurface with a **split skin graft**, held by a Xeroform bolster tied with 2-0 silk. Empty the stomach with an orogastric tube and pass a fine-bore feeding tube, fixed to the septum with 3-0 silk.
!see What you should see
- The graft flat against the bed under the bolster, with no blood beneath it.
!!
## Soft tissue resection with marginal mandibulectomy
1. Mark the mucosal margins; the bony margins should include **1 to 1.5 cm** of normal bone laterally. Resect bone and soft tissue **en bloc** to orient the pathologist.
2. In the dentate, extract the tooth next to the planned cut and make the vertical cut **in the front of the socket**, keeping as much bone as possible to support the next tooth.
3. Incise with a needle tip through mucosa and periosteum onto bone, across the alveolus and joined in front; lift the mucoperiosteum with a Freer.
4. Draw the cuts on the bone. Make the vertical cuts with a reciprocating saw, taking only the alveolar process and leaving at least **1 cm** of bone along the lower border; join them with a horizontal cut.
5. Cut right through **both the buccal and lingual plates**, or the bone splinters or fractures.
6. Lift the freed bone up and back with the soft tissue, and complete the resection.
7. Smooth the bone edges with a cutting bur. Reconstruct with a skin graft over soft tissue and bone, a submental or supraclavicular island flap, or a radial forearm flap. Orogastric tube, feeding tube.
!mf 5 | (A) A second primary floor of mouth cancer 25 years after a first, previously reconstructed with a deltopectoral flap; (B) the old donor site; (C) the floor rebuilt with a deltopectoral flap (left) and a split skin graft (right).
!mf 6 | (A) Recurrent cancer behind an old skin graft, excised en bloc with an anterior marginal mandibulectomy; (B) a radial forearm flap being raised; (C) the soft, pliable flap does not restrict the tongue.
!see What you should see
- An en bloc specimen, a lower rim of mandible at least 1 cm high, and smooth cut edges.
!!
!danger The remaining mandible
Leaving less than 1 cm of bone below a marginal mandibulectomy invites a pathological fracture.
!!
## Extensive soft tissue cancer without bone involvement
Make a tracheostomy and dissect both necks. Then choose:
- **Pull-through:** set the lateral margins with a needle tip and sharp dissection; at the level of the sublingual glands, approach the cancer from the neck: raise a subplatysmal flap to the lower border of the mandible, divide mylohyoid and digastric to enter the deep floor of mouth, deliver the floor of mouth into the neck and resect. The hypoglossal and lingual nerves, already found in the neck, can be kept if clearance allows. Reconstruct with pectoralis major, radial forearm or supraclavicular flap.
- **Mandibulotomy**, through a **visor flap** (raise the upper cervical flap to the mandible, incise the gingivobuccal sulcus 1 cm from the bone without stripping the anterior periosteum or injuring the mental nerves, and join them) or a **lip split** with a notch in the midline of the lower lip for camouflage, carried down to the neck incision. Pre-bend a plate to the native mandible, then make a midline mandibulotomy through both tables with an oscillating saw to expose and remove the cancer. Reconstruct with the plate and a skin graft or flap.
!see What you should see
- The floor of mouth delivered into the neck with the hypoglossal and lingual nerves in view.
!!
## Segmental mandibulectomy
Use a visor flap or lip split; if the cancer has destroyed the anterior mandible and infiltrates the chin skin, take the skin with it. Extract teeth at the osteotomy sites, make vertical cuts **at least 1 cm** from the tumour with a reciprocating saw at the front of the extraction sockets, free the periosteum, and resect cancer, bone and skin en bloc. Rebuild with a fibula or scapula flap.
!mf 7 | (A) Floor of mouth cancer destroying the anterior mandible and chin skin; (B) a bivalved scapular osteocutaneous flap replacing the bone; (C) one skin paddle for the floor of mouth; (D) the other for the chin.
!see What you should see
- Bone cuts at least 1 cm clear of tumour, and the condyles held in their preoperative position by the plate.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Swelling in the neck or mouth after surgery
Recognise | intraoral or neck swelling, skin colour change: a haematoma
Act | the airway comes first
!!
!path Saliva in the drain
Recognise | an orocutaneous fistula: watch the drain output and the suture lines
!!
## Common errors in technique
!pit
- Not orienting the specimen.
- An overaggressive marginal mandibulectomy leaving under 1 cm of bone.
- Not cutting fully through both plates, so the remaining bone splinters or fractures.
- Placing the vertical cut at the back of the socket, leaving the next tooth unsupported.
!!

# Aftercare | Bolster, rinses, feeding.
## After a skin graft or local flap
Remove the bolster on day 5; 15 mL oral rinse four times daily for a week; trickle nasogastric feeds from day 1; **clear fluids at 1 week** if not irradiated, at **2 weeks** if irradiated; remove the drain when under 30 mL in 24 hours; speech and swallowing review; analgesia. Once healed, full dentures can be made; after a marginal mandibulectomy, implants may be needed to hold them.
## After a free flap
Intensive care for 48 hours with hourly flap checks, routine tracheostomy care, a week of clear fluids then 6 weeks of soft diet, and the drain out when under 30 mL a day.
## Complications
!table Complications
Complication | Watch for
Orocutaneous fistula | Drain output, the suture line and skin
Haematoma | Swelling in the mouth or neck, skin colour
Wound infection | Pus in the drain, red or discharging wound
Systemic infection | Vital signs
Hypoglossal injury | Tongue movement to the midline and sides
Lingual nerve injury | Tongue sensation
Pathological fracture, non-union | Bimanual palpation; orthopantomogram if suspected
!!
## Alternatives
Radiotherapy, chemoradiotherapy, brachytherapy.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## The clinically negative neck in early cancer
T3 and T4 tumours get bilateral neck dissections; T1 and T2 with clinical or radiological nodes get an ipsilateral dissection. For T1 to T2 N0 there are three options. **Watchful waiting:** about 70% never need treatment, but 30% develop nodes and only about half of those are salvaged. **Sentinel node biopsy:** promising elsewhere in the mouth, but in the floor of mouth Ross and colleagues found sensitivity of only **80%** (near 100% at other subsites) and neck disease in nearly 4% within 2 years. **Elective neck dissection:** more cost and morbidity, but a randomised trial of T1 to T2 oral cancer showed a **23.6% survival benefit**, with **eight** patients treated to prevent one death.
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- The epidemiology and the 30% occult nodal rate.
- Bimanual palpation as the best test of cortical invasion.
- The ladder: soft tissue, marginal, segmental, mandibulotomy, and their indications and contraindications.
- 1 cm mucosal margins; 1 to 1.5 cm of bone; at least 1 cm of bone left below a marginal.
- Rerouting Wharton's duct.
- The airway: half of deaths.
- The cN0 neck options and the evidence.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Injecting local anaesthetic before palpating and marking.
- Using monopolar diathermy for haemostasis in the floor of mouth.
- Missing cortical invasion and then improvising the reconstruction.
- A mandibulotomy through an atrophic mandible.
!!
