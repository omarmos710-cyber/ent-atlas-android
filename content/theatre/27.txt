@stand Lip cancer is usually a squamous carcinoma of the sun-damaged lower lip in an outdoor worker, and usually caught early. Excision with clear margins is straightforward; the art is the reconstruction, because the lip is a sphincter that must open wide enough to eat and close tightly enough not to drool. Up to a third of the lip closes directly; larger defects borrow lip from the other lip or the cheeks, choosing between a functional sphincter and an adequate mouth opening.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Excision and reconstruction of lip cancer
 | Summary
Indications | Biopsy-proven malignancy of the lip; neck dissection or sentinel node biopsy for risk factors (thick tumours, upper lip, commissure, recurrence); therapeutic neck dissection for nodes
Margins | Full-thickness excision with clear margins (frozen sections help); vermilionectomy (lip shave) for field change or in situ disease
Reconstruction by defect | Under about a third (up to half): direct closure (V or W wedge). A third to two-thirds: advancement flaps, Abbe (lip switch) or Estlander (commissure) flap, Karapandzic flap. Over two-thirds: Bernard-Burow (Webster) cheek advancement, Gillies fan flap, free flap (radial forearm with tendon sling)
Anaesthesia | Local for small wedges; general for flaps and neck surgery
Tray | Fine plastic instruments, marking pen, 6-0 sutures for vermilion, bipolar
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **A scar,** and a smaller mouth (**microstomia**), especially after large reconstructions.
- **Drooling** if the sphincter is weak.
- **Numbness** of the lip and chin.
- Abbe flap: a **two-stage** operation, with the lips joined for 2 to 3 weeks.
- **Recurrence,** and neck treatment if nodes are involved.
!!
## Before you start
Mark the vermilion border before injecting (adrenaline blanches it and hides the line). Palpate the neck (lower lip drains to submental and submandibular nodes, crossing the midline; the upper lip to submandibular, parotid and preauricular nodes).
!mf 1 | Lymphatic drainage of the lip.

# The operation, stage by stage | Excise, then rebuild the sphincter.
## Excise
Excise the tumour full thickness with clear margins: a V or W-shaped wedge for small lower lip lesions (the W keeps the scar above the mental crease), a rectangle when an advancement flap will close it. Send margins for frozen section.
!mf 2 | A wedge excision of a lower lip lesion and its closure.
!mf 7 | A large lip cancer marked with frozen section margins.
!see What you should see
- A full-thickness defect with clear margins and the labial arteries controlled.
!!
## Close a small defect in three layers
Align the vermilion border first with a single exact suture, then close mucosa, orbicularis oris (to restore the sphincter) and skin with everting sutures.
!see What you should see
- A continuous vermilion line without a step and a muscle layer closed under the skin.
!!
!danger The white roll
A step of 1 mm in the vermilion border is visible at conversational distance. Mark it before injecting and align it first.
!!
## Medium defects: borrow from the other lip
**Abbe flap:** a full-thickness wedge from the opposite lip, about half the width of the defect, pedicled on the labial artery near the vermilion, is rotated into the defect; the pedicle is divided at 2 to 3 weeks. **Estlander flap:** the same idea for a defect at the commissure, rotated around it in one stage (it blunts the commissure). **Karapandzic flap:** circumoral incisions that rotate and advance the remaining lip with its nerves and vessels preserved, keeping a working sphincter at the cost of a smaller mouth.
!mf 4 | An Abbe flap for an extensive upper lip cancer.
!mf 5 6 | Reconstruction of lower lip and commissure defects.
!see What you should see
- A flap with its labial artery pedicle visible and the orbicularis repaired end to end.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
Innervated muscle matters more than appearance. Flaps that keep the orbicularis with its nerve supply (Karapandzic) give a competent mouth; adynamic flaps and free flaps fill the gap but drool.
!!
## Large defects
For more than about two-thirds of the lip, use bilateral cheek advancement (Bernard-Burow or Webster), fan flaps, or a free radial forearm flap with a palmaris longus tendon sling. Reconstruct the vermilion with a mucosal advancement.
!see What you should see
- A closed oral sphincter with an opening wide enough for a spoon and, ideally, dentures.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills

!!

Act | release tight sutures ; leech or pinprick bleeding ; avoid avulsing the pedicle
!!

# Aftercare | Eat with care.
## After surgery
Soft diet, mouth care, protect the Abbe pedicle (no wide opening); divide it at 2 to 3 weeks. Commissuroplasty later for microstomia if needed. Sun protection for life.
## Complications
Bleeding, infection, wound breakdown, microstomia, oral incompetence and drooling, numbness, notching of the vermilion, recurrence, nodal disease.
!update Current evidence
Lip and oral cavity cancer remains a major global burden, with ultraviolet exposure and tobacco the main preventable causes of lip cancer (GBD 2019 Lip, Oral, and Pharyngeal Cancer Collaborators, *JAMA Oncol* 2023). Evidence comparing reconstructive techniques is from case series; the choice rests on defect size and preserving an innervated sphincter.
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Myers: direct closure under half the lip, advancement and Abbe flaps for a half to two-thirds, larger flaps and free tissue above that, with neck treatment for risk factors.
K. J. Lee: the classic thresholds (a third, two-thirds), Estlander and Karapandzic flaps, and nodal drainage by lip.
!!
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Risk factors** and nodal drainage.
- **Reconstruction by defect size** and the named flaps.
- **Vermilion alignment.**
- **Sphincter function** versus mouth opening.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Injecting before marking the vermilion border.
- Closing skin without repairing the orbicularis.
- Reconstructions that leave a tiny mouth.
- Ignoring the neck in high-risk tumours.
!!
