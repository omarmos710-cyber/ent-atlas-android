@stand A bent septum is the commonest structural reason a nose stays blocked after decongestants and medical treatment have been tried. Septoplasty is often the first nasal operation a resident does, and it looks simple, but it humbles surgeons who treat it as routine: the deviation can be cartilage, bone or both; it can tilt, curve, spur, twist or buckle; and it can sit in a nose that is straight, crooked or collapsing, with or without trouble at the nasal valves. No single operation fixes every septum. This chapter teaches you to examine the nose so you recognise the septum that a standard septoplasty will not correct, then takes you through the classic endonasal operation, endoscopic septoplasty and spur removal, and the complications you must prevent.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Septoplasty
 | Summary
Indications | Symptomatic nasal obstruction with a deviated septum; access to the sinuses for endoscopic sinus surgery; access to the sphenoid and pituitary; access for epistaxis behind a deviation; access for endoscopic dacryocystorhinostomy
Contraindications | Unfit for general anaesthesia; a bleeding tendency or anticoagulation that cannot be stopped around the operation; acute nasal or sinus infection
Anaesthesia | General, with an oral RAE tube resting in the midline; local with topical anaesthesia is possible
Position | Supine; for endoscopic work, head up 20 to 30 degrees and tilted 15 degrees toward the surgeon, with surgeon, patient and screen in one line
Antibiotics | First-generation cephalosporin; clindamycin or gentamicin if allergic to penicillin
Monitoring | Standard anaesthetic monitoring; a heart rate around 60 and mean arterial pressure around 60 mm Hg reduce bleeding where safe
Imaging | Not needed routinely; CT only if another cause of obstruction or sinus disease is suspected
Tray | Basic septorhinoplasty set (speculums, Woodson and Cottle elevators, double-action scissors and rongeurs, swivel knife, 4 to 6 mm chisel or osteotome, mallet); 0- and 30-degree telescopes, camera and tower for the endoscopic technique; Doyle splints; 4-0 and 5-0 plain gut, 3-0 Prolene
Before theatre | Stop antiplatelets and herbal supplements; clotting tests if there is a bleeding history; medical clearance where needed; a trial of topical steroid if allergy is suspected
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **What it does:** straightens the partition inside the nose to improve breathing; it is done through the nostril and leaves no outside scar.
- **What it does not do:** it will not cure allergy or rhinitis, and it will not change the shape of the nose unless a separate rhinoplasty is planned.
- **Bleeding**, usually minor; occasionally packing or a return to theatre.
- **A blood clot or infection between the layers of the septum**, which must be drained quickly.
- **A hole in the septum** (perforation), which can whistle, crust or bleed.
- **A change in the shape of the nose**: a dip above the tip if support is lost; uncommon.
- **The blockage may persist** or recur, and further surgery may be needed.
- **Rare:** a leak of brain fluid, loss of smell (about one in a million), toxic shock syndrome.
- **Splints** stay in for about a week; numbness of the upper front teeth can last weeks.
!!
## Before you start
### The history
Ask which symptom bothers the patient most, because that guides what you promise: blockage, reduced smell, bleeding, facial pressure, discharge or postnasal drip. How long has it been there, and is it worse on one side? (An S-shaped septum blocks both sides.) What happened with steroid sprays, decongestants and external nasal dilator strips? Ask about allergy, previous nasal surgery (cosmetic or functional: it raises the chance of weak cartilage support and of scarred flaps that tear), previous nasal trauma, medical illness, antiplatelets, herbal products, alcohol and smoking.
### The examination
- **Watch the patient breathe** quietly and then sniff firmly: does the side wall collapse inwards (static or dynamic valve collapse)?
- **Anterior rhinoscopy:** describe the deviation (tilt, curve, angle, spur, groove, where it lies), the position of the caudal septum, and whether the angle of the internal valve is narrowed.
- **Palpate the caudal septum:** its position, strength, angle and relationship to the dorsal strut.
- **Endoscopy in three passes:** along the floor (inferior turbinate and meatus, nasolacrimal duct opening, nasopharynx with the fossa of Rosenmüller and tubal opening); toward the middle meatus (bulla, uncinate, sphenoethmoidal recess, superior turbinate, sphenoid ostium); and up to the olfactory cleft with a 30-degree scope. Look for polyps, masses and adenoids, and record any existing perforation.
!flag Four questions before you book a routine septoplasty
- Does the **nasal valve** narrow or collapse?
- Is the **dorsal** part of the septum bent?
- Is the **caudal** end of the septum off the midline?
- Does the deformity involve cartilage that holds up the dorsum or tip, so that it must be rebuilt, not just removed?
A yes to any of these means a standard excisional septoplasty may fail; consider a functional septorhinoplasty, usually by an external approach, or refer to a surgeon who does it.
!!

# Anatomy you must see | The framework and the plane.
## The septal framework
- **Quadrangular cartilage** in front, sitting on the **maxillary crest** and the **anterior nasal spine** below.
- **Perpendicular plate of the ethmoid** above and behind, joined to the cribriform plate at the skull base.
- **Vomer** below and behind.
- The **keystone area** at the rhinion, where the cartilage meets the nasal bones and the perpendicular plate; separating the cartilage here drops the bridge of the nose.
## The plane and the struts
- The correct plane is **beneath the perichondrium** over cartilage and **beneath the periosteum** over bone: the cartilage appears pale blue and the dissection is quick and almost bloodless.
- At least **1 cm of dorsal strut and 1 cm of caudal strut** (an L-shaped strut) must be preserved to hold up the dorsum and the tip.
- The **internal nasal valve** (between the septum and upper lateral cartilage) and **external valve** (the vestibule) are the narrowest, highest-resistance points of the airway: small deviations here matter far more than large ones on the floor.
!mf 4 | A caudal septum displaced off the anterior nasal spine: excising a small wedge of cartilage lets the caudal strut sit back in the midline over the spine.

# The operation: classic septoplasty | Eight stages.
## Re-examine, inject and decongest
After induction, look in the nose again and palpate the caudal septum to confirm what you found in clinic. Infiltrate both sides of the septum with 5 to 10 ml of 1% lidocaine with 1:100,000 adrenaline, then place oxymetazoline-soaked pledgets for 10 to 15 minutes.
!see What you should see
- A blanched, well-decongested septum and turbinates, and a caudal septum whose position you have felt.
!!
## The hemitransfixion incision
Expose the caudal end of the septum with a small speculum and cut along its free caudal edge with a size 15 blade, usually on the left. If the caudal septum is pushed so far into the right nostril that the left side is hard to reach, use the right. Go through the perichondrium until you see the **ice-blue cartilage**.
!mf 1 | The left hemitransfixion incision along the caudal edge of the septum.
!see What you should see
- Blue-grey cartilage with no perichondrium on it, and the cut edge of mucoperichondrium clearly separate.
!!
## Raise the flaps on both sides
Start with a Woodson elevator low in the nose under a short speculum, and switch to a Cottle elevator further back, where its flatter profile lets you see past it, using a longer Killian speculum. Raise submucoperichondrial and submucoperiosteal flaps on both sides. In the right plane this is quick and dry. Take particular care over grooves, angles and spurs, where the mucosa is thin and tears easily.
!mf 2 | The speculum holding the left flap aside: septal cartilage on one side of the elevator, the mucosal flap on the other.
!see What you should see
- Smooth white cartilage and bone under a flap that remains intact and does not bleed.
!!
!danger Opposing tears
A tear on one side is usually harmless; tears on both sides at the same point leave the septum with no cover there, and a perforation follows. Work around angles and spurs patiently, and repair any tear at the end.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
Raising both flaps does more than give you room: it releases the mucosal tension that holds the septum bent, and gives you access if you need to reconstruct. Reassess the deviation once both flaps are up; it often looks different.
!!
## Separate the cartilage from the bone
Press steadily with the Cottle elevator at the junction of the quadrangular cartilage and the perpendicular plate; the two separate. Carry the separation up to **within 1 to 1.5 cm of the dorsum**, and no further.
!mf 3 | Separating the quadrangular cartilage from the perpendicular plate (left), then the high and low bone cuts with removal of the bent segment between them (right).
!danger The keystone
Separating the cartilage all the way to the dorsum disarticulates it at the keystone and the bridge drops (a supratip depression or mild saddle).
!!
!see What you should see
- The white, rigid perpendicular plate behind the bluish-grey cartilage, and the elevator slipping through to the opposite subperiosteal tunnel with the far flap intact.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
Raise the opposite flap from behind this junction, starting on bone: the plane is easier there and the cartilage in front keeps its perichondrium on one side.
!!
## Remove the bent bone
Work from the back of the nose to the front, so that the caudal septum, which supports the tip, is dealt with last. Bent bone is rigid and holds the softer cartilage off the midline, so remove enough of it. Make the upper cut sharply with double-action scissors, high toward the dorsum, and a lower cut low over the crest, then take out the segment between them with a double-action rongeur.
!danger The cribriform plate
Never twist or pull bone out before the upper cut has been made. The perpendicular plate is attached to the cribriform plate, and avulsing it can fracture the skull base and cause a CSF leak.
!!
!see What you should see
- Clean edges of bone above and below, both flaps intact, and no bone left pushing the flaps apart at the back when you close them together.
!!
## Cartilage and the maxillary crest
If the cartilage is still off the midline, outline the segment to remove with two cuts under direct vision, one parallel to the dorsum and one parallel to the caudal edge, and **remove as little as is needed**, keeping the 1 cm dorsal and caudal struts. Straight pieces of removed cartilage can be replaced between the flaps for support, placed above the caudal strut and behind the dorsal strut so they do not overlap and obstruct. Spurs of the maxillary crest that touch the inferior turbinate, large spurs, or a crest that pushes the cartilage across should be removed with a rongeur or a 4 to 6 mm chisel after carefully raising the thin, adherent mucoperiosteum over them; small deviations low on the floor are usually harmless.
!see What you should see
- A septum that sits in the midline from the back of the nose to the front, with the dorsal and caudal struts intact.
!!
## The caudal septum
Deal with the caudal septum according to its shape:
- **Tilted off the spine, or gently bowed:** remove a small wedge of cartilage over the anterior nasal spine, no more than 2 to 3 mm, a little at a time, so the caudal strut sits **on** the spine without tension. Too little leaves the tilt or a clicking septum; too much lets the strut hang and the supratip settle.
- **Angulated, as after a fracture:** a wedge does not remove the angle. Divide the caudal strut vertically through the apex of the angle, then overlap the two segments and suture them, trying both overlap directions to see which opens the airway best; or trim the overlap, bring the ends together, and bridge them with a sutured batten graft of septal cartilage.
- **Rotated so that the caudal edge points sideways:** this needs the caudal septum removed and replaced or reconstructed, best through an external septorhinoplasty by an experienced surgeon.
!mf 5 | A gently bowed caudal septum released by shortening it a little above the spine, so that it straightens into the midline.
!mf 6 | An angulated caudal septum divided at the apex; the segments are overlapped and sutured, or set end to end with a batten graft.
!senior Senior's pearl
Caudal and dorsal deviations are the commonest reasons a septoplasty fails, because they sit at the valves where resistance is highest. If you cannot see the head of the middle turbinate easily without forcing the speculum open or pushing it deep, the internal valve is still narrow: deal with it.
!!
!see What you should see
- The caudal strut sitting in the midline on the anterior nasal spine without spring, and an L-strut at least 1 to 1.5 cm wide along the dorsum and the caudal edge.
!!
!danger The L-strut
Weaken or shorten the dorsal or caudal strut below about 1 cm and the tip droops, the columella retracts and the dorsum saddles. This cartilage is the one part of the septum that cannot be sacrificed.
!!
## Check the airway, quilt and splint
Run through the same checklist every time, from back to front: is the nose open at the back? Is it open at the internal valve? Is it open along the floor? Is the caudal septum straight and sitting in the midline without tension? Then quilt the flaps together with a running through-and-through 4-0 plain gut suture to close the dead space, and close the incision with 5-0 plain gut or through-and-through septocolumellar sutures. Place Doyle splints on both sides, secured with a through-and-through 3-0 Prolene suture.
!see What you should see
- A straight septum, both valves open, the flaps lying flat with no dead space, and splints sitting without pressure on the turbinates.
!!

# The operation: endoscopic septoplasty | Endoscopic septoplasty gives an excellent view of posterior and high deviations and suits patients who also need endoscopic sinus surgery. It is also used for isolated spurs.
## Stages
Raise the flap through the hemitransfixion incision far enough (2 to 3 cm) to admit the scope, then continue with a 0-degree telescope, raising the flaps with a Cottle elevator while keeping an instrument or the scope in the plane so that the flap is not handled repeatedly. If the caudal septum is straight, raise the flap on one side only at the front, and cross through the cartilage to the other side further back, just in front of the deviation (measure the distance from the columella with the elevator). Remove the bent cartilage with a Ballenger swivel knife or through-cutting Blakesley forceps, keeping the struts; make the upper bone cut with double-action scissors held with the jaws vertical so that you do not injure the middle turbinates.
!mf 7 | The endoscopic view with a 0-degree telescope: the mucosal flap on one side of the sucker and the quadrangular cartilage on the other.
!mf 8 | Raising the flaps on both sides with a Cottle elevator.
!see What you should see
- Once the deviation is removed, the **axilla of the middle turbinate on both sides**, which you need if frontal sinus surgery is planned.
!!
!mf 9 | The axilla of the middle turbinate is now visible, giving access to the sinuses, especially the frontal.
## Isolated spurs
Raise a small C-shaped flap in front of the spur, remove the spur with through-cutting Blakesley or Grünwald forceps, and lay the mucosa back over the defect. There is no need for large flaps.
!mf 10 | A local flap raised in front of a spur, the spur removed, and the mucosa replaced.
!see What you should see
- The spur's apex exposed on both sides before it is cut, and mucosa covering the defect at the end.
!!
!danger Opposing tears
A tear on the convex side is common and harmless if the other flap is intact; tears facing each other become a perforation. Leave or replace cartilage between them.
!!
## Closing
Close the incision with interrupted 5-0 fast-absorbing sutures and quilt the flaps with 4-0 plain gut. The quilting stitch can include the middle turbinates (a medialisation suture) to stop them drifting laterally and scarring. Place the Doyle splints under endoscopic view so that they do not push the middle turbinate laterally, trimming the upper back corner so it does not cut the medialisation suture.
!see What you should see
- Flaps lying together with no dead space, a straight septum, and both middle turbinates visible without forcing the speculum.
!!
!danger Septal haematoma
Blood between the flaps strips the cartilage of its supply; infection then melts it and the dorsum collapses. The quilting stitch is what prevents this.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Bleeding after the operation
First | oxymetazoline spray ; gentle packing with absorbable haemostatic mesh over the splints
Then | remove the splints and cauterise a bleeding point if seen
Escalate | examination and control in theatre
!!
!path Septal haematoma
Recognise | increasing pain, blockage and a swollen septum on both sides, in the days after surgery
Act | aspirate with an 18 gauge needle ; if incomplete, open, drain and quilt or pack early, with antibiotics
Why | an unrecognised haematoma kills the remaining cartilage and causes a saddle nose
!!
!path Toxic shock syndrome
Recognise | early fever, vomiting or severe diarrhoea, rash, low blood pressure, cold or ischaemic limbs
Act | remove and culture any packing or splints ; anti-staphylococcal antibiotics, fluids and close monitoring
Escalate | intensive care as needed
!!
!path Clear fluid from the nose
Recognise | clear watery discharge, especially on bending, after bone was pulled rather than cut
Act | test for beta-2 transferrin ; CT to find the defect
Treat | bed rest and observation for a small leak ; endoscopic repair if it persists
!!
!path Unexpected bleeding between the flaps at the end
Act | make a small venting stab in the posterior septal mucosa (if there is no tear) so blood can escape
Then | quilt carefully to close the dead space
!!

# Aftercare | Home the same day.
## After the operation
- Most patients go home the **same day**; those with obstructive sleep apnoea are better observed overnight.
- **Antibiotic cover against S. aureus** while the splints are in.
- **Saline rinses**, a high-volume bottle three to four times a day, keep the splints clear; then clean the nostrils with a cotton bud and apply an antibiotic ointment.
- No nose blowing or strenuous exercise for about a week; sneeze with the mouth open.
- **Splints out at 5 to 7 days.**
- Sinusitis: culture-directed antibiotics, or amoxicillin-clavulanate (doxycycline if allergic to penicillin).
## Complications
!table Complications of septoplasty
Complication | Cause or prevention
Bleeding | Usually minor; spray, packing, cautery or theatre
Septal haematoma and abscess | Dead space; prevented by quilting
Perforation | Opposing mucosal tears; repair tears at the end
Supratip depression or saddle | Separation at the keystone, or too much dorsal strut removed
Columellar retraction | Caudal strut not preserved
Persistent obstruction | Missed caudal or dorsal deviation, valve collapse, too little bone removed
Flapping septum | Too much framework removed
CSF leak | Avulsion of the perpendicular plate
Loss of smell | Very rare
Toxic shock syndrome | Staphylococcal toxin; rare with modern splints
!!
## Alternatives
Allergy assessment and treatment of rhinitis or turbinate swelling; a trial of steroid spray and antihistamine where allergy is likely. Patients with no inflammatory history and an obvious structural deformity may go straight to surgery. Severe caudal, dorsal or valve problems call for a functional septorhinoplasty. A camouflage onlay graft of crushed cartilage can hide a mild saddle afterwards. Outcomes are now measured with the validated **NOSE scale**, on which primary and revision septoplasty both show significant improvement and high patient satisfaction.

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Dhingra: separates the old **submucous resection (SMR)**, which removes nearly all the cartilage and bone through bilateral flaps leaving only dorsal and caudal struts and is not done before 17 years, from **septoplasty**, which keeps as much framework as possible, raises a full flap on one side only, can be done in children, and reshapes cartilage by scoring the concave side, cross-hatching, shaving, wedge excision and repositioning on the crest; describes the Killian incision on the concave side for most deviations and the hemitransfixion (Freer) incision for caudal dislocation; packs the nose for 24 hours; and keeps SMR today only to harvest graft material.
Lee: places septoplasty among nasal procedures for obstruction and sleep apnoea, stresses that nasal surgery does not lower the apnoea index, though it can improve sleep quality and CPAP tolerance, and that septoplasty is often part of sinus surgery for access.
Myers raises flaps on **both** sides through a hemitransfixion incision as a routine, to release tension and allow reconstruction; uses quilting sutures and Doyle splints instead of packs; gives a back-to-front intraoperative checklist; and classifies caudal deformities into tilted, angulated and rotated, with a correction for each.
!!
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- The **difference between SMR and septoplasty**, and why septoplasty replaced it.
- The **incisions**: Killian, hemitransfixion, transfixion, and a horizontal incision over a spur.
- The **L-strut**: at least 1 cm dorsal and 1 cm caudal; what happens if either is lost (supratip depression, columellar retraction).
- The **keystone area** and the danger of separating the cartilage up to the dorsum.
- Why you **cut the perpendicular plate before removing it** (cribriform plate, CSF leak).
- The **internal and external valves**, the Cottle test, and why small deviations there matter.
- Recognising and draining a **septal haematoma**.
- The **NOSE scale**.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Booking a routine septoplasty for a caudal, dorsal or valve problem.
- Raising the flap above the perichondrium: bleeding and tears.
- Tears on both sides at the same point.
- Separating the cartilage all the way to the dorsum.
- Twisting out bone before the upper cut.
- Leaving bent bone that drags the cartilage off the midline.
- Removing the dorsal or caudal strut.
- Forgetting to quilt, leaving dead space.
!!
