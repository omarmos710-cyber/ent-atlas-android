@stand The midface is a set of thin bony plates hung on strong vertical and horizontal buttresses. Fractures of the zygoma, orbit, naso-orbito-ethmoid complex and maxilla matter because they change the face's width, height and projection, the volume of the orbit and the bite. Repair means exposing each fracture through hidden incisions, putting every buttress back in three dimensions, fixing it with small plates, rebuilding the orbital walls to their original volume, and using the occlusion as the template for the maxilla. Most can wait for swelling to settle; an entrapped muscle in a child cannot.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Repair of orbital and midface fractures
 | Summary
Indications | Displacement causing deformity (flattened cheek, widened face, telecanthus) or function loss: diplopia, muscle entrapment, enophthalmos, malocclusion, trismus from an arch impinging on the coronoid, blocked nasolacrimal duct
Urgent | Trapdoor (white-eyed) orbital floor fracture with entrapment in a child (within 24 to 48 hours); retrobulbar haematoma (canthotomy now); frontal sinus or skull base fracture with CSF leak (with neurosurgery)
Timing otherwise | Within about 2 weeks, when swelling has settled; late repair means remodelling and harder reduction
Assessment | Trauma survey (ATLS), eye examination by ophthalmology (acuity, pupils, movements, globe), CT with coronal and sagittal reformats, dental occlusion
Approaches | Coronal; brow or upper lid crease; transconjunctival (with lateral cantholysis if needed), subciliary or mid-lid; transcaruncular for the medial wall; upper buccal sulcus; Gillies temporal approach for the arch
Fixation | Low-profile plates and screws at the frontozygomatic suture, infraorbital rim and zygomaticomaxillary buttress; orbital implants (titanium mesh, preformed or custom plates, porous polyethylene); maxillomandibular fixation for Le Fort fractures
Tray | Maxillofacial plating set, orbital implants, disimpaction forceps, Rowe's elevator, Gillies elevator, arch bars or MMF screws, corneal shields, navigation or intraoperative CT if available
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Scars** (hidden in hairline, eyelid or mouth) and **lower eyelid** malposition (ectropion, entropion).
- **Double vision** may persist or appear; **sunken eye** (enophthalmos).
- **Blindness** (rare) from bleeding behind the eye.
- **Numbness** of the cheek and upper lip (infraorbital nerve).
- **Bite problems**, further surgery, **plate infection** or removal.
- **Facial nerve** weakness of the forehead (coronal and arch approaches).
!!
## Before you start
Clear the cervical spine and head injury with the trauma team. Get an ophthalmology examination before surgery. Read the CT for each buttress, the orbital floor and medial wall (especially the posterior bulge), the naso-orbito-ethmoid region and the frontal sinus. Record the pre-injury bite (old photographs, wear facets). Plan intubation with the anaesthetist: oral, nasal or submental, avoiding nasal tubes in skull base fractures.

# The operation, stage by stage | Expose, reduce, fix, rebuild the orbit.
## Prepare and approach
Supine with a shoulder roll; corneal shields; prepare the face and wash the mouth with chlorhexidine; throat pack announced in and out. Inject the incisions. Choose the approach for each fracture: a vestibular incision 5 to 7 mm above the mucogingival junction for the buttresses and maxilla; transconjunctival or lid incisions for the floor and rim; a brow or lid crease incision for the frontozygomatic suture; a coronal flap for the frontal sinus, NOE complex, arch and upper face.
!mf 1 | The coronal flap, dissected deep to the temporoparietal fascia above the arch to protect the frontal branch.
!mf 2 | Lower eyelid approaches: transconjunctival, subciliary, mid-lid and infraorbital, and the transcaruncular approach.
!see What you should see
- Each fracture exposed in the subperiosteal plane, with the infraorbital nerve and the frontal branch protected.
!!
## Zygoma
The zygoma is a tetrapod, not a tripod: it joins the frontal bone, the maxilla at the infraorbital rim and buttress, the temporal bone at the arch and the sphenoid wing on the lateral orbital wall. Reduce it (Rowe's elevator or a hook under the body), check alignment at the sphenoid wing (the best guide to rotation), and fix it at one to three points. Isolated arch fractures are elevated through the Gillies approach (an incision in the temporal hair, through the deep temporal fascia, an elevator passed under the arch between fascia and muscle) and usually stay without fixation.
!mf 4 | A zygoma fracture with medial displacement, needing three-dimensional repositioning through several incisions.
!mf 5 | A comminuted arch, repaired as a flat bar rather than a curved arch.
!see What you should see
- Smooth contours at the frontozygomatic suture, the infraorbital rim, the buttress and the sphenoid wing.
!!
## Orbit
Repair the rim first, then the walls. Dissect subperiosteally beyond the edges of the defect on all sides until stable bone ledges are found, free the herniated orbital contents from the fracture, and place an implant that restores the posterior floor and medial bulge. Check forced duction before and after placing the implant. Repair a lateral cantholysis to the periosteum; a Frost suture can support the lower lid.
!mf 6 | A trapdoor fracture in a child with an entrapped inferior rectus, repaired with an implant positioned by navigation.
!see What you should see
- Free globe movement on forced duction, and the implant sitting on the posterior ledge without impinging on the optic canal.
!!
!danger The posterior ledge and the optic nerve
Orbital implants must rest on intact bone at the back of the defect; an implant left short causes enophthalmos and one driven too far threatens the optic nerve. Check pupils after the case and image if doubtful.
!!
## Naso-orbito-ethmoid and frontal sinus
Through a coronal flap, find the medial canthal tendon and its bone fragment, reduce the central fragment, and re-attach it by transnasal wiring to prevent telecanthus. Reduce anterior table fractures; cranialise the sinus (after removing all mucosa) when the posterior table is badly fractured with outflow tract injury, with dural repair by neurosurgery.
!mf 3 | Combined cranial, NOE, orbital and Le Fort injuries reconstructed with cranial bone grafts and transnasal canthal wiring.
!see What you should see
- A normal intercanthal distance and a sealed anterior skull base.
!!
## Le Fort fractures
Disimpact the maxilla with disimpaction forceps, place the patient into maxillomandibular fixation in the pre-injury occlusion, then plate the buttresses (nasomaxillary and zygomaticomaxillary) through an upper vestibular incision; avoid palatal incisions, which carry the blood supply. Work from stable bone: fix the mandible first if it is also broken, and use the upper face for height and projection.
!mf 7 | The Le Fort I, II and III patterns.
!see What you should see
- The pre-injury bite with the jaws released, and plated buttresses restoring facial height.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
Width, height, projection, in that order. A zygoma or arch reduced flat widens the face; a maxilla fixed in the wrong bite will never look right. Rebuild the outer frame first, then the orbit inside it.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Retrobulbar haematoma (pain, proptosis, tense orbit, falling vision)
Act | lateral canthotomy and inferior cantholysis now ; then medical treatment (mannitol, acetazolamide, steroids) ; evacuate in theatre
!!
!path Child with entrapment, bradycardia and vomiting on upgaze
Act | urgent release of the entrapped muscle (within 24 to 48 hours) ; the oculocardiac reflex resolves when the muscle is freed
!!
!path Malocclusion after surgery
Act | elastics for minor discrepancy ; return to theatre to re-plate if the segment is malpositioned
!!

# Aftercare | Eyes, bite and sinuses.
## After surgery
Check vision and pupils hourly at first after orbital surgery. Head up, ice, no nose-blowing, antibiotics as per local practice. Soft diet after Le Fort fixation. Orthoptic review for diplopia; postoperative CT for complex orbital repairs.
## Complications
Bleeding, retrobulbar haematoma, blindness, CSF leak, persistent diplopia, enophthalmos, ectropion or entropion, infraorbital numbness, temporal hollowing after a coronal flap, frontal branch injury, malocclusion, malunion, plate infection, frontal sinus mucocele.
!update Current evidence
A meta-analysis of 7 studies (1,283 patients) found that orbital fracture repair within 14 days reduced diplopia (OR 0.53) and enophthalmos (OR 0.32) compared with later repair, and that the transconjunctival approach caused less ectropion than the subciliary approach (OR 0.20) (Zhang et al., *Ann Transl Med* 2022). A 2026 meta-analysis of 15 cohort studies (2,666 patients) confirmed better recovery of diplopia and motility with repair within 7 to 14 days, especially with entrapment (Charmila et al., *J Maxillofac Oral Surg* 2026). One-point fixation gives stable results with few complications in non-comminuted zygomaticomaxillary fractures (Neto et al., *J Maxillofac Oral Surg* 2023).
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Myers: approaches by fracture, reduction at every articulation of the zygoma, orbital reconstruction with shaped implants and navigation, and the occlusion as the guide for Le Fort fractures.
K. J. Lee: the classifications (Le Fort, zygoma, NOE, frontal sinus), the white-eyed fracture in children, and the Gillies and Keen approaches to the arch.
The literature: orbital repair within 14 days for better function, and transconjunctival access for fewer lid complications.
!!
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Buttresses** of the midface and the **Le Fort** patterns.
- **Zygoma** articulations and points of fixation.

- **Retrobulbar haematoma** recognition and canthotomy.

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Missing entrapment in a child with a quiet-looking eye.
- Fixing the zygoma without checking the sphenoid wing.
- An orbital implant short of the posterior ledge.
- Plating the maxilla in the wrong bite.
!!
