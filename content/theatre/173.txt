@stand A skin graft is skin cut free of its blood supply and laid on a wound bed, which must feed it first by soaking (imbibition) and then by new vessels growing in within days. It survives only on a bed with a blood supply, held perfectly still against it, without blood or fluid between. Full-thickness grafts match facial skin and contract little; split-thickness grafts take on poorer beds and cover large areas, including the inside of the mouth, but shrink and look shiny.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Skin grafting in the head and neck
 | Summary
Indications | Defects where direct closure or a flap would distort a free margin (eyelid, ala, lip), cause donor morbidity, or be impossible; oral, oropharyngeal and maxillectomy lining; after Mohs excision; patients unfit for long operations
Full-thickness (FTSG) | Face (nose, eyelids, ear); donors: postauricular, pre-auricular, supraclavicular, upper eyelid, Burow's triangle from a flap
Split-thickness (STSG) | Large defects, scalp, mucosal lining, mastoid cavities, muscle flaps; donor: thigh, buttock or arm, by dermatome (about 0.3 to 0.45 mm)
Will not take on | Bare bone without periosteum, cartilage without perichondrium, tendon without paratenon, irradiated or infected beds (use a flap)
Anaesthesia | Local or general
Tray | Dermatome (with blades, mineral oil), mesher, scalpel, scissors, bolster materials or negative-pressure dressing, sutures
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Graft failure** in part or whole, needing dressings or regrafting.
- **Colour and texture mismatch;** split grafts can look shiny or pale.
- **Contraction,** especially of split grafts.
- **Donor site** pain and healing over 2 to 3 weeks (split) or a linear scar (full thickness).
- **Infection, haematoma.**
!!
## Before you start
Choose the graft by the site and the bed. Match colour and texture for the face (postauricular skin for the nose tip, upper eyelid skin for the lower lid). Make sure the bed bleeds and is free of infection.

# The operation, stage by stage | Bed, harvest, inset, hold.
## Prepare the bed
Achieve haemostasis with care (no char), excise granulation if heavy, and make sure perichondrium, periosteum or paratenon covers exposed cartilage or bone. Make a template of the defect.
!see What you should see
- A healthy, bleeding, flat bed without exposed avascular tissue.
!!
## Harvest a full-thickness graft
Cut the template shape from the donor site, defat the underside completely with curved scissors until the dermis is white and shiny, and close the donor site directly.
!see What you should see
- A thin graft with no fat on its underside.
!!
## Harvest a split-thickness graft
Lubricate the donor skin with mineral oil and keep it taut. Hold the dermatome at about 45 degrees, set the thickness and width, and advance it steadily with even pressure. Mesh the graft if it must cover a large or uneven area or let fluid escape.
!mf 1 | The air-powered dermatome.
!mf 2 | Harvesting from a lubricated, tensioned donor site.
!mf 3 | Meshing the graft.
!see What you should see
- Fine pinpoint bleeding on the donor site (correct depth) and a translucent, even graft.
!!
!danger Upside down
A graft placed epidermis down will not take. The dermal side is shiny and curls inwards; check before you lay it on the bed.
!!
## Inset
Lay the graft dermal side down, trim it to fit, and suture it edge to edge without stretching (a tented graft does not touch its bed). Make small pie-crust holes in a sheet graft to let blood escape.
!see What you should see
- Graft edges matching the wound edges, lying flat on the bed throughout.
!!
## Immobilise
Apply a tie-over bolster (cotton or foam with long sutures tied over it) or a negative-pressure dressing for irregular or mobile sites, kept for about 5 to 7 days. Dress the donor site with a moist, non-adherent dressing.
!see What you should see
- The graft pressed evenly onto the bed with no movement.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
Shear and fluid are what kill grafts. If a graft fails, look for movement or a haematoma before blaming the bed.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Graft dusky or lifted at the first dressing
Act | evacuate any haematoma or seroma through a small cut ; re-bolster
Lost | dressings until granulation ; regraft or flap
!!

# Aftercare | Protect for weeks.
## After surgery
Remove the bolster at 5 to 7 days; moisturise and protect from sun; massage once healed. Donor sites heal in about 2 weeks.
## Complications
Graft loss (haematoma, seroma, shear, infection, poor bed), contraction, colour mismatch, hypertrophic scarring, donor site pain or infection.
!mf 4 5 | Recipient and donor sites at 4 weeks, and a healed graft.
!update Current evidence
A meta-analysis of 12 studies (653 patients) found negative-pressure wound therapy increased split-thickness graft take and reduced reoperation compared with conventional dressings, without a significant difference in infection (Yin et al., *Int J Surg* 2018). In irregular, mobile areas, a randomised trial found better take with negative pressure than with tie-over bolsters (Cao et al., *Plast Reconstr Surg* 2022).
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Myers: full- and split-thickness grafts, dermatome technique, meshing, bolsters, and grafts combined with flaps (Burow grafts) to avoid distortion.
K. J. Lee: the stages of take (imbibition, inosculation, revascularisation) and primary versus secondary contraction.
The literature: negative-pressure dressings improve take, especially on irregular areas.
!!

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Grafting onto bare cartilage or bone.
- Leaving fat on a full-thickness graft.
- Placing the graft upside down.
- Inadequate immobilisation.
!!
