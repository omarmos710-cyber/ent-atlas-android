@stand Tumours of the upper nose that reach the cribriform plate, such as olfactory neuroblastoma, adenocarcinoma and undifferentiated carcinoma, must be removed with the skull base, dura and olfactory bulbs above them. The classic craniofacial resection does this from above through a bicoronal frontal craniotomy and from below through the face; the endoscopic endonasal resection now does it entirely through the nose in suitable tumours, with equal control for low-stage disease. Either way the defect between nose and brain must be closed in layers with vascularised tissue, the pericranial flap or the nasoseptal flap.

# Operating note | The case on one page.
## The case at a glance
!table Anterior cranial base resection
 | Summary
Indications | Benign and malignant tumours of the ethmoid, nasal cavity and cribriform plate reaching or crossing the anterior cranial base, after multidisciplinary planning
Approaches | Endoscopic endonasal transcribriform resection; open craniofacial resection (bicoronal frontal craniotomy plus lateral rhinotomy, midfacial degloving or endoscopy from below); combined
Limits of endoscopy | Extensive brain, orbital (beyond periorbita), skin or anterior frontal sinus wall involvement; lateral spread over the orbital roof
Reconstruction | Pericranial flap (open), nasoseptal flap or extracranial pericranial flap (endoscopic), multilayer fascia grafts
Anaesthesia | General; antiseizure drugs as needed; lumbar drain selectively
Tray | Craniotomy set, bicoronal set, endoscopes and navigation, drills with diamond burrs, bipolar, dural repair materials
!!
## Consent
!consent What to tell the patient
- **Loss of smell** is permanent when the olfactory bulbs are removed.
- **CSF leak, meningitis, pneumocephalus,** seizures, brain injury, stroke.
- **Bleeding,** rarely from the carotid; transfusion.
- **Vision loss** or double vision from orbital or ophthalmic artery injury.
- Open approach: **scar,** forehead numbness, frontal branch weakness, flap necrosis, cosmetic forehead changes.
- **Radiotherapy** is usually needed afterwards; recurrence is possible.
!!
## Before you start
Biopsy the tumour endoscopically. Stage it with CT (bone), MRI (dura, brain, orbit, perineural spread) and neck and chest imaging. Discuss in the skull base multidisciplinary team. Plan the reconstruction: measure the length the pericranial flap must reach, and preserve the nasoseptal flap pedicle if the septum is not involved.

# The operation, stage by stage | Open and endoscopic.
## Bicoronal exposure and pericranial flap
Infiltrate and make a bicoronal incision at least 2 cm behind the hairline, extending into the preauricular creases. Raise the scalp in the subgaleal plane, leaving the pericranium on the bone, and stay deep to the temporoparietal fascia laterally to spare the frontal branch. Raise a long U-shaped pericranial flap based on the supraorbital and supratrochlear vessels, keep it moist, and free the supraorbital bundles from their foramina.
!mf 3 | The coronal incision, extendable in front of the ear.
!mf 4 5 | Raising the pericranial flap and freeing the supraorbital nerve.
!see What you should see
- A long, intact pericranial flap with its vascular pedicle at the brow.
!!
## Frontal craniotomy and dissection
Remove a low frontal bone flap (with or without the supraorbital bar for low access). Elevate the dura off the anterior cranial fossa floor, or open it and remove involved dura and bulbs, and define the tumour's upper limit.
!mf 6 | Segments of frontal bone and orbital rim that can be removed for access.
!see What you should see
- The cribriform plate and fovea ethmoidalis from above, with the tumour's intracranial margin.
!!
## Facial or endoscopic resection from below
Through a lateral rhinotomy, midfacial degloving or the endoscope, free the tumour from the nasal and sinus walls and deliver it with the cribriform plate and ethmoid roof en bloc.
!mf 1 | The lateral rhinotomy incision.
!mf 2 | The midfacial degloving approach.
!see What you should see
- The specimen with the skull base attached, and clear margins on frozen section.
!!
## Endoscopic transcribriform resection
Debulk the nasal component to find its origin, complete bilateral ethmoidectomies, a Draf III and wide sphenoidotomies, and take the septum below the tumour. Ligate the anterior and posterior ethmoidal arteries. Drill a rectangular craniotomy: the posterior frontal table in front, the fovea ethmoidalis on each side to the laminae, and the planum in front of the optic canals behind. Open the dura, divide the falx in front of the crista galli (drilled thin), and remove dura, bulbs and tumour together; send olfactory nerve margins for frozen section.
!mf 9 | The endoscopic view of the anterior cranial base and the transcribriform resection.
!see What you should see
- A rectangular skull base and dural defect from the frontal sinus to the planum, with the tumour removed and margins clear.
!!
!danger The ophthalmic and ethmoidal arteries, and the optic canals
The posterior limit is the planum just in front of the optic canals; the lateral limits are the laminae. Ligate the ethmoidal arteries at the orbit before cutting, or they retract and bleed into the orbit.
!!
## Reconstruct
Close in layers: an inlay graft under the dura, an onlay graft, and a vascularised flap over everything (the pericranial flap laid along the floor of the anterior fossa in open surgery; a nasoseptal flap, or a pericranial flap passed through a small glabellar opening, endoscopically). Cover burr holes and bone defects for cosmesis.
!mf 7 8 | The anterior cranial base defect closed with the pericranial flap, and burr-hole covers.
!see What you should see
- A watertight, multilayer repair with a well-perfused flap covering the whole defect.
!!
!senior Senior's pearl
The reconstruction is planned before the first cut. A pericranial flap that is too short, or a nasoseptal flap whose pedicle was sacrificed during the approach, leaves a defect you cannot close.
!!

# When it goes wrong | Drills.
## Crisis drills
!path Sudden fall in conscious level after surgery
Act | CT for tension pneumocephalus or haematoma
Tension pneumocephalus | needle aspiration or burr hole with neurosurgery ; check the repair
!!
!path CSF leak after surgery
Act | head up ; consider lumbar drain
Persisting | re-explore and reinforce the repair
!!

# Aftercare | Intensive care, then radiotherapy.
## After surgery
Neurosurgical observation, antiseizure drugs if used, no nose blowing or straining, saline douching once healed, endoscopic surveillance and MRI. Adjuvant radiotherapy is usual for malignancy.
## Complications
CSF leak, meningitis, pneumocephalus, seizures, brain injury, haemorrhage, visual loss, flap necrosis, frontal branch palsy, anosmia, crusting.
!update Current evidence
A pooled analysis of 226 patients with sinonasal malignancies (mostly olfactory neuroblastoma, adenocarcinoma and undifferentiated carcinoma) found no significant difference between endoscopic and craniofacial resection for low-stage tumours (5-year overall survival about 87% endoscopic against 77% open), while data for high-stage tumours were too sparse to compare (Higgins et al., *Int Forum Allergy Rhinol* 2011). CSF leak rates after extended endonasal surgery have fallen substantially with multilayer and vascularised flap repair (Zamanipoor Najafabadi et al., *Acta Neurochir* 2021).
!!

# For the examination | What the examiner wants to hear.
## Where the books differ
!differ
Myers: the bicoronal approach with a pericranial flap, facial approaches from below, and the endoscopic transcribriform resection with its limits and multilayer reconstruction.
K. J. Lee: the tumours of the anterior skull base, staging (Kadish for olfactory neuroblastoma) and the indications for open versus endoscopic surgery.
The literature: equivalent outcomes for selected low-stage tumours; endoscopic surgery avoids facial incisions and craniotomy.
!!
## Viva essentials
!exam What the examiner is looking for
- **Indications** and the multidisciplinary work-up.
- **Open versus endoscopic** approaches and their limits.
- The **boundaries** of the transcribriform resection.
- **Reconstruction**: pericranial and nasoseptal flaps.
- **Complications**, especially CSF leak and pneumocephalus.
!!
## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Not planning the reconstruction before the approach.
- Cutting the supraorbital vessels and losing the pericranial flap.
- Missing skip lesions in the olfactory nerves.
- Leaving the ethmoidal arteries uncontrolled.
!!
