@stand An incision is a compromise between access and the scar. On the face and neck the scar is on show for life, and it sits over nerves (the marginal mandibular, the frontal branch, the great auricular, the accessory) that a careless line will cut. A good incision gives the exposure you need for the worst case, lies in or parallel to a natural crease, avoids the nerves, keeps the blood supply to every flap, and can be extended if the operation grows.
@src Oxford Textbook of Otolaryngology; K. J. Lee; Myers; Dhingra; the literature

# Principles | Where and how to cut.
## Relaxed skin tension lines {day}
The relaxed skin tension lines run at right angles to the pull of the underlying muscles and correspond to the wrinkles that appear with age (forehead creases, crow's feet, nasolabial folds, horizontal neck creases). Incisions placed in or parallel to them heal with the narrowest scars. Langer's lines, mapped in cadavers, do not always match them on the face; the relaxed skin tension lines are the ones to use. Ask the patient to frown, smile or bend the neck to show the creases before marking.
## Facial aesthetic units {junior}
The face is a set of units (forehead, eyelids, cheeks, nose, lips, chin) with their subunits. Scars on the border between units, or replacement of a whole subunit, hide better than scars across the middle of one.
## Making the incision {day}
!how A clean incision
1. Mark with a skin pen before injecting (local anaesthetic distorts landmarks), including any planned extension.
2. Stretch the skin between finger and thumb across the line.
3. Hold the scalpel like a pen for fine work or like a table knife for long incisions, and use the belly of a 10 or 15 blade, not the tip.
4. Cut perpendicular to the skin in one confident stroke to the right depth, except in hair-bearing skin, where you bevel parallel to the follicles.
5. Do not saw or make multiple shallow cuts, which leave ragged edges.
!!

# The incisions | A map of the head and neck.
!dr incisions | Common incisions. Each lies in a crease or behind a natural landmark.
## The neck {day}
- **Collar (Kocher) incision:** thyroid, parathyroid, larynx; in a natural crease about two fingerbreadths above the sternal notch.
- **Tracheostomy:** horizontal, midway between the cricoid and the sternal notch (vertical in an emergency).
- **Submandibular:** parallel to and at least 2 cm (two fingerbreadths) below the lower border of the mandible, in a crease, to stay below the marginal mandibular nerve.
- **Neck dissection:** an apron (from mastoid down and across), a hockey stick or utility incision, or two parallel MacFee incisions in an irradiated neck; avoid three-point junctions over the carotid artery, where skin necrosis exposes the vessel.
## The face and ear {day}
- **Modified Blair (parotid):** preauricular in the crease, around the lobule, then down in a neck crease; or a facelift incision hidden behind the ear and into the hairline.
- **Postauricular:** a few millimetres behind the postauricular sulcus, for mastoid and middle ear surgery; endaural and permeatal incisions for smaller ear operations.
- **Nose and midface:** columellar (open rhinoplasty), lateral rhinotomy along the side of the nose (Weber-Fergusson extends it under the eye and through the lip), sublabial (inside the upper lip) and midfacial degloving with no external scar.
- **Coronal:** in the hair from ear to ear, for frontal sinus and upper facial skeleton.
- **Eyelid and orbit:** transconjunctival, subciliary, upper lid crease.
!danger Nerves under the line
The marginal mandibular nerve runs at or below the lower border of the mandible in a substantial proportion of people, more often behind the facial artery; the frontal (temporal) branch crosses the zygomatic arch roughly from a point below the tragus to above the lateral eyebrow; the great auricular nerve crosses sternomastoid about 6 cm below the external ear canal; the accessory nerve emerges from behind sternomastoid near Erb's point. Plan incisions and planes around them.
!!

# Flaps of skin | Keeping them alive.
## Designing skin flaps {junior}
- Keep the base broad and the flap no longer than its blood supply allows; on the face a ratio of up to about 4:1 survives, on the neck less, and much less in irradiated skin.
- Raise neck flaps in the subplatysmal plane, where the vessels run with the platysma.
- Round the corners of flaps; sharp points necrose.
- Avoid incisions that leave a skin bridge between two parallel cuts too narrow to survive.
!senior Senior's pearl
Plan for the operation you might have to do, not the one you hope to do. A thyroid incision that cannot be extended to a neck dissection, or a parotid incision that cannot reach the neck, turns a complication into a second scar.
!!

# Evidence | What the studies show.
## Current evidence
!evidence
A systematic review and meta-analysis of 28 anatomical studies (1,861 sides) found that at least one branch of the marginal mandibular nerve ran below the lower border of the mandible in 39% of cases, and that the nerve often has two or more branches (Marcuzzo et al., *Clin Anat* 2020). In a cadaveric study, the lowest branch ran up to 17.7 mm below the mandible (mean about 7 mm), supporting submandibular incisions at least 2 cm below the bone (Anthony et al., *Patient Saf Surg* 2018).
!!

## Common pitfalls
!pit
- Marking after injecting local anaesthetic.
- A submandibular incision too close to the mandible.
- Three-point junctions over the carotid.
- An incision that cannot be extended.
!!
